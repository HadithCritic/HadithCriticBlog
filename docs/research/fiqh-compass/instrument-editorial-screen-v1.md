# Instrument candidate bank: construct and wording screen v1

Status: **initial editorial screen only; not a scholarly review, translation review,
reader comprehension study, psychometric evaluation, or approval to score.**
Date: 4 October 2026.  
Input: `instrument-candidate-bank-v1.json` (96 prompts; Q01–Q24 are the current
prototype set, Q25–Q96 are private candidates).  
Scope: prompt wording, proposed construct fit, role/domain consistency, likely
overlap, and whether paired directions test meaningfully contrasting claims.

## Disposition

The bank meets its structural target of eight prompts per provisional axis, with
four prompts in each scoring direction per axis. That balance is mechanical; it
does not show that the prompts measure a coherent construct or form opposing
ends of one scale. Keep Q25–Q96 out of the public quiz and all candidate
historical positions unscored. The findings below are questions for editorial,
specialist, and reader review—not decisions about doctrine.

Q01–Q24 currently appear in the prototype and respondent-axis summaries are
enabled. Their summaries must continue to be labeled provisional. Before a
release candidate, review whether each retained item has one interpretable
construct, whether reverse-direction items really contrast the same proposition,
and whether the result remains meaningful when only two answers contribute to
an axis. Do not treat the 96-item quota or the synthetic scorer tests as evidence
of instrument validity.

## Axis-by-axis findings

| Axis | Candidate items | Screen finding | Required next decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| A01 Sources of binding law | Q01–Q02, Q25–Q30 | “Qurʾānic authorization,” “accepted source,” explanation, and independent obligation are related but not identical. Q01–Q02 can elicit positions about Sunna authority, the authority of a report, and the scope of Qurʾānic authorization at once. Q26 and Q30 substantially revisit Q01. | Define the authority categories to be compared; ask separately about source type and a precisely bounded legal case. Test whether respondents distinguish authority in principle from acceptance of a particular report. |
| A02 Report sufficiency | Q03–Q04, Q31–Q36 | “Broad” or “mass” transmission and “reliability” are undefined. Several prompts combine number of routes with legal sufficiency, though these are analytically distinct. Some opposing items imply that reliability plus limited transmission is the only alternative to mass transmission. | Define the transmission categories and the relevant legal consequence. Add conditional routing or inapplicability when a respondent does not accept the report type assumed by a follow-up. |
| A03 Inherited practice | Q05–Q06, Q37–Q42 | “Relevant community,” “early,” “continuous,” and “inherited” leave the community, period, and evidence of continuity unspecified. A community’s practice, its transmission, and its independent authority can be conflated. Q05 and Q41 are near duplicates. | Specify whose practice and period are at issue and whether the question concerns evidentiary reliability, independent legal force, or reconciliation with a report. Reduce duplicate prompts. |
| A04 Consensus | Q07–Q08, Q43–Q48 | Identifying participants and evidence (Q08/Q44/Q48) is a documentation standard, not necessarily the opposite of believing consensus binding (Q07/Q43/Q45/Q47). Population, period, tacit agreement, and preservation of individual reasoning are separate questions. | Split criteria for establishing a consensus claim from the normative force of a demonstrated consensus. Specify the relevant population and whether tacit consensus is included. |
| A05 Analogy | Q09–Q10, Q49–Q54 | The positive items assume a qualified jurist and an established shared cause; the caution items range from general caution to a categorical limit in ritual. “Resemblance,” analogy, and extension into another domain are not interchangeable. | State whether the item measures acceptance of analogy as a method, standards for identifying an effective cause, or analogy in a named domain. Avoid presenting caution as the reverse of acceptance. |
| A06 Independent rational judgment | Q11–Q12, Q55–Q60 | Items mix moral knowledge, harm assessment, factual reasoning, and the authority to establish a binding religious rule. “Accepted revelatory or transmitted basis” may imply contested source assumptions. Injustice and transaction unfairness are not equivalent examples. | Separate reasoning about facts/consequences from claims about independent moral or legal authority. Define the respondent’s role and the type of conclusion at stake. |
| A07 Public welfare | Q13–Q14, Q61–Q66 | “Clear public welfare,” “otherwise unregulated,” and “accepted legal principles” lack criteria. Religious legal rules and administrative regulation are mixed (notably Q64–Q65); convenience is not an adequate counterpoint to public welfare. | Separate juristic grounds for a religious rule from public administration. Specify how a benefit is established, who is affected, and whether the scenario concerns a rule or regulation. |
| A08 Custom | Q15–Q16, Q67–Q72 | Custom may interpret language, establish facts, supply an unstated contract term, or affect an obligation’s measure. These are different legal functions. Q70 assumes custom cannot create an unaccepted term, which risks begging the question about implied agreement; Q16/Q72 use an overly broad “change outcome” frame. | Ask separate, bounded questions for interpretation, implied contractual terms, and determining fulfillment. Avoid treating those functions as one scale. |
| A09 Context and application | Q17–Q18, Q73–Q78 | The bank mixes a rule’s operative condition, changing facts, changed social circumstances, and adjustment of application. It does not consistently distinguish a changed rule from changed application. Q73/Q77 and Q76/Q78 are near duplicates. | Anchor items to a specified condition and case. Preserve the rule/application distinction and remove redundant stability formulations. |
| A10 Adherence to legal authority | Q19–Q20, Q79–Q84 | Lay following, qualified juristic reassessment, school consistency, and selecting across authorities are different respondent roles and practices. “Qualified authority,” “recognized reason,” and “consistent” are undefined. The paired items can compare unlike actors. | Route by respondent role or limit the axis to one role. Separate lay following from juristic departure and define the choice being compared. |
| A11 Uncertainty | Q21–Q22, Q85–Q90 | The prompts span an unestablished obligation, doubt after completing worship, transaction avoidance, and punishment under evidentiary uncertainty. These settings can yield different rules and actions; they are not endpoints of one undifferentiated precaution scale. | Retain domain-specific subresults or split the construct. Do not combine worship, ordinary conduct, transactions, and punishment into one historical or respondent coordinate without a defensible mapping. |
| A12 Abrogation | Q23–Q24, Q91–Q96 | The set combines chronology, conflict resolution, within-report supersession, within-scripture supersession, and cross-source supersession. Accepting one relation does not entail accepting another. Q92/Q94 overlap while Q95/Q96 introduce a distinct cross-source dispute. | Treat source relationships as categorical questions or develop separate constructs. Ask chronology and evidentiary threshold separately; do not infer a single linear position from unlike relationships. |

## Bank-wide actions

1. Replace binary “positive versus reverse-worded” assumptions with a reviewed
   item map that records construct, actor/role, domain, case assumptions,
   direction, and evidence needed for each historical comparison.
2. Identify duplicated or near-duplicated candidates before selecting a short
   form; apparent wording variety is not added measurement coverage.
3. Review categorical authority and conditional follow-up needs, especially
   report-specific items and respondent-role questions, before freezing routes.
4. Keep research issue links as discovery pointers only. Q25–Q96 have no
   reviewed evidence mapping, and topical relation does not support a historical
   answer.
5. Conduct bilingual specialist review and the roadmap’s 5–8-reader
   comprehension exercise on retained wording; log each participant’s
   interpretation and resulting keep/revise/remove decision. This screen does
   not substitute for either activity.
6. Re-run the scoring sensitivity walkthrough after item selection. If a
   provisional axis cannot be interpreted consistently or supported with
   reviewed items, report it unavailable or revise the axis instead of forcing
   a coordinate.

## Acceptance state

This screen changes no question text, evidence link, profile position, or score.
It is a pre-review checkpoint only. M3 remains open pending construct decisions,
evidence mappings, route design, specialist and bilingual review, comprehension
testing, sensitivity analysis, and a frozen release specification.
