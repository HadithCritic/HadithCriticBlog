::: passage 63:1-3 | 4:538-539

By their saying “*We bear witness that you are the Messenger of God*” they meant a witness in which their hearts agreed with their tongues. So God, mighty and majestic is He, said: they said that, “*GOD knows*” that the matter is as their saying “*you are the Messenger of God*” points to, “*and God bears witness that the hypocrites are liars*” in their saying “we bear witness” and their claim of agreement in it. Or: they are liars in it, because when it is empty of agreement it is not witness in reality, so they lie in calling it witness. Or He meant: and God bears witness that they are liars in their own eyes, because they believed that their saying “*you are the Messenger of God*” was a lie and a report against how the thing reported about stood.

{Q} What use is there in the saying of God Most High “*GOD knows that you are His messenger*”? {A} Had He said, “They said, ‘We bear witness that you are the Messenger of God,’ and God bears witness that they are liars,” it would have made one imagine that this saying of theirs was a lie. So He put between the two His saying “*GOD knows that you are His messenger*,” to remove this false impression.

{L|Under the guise of their apparent faith|ittakhadhū aymānahum junnatan} it may be meant that their saying “we bear witness that you are the Messenger of God” is an oath of their lying oaths, because witnessing runs as swearing does in the emphasis meant by it. A man says, “I bear witness,” and “I bear witness by God,” “I resolve,” and “I resolve {p|539}by God,” in place of “I swear” and “I vow.” By it Abū Ḥanīfa ﵀ found proof that “I bear witness” is an oath. It may also be a description of the hypocrites in their shielding themselves with oaths. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī read *īmānahum*, that is, the faith they showed with their tongues; and the saying of God Most High “*This is because they believed, then disbelieved*” supports it. “*Miserable indeed is what they do*,” of their hypocrisy and their turning people from the way of God. In “*evil*” is the meaning of wonder, which is making their matter great to the hearers. “*That*” points to His saying “*evil is what they used to do*,” that is: that saying, which witnesses against them that they are the worst of people in works, is because “*they believed, then disbelieved*.” Or it points to what was described of their state in hypocrisy, lying and shielding themselves with oaths, that is: all that is because they believed, then disbelieved, “*so their hearts were sealed*,” and they dared every enormity.

{Q} The hypocrites were never anything but in fixed, lasting unbelief, so what is the meaning of His saying “*they believed, then disbelieved*”? {A} There are three ways in it. One of them: “*they believed*,” that is, they uttered the word of witness and did as one does who enters Islam; “*then disbelieved*”: then their unbelief appeared after that and became clear through what was found out of their saying, “If what Muḥammad says is true, we are asses,” and their saying in the raid of Tabūk, “Does this man hope that the palaces of Chosroes and Caesar will be opened to him? Far from it!” Like it is the saying of God Most High “*They swear by GOD that they never said it, although they have uttered the word of disbelief; they have disbelieved after becoming submitters*” (Q 9:74), that is, their unbelief appeared after they had become Muslim; and like it is the saying of God Most High “*Do not apologize. You have disbelieved after having believed*” (Q 9:66). The second: “*they believed*,” that is, they uttered faith before the believers, then uttered unbelief before their devils, mocking Islam, as in the saying of God Most High “*And when they meet those who believe*” up to His saying “*we were only mocking*” (Q 2:14). The third: that the people of apostasy among them are meant. *Fa-ṭabaʿa ʿalā qulūbihim* is also read; and Zayd ibn ʿAlī read *fa-ṭabaʿa llāhu*.

::: passage 63:4-4 | 4:540-541

ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ubayy was a large, handsome man, eloquent, of fluent tongue; and a group of the hypocrites were of the like of his description. They were the chiefs of Medina, and they used to attend the sitting of the Messenger of God ﷺ and lean back in it; they had striking looks and eloquent tongues. The Prophet ﷺ and those present admired their frames and listened to their speech.

{Q} What is the meaning of His saying “*as though they were propped-up timbers*”? {A} In their leaning back, while they are nothing but bodies empty of faith and good, they were likened to timbers propped against the wall; and because when timber is put to use it is in a roof or a wall or some other place of use, and while it is left idle and unused it is propped against the wall, so they were likened to it in being of no use. By the propped-up timbers may also be meant idols carved from wood and propped against the walls; they were likened to them in their fine forms and the little good in them. The address in “*When you see them, you may be impressed by their looks*” is to the Messenger of God, or to anyone who is addressed. *Yusmaʿ* is also read, built for the passive. The place of “*as though they were timbers*” is the nominative, on: they are as though they were timbers; or it is a new sentence with no place in the case system. *Khushb* is also read, the plural of *khashaba*, like *badana* and *budn*; and *khashab*, like *thamara* and *thamar*; and *khushub*, like *madara* and *madar*, and it is in the reading of Ibn ʿAbbās. From al-Yazīdī: he said of *khushub* that it is the plural of *khashbāʾ*, and *al-khashbāʾ* is the timber whose inside has rotted; they were likened to it in their hypocrisy and the corruption of their insides.

{L|Against them|ʿalayhim} the second object of “*they think*,” that is: they think every shout falls on them and harms them, from their cowardice and their alarm and the terror in their hearts. When a crier cried out in the camp, or a beast broke loose, or a stray was cried for, they thought it was an attack on them. It is also said: they were in dread that God would send down about them what would tear away their covers and make their blood and wealth lawful. From this al-Akhṭal took:

> You kept thinking everything after them / {p|541}was horsemen charging at them, and men.

The pause is made on “*against them*,” and one begins, “*These are the real enemies*,” that is, those complete in enmity; because the worst of enemies is the enemy who dissembles, who grins at you while under his ribs is a festering disease. “*Beware of them*,” and do not be deceived by their outside. “*These are the real enemies*” may also be the second object, as if you had dropped the pronoun.

{Q} Then it ought to be said, “it is the enemy.” {A} It is looked at from the side of the predicate, as was mentioned on “*this is my lord*” (Q 6:76); or a dropped first term of annexation is implied, on: they think every people of a shout.

{L|GOD condemns them|qātalahumu llāhu} a prayer against them, and asking of His own self that He curse them and disgrace them; or a teaching to the believers to pray against them with that. “*They have deviated*” (*annā yuʾfakūn*, “how they are turned away!”): how they swerve from the truth! In wonder at their ignorance and their error.

::: passage 63:7-8 | 4:541-543

It is related that when the Messenger of God ﵌ met the Banū l-Muṣṭaliq at al-Muraysīʿ, a water of theirs, routed them and killed some of them, there was a crush at the water between Jahjāh ibn Saʿīd, a hired man of ʿUmar who was leading his horse, {p|542}and Sinān al-Juhanī, an ally of ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ubayy, and they fought. Jahjāh cried out, “*Help, emigrants!*” and Sinān, “*Help, Anṣār!*” Juʿāl, of the poor of the emigrants, helped Jahjāh and slapped Sinān. ʿAbd Allāh said to Juʿāl, “*And you are there too!*” and said, “*We kept company with Muḥammad only to be slapped. By God, our case and theirs is only as he said: ‘Fatten your dog and it will eat you.’ By God, if we return to Medina, the mightier will drive out from it the more abased*.” By the mightier he meant himself, and by the more abased the Messenger of God ﷺ. Then he said to his people: “*What have you done to yourselves? You let them settle in your land and shared your wealth with them. By God, had you held back the spare food from Juʿāl and his like, they would not have ridden on your necks, and they would soon have moved away from you. So do not spend on them until they break away from around Muḥammad*.” Zayd ibn Arqam heard that, and he was a youth, and said, “*You, by God, are the abased, the few, the hated among your people; and Muḥammad is in might from the Merciful and strength from the Muslims*.” ʿAbd Allāh said, “*Be quiet; I was only joking*.” Zayd told the Messenger of God. ʿUmar said, “*Let me strike off the head of this hypocrite, Messenger of God*.” He said, “*Then many noses would quiver in Yathrib*.” He said, “*If you dislike that an emigrant should kill him, command an Anṣārī to do it*.” He said, “*And how will it be when people say that Muḥammad kills his companions?*” He ﵊ said to ʿAbd Allāh, “*Are you the one whose speech has reached me?*” He said, “*By God who sent down the Book to you, I said nothing of that, and Zayd is a liar*”; and that is the saying of God Most High “*They have taken their oaths as a shield*.” Those present said, “*Messenger of God, he is our elder and our chief; do not believe a boy’s words against him; perhaps he was mistaken*.” It is related that the Messenger of God said to him, “*Perhaps you were angry with him?*” He said, “*No*.” He said, “*Perhaps your hearing missed?*” He said, “*No*.” He said, “*Perhaps it was made to seem so to you?*” He said, “*No*.” When it was revealed, the Messenger of God overtook Zayd from behind, rubbed his ear, and said, “*Your ear was true, boy; God has confirmed you and given the lie to the hypocrites*.” When ʿAbd Allāh wanted to enter Medina, his son Ḥubāb stood in his way (he was ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAbd Allāh; the Messenger of God changed his name and said, “*Ḥubāb is the name of a devil*”), and he was sincere. He said, “*Back! By God, you will not enter it until you say that the Messenger of God is the mightier and I am the more abased*.” He stayed held in his hand until the Messenger of God commanded him to let him go. It is related that he said to him, “{p|543}*If you do not confess might to God and His Messenger, I will strike off your head*.” He said, “*Woe to you, would you do it?*” He said, “*Yes*.” When he saw that he was in earnest, he said, “*I bear witness that might belongs to God and His Messenger and the believers*.” The Messenger of God said to his son, “*May God reward you well on behalf of His Messenger and the believers*.” When ʿAbd Allāh’s lie became clear, it was said to him, “*Severe verses have been revealed about you; go to the Messenger of God ﷺ so that he may ask forgiveness for you*.” He turned his head and said, “*You commanded me to believe and I believed, and you commanded me to give the alms tax on my wealth and I gave it; there is nothing left but that I prostrate to Muḥammad*.” Then was revealed: “*And when it is said to them, ‘Come, the Messenger of God will ask forgiveness for you’*.” He stayed only a few days before he fell ill and died.

{L|It is the same for them|sawāʾun ʿalayhim} asking forgiveness and its absence, because they pay no attention to it and take no account of it, for their unbelief; or because God will not forgive them. *Istaghfarta* is also read, with the interrogative particle dropped because the balancing *am* points to it. Abū Jaʿfar read *āstaghfarta*, lengthening the interrogative *hamza* to make it plain and clear, not changing the *hamza* of joining into an *alif*, as in *āl-siḥru* (Q 10:81) and *āllāhu* (Q 10:59). “*Perhaps they abandon him*” (*yanfaḍḍū*): they scatter. *Yunfiḍū* is also read, from *anfaḍa l-qawmu*, when their provisions were used up; and its reality is: the time came for them to shake out their provision bags. “*However, GOD possesses the treasures of the heavens and the earth*”: in His hand are provisions and portions, and He provides for them from them, even if the people of Medina refuse to spend on them. But ʿAbd Allāh and his like are ignorant; “*they do not understand*” that, so they rave with what Satan makes fair to them. *La-yakhrujanna l-aʿazzu minhā l-adhalla* is also read, with *fatḥa* on the *yāʾ*; and *la-yukhrajanna*, built for the passive. Al-Ḥasan and Ibn Abī ʿAbla read *la-nukhrijanna*, with *nūn*, and *al-aʿazza* and *al-adhalla* in the accusative; and its meaning is: as the going out of the more abased, or the driving out of the more abased, or like the more abased.

{L|All dignity belongs to GOD|wa-li-llāhi l-ʿizzatu} dominance and strength; and to whomever God has made mighty and supported, of His Messenger and the believers, and they are the ones singled out for it; as abasement and lowliness belong to Satan and his kind, the unbelievers and hypocrites. From one of the righteous women, who was in a shabby state: “*Am I not on Islam? It is the might with which there is no abasement, and the wealth with which there is no poverty*.” From al-Ḥasan ibn ʿAlī ﵄: a man said to him, “*People claim there is haughtiness in you*.” He said, “*It is not haughtiness, but might*,” and recited this verse.

::: passage 63:9-9 | 4:543-544

{p|544}{L|Do not be distracted|lā tulhikum} let not busy you “*your wealth*,” and dealing in it, striving to manage its affairs, throwing yourselves into seeking its growth by trade and drawing income, seeking the young of livestock, taking pleasure in it and enjoying its benefits; “*and your children*,” your delight in them, your tender care for them, providing for their needs, and arranging what will set right their living in your lifetime and after your death. You have known the measure of the benefit of wealth and children, and that it is the slightest and lowest of things beside what is with God; “*from remembering GOD*,” and preferring it over them. “*Those who do this*”: He means being busied with this world away from religion, “*are the losers*” in their trade, since they sold the great and lasting for the paltry and passing. It is said: the remembrance of God is the five prayers. From al-Ḥasan: all the obligations, as though He said: from obedience to God. It is also said: the Qurʾān. From al-Kalbī: striving with the Messenger of God ﷺ.

::: passage 63:10-11 | 4:544-545

*Min* in “*of what We have provided you*” is partitive, and what is meant is the obligatory spending. “*Before death comes to you*”: before he sees the signs of death and beholds what leaves him with no hope of respite, his throat grows tight, spending becomes impossible for him, and the time of acceptance passes; then he grieves over his withholding and bites his fingertips over losing what had been within his power. From Ibn ʿAbbās ﵁: “*Give alms before the power of death comes down on you, when repentance is not accepted and work does not profit*.” And from him: “*What keeps one of you, when he has wealth, from paying the alms tax, and when he is able to make the pilgrimage, from making it, before death comes to him and he asks his Lord for a return and is not given it?*” And from him: it was revealed about those who withhold the alms tax; and by God, had he seen good he would not have asked for a return. It was said to him, “*Do you not fear God? Do the believers ask for a return?*” He said, “*Yes, I will recite Qurʾān to you about it*”; he means that it was revealed about the believers, and they are the ones addressed by it. So too from al-Ḥasan: “*No one who did not pay the alms tax, did not fast and did not make the pilgrimage, but asks for a return*.” From ʿIkrima: it was revealed about the people of the qibla.

{L|If only You could delay this|law lā akhkhartanī} *akhkhartani* is also read. He means: why did You not delay my death “*to a near term*,” to a short time, “*I would then be charitable*.” Ubayy read *fa-atasaddaqa*, on the original form. *Wa-akun* is read, coordinated on the place of “*I would then be charitable*,” as though it were said: if You delay me, I will give alms and be. Whoever read *wa-akūna*, in the accusative, did so by the wording. ʿUbayd ibn ʿUmayr read *wa-akūnu*, on: and I will be, a promise from him to be righteous. “*GOD never delays*” is a denial of delay by way of emphasis, whose meaning is that what is denied is contrary to wisdom. The meaning is: when you know that delaying death from its time is a thing there is no way to, that it will rush on without fail, and that God knows your works and repays {p|545}them, of withholding what is obligatory and other things, nothing remains but to hasten to discharge the obligations and to make ready to meet God. *Taʿmalūna* is read with *tāʾ* and with *yāʾ*.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-Munāfiqīn is free of hypocrisy*.”
