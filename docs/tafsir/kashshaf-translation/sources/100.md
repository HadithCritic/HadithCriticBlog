surah: 100
title: Sūrat al-ʿĀdiyāt (100)
revelation: Meccan; it is also said Medinan. Eleven verses. [Revealed after al-ʿAṣr (103).]

::: passage 100:1-11 | 4:786-789

He swears by the horses of the raiders as they run and pant. *Al-ḍabḥ* is the sound of their breathing when they run. From Ibn ʿAbbās: he imitated it and said, “*Aḥ, aḥ*.” ʿAntara said: “*And the horses toil, panting in the pools of death, a panting*.” *Ḍabḥan* is accusative through [an implied] *yaḍbaḥna ḍabḥan* (“they pant a panting”), or through *al-ʿādiyāt*, as if it were said *al-ḍābiḥāt* (“those that pant”), because panting comes with running; or it is circumstantial, that is, “panting.”

{L|Igniting sparks|fa-l-mūriyāt} they strike the *ḥubāḥib* fire, which {p|787}is what flashes from their hooves. “*Sparks*” (*qadḥan*, “striking”): striking, knocking the stones with their hooves. *Al-qadḥ* is knocking, and *al-īrāʾ* is bringing out fire. You say *qadaḥa fa-awrā* (“he struck and it kindled”) and *qadaḥa fa-aṣlada* (“he struck and it did not kindle”). *Qadḥan* is accusative for the same reason as *ḍabḥan*.

{L|Invading (the enemy)|fa-l-mughīrāt} they raid the enemy “*by morning*” (*ṣubḥan*), at the time of dawn. “*Striking terror therein*” (*fa-atharna bihi naqʿan*, “and raise with it dust”): at that time they stirred up dust. “*Penetrating to the heart*” (*fa-wasaṭna bihi*, “and cleave with it into the midst”): at that time, or with the dust, that is, they cleaved into the midst of the throng with the dust; or they cleaved, wrapped in it, into the midst of *jamʿan* (“a throng”), one of the throngs of the enemy. *Wasaṭahu* has the sense of *tawassaṭahu* (“he went into the middle of it”). It is also said that the pronoun refers to the place of the raid, and it is also said that it refers to the running indicated by *wa-l-ʿādiyāt*.

By *naqʿ* shouting may also be meant, from his saying ﵇, “*so long as there is no naqʿ and no laqlaqa*,” and the saying of Labīd: “*So when the cry of a true alarm is raised (yanqaʿ)*.” That is, they stirred up shouting and uproar among those they raided. Abū Ḥaywa read *fa-aththarna*, with doubling, in the sense “they made dust appear with it,” because *taʾthīr* carries the sense of showing; or else *thawwarna* was turned into *wattharna* and the *wāw* into a *hamza*. *Fa-wassaṭna* is also read, with doubling, to make the verb transitive, the *bi-* being added for emphasis, as in His saying *wa-utū bihi* (Q 2:25); it intensifies *wasaṭna*.

{From|Ibn ʿAbbās} “*I was sitting in the Ḥijr when a man came and asked me about ‘the racers, panting,’ and I explained them as horses. He went to ʿAlī, who was beneath the water-station of Zamzam, asked him, and told him what I had said. He said, ‘Call him to me.’ When I stood over him he said, ‘You give people rulings on what you have no knowledge of! By God, the first raid in Islam was Badr, {p|788}and we had only two horses with us: a horse of al-Zubayr and a horse of al-Miqdād. “The racers, panting” are the camels from ʿArafa to al-Muzdalifa, and from al-Muzdalifa to Minā.’*” If the report is sound, *ḍabḥ* has been borrowed for camels, as *mashāfir* (a camel’s lips) and *ḥāfir* (hoof) have been borrowed for humans, *shafatān* (lips) for the foal, *thafr* for the cow, and the like. It is also said that *ḍabḥ* belongs only to the horse, the dog and the fox. It is also said that *ḍabḥ* means *ḍabʿ*: one says *ḍabaḥat al-ibil* and *ḍabaʿat* when camels stretch out their forelegs (*aḍbāʿ*) on the march; but this is not established. *Jamʿ* is al-Muzdalifa.

{Q} To what is *fa-atharna* (“and raise”) joined? {A} To the verb whose place the active participle has taken, because the meaning is: by those that ran, then kindled, then raided, then raised.

{L|Unappreciative|kanūd} *Al-kanūd* is the ungrateful; one says *kanada l-niʿma kunūdan* (“he was ungrateful for the favor”). From it Kinda was named, because he was ungrateful to his father and left him. From al-Kalbī: *al-kanūd* in the dialect of Kinda is the disobedient, in that of Banū Mālik the miser, and in that of Muḍar and Rabīʿa the ungrateful. The meaning is that he is especially ungrateful for his Lord’s favor, because his failure to thank a favor from someone other than God is a smaller failure, the favor being small; the greatest favor a human receives from his like is that of his parents, and even the greatest of it is small and slight beside the least favor of God.

{L|He|wa-innahu} that is, the human being, “*to this fact*” (*ʿalā dhālika*), to his ingratitude, “*bears witness*” (*la-shahīd*, “a witness”): he bears witness against himself and cannot deny it, so plain is his case. It is also said: and God is a witness to his ingratitude, by way of threat.

{L|Material things|al-khayr} wealth, from the saying of God Most High “*you shall write a will*” (*in taraka khayran*, “if he leaves behind good,” Q 2:180). *Al-shadīd* is the miser, the tight-fisted; one says *fulān shadīd* and *mutashaddid*. Ṭarafa said: “*I see death choosing the noble, and picking out the finest of the wealth of the gross and tight-fisted (al-mutashaddid)*.” That is: and he, because of love of wealth and because spending it weighs on him, is miserly and tight-fisted. Or by *shadīd* He meant strong: for love of wealth and for preferring this world and seeking it he is strong and able, while for love of worshiping God and thanking His favor he is weak and sluggish. You say *huwa shadīd li-hādhā l-amr* (“he is strong for this matter”), and *qawiyy lahu*, when he is able to handle it. Or He meant that he is not eager and open-handed for love of good things, but tight and withdrawn.

{L|Are opened|buʿthira} raised up. It is also read *buḥthira* and *buḥitha*; and *baḥthara* and *ḥaṣṣala*, both verbs in the active; and *ḥaṣala*, without doubling. The meaning of “*are brought out*” (*ḥuṣṣila*) is: gathered in {p|789}the records, that is, made manifest, collected and gathered together. It is also said: its good was separated from its bad; hence a sieve is called *al-muḥaṣṣil*.

The meaning of His knowing them on the Day of Resurrection is His requiting them according to the measure of their deeds, because that is the effect of His being aware of them. Abū l-Sammāl read *inna rabbahum bihim yawmaʾidhin khabīr*.

{From|the Messenger of God ﵌} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-ʿĀdiyāt will be given a reward of ten good deeds for every person who spent the night at al-Muzdalifa and was present at Jamʿ*.”
