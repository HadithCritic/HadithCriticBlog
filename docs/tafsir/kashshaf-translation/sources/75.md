::: passage 75:1-6 | 4:657-660

{p|658}Bringing the negating *lā* in before the verb of the oath is widespread in their speech and their poems. Imruʾ al-Qays said:

> No, by your father, daughter of al-ʿĀmirī, / the people shall not claim that I flee.

And Ghawtha ibn Salmā said:

> Umāma has called out the departure, / to grieve me; but no, by you, I do not care.

Its use is to stress the oath. They said that it is extra, like it in “*Thus, the followers of previous scripture should know*” (*li-allā yaʿlama*, Q 57:29), and in his saying, “*in a well of no return* (lā ḥūr) *he went by night, and did not know*.” They objected to it that it is only added in the middle of speech, not at its start; and they answered that the Qurʾān is in the rule of one sūra, one part joined to another. The objection is sound, because it has only fallen as extra in the middle of speech; but the answer is not right. Do you not see how Imruʾ al-Qays added it at the opening of his ode? The right face is to say: it is for negation. And the meaning in that is that one swears by a thing only to make it great; the saying of God Most High points you to it, “*I swear by the positions of the stars. This is an oath, if you only knew, that is awesome*” (*fa-lā uqsimu bi-mawāqiʿi l-nujūm*, Q 56:75–76). So it is as though, by bringing in the particle of negation, He says: My making it great by swearing by it is as no making great; He means that it deserves more than that. It is also said: *lā* is a negation of speech {p|659}and a rejection of it before the oath, as though they denied the raising, and it was said, “No,” that is, the matter is not as you have said; then it was said, “I swear by the Day of Resurrection.”

{Q} In the saying of God Most High “*Never indeed, by your Lord; they are not believers*” (Q 4:65), and in the verses you have recited, what is sworn on is negated. So why do you not claim that the *lā* before the oath was added to prepare for the negation after it and stress it, and estimate what is sworn on, dropped here, as negated, as in your saying, “No, I swear by the Day of Resurrection, you will not be left to wander”? {A} If the matter were limited to negation, without affirmation, this saying would have a way through; but it is not limited. Do you not see how “*I solemnly swear by this town*” (*lā uqsimu bi-hādhā l-balad*) was met by His saying “*We created the human being*” (Q 90:1–4); and likewise “*I swear by the positions of the stars*” (*fa-lā uqsimu bi-mawāqiʿi l-nujūm*) by His saying “*This is an honorable Quran*” (Q 56:75–77)? *La-uqsimu* is also read, on the view that the *lām* is that of the fresh start, and “*I swear*” is the predicate of a dropped subject, meaning: I indeed swear. They said: it is supported by its being in the Imām codex without *alif*.

{L|By the blaming soul|bi-l-nafsi l-lawwāmati} by the God-fearing soul that blames the souls on it, that is, on the Day of Resurrection, for their falling short in fear of God. Or by the one that does not stop blaming itself, even when it strives in doing good. From al-Ḥasan: “*You see the believer only blaming himself; and the unbeliever goes straight on, not reproaching himself*.” It is also said: it is the one that blames itself on that day for leaving off doing more, if it did good, and for neglect, if it did evil. It is also said: it is the soul of Adam, which did not stop blaming itself for its deed by which it went out of the Garden. The answer to the oath is what His saying “*Does the human being think that we will not reconstruct his bones?*” points to, and it is: you will surely be raised. Qatāda read *an lan tujmaʿa ʿiẓāmuhu*, built for the passive. And the meaning is: We will gather them after their scattering and their turning to rotten bone and dust mixed with earth, and after the winds have blown them and sent them flying to the far parts of the earth. It is said: ʿAdī ibn Abī Rabīʿa, the in-law of al-Akhnas ibn Sharīq (they are the two of whom the Messenger of God ﷺ used to say, “*O God, spare me my two bad neighbors*”), said to the Messenger of God ﷺ, “*Muḥammad, tell me about the Day of Resurrection: when will it be, and how is its matter?*” The Messenger of God ﷺ told him, and he said, “*If I saw that day with my eyes I would not believe you, Muḥammad, nor believe in it. Or will God gather the bones?*” And it was revealed.

{L|Yes|balā} affirmed what is after the negation, which is the gathering; so it is as though it were said: yes, We will gather them. “*Able*” is a circumstantial clause from the pronoun in “*We gather*,” that is: We gather the bones, able to put all of them together and bring them back to the first build, even to shaping his fingertips, that is, his fingers, which are his extremities and the last thing by which his making is completed. Or able to shape his fingertips and join his finger bones, for all their smallness and fineness, one to another as they were at first, without lack or unevenness; so how about the large bones? It is also said: its meaning is, yes, We gather them, and We are able to make the fingers of his hands {p|660}and feet even, that is, make them level, one thing, like the camel’s foot and the ass’s hoof, not separate; so he could not do with them anything of what he does with his separate fingers, possessed of joints and tips, of the kinds of work, spreading and grasping, and managing what he wants of his needs. *Qādirūna* is also read, that is: We are able.

{L|But the human being tends|bal yurīdu} is joined to “*Does the human being think*.” So it may be, like it, a question; or an affirmation, on the view that it turns from one thing asked about to another; or turns from a thing asked about to an affirmed one. “*To believe only what he sees in front of him*” (*li-yafjura amāmahu*, “to go on in wickedness before him”): to keep on his wickedness in the times that are before him and in what he faces of time, not giving it up. From Saʿīd ibn Jubayr ﵁: he puts the sin first and puts off repentance, saying, “*I will repent, I will repent*,” until death comes to him in the worst of his states and the worst of his works. “*He doubts*” (*yasʾalu*, “he asks”): the asking of one who makes trouble and thinks the Hour far off, in his saying *ayyāna yawmu l-qiyāma* (“when is the Day of Resurrection?”) Like it is “*They challenge: ‘When will this prophecy come to pass’*” (Q 10:48).

{L|Once the vision is sharpened|bariqa l-baṣaru} it is bewildered with fright. Its origin is from *bariqa l-rajulu*, when he looked at the lightning and his sight was stunned. *Baraqa* is also read, from glittering (*barīq*), that is, it gleams from how hard it stares. Abū l-Sammāl read *balaqa*, when it opened and parted; one says, *balaqa l-bābu*, *ablaqtuhu* and *balaqtuhu*: I opened it. “*And the moon is eclipsed*”: and its light goes, or it goes itself. *Wa-khusifa* is also read, built for the passive. “*And the sun and the moon crash into one another*”: when God makes them both rise from the west. It is also said: they are joined in the going of the light. It is also said: they are joined, black and rolled up, as though they were two hamstrung bulls in the Fire. It is also said: they are joined, then thrown into the sea, and become God’s greatest fire. “*The flight*,” *al-mafarr* with *fatḥa*, is the verbal noun; and with *kasra* the place. It may also be a verbal noun, like *al-marjiʿ*; and both are read. “*Absolutely*”: a restraint from seeking flight. “*There is no escape*” (*lā wazar*, “no refuge”): no shelter. Everything you take shelter in, a mountain or other, and get free by, is your *wazar*.

{L|To your Lord|ilā rabbika} only, “*on that day, is the final destiny*” of the servants, that is, their coming to rest. He means that they are not able to come to rest at another than Him and make their way to him. Or to His judgment the matters of the servants go back; none other than He judges in them, as in His saying “*To whom belongs all sovereignty on that day?*” (Q 40:16). Or to your Lord is their resting place, that is, the place of their abode, of Garden or Fire; that is, that is left to His will: whom He wills {p|661}He brings into the Garden, and whom He wills He brings into the Fire. “*Of what he put forward*” of a work he did, and of what he “*put back*” of it, which he did not do. Or of what he put forward of his wealth and gave in charity, or what he put back and left behind. And of what he put forward of a work of good and evil, and what he put back, of a good or evil custom that was acted on after him. From Mujāhid: of the first of his work and its last. Like it is “*then inform them of everything they had done. GOD has recorded everything, while they have forgotten it*” (Q 58:6).

{L|His own judge|baṣīratun} a clear proof, described as seeing by figure, as the signs are described as making see in His saying “*When our miracles were presented to them, clear and profound*” (*mubṣiratan*, “making see,” Q 27:13). Or a seeing eye. And the meaning is that he is told of his works; and if he is not told, there is in him what suffices instead of telling; because he is a witness against them for what they did, because his limbs speak of that: “*The day will come when their own tongues, hands, and feet will bear witness to everything they had done*” (Q 24:24). “*No excuses will be accepted*” (*wa-law alqā maʿādhīrahu*, “even if he puts forward his excuses”): even if he brings every excuse with which he makes excuse for himself and argues for himself. From al-Ḍaḥḥāk: even if he lets down his curtains; and he said: *al-maʿādhīr* are the curtains, the singular being *miʿdhār*. If that is sound, it is because it keeps one hidden from being seen, as the excuse keeps punishment from the sinner.

{Q} Is not the rule for *maʿdhira* that it be gathered as *maʿādhir*, not *maʿādhīr*? {A} *Al-maʿādhīr* is not the plural of *maʿdhira*; it is only a collective noun for it; and like it is *al-manākīr* for *al-munkar*.

::: passage 75:16-25 | 4:661-663

The pronoun in “*with it*” is for the Qurʾān. When the revelation was being given to him, the Messenger of God ﷺ would vie with Gabriel in reciting, and not wait for him to finish it, hurrying to learn it by heart and fearing that it would slip from him. So he was commanded to keep silent for it, giving it his heart and his hearing, until its revelation was finished to him; then to follow it with study until it was fixed in him. And the meaning is: do not move your tongue with reciting the revelation while Gabriel ﵊ is reciting, “*to hasten it*,” to take it in haste, and so that it does not slip from you. Then He gave the reason for the prohibition of haste by His saying “*Upon Us is its gathering*” in your breast and the fixing of its recitation on your tongue. “*Once we recite it*”: He made Gabriel’s reciting His reciting; and *al-qurʾān* is the reciting. “*You shall follow such a Quran*” (*fa-ttabiʿ qurʾānahu*, “then follow its recitation”): then come after him in it and do not go along with him; and set your mind at rest that it will not remain unlearned, for We stand guarantor for making you learn it. “*Then upon Us is its explaining*,” when anything of its meanings is hard for you; as though he used to hasten in learning by heart and in asking about the meaning together, as you see some of those eager for knowledge. And like it is “*Do not rush into uttering the Quran before it is revealed to you*” (Q 20:114).

{L|Indeed|kallā} a restraint of the Messenger of God ﷺ from the habit of haste and a disavowal of it in him, and an urging to deliberateness and calm. He went to great lengths in that by following it with His saying {p|662}“*Indeed, you love this fleeting life*,” as though He said: rather, you, sons of Adam, because you were created of haste and stamped with it, hasten in everything; and from that you love the hasty life “*While disregarding the Hereafter*.” It is also read with *yāʾ*, and that is more forceful.

{Q} How is His saying “*Do not move your tongue*,” to its end, connected with the mention of the Resurrection? {A} Its connection with it is by way of this: to slip out from it to the rebuke for loving the hasty life and leaving off concern for the Hereafter.

The face is an expression for the whole person. *Al-nāḍira* is from “*the joy of bliss*” (*naḍrata l-naʿīm*, Q 83:24). “*Looking at their Lord*”: they look to their Lord alone, and do not look to any other; and this is the meaning of putting the object first. Do you not see His saying “*To your Lord, on that day, is the final destiny*” (Q 75:12), “*To your Lord, on that day, is the summoning*” (Q 75:30), “*all matters are controlled by GOD*” (*ilā llāhi taṣīru l-umūr*, Q 42:53), “*To GOD is the ultimate destiny*” (Q 3:28), “*to Him you will be returned*” (Q 2:245), “*I have put my trust in Him. To Him I have totally submitted*” (*ʿalayhi tawakkaltu wa-ilayhi unību*, Q 11:88): how the putting first in them points to the meaning of singling out? And it is known that they look at things that cannot be counted and do not come under number, in a gathering place in which all the creatures are gathered; for the believers are onlookers on that day, because they are the safe ones on whom there is no fear and who do not grieve. So that their looking should be singled out to Him, if He were to be looked at, is impossible; so it must be taken in a meaning with which the singling out is sound. And the one with which it is sound is that it be from people’s saying, “I am looking to So-and-so, what he will do for me,” by which you mean the meaning of expecting and hoping. From it is the saying of the poet:

> And when I look to you, what a king! / and the sea falls short of you, you have increased me in favors.

And I heard a beggar woman of the Sarāt at Mecca, at noon, when people shut their doors and go to their resting places, say: “*My poor little eye is looking a little to God and to you*.” And the meaning is that they do not expect blessing and honor except from their Lord, as in this world they feared and hoped only Him. *Al-bāsir* is the one who frowns hard, and *al-bāsil* is harder {p|663}than it; but it has come mostly to be used of the brave man when his grimace is hard. “*Expecting*” (*taẓunnu*, “it thinks”): it expects that a deed will be done to it that is, in its hardness and hideousness, “*the worst*” (*fāqira*, “a back-breaker”): a calamity that breaks the vertebrae of the back; as the radiant faces expected that every good would be done to them.

::: passage 75:26-30 | 4:663-664

{L|Indeed|kallā} a restraint from preferring this world to the Hereafter; as though it were said: hold back from that, and take notice of the death that is before you, at which the hasty life is cut off from you and you move to the deferred one in which you will stay forever. The pronoun in “*reaches*” (*balaghat*, “it reaches”) is for the soul, though it has not been mentioned, because the speech it falls in points to it; as Ḥātim said:

> Māwī, what does wealth avail the young man / when one day it rattles and the breast is tight with it?

And the Arabs say “it has let loose,” meaning “the rain has come”; and you hardly hear them mention the sky. “*The throat*” (*al-tarāqī*, “the collarbones”): the bones that flank the hollow of the throat on the right and the left. He reminded them of the hardness of death, which is the first stage of the Hereafter, when the spirit reaches the collarbones and its going out is near; and those present with its owner, who is the dying man, say to one another, “*Who is a charmer?*” Which of you will charm him from what is on him? It is also said: it is of the speech of the angels of death: which of you will go up (*yarqā*) with his spirit, the angels of mercy or the angels of punishment? “*He knows*” (*ẓanna*, “he thinks”), the dying man, “*it is the end*” (*annahu l-firāq*, “that it is the parting”): that this which has come down on him is the parting from the beloved world. “*Each leg will lay motionless next to the other leg*” (*wa-ltaffati l-sāqu bi-l-sāq*, “and shank is wound with shank”): his shank is wound with his shank and twisted on it at the shiver of death. From Qatāda: his two feet have died, and they do not carry him, though he used to go about much on them. It is also said: the hardness of parting from this world with the hardness of the coming on of {p|664}the Hereafter, on the view that the shank is a proverb for hardness. From Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyab: they are his two shanks when they are wrapped in his shrouds. “*The summoning*” (*al-masāq*, “the driving”): that is, he is driven to God and to His judgment.

{L|For he observed neither the charity, nor the contact prayers (Salat)|fa-lā ṣaddaqa wa-lā ṣallā} He means the man in His saying “*Does man reckon that We will not gather his bones?*” Do you not see His saying “*Does man reckon that he will be left to wander?*” It is joined to “*He asks, ‘When is the Day of Resurrection?’*”, that is: he does not believe in the raising, so he neither affirmed the Messenger and the Qurʾān nor prayed. It may also be meant: he did not give his wealth in charity, in the sense of: he did not pay its alms. It is also said: it was revealed about Abū Jahl. “*He acted arrogantly*” (*yatamaṭṭā*, “swaggering”): strutting. Its origin is *yatamaṭṭaṭu*, that is, he stretches himself, because the one who struts stretches his steps. It is also said: it is from *al-maṭā*, which is the back, because he twists it. In the ḥadīth: “*When my community walks with a swagger and Persia and Rome serve them, their strife will be set among themselves*.” He means: he denied the Messenger of God ﷺ, turned away from him and shunned him, then went to his people strutting, in pride at that. “*Woe to you*” is in the sense of “woe be to you”; and it is a prayer against him, that what he hates may be close to him.

{L|Then He created|fa-khalaqa} then He measured, *fa-sawwā* (“and shaped”): and made even. *Minhu* (“from him”): from man, “*male or female*” (*al-zawjayn*, “the two pairs”): the two kinds. “*Is He then*” (*a-laysa dhālika*, “is not that One”), who brought about this bringing into being, “*able*” to bring back? It is related that the Messenger of God ﷺ, {p|665}when he recited it, would say, “*Glory be to You! Yes*.”

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-Qiyāma, I and Gabriel will bear witness for him on the Day of Resurrection that he believed in the Day of Resurrection*.”
