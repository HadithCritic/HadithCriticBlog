::: passage 79:1-14 | 4:692-695

He, glory be to Him, swore by the bands of angels that pull the spirits out of the bodies; by the bands that draw them out, {p|693}that is, bring them out, from *nashaṭa l-dalwa mina l-biʾri*, when he drew the bucket out of the well; and by the bands that swim in their going, that is, hurry, and so outstrip to what they were commanded, and so manage an affair of the affairs of the servants, of what sets them right in their religion or their world, as was laid down for them. “*Forcibly*” (*gharqan*, “drowning”): going deep in the pulling, that is, they pull them out from the furthest parts of the bodies, from their fingertips and their nails. Or He swore by the horses of the raiders that strain at their reins with a straining in which the reins are drowned, for the length of their necks, because they are pure-bred; and that go out from the abode of Islam to the abode of war, from your saying, “a *nāshiṭ* bull,” when it goes from land to land; and that swim in their running and so outstrip to the goal, and so manage the matter of overcoming and victory. Managing was attributed to them because they are among its causes. Or He swore by the stars that move from the east to the west; and their going deep in the moving is that they cross the whole sphere until they come down in the furthest west; and that go out from sign to sign of the zodiac; and that swim in the sphere, of the planets, and so outstrip, and so manage an affair of the science of reckoning. It is also said: the pullers are the hands of the raiders, or their selves, that draw the bows, drowning the arrows; and those that draw out the lassos.

What is sworn on is dropped, and it is “you will surely be raised,” because what is after it points to it, of the mention of the Resurrection. “*The day the quake quakes*” is put in the accusative by this implied verb. *Al-rājifa* is the event at which the earth and the mountains quake, and it is the first blast; it was described by what happens with its happening. “*Followed by the second blow*” (*tatbaʿuhā l-rādifa*, “the one following after follows it”): that is, the event that follows after the first, which is the second blast. It may also be that *al-rādifa* is from the saying of God Most High “*Say: perhaps some of what you seek to hasten is following close after you* (radifa lakum)” (Q 27:72), that is, the Resurrection that the unbelievers seek to hasten, thinking it far off; and it is following close after them, for its nearness. It is also said: *al-rājifa* is the earth and the mountains, from His saying “*the earth and the mountains will quake*” (Q 73:14); and *al-rādifa* is the heaven and the stars, because it splits and its stars are scattered in the track of that.

{Q} What is the place of “*follows it*”? {A} A circumstantial clause, that is: it quakes, the one following after following it.

{Q} How did you make “*The day the quake quakes*” an adverbial for the implied “you will surely be raised,” when they are not raised at the first blast? {A} The meaning is: you will surely be raised in the wide time in which the two blasts happen; and they are raised in part of that wide time, which is the time of the other blast. What points to that is that His saying “*Followed by the second blow*” was made a circumstantial clause from “*the quake*.” “*The day the quake quakes*” may also be put in the accusative by what “*Certain minds will be terrified*” (*qulūbun yawmaʾidhin wājifa*, “hearts that day pounding”) points to, that is: the day it quakes, the hearts pound. “*Terrified*” (*wājifa*): very much shaken; *al-wajīb* and *al-wajīf* are brothers. “*Subdued*”: abased.

{Q} How was it allowed to begin with an indefinite? {A} “*Hearts*” is in the nominative as subject, *wājifa* is its description, and “*Their eyes will be subdued*” is its predicate; so it is like His saying “*A believing man is better than an idolater*” (*wa-la-ʿabdun muʾminun khayrun min mushrik*, Q 2:221).

{Q} How was it sound to annex the eyes to the hearts? {A} Its meaning is the eyes of their owners, by proof of His saying “*They will say*.” “*from the grave*” (*fī l-ḥāfira*, “in the first state”): in the first state; they mean life after death.

{Q} What is the real sense of this word? {A} One says, “So-and-so went back in his *ḥāfira*,” that is, in {p|694}his road that he came by and dug (*ḥafarahā*), that is, left his mark on it by walking in it; he made the mark of his feet a digging; as it is said, “his teeth were dug,” when decay left its mark on their roots; and the line dug in the rock. It is also said: *ḥāfira*, as it is said “*a happy life*” (*ʿīshatin rāḍiya*, “a pleased life,” Q 69:21), that is, related to digging and to pleasure; or like their saying, “Your day is fasting.” Then it was said of one who was in a matter, went out of it, then came back to it: “He went back to his *ḥāfira*,” that is, his way and his first state. He said:

> A return to the first state, with baldness and gray hair? / God forbid! From folly and disgrace.

He means: is it a going back to a *ḥāfira*? It is also said: “Payment is at the *ḥāfira*,” by which they mean at the first state, which is the striking of the bargain. Abū Ḥaywa read *fī l-ḥafira*; and *al-ḥafira* is in the sense of the dug. One says, “his teeth *ḥafarat*, they were dug, a digging,” and it is *ḥafira*. This reading is proof that *al-ḥāfira*, at the root of the word, is in the sense of the dug.

One says, “the bone *nakhira*,” so it is *nakhir* and *nākhir*, as you say, “he was greedy (*ṭamiʿa*),” so he is *ṭamiʿ* and *ṭāmiʿ*; and *faʿil* is stronger than *fāʿil*; and both have been read. It is the decayed, hollow bone through which the wind passes, so that a whistling (*nakhīr*) is heard from it. *Idhā* is in the accusative by something dropped, whose estimate is: when we were bones, will we be brought back and raised? “*An impossible recurrence*” (*karratun khāsira*, “a losing return”): related to loss; or whose people lose. And the meaning is: if it is sound, then we are losers, for our denying it. And this is mockery from them.

{Q} With what is His saying “*It will be only a single cry*” connected? {A} With something dropped, whose meaning is: do not think it hard, for it will be only a single cry. He means: do not reckon that return hard for God, Mighty and Majestic, for it is easy and light in His power; it is only one shout. He means the second blast. “*And then they are*” alive on the face of the earth, after they had been dead in its belly; from their saying, “He cried at (*zajara*) the camel,” when he shouted at it. *Al-sāhira* is the white, level ground. It was called that because the mirage runs on it, from their saying, “a wakeful (*sāhira*) spring,” one running with water; and of its opposite, “sleeping.” Al-Ashʿath ibn Qays said:

> {p|695}And many a white plain whose sides the mirage cloaks in the forenoon / I have crossed, veiled to the eyes.

Or because the one who travels it does not sleep, for fear of perishing. From Qatāda: and then they are in Hell.

{L|Go|idhhab} on the intent of saying. In the reading of ʿAbd Allāh, *an idhhab*, because in calling there is the meaning of saying. “Have you a mind for such-and-such” is said with *fī* and with *ilā*, as you say, “Do you desire it” with *fī* and with *ilā*. “*Reform*” (*tazakkā*, “purify yourself”): to cleanse yourself of associating. The people of Medina read *tazzakkā*, with assimilation. “*Let me guide you to your Lord*”: and that I direct you to the knowledge of God, alerting you to Him so that you know Him, “*that you may turn reverent*”; because fear comes only with knowledge. God Most High said: “*the people who truly reverence GOD are those who are knowledgeable*” (Q 35:28), that is, those who know Him. He mentioned fear because it is the mainstay of the matter: whoever fears God, every good comes from him; and whoever feels safe dares every evil. From it is his saying ﵇: “*Whoever fears sets out by night, and whoever sets out by night reaches the stopping place*.” He began his address to him with a question whose meaning is an offer, as a man says to his guest, “Would you like to stay with us?”; and followed it with gentle speech, to draw him by kindness in speaking and to bring him down by courtesy from his insolence; as he was commanded to do in His saying “*Speak to him nicely*” (Q 20:44).

{L|The great miracle|al-āyata l-kubrā} is the turning of the staff into a snake, because it was the first and the root, and the other was like a follower of it; because he would guard himself from it with his hand, and it was said {p|696}to him, “*Put your hand in your pocket*” (Q 27:12). Or he meant both together, but made them one, because the second is as though it were part of the first, for its being its follower. “*But he disbelieved*” Moses and the greatest sign, and called them a sorcerer and sorcery; “*and rebelled*” God Most High after he knew the soundness of the matter and that obedience had become binding on him. “*Then he turned away in a hurry*” (*thumma adbara yasʿā*): that is, when he saw the serpent he turned back terrified, *yasʿā*: hurrying in his walking. Al-Ḥasan said: he was a flighty, light man. Or he turned away from Moses, running and exerting himself in plotting against him. And it was meant: then he came forward running, as you say, “So-and-so came forward doing such-and-such,” in the sense of “he began to do it”; so *adbara* was put in the place of “came forward,” so that he would not be described by coming forward. “*He summoned*” (*fa-ḥashara*, “then he gathered”): then he gathered the sorcerers, as in His saying “*Pharaoh sent to the cities callers*” (Q 26:53). “*And proclaimed*” in the place where they had gathered with him; or he commanded a crier, and he called that out among the people. It is also said: he stood among them as a speaker, and said that enormity. From Ibn ʿAbbās: his first word was “*I have not known of any god for you other than me*” (Q 28:38), and the last “*I am your Lord; most high*.”

{L|Committed him to the retribution|nakāla} is an emphasizing verbal noun, like “*GOD’s promise*” (*waʿda llāhi*, Q 30:6) and “*GOD’s system*” (*ṣibghata llāhi*, “the dye of God,” Q 2:138), as though it were said: God made an example of him, an exemplary punishment of the last and the first. *Al-nakāl* is in the sense of *al-tankīl*, as *al-salām* is in the sense of *al-taslīm*. He means the drowning in this world and the burning in the Hereafter. From Ibn ʿAbbās: the exemplary punishment of his two words, the last, which is his saying “*I am your Lord most high*,” and the first, which is his saying “*I know of no god for you other than me*.” It is said: between the two words were forty years. It is also said: twenty.

::: passage 79:27-33 | 4:696-697

The address is to those who deny the raising. He means: are you harder to create and bring into being, “*than the heaven*”? Then He made clear how He created it, and said “*He constructed it*”; then He made clear the building, and said “*He raised its masses*”: that is, He made the measure of its going in the direction of height drawn out and lofty, a journey of five hundred years. “*And perfected it*”: and made it even, level and smooth, with no unevenness and no cracks in it. Or He completed it with what He knew it would be completed by, and set it right; from your saying, “So-and-so set (*sawwā*) So-and-so’s affair right.” *Ghaṭasha l-laylu* and God *aghṭashahu*, as you say *ẓalima* and *aẓlamahu*. And one also says *aghṭasha l-laylu*, as one says *aẓlama*. {p|697}“*And brightened its morn*” (*wa-akhraja ḍuḥāhā*, “and brought out its forenoon”): and brought forth the light of its sun. The saying of God Most High “*By the sun and its brightness*” (*wa-l-shamsi wa-ḍuḥāhā*, Q 91:1) points to it, by which He means: and its light; and their saying, “the time of the forenoon,” for the time when the sun shines and its power stands up. The night and the sun were annexed to the heaven because the night is its shadow, and the sun is the lamp kindled in its air. “*Its own water*”: its springs gushing with water; “*and pasture*”: and its grazing, and it is originally the place of grazing. “*The earth*” and “*the mountains*” are put in the accusative by implying “spread” and “set firm”; and it is the implying on the condition of explanation. Al-Ḥasan read them both in the nominative, as subjects.

{Q} Why was the particle of joining not brought in on “*He produced*” (*akhraja*)? {A} There are two faces in it. One of them is that the meaning of “*He made the earth egg-shaped*” (*daḥāhā*, “He spread it”) is: He spread it out and smoothed it for dwelling; then He explained the smoothing by what is needed for dwelling in it to come about, of setting right the matter of eating and drinking, and the possibility of settling on it, and stillness, by bringing out the water and the pasture, and setting the mountains firm and fixing them as pegs for it, so that it stays still and is stayed on. The second is that “*He produced*” (*akhraja*) is a circumstantial clause, with *qad* implied, as in His saying “*those who come to you wishing not to fight you*” (*aw jāʾūkum ḥaṣirat ṣudūruhum*, “or come to you with their breasts constricted,” Q 4:90). By “*pasture*” (*marʿāhā*) He meant what people and cattle eat; and grazing was borrowed for man, as grazing at large was borrowed in His saying “*Send him with us tomorrow to run and play*” (*yartaʿ wa-yalʿab*, “that he may graze at large and play,” Q 12:12); and *narʿa* is read, from grazing. For this it is said: God, glory be to Him, pointed, by mentioning water and pasture, to all that is made use of and enjoyed of what comes out of the earth, even salt, because it is from water. “*All this to provide life support for you*”: He did that as an enjoyment for you “*and your animals*”; because the benefit of that smoothing reaches them and their cattle.

{L|The great blow|al-ṭāmma} is the calamity that overflows on the calamities, that is, rises over them and overcomes them. And in their proverbs: “The valley ran and overflowed (*ṭamma*) on the low ground.” It is the Resurrection, for its overflowing on every terrible thing. It is also said: it is the second blast. It is also said: the hour in which the people of Paradise are driven to Paradise and the people of the Fire to the Fire. “*That is the day when the human will remember*” is a substitute for “*when the great blow comes*.” He means: when he sees his works set down in his book he remembers them, though he had forgotten them; as in His saying “*GOD has recorded everything, while they have forgotten it*” (Q 58:6). The *mā* in “*everything he did*” (*mā saʿā*) is relative, or verbal. “*Hell will be brought into existence*” (*wa-burrizati l-jaḥīm*, “and it is made to appear”): it is shown. Abū Nahīk read *wa-barazat*. {p|698}*Li-man yarā* (“for whoever sees”): for all who see, that is, for everyone. He means that it appears with a plain, uncovered appearing; all the people of the plain see it; as in his saying, “*The morning has become clear to anyone with two eyes*,” by which he means: to everyone who has sight; and it is a proverb for the uncovered matter that is hidden from no one. Ibn Masʿūd read *li-man raʾā*; and ʿIkrima read *li-man tarā*, the pronoun being for Hell, as in His saying “*When it sees them from afar*” (Q 25:12). It is also said: for whom you see, Muḥammad.

{L|As for|fa-ammā} is the answer to “*Then, when*,” that is: then when the overwhelming one comes, the matter is so. And the meaning is: Hell is his abode; as you say to a man, “Lower the eye,” meaning “your eye.” The *alif* and *lām* are not a substitute for the annexing; but since it is known that the transgressor is the owner of the abode, and that a man does not lower another’s eye, the annexing was left. The coming in of the article in “*the abode*” and “*the eye*” is for definiteness, because they are known. *Hiya* is a separator or a subject.

{L|When it will take place|ayyāna mursāhā} when is its anchoring, that is, its setting up? They meant: when will God set it up, fix it and bring it into being? {p|699}It is also said: when is its end and its resting place, as the anchorage of a ship is its resting place, where it ends. “*It is not you*” (*fīma anta*, “in what are you”): in what thing are you, that you should mention its time to them and let them know it? He means: you have nothing to do with mentioning it to them and making its time clear. From ʿĀʾisha ﵂: the Messenger of God ﷺ did not stop mentioning the Hour, being asked about it, until it was revealed. On this, it is a making to wonder at how much he mentioned it, as though it were said: in what occupation and concern are you, of mentioning it and asking about it? And the meaning is: they ask you about it, and from your eagerness to answer them you do not stop mentioning it and asking about it. Then He said “*Your Lord decides its fate*,” that is, the end of the knowledge of it; He has not given the knowledge of it to any of His creation. It is also said: *fīma* is a disavowal of their asking, that is: in what is this asking? Then it was said: *anta min dhikrāhā* (“you are of its reminding”), that is: your being sent, while you are the seal of the prophets and the last of the messengers, sent at the first breath of the Hour, is a reminder of its reminders and a sign of its signs. So that was enough of a proof for them of its nearness and its being close at hand and the need to get ready for it; and there is no sense in their asking about it. “*Your mission is to warn those who expect it*”: that is, you were not sent to let them know the time of the Hour, in knowing which there is no use to them; you were only sent to warn of its terrors whoever is such that your warning is a favor to him in fearing it. *Mundhirun* is read with *tanwīn*, and that is the origin; and the annexing is a lightening. Both are fit for present and future; when the past is meant, there is nothing but the annexing, as in your saying, “He is Zayd’s warner yesterday.” That is: as though they had not lingered in this world; it is also said: in the graves, “*one evening or half a day*.”

{Q} How was the annexing of the forenoon to the evening sound? {A} For the connection between them, by their coming together in one day.

{Q} Then why was it not said “but an evening or a forenoon,” and what is the use of the annexing? {A} To point to this: that the length of their lingering is as though it did not reach a full day, but {p|700}one hour of it, its evening or its forenoon. When He left out the day, He annexed it to its evening; so it is like His saying “*it will seem as if they lasted one hour of the day*” (Q 46:35).

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites the Sūra of ‘By the pullers,’ will be of those whom God holds in the grave and the Resurrection, until he enters Paradise, only for the length of an obligatory prayer*.”
