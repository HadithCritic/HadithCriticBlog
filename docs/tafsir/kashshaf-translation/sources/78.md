revelation: Meccan; it is called ʿAmma Yatasāʾalūn and also Sūrat al-Nabaʾ. Forty verses; it is also said forty-one. [Revealed after al-Maʿārij (70).]

{L|What|ʿamma} its origin is *ʿan mā*, on the view that it is a preposition that has come in on the interrogative *mā*; and it is so in the reading of ʿIkrima and ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar. Ḥassān ﵁ said:

> For what (ʿalā mā) does a base man stand reviling me, / like a pig rolling in ashes?

{p|684}Use in the main is with the dropping, and the origin is rare. The meaning of this question is making the matter great, as though He said: about what matter do they ask one another? Like it is the *mā* in your saying, “Zayd, what is Zayd?” You make him, for his having no match and no equal, as though he were a thing whose kind is hidden from you, so that you ask about his kind and search into his substance; as you say, “What is the ghoul, and what is the ʿAnqāʾ?”, meaning: what thing among things is it? This is its origin; then it was stripped to an expression for making great, until it came in the speech of One from whom nothing hidden is hidden. “*Are they questioning*” (*yatasāʾalūna*, “they ask one another”): some of them ask others; or they ask others, the Messenger of God ﷺ and the believers, like *yatadāʿūnahum* and *yatarāʾūnahum*. The pronoun is for the people of Mecca: they used to ask one another about the raising, and ask others about it by way of mockery. “*The great event*” (*ʿani l-nabaʾi l-ʿaẓīm*) makes clear the matter made great. From Ibn Kathīr: he read *ʿammah*, with the *hāʾ* of pause. It must be either that joining is made to run the course of the pause, or that he pauses and begins “*they ask one another about the great news*,” on the view that “*they ask one another*” is implied, because what is after it explains it, like a thing left vague and then explained.

{Q} You have claimed that the pronoun in “*they ask one another*” is for the unbelievers; so what do you do with His saying “*in which they differ*”? {A} Among them were some who were decided in denying the raising, and some who doubted. It is also said: the pronoun is for the Muslims and the unbelievers together, and they all used to ask about it: the Muslim to grow in fear and readiness, and the unbeliever to grow in mockery. It is also said: what is asked about is the Qurʾān. It is also said: the prophethood of Muḥammad ﷺ. *Yassāʾalūna* is read with assimilation, and *sa-taʿlamūna* with *tāʾ*.

{L|Indeed|kallā} a restraint of those asking one another in mockery. “*They will know*” is a threat to them, that they will know that what they ask one another about and laugh at is true, because it is happening, there is no doubt of it. Repeating the restraint with the threat is making that harder. And the meaning of “*then*” is to give a sign that the second threat is more forceful than the first and harder.

::: passage 78:6-16 | 4:685-687

{Q} How is His saying “*Did we not make the earth habitable?*” connected with it? {A} When they denied the raising, it was said to them: has not the One to whom the raising is attributed created these wondrous creations, which point to the perfection of power? So what is the face of denying His power over the raising, when it is only a making like these makings? Or it was said to them: has He not done these many deeds? And the Wise does not do a deed in vain; and what you deny, of the raising and the repayment, leads to His being vain in all He has done.

{L|Habitable|mihādan} a bed. *Mahdan* is also read; and its meaning is that it is for them like the cradle for the child, which is what is smoothed for him and he is put to sleep on; naming the thing smoothed by the verbal noun, like “the prince’s coin” (*ḍarb al-amīr*). Or it is described by the verbal noun; or in the sense of “possessed of smoothing.” “*And the mountains stabilizers*” (*wa-l-jibāla awtādan*, “and the mountains as pegs”): that is, We made it firm with the mountains as a tent is made firm with pegs. “*So you can rest*” (*subātan*, “a cutting off”): death. *Al-masbūt* is the dead, from *al-sabt*, which is cutting, because he is cut off from movement. Sleep is one of the two takings; and it is on the pattern of the diseases. When He made sleep death, He made waking living, that is, life, in His saying “*And the day to seek provisions*” (*wa-jaʿalnā l-nahāra maʿāshan*, “and We made the day a living”): that is, a time of living, in which you wake and turn about in your needs and your earning. It is also said: *al-subāt* is rest. “*A cover*” (*libāsan*, “a garment”): it covers you from eyes when you want flight from an enemy, or a night raid on him, or to hide what you would not like to be seen, of many matters.

> How many a favor has the dark of night done you, / telling that the Manichaeans lie!

{p|686}“*Seven*”: seven heavens; *shidādan* (“strong”): plural of *shadīda*, He means firm, strong of build, on which the passing of ages has no effect. “*Bright*” (*wahhājan*, “blazing”): glittering and burning; He means the sun. *Tawahhajati l-nāru*, when the fire flickered, and so blazed with its light and its heat.

*Al-muʿṣirāt* are the clouds when they *aʿṣarat*, that is, came near to the winds pressing them so that they rain; as in your saying, “the crop *ajazza*,” when the time came for it to be cut. From it is *aʿṣarati l-jāriyatu*, when the girl came near to menstruating. ʿIkrima read *bi-l-muʿṣirāt*, and there are two faces in it: that the winds are meant, whose time has come to press the clouds; or that the clouds are meant, because if the sending down is from them it is by them, as you say, “He gave a dirham from his hand,” and “He gave it with his hand.” From Mujāhid: *al-muʿṣirāt* are the winds possessed of whirlwinds. From al-Ḥasan and Qatāda: they are the heavens; and its interpretation is that the water comes down from the heaven to the clouds, so it is as though the heavens *yuʿṣirna*, that is, bring about the pressing and make it possible.

{Q} Then what is the face of one who read “*from the muʿṣirāt*” and explained them as the winds possessed of whirlwinds, when rain does not come down from the winds? {A} The winds are what raise the clouds and make their udders flow; so it is sound for them to be made the starting point of the sending down. And it has come that God Most High sends the winds, and they carry the water from the heaven to the clouds; if that is sound, the sending down from them is plain.

{Q} It is mentioned from Ibn Kaysān that he made *al-muʿṣirāt* in the sense of those that bring relief, when the one who brings relief is *al-ʿāṣir*, not *al-muʿṣir*; one says *ʿaṣarahu fa-ʿtaṣara*. {A} Its face is that he means those that *aʿṣarna*, that is, whose time has come to *taʿṣir*, that is, to bring relief.

{L|Pouring|thajjājan} poured out in plenty. One says *thajjahu* and *thajja nafsahu*. In the ḥadīth: “*The best pilgrimage is the ʿajj and the thajj*,” that is, raising the voice with the *talbiya* and pouring out the blood of the sacrificial animals. And Ibn ʿAbbās was *mithajj*, pouring forth like a great bucket; he means he poured out speech in his sermon. Al-Aʿraj read *thajājan*. *Mathājij al-māʾ* are its pouring places, and the water *yanthajiju* in the valley. “*Grains and plants*”: He means what is eaten for food, of wheat and barley, and what is given as fodder, of straw and grass; as He said: “*Eat and raise your livestock*” (Q 20:54), {p|687}“*Also grains and the spices*” (Q 55:12). “*Various orchards*” (*alfāfan*, “thick-grown”): wound together; it has no singular, like *al-awzāʿ* and *al-akhyāf*. It is also said: the singular is *liff*. The author of al-Iqlīd said: al-Ḥasan ibn ʿAlī al-Ṭūsī recited to me:

> A garden thick-grown, a life abundant, / and drinking companions all white and shining.

Ibn Qutayba claimed that it is *laffāʾ* and *luff*, then *alfāf*; and I do not think he will find an equal for it, of the like of *khuḍr* and *akhḍār*, *ḥumr* and *aḥmār*. If it were said that it is the plural of *multaffa*, on the estimate of dropping the extra letters, it would be a worthy saying.

{L|Is appointed|kāna mīqātan} was, in God’s decree and judgment, a limit by which this world is timed and at which it ends; or a limit for the creatures, which they end at. “*The day the horn is blown*” is a substitute for “*the Day of Decision*,” or an explaining apposition. “*And you come in throngs*”: from the graves to the place of standing, communities, each community with its leader. It is also said: different groups. From Muʿādh ﵁: he asked the Messenger of God ﷺ about it, and he said: “*Muʿādh, you have asked about a great matter of the matters*.” Then he let his eyes flow and said: “*Ten kinds of my community will be gathered. Some of them in the form of apes; some of them in the form of swine; some of them upside down, their legs above their faces, dragged on them; some of them blind; some of them deaf and dumb; some of them chewing their tongues, which hang down on their breasts, pus flowing from their mouths, so that the people of the gathering find them filthy; some of them with their hands and feet cut off; some of them crucified on trunks of fire; some of them more stinking than carrion; and some of them dressed in long robes of pitch stuck to their skins. As for those in the form of apes, they are the talebearers among people. As for those in the form of swine, they are the people of unlawful gain. As for those upside down on their faces, they are the eaters of usury. As for the blind, they are those who are unjust in judging. As for the deaf and dumb, they are those pleased with their own works. As for those who chew their tongues, they are the scholars and the storytellers whose saying went against {p|688}their works. As for those whose hands and feet were cut off, they are those who harm their neighbors. As for those crucified on trunks of fire, they are those who inform on people to the ruler. As for those more stinking than carrion, they are those who follow desires and pleasures and withheld God’s right in their wealth. As for those who wear the robes, they are the people of pride, boasting and vanity*.”

*Wa-futtiḥat* is read, doubled and light. And the meaning is the great number of its gates opened for the angels to come down, as though it were nothing but opened gates; as in His saying “*we caused springs to gush out of the earth*” (*wa-fajjarnā l-arḍa ʿuyūnan*, Q 54:12), as though all of it were springs gushing. It is also said: the gates are roads and passages, that is, it is stripped away, and its place opens and becomes roads that nothing blocks. “*As if they were a mirage*”: as in His saying “*As if they never existed*” (*fa-kānat habāʾan munbaththan*, “and they become scattered dust,” Q 56:6). He means that they become a thing like nothing, for the scattering of their parts and the dispersing of their substances.

*Al-mirṣād* is the limit at which the lying in wait is. And the meaning is that Hell is the limit of the transgressors at which they are lain in wait for, for punishment; and it is their place of return. Or it is a watch place for the people of Paradise, the angels who meet them there lying in wait for them, because their crossing is over it; and it is a place of return for the transgressors. From al-Ḥasan and Qatāda the like of it: they said, a road and a passage for the people of Paradise. Ibn Yaʿmar read *anna jahannama*, with *fatḥa* on the *hamza*, on giving as the reason for the Hour’s being set up that Hell was a watch place for the transgressors; as though it were said: that was for setting up the repayment.

*Lābithīna* and *labithīna* are read; and *labith* is stronger, because the *lābith* is one in whom lingering has come about, and *labith* is said only of one whose way is lingering, like one who sits fast in a place and hardly leaves it. “*For ages*”: age after age; whenever an age passes, another follows it, without end. *Al-ḥuqb* and *al-ḥiqba* are hardly used except where the following on and succession of times is meant; and the derivation witnesses to that. Do you not see {p|689}the rider’s saddlebag (*ḥaqība*), and the *ḥaqab* that is behind the girth? It is also said: the *ḥuqb* is eighty years. It may also be meant: lingering in it for ages, not tasting in it coolness nor drink except boiling water and pus; then after the ages they are given in exchange, other than boiling water and pus, another kind of punishment. And there is another face in it: that it is from *ḥaqiba ʿāmunā*, when our year’s rain and good were little, and *ḥaqiba fulānun*, when provision missed him, so he is *ḥaqib*, whose plural is *aḥqāb*. Then it is in the accusative as a circumstantial clause from them; He means: lingering in it, barren of good and wretched. And His saying “*They never taste in it coolness, nor a drink*” explains it. The exception is cut off; He means: they do not taste in it coolness and refreshment that would relieve them of the heat of the Fire, nor drink that would calm their thirst; but they taste in it boiling water and pus. It is also said: *al-bard* is sleep; and he recited:

> If you wished, I would forbid myself women other than you, / and if you wished I would taste neither cool water nor sleep.

From one of the Arabs: “*The cold kept away the sleep*” (*manaʿa l-bardu l-barda*). *Ghasāqan* is read light and doubled; it is what runs (*yaghsiqu*), that is, flows, of their pus.

{L|A just requital|wifāqan} is description by the verbal noun; or possessed of fitness. Abū Ḥaywa read *waffāqan*, *faʿʿāl* from *waffaqahu kadhā*. “*Utterly*” (*kidhdhāban*): a denying. *Fiʿʿāl* in the class of *faʿʿala* is all of it widespread in the speech of the eloquent of the Arabs; they say nothing else. One of them heard me explaining a verse, and said, “*You have explained it with an explaining* (fissār) *the like of which was never heard*.” It is also read light, and it is the verbal noun of *kadhaba*, by proof of his saying:

> I told her the truth, and I told her lies, / and a man is profited by his lying (kidhāb).

And it is like His saying “*GOD germinated you from the earth like plants*” (*anbatakum mina l-arḍi nabātan*, Q 71:17); He means: and they denied Our signs, and lied a lying. Or you put it in the accusative by “*they denied*,” because it contains the meaning of “they lied,” since everyone who denies the truth is a liar. If you make it in the sense of trading lies, its meaning is: and they denied Our signs, and traded lies a trading of lies; or they denied them, trading lies; because when they were liars in the Muslims’ eyes, and the Muslims liars in theirs, there was a trading of lies between them. Or because they speak what is excess in lying, the deed of one who strives against another in a matter and reaches in it the furthest of his effort. *Kudhdhāban* is read, and it is the plural of *kādhib*, that is: they denied {p|690}Our signs, being liars. *Al-kudhdhāb* may also be in the sense of a single one far gone in lying; one says, “a man *kudhdhāb*,” as in your saying “*ḥussān*” and “*bukhkhāl*.” Then it is made a description of the verbal noun of “*they denied*,” that is: a denying that is a lying one, excessive in its lying.

Abū l-Sammāl read *wa-kullu shayʾin aḥṣaynāhu*, in the nominative, as subject. “*In writing*” is a verbal noun in the place of “counting”; and “*We counted*” is in the sense of “We wrote,” because counting and writing meet in the meaning of fixing and recording. Or it is a circumstantial clause, in the sense of written in the Tablet and in the sheets of the guardians. And the meaning is the counting of their sins, as in His saying “*GOD has recorded everything, while they have forgotten it*” (Q 58:6); and it is a parenthesis. His saying “*Suffer the consequences*” is caused by their unbelief in the reckoning and their denying the signs. It is a verse of the utmost hardness. Enough for you is “*we will only increase your retribution*” (*fa-lan nazīdakum illā ʿadhāban*, “We will not increase you except in punishment”), and its pointing to leaving off the increase being like the impossible that does not come under soundness; and its coming by way of turning, as a witness that the anger has gone to its height. From the Prophet ﷺ: “*This verse is the hardest thing in the Qurʾān on the people of the Fire*.”

{L|Have deserved a reward|mafāzan} winning and attaining the aim; or a place of winning. It is also said: escape from what those others are in; or a place of escape. The place of winning is explained by what is after it. *Al-ḥadāʾiq* are the orchards with kinds of fruit-bearing trees in them; and *al-aʿnāb* are the vines. *Al-kawāʿib* are those whose breasts have grown round, and they are the swelling-breasted. *Al-atrāb* are those of equal age. *Al-dihāq* is the brimming. *Adhaqa l-ḥawḍa*: he filled the basin until it said, “Enough.” *Wa-lā kidhdhāban* is read doubled and light, that is: they do not deny one another, nor does one deny another, or trade lies with him. From ʿAlī ﵁: he read both light. “*A repayment*” is an emphasizing verbal noun put in the accusative by the meaning of His saying “*for the God-fearing is a place of winning*,” as though He said: He repaid the God-fearing with a place of winning. “*A gift*” is put in the accusative by “*A reward*” (*jazāʾan*), as the object is, that is: He repaid them a gift. “*Generous*” (*ḥisāban*, “sufficient”) is a description in the sense of sufficing, from *aḥsabahu l-shayʾu*, when it sufficed him until he said, “Enough for me” (*ḥasbī*). It is also said: according to their works. Ibn Quṭayb read *ḥissāban*, doubled, on the view that *al-ḥissāb* is in the sense of *al-muḥsib*, like *al-darrāk* in the sense of *al-mudrik*.

{p|691}*Rabbu l-samāwāti* and *al-raḥmānu* are read in the nominative, on: He is the Lord of the heavens, the Merciful. Or “*Lord of the heavens*” is the subject, “*the Merciful*” a description, and “*they have no power*” the predicate; or both are predicates. And in the genitive, as a substitute for “*your Lord*”; and with the first in the genitive and the second in the nominative, on the view that it is the subject whose predicate is “*they have no power*”; or: He is the Merciful; they have no power. The pronoun in “*they have no power*” is for the people of the heavens and the earth, that is: they have not in their hands, of what God addresses and commands concerning reward and punishment, a single address that they can dispose of as owners dispose, adding to it or taking from it. Or: they have no power to address Him with anything of lessening punishment or adding to reward, unless He grants them that and gives them leave for it. “*The day will come when the Spirit and the angels will stand*” is connected with “*No one can abrogate His decisions*” (*lā yamlikūna minhu khiṭāban*, “they have no power”), or with “*None will speak*.” And the meaning is that those who are the best of creatures and the noblest of them, the most obedient of them and the nearest of them to Him, who are the Spirit and the angels, have no power to speak before Him; so what do you think of the others of the people of the heavens and the earth? The Spirit is greater in build than the angels, nobler than they and nearer to the Lord of the worlds. It is also said: it is a great angel; God created no creation, after the Throne, greater than it. It is also said: they are not of the angels, and they eat. It is also said: Gabriel. They are two conditions: that the one who speaks be given leave to speak, and that he speak what is right; so he does not intercede for one not approved, for the saying of God Most High “*They do not intercede, except for those already accepted by Him*” (Q 21:28).

{L|Everyone|al-marʾu} is the unbeliever, for the saying of God Most High “*We have sufficiently warned you about an imminent retribution*”; and “*the disbeliever*” is an explicit noun put in the place of the pronoun, to add to the blame. He means by “*what his hands have sent forth*” of evil, as in His saying “*Suffer the retribution of Hell. This is the consequence of your own works*” (Q 3:181–182), “*we commit him on the Day of Resurrection to the agony of burning. This is what your hands have sent ahead for you*” (Q 22:9–10), {p|692}“*because of what their hands have sent forth. GOD is fully aware of the wicked*” (Q 2:95). *Mā* may be interrogative, in the accusative by *qaddamat* (“sent ahead”), that is: he looks at what thing his hands sent ahead; or relative, in the accusative by “*looks*”; one says *naẓartuhu* in the sense of “I looked at him,” and the returning pronoun of the complement is dropped. It is also said: “the man” is general, and the unbeliever is singled out of it. From Qatāda: it is the believer. “*Oh, I wish I were dust*” in this world, so that I had not been created nor charged; or would that I were dust on this day, so that I had not been raised. It is also said: God gathers the animals that are not charged, until the hornless is avenged on the horned; then He turns them back to dust, and the unbeliever wishes for their state. It is also said: the unbeliever is Iblīs; he sees Adam and his children and their reward, and wishes he were the thing he despised when he said “*You created me from fire, and created him from mud*” (Q 7:12).

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites the Sūra of ‘About what do they ask one another,’ God will give him to drink of the cool drink on the Day of Resurrection*.”
