{L|Is shattered|infaṭarat} is cleft apart. “*Are exploded*” (*fujjirat*): they are opened into one another, so that the fresh water mixes with the salt, the barrier between them goes, and the seas become one sea. It is related that the earth will soak up the water after the seas are filled, and become level, and that this is the meaning of *tasjīr* (“filling”) according to al-Ḥasan. *Fujirat* is also read, without doubling. Mujāhid read *fajarat*, in the active and without doubling, meaning that they overstep their bounds once the barrier is gone, with an eye to the saying of God Most High “*to prevent them from transgressing*” (*lā yabghiyān*, Q 55:20), because overstepping (*baghy*) and wickedness (*fujūr*) are brothers.

{L|Are opened|buʿthirat} *baʿthara* and *baḥthara* have one meaning. Both are made from *baʿth* (“raising”) and *baḥth* (“searching”) {p|715}with a *rāʾ* added to them. The meaning: they are searched and their dead are brought out. It is also said that this is why Barāʾa is called al-Mubaʿthira, “the one that overturns,” because it laid bare the secrets of the hypocrites.

::: passage 82:6-8 | 4:715-716

{Q} What does His saying “*what diverted you from your Lord, Most Honorable?*” (*mā gharraka bi-rabbika l-karīm*, “what has deluded you about your Lord, the Generous?”) mean? How does describing Him as generous fit with condemning being deluded about Him, when it is precisely the generous one people are deluded about? So it is related of ʿAlī ﵁ that he called out to a slave of his several times and he did not answer. He looked, and there he was at the door. He said to him, “*What is the matter with you, that you did not answer me?*” He said, “*My trust in your forbearance and my feeling safe from your punishment*.” He liked his answer and set him free. And people say: “Part of a man’s generosity is his slaves’ bad manners.” {A} Its meaning is that man ought not to be deluded by God’s generosity to him in creating him alive to do him good, and by His favor to him in this, so far that, after He has given him the power and laid duties on him, and he has disobeyed and been ungrateful for the favor given him, he hopes that God will favor him with reward and drop the punishment, deluded by the first favor. That is condemned and outside the bounds of wisdom. This is why the Messenger of God ﷺ said, when he recited it: “*His ignorance deluded him*.” ʿUmar ﵁ said: “*His folly and his ignorance deluded him*.” Al-Ḥasan said: “*By God, his foul devil deluded him*”: that is, he made acts of disobedience look fair to him and said to him, “Do as you like, for your Lord is the Generous One who favored you as He did at first, and He will favor you at last,” until he had trapped him.

Al-Fuḍayl ibn ʿIyāḍ was asked, “*If God stood you up on the Day of Resurrection and said to you, ‘What has deluded you about your Lord, the Generous?’ what would you say?*” He said, “*I would say: Your curtains, let down, deluded me*.” This is a confession of the error of being deluded by being kept covered, not an excuse, as the greedy suppose and as the storytellers of the Ḥashwiyya suppose of it. They relate from their imams that He said “*your Lord, Most Honorable*” (*rabbika l-karīm*), rather than any other of His attributes, to prompt His servant with the answer, so that he would say, “The generosity of the Generous One deluded me.”

Saʿīd ibn Jubayr read *mā agharraka*, either as an exclamation of wonder or as a question, from your saying *gharra l-rajul fa-huwa ghārr*, when he is off his guard, {p|716}as in your saying, “The enemy fell on them by night while they were off their guard (*ghārrūn*).” *Agharrahu ghayruhu*: another put him off his guard.

{L|Designed you|fa-sawwāka} made you sound, with every limb intact. “*And perfected you*” (*fa-ʿadalaka*): made you evenly balanced, well proportioned in form, with no unevenness in it. He did not make one hand longer than the other, or one eye wider, or some limbs white and some black, or some hair jet black and some fair. Or He made you even in form, walking upright, not like the beasts. *Fa-ʿadalaka* is also read without doubling, and there are two ways of taking it. One is that it has the sense of the doubled form, that is, He evened your limbs with one another until you were evenly balanced. The other is that *fa-ʿadalaka* means “then He turned you”; one says *ʿadalahu ʿan al-ṭarīq*, “he turned him from the road.” He means: He turned you away from the form of others and created you in a fine form unlike the rest of creation; or He turned you toward one of the shapes and forms.

{L|In whatever design He chose|fī ayyi ṣūratin mā shāʾa} the *mā* in “*mā shāʾa*” is extra. That is, He put you together in whatever form His will and wisdom called for, among forms that differ in beauty and ugliness, tallness and shortness, maleness and femaleness, and resemblance to some relatives or unlikeness to them.

{Q} Why was this clause not joined on, as the ones before it were? {A} Because it explains “*and perfected you*.”

{Q} What does the preposition attach to? {A} It may attach to “*He constructed it*” (*rakkabaka*, “He put you together”), in the sense that He placed you in one of the forms and set you firmly in it; or to something left unstated, that is, He put you together being in one of the forms, in which case it is in the accusative position as a circumstantial clause. It may also attach to “*and perfected you*,” with *ayy* expressing wonder, that is, He made you even in a wondrous form; and then He said “*In whatever design He chose, He constructed it*,” that is, He put you together in whatever composition He willed, meaning a fine composition.

{L|Indeed|kallā} hold back from being deluded by God’s generosity and climbing on it, when it calls for thanks and obedience, to their opposite, which is unbelief and disobedience. Then He said “*you disbelieve in the religion*” (*bal tukadhdhibūna bi-l-dīn*, “rather, you deny the Judgment”) altogether, which is the recompense; or the religion of Islam; so you believe in neither reward nor punishment, which is worse than that condemned hope.

{L|Keepers around you|wa-inna ʿalaykum la-ḥāfiẓīn} this affirms the recompense they deny. He means: you deny the recompense, while the scribes write down your deeds against you so that you will be repaid for them. In honoring the scribes with praise there is honor for the matter of recompense, showing that it is one of the great matters with God. Were it not so, He would not have charged the noble angels, the guardians and scribes, with recording what one will be called to account for and repaid by. In it is a warning, a fright and a shaming for the disobedient, and a grace for the believers. From al-Fuḍayl: when he recited it he would say, “*How hard a verse this is on the heedless!*”

{L|They never leave it|wa-mā hum ʿanhā bi-ghāʾibīn} like His saying “*they can never exit therefrom*” (Q 5:37). It may also mean: they will burn in the Fire on the Day of Judgment, and they are not absent from it before then, that is, in their graves. It is said that in this sūra God has told that the son of Adam has three states: the state of life, in which his deeds are kept; the state of the Hereafter, in which he is repaid; and the state of the interval (*barzakh*), which is His saying “*They never leave it*.”

{L|Awesome is|wa-mā adrāka} He means that the matter of the Day of Judgment is such that no knower can know its true nature in terror and hardship: however you picture it, it is beyond that and many times over. The repetition is to add to the terror. Then He summed up its description and said “*the day when no soul can help another soul*,” that is, it cannot ward anything off it or help it in any way, and the command is God’s alone.

Those who read it in the nominative take it as a substitute for “*the Day of Judgment*,” or as: it is a day when no soul will have power. Those who read it in the accusative take it through an implied *yudānūna* (“they will be judged”), since *al-dīn* points to it, or through an implied “remember.” It may also take *fatḥa* because it is annexed to what is not declined, while it is in the nominative position.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites idhā l-samāʾu nfaṭarat, God will write for him a good deed for every drop from the sky and a good deed for every grave*.”
