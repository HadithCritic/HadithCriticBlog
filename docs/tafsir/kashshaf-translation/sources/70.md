title: Sūrat al-Maʿārij (70)
revelation: Meccan. Forty-four verses. [Revealed after al-Ḥāqqa (69).]

::: passage 70:1-18 | 4:608-611

“*Question*” (*saʾala*, “asked”) was made to contain the meaning of “called for,” so it was given its construction, as though it were said: a caller called for a punishment bound to befall; from your saying, “He called for such-and-such,” when he summoned it and sought it. From it is the saying of God Most High “*They enjoy in it all kinds of fruits*” (Q 44:55). From Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄: he is al-Naḍr ibn al-Ḥārith, who said, “*if this is really the truth from You, then shower us with rocks from the sky, or pour upon us a painful punishment*” (Q 8:32). It is also said: he is the Messenger of God ﷺ, who asked for a punishment to be hastened on the unbelievers. *Sāla sāʾilun* is also read, and it has two faces. Either it is from asking, and it is the dialect of Quraysh: they say *sila tasālu*, and “they ask one another” (*yatasāyalān*). Or it is from flowing. That is supported by the reading of Ibn ʿAbbās, *sāla saylun*; and *al-sayl* is a verbal noun in the sense of what flows, like *al-ghawr* in the sense of *al-ghāʾir*. And the meaning is: a valley of punishment rushed on them, swept them away and destroyed them. From Qatāda: a questioner asked about God’s punishment, on whom it comes down and on whom it befalls, and it was revealed. On this face “*question*” is made to contain the meaning of being concerned and anxious.

{Q} With what is His saying {p|609}“*for the unbelievers*” connected? {A} On the first saying it is connected with “*punishment*,” as a description of it, that is: for a punishment bound to befall, being for the unbelievers. Or with the verb, that is: he called down on the unbelievers a punishment bound to befall. Or with “*befalling*,” that is: for a punishment coming down for their sake. On the second, it is a speech begun afresh, an answer to the questioner, that is: it is for the unbelievers.

{Q} Then with what is His saying “*from God*” connected? {A} It is connected with “*befalling*,” that is, befalling from Him; or with “*stopper*,” in the sense that it has no stopper from His side when its time comes and wisdom makes its happening necessary. “*Possessor of the highest Height*” (*dhī l-maʿārij*, “Lord of the ascents”): Lord of the stairways; plural of *maʿraj*. Then He described the stairways and how far they reach in height and loftiness, and said: “*The angels and the Spirit, with their reports, climb to Him*,” to His Throne and the place from which His commands come down, “*in a day that equals*” like the measure of a span of “*fifty thousand years*,” of what people count. The Spirit is Gabriel ﵇; He set him apart for his being distinguished by his rank. It is also said: the Spirit is a creation who are guardians over the angels, as the angels are guardians over people.

{Q} With what is His saying “*so be patient*” connected? {A} With “*A questioner may question*”: because the asking for victory to be hastened by punishment was only by way of mocking the Messenger of God ﷺ and denying the revelation; and that was among what wearied the Messenger of God ﷺ, so he was commanded to be patient with it. Likewise one who asked about the punishment, for whom it is, only asked by way of making trouble; and he was of the unbelievers of Mecca. Whoever reads *sāla sāʾilun*, or *saylun*, its meaning is: the punishment has come, because its happening is near, so be patient, for you are close to the vengeance. “*In a day*” has been made part of the complement of “*befalling*,” that is: it befalls in a long day whose measure is fifty thousand years of your years, which is the Day of Resurrection: either as reckoning it long for its hardness on the unbelievers, or because it is truly so. It is said: in it are fifty stations, each station a thousand years; and its measure on the believer is only as what is between noon and the afternoon.

The pronoun in “*they see it*” is for the punishment bound to befall, or for the Day of Resurrection, with one who connects “*in a day*” with “*befalling*”; that is, they think it far, by way of holding it impossible, “*While we see it very close*”: easy in Our power, not far for Us nor hard to bring about. So by the far is meant the far from possibility, and by the near the near to it. “*The day will come when the sky will be*” is put in the accusative by “*very close*,” that is, it is possible and not hard on that day. Or by an implied “befalls,” because “*befalling*” points to it. Or: the day the heaven is like molten brass, such-and-such will be. Or it is a substitute for “*in a day*,” with one who connects that with “*befalling*.” “*like molten rocks*” (*ka-l-muhl*): like the dregs of oil. From Ibn Masʿūd: like melted silver in its changing colors. “*Like fluffy wool*” (*ka-l-ʿihn*): like wool dyed in colors; because the mountains are “*the peaks are white, or red, or some other color. And the ravens are black*” (Q 35:27), and when they are crumbled and sent flying through the air, they resemble carded wool when the wind sends it flying.

{L|No friend will care about his close friend|wa-lā yasʾalu ḥamīmun ḥamīman} that is, will not ask him “How are you?” nor speak to him, because each has what keeps him busy from asking one another. “*When they see them*” (*yubaṣṣarūnahum*, “they are made to see them”): that is, the friends see the friends, and they are not hidden from them. So what keeps them from asking one another is not {p|610}that some of them do not see others; what keeps them is only being busy. *Yubṣirūnahum* is also read. *Wa-lā yusʾalu* is also read, built for the passive, that is: it is not said to a friend, “Where is your friend?” nor is he sought from him; because they see them and have no need of asking and seeking.

{Q} What is the place of “*When they see them*”? {A} It is a speech begun afresh; as though, when He said “*No friend will care about his close friend*,” it were said, “Perhaps he does not see him”; and it was said, “They are made to see them, but for their being busy they were not able to ask one another.”

{Q} Why were the two pronouns in “*When they see them*” made plural, when they are for two friends? {A} The meaning is general, for every two friends, not for two friends only. It may also be that “*When they see them*” is a description, that is: a friend made to see, made known to them.

*Yawmiʾidhin* is read with the genitive, and with *fatḥa* by indeclinability, for its being annexed to what is not declinable; and *min ʿadhābin yawmaʾidhin*, with *tanwīn* on “*punishment*” and the accusative on “*that day*,” being put in the accusative by “*punishment*,” because it is in the sense of punishing. “*Even his whole tribe*” (*wa-faṣīlatihi*): his nearest clan, from whom he was separated (*fuṣila*). “*That raised him*” (*allatī tuʾwīhi*, “that shelters him”): that gathers him to itself, by belonging to it, or by taking refuge in it in misfortunes. “*If it would save him*” is joined to “*as ransom*,” that is: he would wish he could ransom himself, then that the ransom might save him. Or: those on the earth might save him. And “*then*” is for thinking the saving far off. He means: he wishes all these were under his hand and he gave them in ransom for himself, then that that saved him; and how far it is from saving him! “*No*”: a turning back of the criminal from the wish, and a warning that the ransom does not profit him nor save him from the punishment.

Then He said “*it is*”; and the pronoun is for the Fire, though it had not been mentioned, because the mention of the punishment points to it. It may also be a vague pronoun that the predicate explains, or the pronoun of the matter. *Laẓā* is a proper name for the Fire, carried over from *al-laẓā* in the sense of the flame. It may also be that the flame is meant. *Nazzāʿatun* is a predicate after a predicate for *inna*; or a predicate for *laẓā* if the *hāʾ* is the pronoun of the matter; or a description of it if you mean the flame, and the feminine is because it is in the sense of the Fire. Or it is in the nominative for terrifying, that is: it is a stripper away. *Nazzāʿatan* is also read in the accusative, as an emphasizing circumstantial clause; or on: flaring, a stripper away; or as accusative of specification, for terrifying. *Al-shawā* are the limbs; or it is the plural of *shawāt*, which is the skin of the head, which it strips away utterly and cuts off, then it is put back. “*It calls*” is a figure for bringing them in, as though it called them and brought them. Like it is the saying of Dhū l-Rumma, “*the fresh pastures call his nose*,” {p|611}and his saying, “*Nights when play allured me, and I followed it*,” and the saying of Abū l-Najm, “*It says to the scout, ‘You have found grass: come down.’*” It is also said: it says to them, “To me, to me, unbeliever! hypocrite!” It is also said: it calls the hypocrites and the unbelievers with a clear tongue, then picks them up as grain is picked up. For it may be that God creates speech in it, as He creates it in their skins, their hands and their feet, and as He created it in the tree. It may also be the calling of the guards of Hell. It is also said: “*it calls*” is “it destroys,” from the saying of the Arabs, “God call you,” that is, destroy you. He said: “*God call you, man that you are, with a viper!*” “*those who turned away*” (*man adbara*, “whoever turned his back”) on the truth “*and turned away*” from it, “*Those who hoarded*” wealth, put it in a vessel and hoarded it, and did not pay the alms and the dues owed in it, and busied himself with it away from religion, and was vain over possessing it and proud.

By “*man*” is meant people; for that reason He excepted from it “*Except for the worshipers*.” *Al-halaʿ* is quickness to despair when the hateful touches, and quickness to withhold when good touches; from their saying, “A she-camel *hilwāʿ*,” swift in going. From Aḥmad ibn Yaḥyā: Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ṭāhir said to me, “What is *al-halaʿ*?” I said, “God has explained it, and no explanation is clearer than His explanation. He is the one who, when evil reaches him, shows great despair; and when good reaches him, is stingy with it and keeps it from people.” The good is wealth and riches, and the evil is poverty. Or health and sickness: when the rich man is well he withholds kindness and is miserly with his wealth, and when he is sick he despairs and starts making bequests. The meaning is: man, for his preferring despair and withholding, and their being fixed in him and rooted in him, is as though he were molded on them and stamped with them, and as though it were a matter of his making, compelled, not chosen; as in the saying of God Most High “*The human being is impatient by nature*” (Q 21:37). The proof of that is that when he was in the belly and the cradle he had no *halaʿ*; and because it is blamed, and God does not blame His own act. The proof of it is the exception of the believers, who struggled against themselves, carried them to hateful things and held them back from desires, until they were neither despairing nor withholding. From the Prophet ﷺ: “*The worst that the son of Adam has been given is anxious miserliness and unnerving cowardice*.”

{Q} How did He say “*who are constant in their prayer*,” then “*who guard their prayer*”? {A} The meaning of their being constant in it is that they keep up performing it, do not neglect it, and are not kept from it by any of the things that busy one; as is related from the Prophet ﷺ{p|613}: “*The best of works is the most constant of them, even if it is little*”; and the saying of ʿĀʾisha: “*His work was a steady rain*.” And their guarding it is that they attend to doing the ablution for it fully and to its times, set up its pillars, complete it with its customs and courtesies, and guard it from being made void by committing sins. So constancy goes back to the prayers themselves, and guarding to their states.

{L|Part of their money is set aside|ḥaqqun maʿlūmun} is the alms, because it is measured and known; or a charity a man lays on himself and pays at known times. The beggar is the one who asks; “*and the deprived*” is the one who holds back from asking and is thought rich, and so is deprived. “*They believe in the Day of Judgment*”: an affirming by their works and their making ready for it; and they are in fear of their Lord’s punishment. He put in a parenthesis His saying “*Truly the punishment of their Lord is not to be felt safe from*,” that is, no one ought to feel safe from it, even if he goes to the utmost in obedience and effort; and he ought to be swaying between fear and hope. *Bi-shahādatihim* and *bi-shahādātihim* are both read. Testimony is among the trusts; He singled it out from among them to make its merit plain, because in giving it is the bringing to life of rights and setting them right, and in keeping it back is losing them and making them void.

::: passage 70:36-44 | 4:613-614

The idolaters used to crowd around the Prophet ﷺ in circles and in bands, listening and mocking his speech, and saying, “*If these enter Paradise, as Muḥammad says, we will surely enter it before them*.” And it was revealed. “*Joining you*” (*muhṭiʿīn*, “rushing”): hurrying toward you, stretching their necks to you, turning their eyes on you. “*They flee*” (*ʿizīn*, “in companies”): in bands {p|614}scattered; plural of *ʿiza*, whose origin is *ʿizwa*; as though each band traces itself (*taʿtazī*) to someone other than the one the other traces itself to, so they are split apart. Al-Kumayt said:

> We and Jandal, the oppressor, left / the battalions of Jandal scattered in companies.

It is also said: the mockers were five bands. “*No*”: a restraint of them from their hope of entering Paradise. Then He gave the reason for that by His saying “*we created them, and they know from what*,” to the end of the sūra; and it is speech that points to their denying the raising, as though He said: no, they deny the raising and the repayment, so from where do they hope to enter Paradise?

{Q} In what way does this speech point to denying the raising? {A} In that it is arguing against them by the first bringing into being, as it is argued against them in places of the revelation; and that is His saying “*we created them, and they know from what*,” that is, from the drops of sperm; and by the power to destroy them and bring in exchange people better than they; and that He is not outstripped in what He wills to bring into being, nothing frustrating Him. The aim is that one who is able for that is not frustrated by bringing back. It may also be meant: We created them from what they know, that is, from the filthy drop, which is their origin, than which no origin is lower. For that reason He left it vague and hidden, as a sign that it is an origin one is ashamed to mention. So from where do they claim honor and claim precedence, and say, “We will surely enter Paradise before them”? It is also said: its meaning is, We created them from a drop as We created all the children of Adam, and of Our judgment is that none of them enters Paradise except by faith and righteous work; so why does one who has no faith and no work hope to enter it?

*Bi-rabbi l-mashriqi wa-l-maghribi* is also read. And *yakhrujūna* and *yukhrajūna*; and *mina l-ajdāthi sirāʿan*, with the letters pronounced plainly and assimilated; and *nuṣb* and *naṣb*: which is everything set up and worshiped besides God. “*In a hurry*” (*sirāʿan*): hurrying to the caller, trying to get ahead of one another, as they used to try to get ahead of one another to their idols.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites the Sūra of ‘A questioner asked,’ God gives him the reward of those who keep their trusts and their covenant*.”
