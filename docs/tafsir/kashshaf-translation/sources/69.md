{L|The Incontestable|al-ḥāqqatu} is the Hour whose happening is bound to be and whose coming is fixed, which is coming, there is no doubt of it. Or the one in which are the things that are made true (*ḥawāqq al-umūr*): the reckoning, the reward and the punishment. Or the one in which things are known in their reality, from your saying, “I do not *aḥiqqu* this,” that is, I do not know its reality. He gave the verb to it, while it belongs to its people. It is in the nominative as subject, and its predicate is “*What an incontestable (event)!*.” The origin is: the Incontestable, what is it? That is, what thing is it? To make its matter great and its terror vast. So the explicit noun was put in the place of the pronoun, because that is more terrifying for it. “*It is truly incontestable*” (*wa-mā adrāka mā l-ḥāqqa*, “and what will make you know what the Incontestable is”): and what thing has taught you what the Incontestable is? He means: you have no knowledge of its essence and how far its greatness reaches, as it is of such greatness and hardness that no one’s knowledge or imagining reaches it, and however you estimate its state, it is greater than that. *Mā* is in the place of the nominative as subject, and “*will make you know*” is suspended from it, because it contains the meaning of a question.

{L|The Shocker|al-qāriʿa} that shocks people with frights and terrors, the heaven with splitting and cleaving, the earth and the mountains with leveling and blowing away, and the stars with blotting out and falling. It was put in the place of the pronoun to point to the meaning of shocking. In “*the Incontestable*” there is an addition in describing its hardness. When He had mentioned it and made it great, He followed the mention of that with the mention of those who denied it and what came on them for denying, to remind the people of Mecca and to make them fear the end of their denying. “*By the devastating (quake)*” (*bi-l-ṭāghiya*): by the event that passes the limit in hardness. They differ about it. {p|599}It is said: the quake. From Ibn ʿAbbās: the thunderbolt. From Qatāda: God sent a shout on them that left them lifeless. It is also said: *al-ṭāghiya* is a verbal noun like *al-ʿāfiya*, that is, for their overflowing; and that is not so, because there is no match between it and His saying “*by a wind ṣarṣar*.” *Al-ṣarṣar* is the wind loud in its sound, which has a roaring. It is also said: the cold one, from *al-ṣirr*, as though it were one in which the cold was repeated and multiplied, so that it burns for the hardness of its cold. “*Violent*” (*ʿātiya*): hard in its blowing; and *al-ʿutuww* is a borrowing. Or it raged against ʿĀd, and they could not turn it back by any device, of hiding in a building, taking refuge in a mountain, or concealing themselves in a pit; for it pulled them out of their hiding places and destroyed them. It is also said: it raged against its keepers, and came out without measure or weight. It is related from the Messenger of God ﷺ: “*God has not sent a ship’s load of wind but by measure, nor a drop of rain but by measure, except on the day of ʿĀd and the day of Noah. The water on the day of Noah overflowed against the keepers, and they had no way over it*”; then he recited “*The flood was devastating, so we carried you on the floating (ark)*” (Q 69:11); “*and the wind on the day of ʿĀd raged against the keepers, and they had no way over it*”; then he recited “*by a wind ṣarṣar, raging*.” And perhaps it is an expression for hardness and excess in it.

*Al-ḥusūm* must be either the plural of *ḥāsim*, like *shuhūd* and *quʿūd*, or a verbal noun like *al-shukūr* and *al-kufūr*. If it is a plural, the meaning of His saying “*ḥusūman*” is: ill-omened days that cut off every good and uprooted every blessing. Or following on one another in the blowing of the winds, not dying down for an hour until they finished them; a likening of their following on one another to the following on one another of the act of one who cauterizes (*al-ḥāsim*), repeating the burning on the disease time after time until it is cut off. If it is a verbal noun, it is either in the accusative by its verb implied, that is, it cuts off utterly, in the sense of uproots utterly; or it is a description, as in your saying, “possessed of cutting off”; or it is an object of purpose, that is, He subjected it to them for uprooting. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz ibn Zurāra al-Kilābī said:

> A time parted them all, / in which years of cutting off followed one another.

Al-Suddī read *ḥasūman*, with *fatḥa*, as a circumstantial clause from the wind, that is, He subjected it to them uprooting. It is also said: they are the days of the old woman (*al-ʿajūz*); that is, an old woman of ʿĀd hid in a tunnel, and the wind pulled her out on the eighth day and destroyed her. It is also said: they are the days of the rump (*al-ʿajz*), which are the end of winter, and their names are al-Ṣinn, al-Ṣinnabr, al-Wabr, al-Āmir, {p|600}al-Muʾtamir, al-Muʿallil and Muṭfiʾ al-Jamr. It is also said: Mukfiʾ al-Ẓuʿun, the overturner of litters. The meaning of “*He subjected it to them*” is: He gave it power over them as He willed. “*The people*” (*fīhā*, “in it”): in the places it blew; or in the nights and days. *Aʿjāzu nakhīlin* is also read. “*Any trace of them*” (*min bāqiya*): any remaining part, or any remaining soul; or any remaining, like *al-ṭāghiya* in the sense of *al-ṭughyān*.

{L|Others before him|wa-man qablahu} He means: and those who were with him, of his followers. *Wa-man qiblahu* is also read, that is: and those who went before him. The first is supported by the reading of ʿAbd Allāh and Ubayy, *wa-man maʿahu*, and the reading of Abū Mūsā, *wa-man tilqāʾahu*. “*The sinners (of Sodom)*” (*wa-l-muʾtafikāt*): the towns of Lot’s people. “*Were wicked*” (*bi-l-khāṭiʾa*, “with the sin”): with sin; or with the deed, or the deeds, possessed of great sin. “*Devastating*” (*rābiya*): hard, increasing in hardness, as their foul deeds increased in foulness. One says, *rabā l-shayʾu yarbū*, when it increases: “*to increase some people’s wealth*” (Q 30:39).

{L|We carried you|ḥamalnākum} We carried your fathers “*in the running one*,” in a ship. Because they were of the offspring of those carried and saved, the carrying of their fathers was a favor to them, and it was as though they were the ones carried, because their fathers’ being saved was the cause of their being born. “*We rendered it*”: the pronoun is for the deed, which is the saving of the believers and the drowning of the unbelievers; “*a reminder*,” an admonition and a lesson. “*Any listening ear*”: one whose way is to heed and keep what it hears, and not lose it by leaving it unacted on. Whatever you keep in yourself you have heeded (*waʿaytahu*); and whatever you keep in something other than yourself you have stored (*awʿaytahu*), as in your saying, “I stored the thing in the vessel.” From the Prophet ﷺ: he said to ʿAlī ﵁ when this verse was revealed, “*I asked God to make it your ear, ʿAlī*.” ʿAlī ﵁ said: “*After that I never forgot anything, and it was not for me to forget*.”

{Q} Why was it said “*any listening ear*,” singular and indefinite? {A} To give notice that those who heed are few among them; to rebuke people for how few of them heed; and to point to this: that a single ear, when it heeds and understands from God, is the great multitude with God, and that what is besides it is paid no heed at all, even if they fill what is between the east and the west. *Wa-taʿyahā* is also read, with the *ʿayn* quiescent, to lighten it; *taʿī* being likened to *kabid*.

He gave the verb to the verbal noun, and making it masculine was good because of the separation. Abū l-Sammāl read *nafkhatan wāḥidatan*, in the accusative, giving the verb to the preposition and its object.

{Q} They are two blasts; why was it said “*one*”? {A} Its meaning is that it is not repeated at its time.

{Q} Which of the two blasts is it? {A} The first, because at it is the ruin of the world; and so it is related from Ibn ʿAbbās. It is also related from him that it is the second.

{Q} Did He not say after it, “*On that day, you will be exposed*,” when the presenting is only at the second blast? {A} He made the day a name for the wide time in which the two blasts, the swoon, the raising, the standing and the reckoning happen; for that reason it was said “*On that day, you will be exposed*,” as you say, “I came to him in such-and-such a year,” when your coming was only at one of its times.

{L|Will be carried off|wa-ḥumilat} and are lifted from their places by a wind whose blowing reaches such strength that it carries the earth and the mountains; or by a creation of the angels; or by God’s power without a cause. *Wa-ḥummilat* is also read, with the carrier dropped, which is one of the three. “*Crushed*” (*fa-dukkatā*): the two masses, the mass of the earths and the mass of the mountains, are leveled, and are struck one against the other until they are crushed and turn to a heap of poured sand and scattered dust. *Al-dakk* is stronger than *al-daqq*. It is also said: they are spread out in one spreading, and become ground in which you see no crookedness or curve, from your saying, “The hump *indakka*,” when it spread flat; and a camel *adakk* and a she-camel *dakkāʾ*; and from it is *al-dukkān*, the bench. “*That is the day when the inevitable event will come to pass*”: then at that time the calamity will come down, which is the Resurrection. “*Fall apart*” (*wāhiya*): slack, with its strength fallen away entirely, after it had been firm and holding fast. He means: and the creation that is called the angel; and He gave back the pronoun to it in the plural in His saying “*above them*,” according to the meaning.

{Q} What is the difference between His saying “*and the angel*” and saying “and the angels”? {A} The angel is more general than the angels. Do you not see that your saying, “There is no angel but he is a witness,” is more general than your saying, “There are no angels”? “*All around*” (*ʿalā arjāʾihā*, “on its sides”): on its edges; the singular is *rajan*, with a short final *alif*. He means that it splits, while it is the dwelling of the angels, and they take refuge at its ends and what is around it, of its rims. “*Eight*”: that is, eight {p|602}of them. From the Messenger of God ﷺ: “*They are four today; and on the Day of Resurrection God will support them with four others, and they will be eight*.” It is related: eight angels, their feet in the bounds of the seventh earth and the Throne above their heads, and they bow their heads, glorifying. It is also said: some of them are in the form of a man, some in the form of a lion, some in the form of a bull, and some in the form of an eagle. It is related: eight angels in the form of mountain goats, between whose hoofs and their knees is a journey of seventy years. From Shahr ibn Ḥawshab: four of them say, “Glory be to You, O God, and with Your praise; to You be praise for Your pardon after Your power”; and four say, “Glory be to You, O God, and with Your praise; to You be praise for Your forbearance after Your knowledge.” From al-Ḥasan: God knows best how many they are, whether eight or eight thousand. From al-Ḍaḥḥāk: eight ranks whose number none knows but God. It may also be that the eight are of the Spirit, or of another creation; for He is able for every creation: “*Glory be to the One who created all kinds of plants from the earth, as well as themselves, and other creations that they do not even know*” (Q 36:36).

The presenting is an expression for the reckoning and the questioning, likened to the sultan’s reviewing the army to learn its state. It is related that on the Day of Resurrection there are three presentings. As for two of them, they are excuse, argument and rebuke; and as for the third, in it the books are spread out, and the winner takes his book in his right hand and the one destroyed takes his book in his left. “*Nothing of you can be hidden*” (*khāfiya*): a secret and a state that was hidden in this world by God’s covering of you.

{L|As for|fa-ammā} is setting out the presenting in detail. *Hāʾ* is a sound that is uttered and from which the meaning of “take” is understood, like *uff* and *ḥass* and the like of that. “*My book*” is in the accusative by “*here, take*” with the Kūfans, and with the Baṣrans by “*read*,” because it is the nearer of the two governors. Its origin is: take my book, read my book; and the first was dropped because the second points to it. Its like is “*Help me pour tar on top of it*” (Q 18:96). They said: if the governor were the first, it would have been said, “read it,” and “pour it.” The *hāʾ* in *kitābiyah* is for the pause, and likewise in *ḥisābiyah*, *māliyah* and *sulṭāniyah*. The right of these *hāʾ*s is {p|603}to stand in the pause and fall in joining. Preferring the pause has been held commendable, out of preference for keeping them, because they stand in the codex. It is also said: there is no harm in joining and dropping them. Ibn Muḥayṣin read it with the *yāʾ* quiescent, without *hāʾ*. A group read it keeping the *hāʾ* in joining and in the pause together, to follow the codex. “*I did believe*” (*ẓanantu*, “I thought”): I knew. Thinking was only made to run the course of knowing because predominant thinking is put in the place of knowing in custom and in rulings. One says, “I think, with a thinking like certainty, that the matter is thus and so.”

{L|A happy life|rāḍiya} related to pleasure, like *al-dāriʿ* and *al-nābil*. Relating is of two kinds: relating by the letter, and relating by the form. Or He gave the verb to it by figure, while it belongs to its owner. “*Exalted*”: high of place in the heaven; or high of degrees; or high of buildings, palaces and trees. “*Within reach*”: reached by the one sitting and the one lying down. It is said to them: “*Eat and drink happily*,” an eating and drinking with relish; or: may you have relish, as a verbal noun. “*In return for your works*”: for what you put forward of righteous works, “*in days past*,” past, of the days of this world. From Mujāhid: the days of fasting, that is, eat and drink in place of what you held back from of eating and drinking for God’s face. It is related: God, Mighty and Majestic, says, “*O My friends, long have I looked at you in the world, with your lips drawn from drinks, your eyes sunken and your bellies empty; so be today in your bliss, and eat and drink with relish for what you sent ahead in the days gone by*.”

The pronoun in “*would that it*” is for the death. He says: would that the death I died “*had been the ending*,” that is, the one that cut off {p|604}my matter, so that I was not raised after it and did not meet what I meet. Or for the state, that is: would that this state had been the death that finished me; because he saw that state more hideous and bitter than what he had tasted of the bitterness of death and its hardness, so he wished for it at that. “*My money cannot help me*” (*mā aghnā*): a denial, or a question by way of disavowal, that is: what thing has availed me, of the ease I had? “*All my power is gone*”: my rule and my power over people, and I am left poor and abased. From Ibn ʿAbbās: it was revealed about al-Aswad ibn ʿAbd al-Ashadd. From Fanākhusraw, who was called ʿAḍud: when he said,

> ʿAḍud al-Dawla and son of its pillar, / king of kings, conqueror of fate,

he did not prosper after it, and went mad, and his tongue would speak only this verse. Ibn ʿAbbās said: my argument has gone astray from me; and its meaning is: the argument I used to argue with in the world has come to nothing.

{L|Burn him in Hell|thumma l-jaḥīma ṣallūhu} then burn him in nothing but Hell, which is the greatest Fire, because he was a ruler who made himself great over people. One says, *ṣaliya l-nāra* and *ṣallāhu l-nāra*. His being put into the chain is that it is wound around his body until {p|605}its folds wrap around him, while he is between them, pressed hard and confined, not able to move. He made it seventy cubits, meaning to describe it as long, as He said: “*If you ask forgiveness for them seventy times*” (Q 9:80), meaning many times; because when it is long the pressing is harder. The meaning in putting the chain before the putting-in is like that in putting Hell before the burning, that is: put him in nothing but this chain, as though it were more dreadful than the other places of pressing in Hell. The meaning of “*then*” is to point to the gap between the shackling and the burning in Hell, and between that and the putting into the chain; not to the lapse of time. “*Truly he*” is giving a reason by way of a fresh start, which is more forceful, as though it were said: what is the matter with him, that he is punished with this hard punishment? And it was answered with that.

In His saying “*Nor did he advocate the feeding of the poor*” there are two strong proofs of how great a crime it is to deprive the poor. One of them is joining it to unbelief and making it its companion. The second is mentioning the urging and not the doing, so that it is known that one who leaves off urging is in this station; then how about one who leaves off doing? And how good is the saying of the poet:

> When guests come down, he is ill-tempered / with the tribe, until its cooking pots are lifted.

He means he urged them to the hospitality, hurried them on and was hard with them. From Abū l-Dardāʾ: he used to urge his wife to make plenty of broth for the sake of the poor, and he would say, “*We have taken off half the chain by faith; shall we not take off its other half?*” It is also said: it is the unbelievers’ refusing, and their saying, “*Why should we give to those whom GOD could feed, if He so willed?*” (Q 36:47). And the meaning is “on giving the food of the poor.” “*Friend*”: a near one who defends him and grieves for him, because they shun him and flee from him, {p|606}as in His saying “*No friend will care about his close friend*” (Q 70:10). *Al-ghislīn* is the washings of the people of the Fire, and what flows from their bodies of pus and blood; it is *fiʿlīn* from *al-ghusl*. “*The sinners*”: those guilty of sin, the people of offenses. *Khaṭiʾa l-rajulu* is said when he did wrong on purpose; and they are the idolaters, from Ibn ʿAbbās. *Al-khāṭiyūn* is also read, with the *hamza* turned to *yāʾ*, and *al-khāṭūn* with it dropped. From Ibn ʿAbbās: “*What are al-khāṭūn? All of us step* (*nakhṭū*).” And Abū l-Aswad al-Duʾalī related from him: “*What are al-khāṭūn? It is only al-khāṭiʾūn. What are al-ṣābūn? It is only al-ṣābiʾūn*.” It may also be that those are meant who step over the truth to falsehood and go past God’s limits.

It is an oath by all things together, embracing and encompassing them, because they do not go out of two kinds: seen and not seen. It is also said: this world and the Hereafter; bodies and spirits; men and jinn; the creation and the Creator; blessings outward and inward. That this Qurʾān “*This is the utterance of an honorable messenger*”: that is, he says it and speaks it by way of a message from God. “*Not the utterance of a poet*,” nor of a soothsayer, as you claim. The fewness is in the sense of nothing, that is: you do not believe, nor take heed, at all. And the meaning is: how unbelieving you are, and how heedless! “*A revelation*”: it is a sending down, making clear that it is the word of a messenger to whom it was sent down “*from the Lord of the universe*.” Abū l-Sammāl read *tanzīlan*, that is, it was sent down in a sending down. It is also said: “the noble messenger” is Gabriel ﵇; but His saying “*Not the utterance of a poet*” is proof that it is Muḥammad ﷺ, because the meaning is on establishing that he is a messenger, not a poet and not a soothsayer.

{p|607}*Al-taqawwul* is fabricating speech, as though there were a forcing in it from the fabricator. He called the fabricated sayings “*aqāwīl*” to belittle them and make them contemptible, as in your saying, “*al-aʿājīb*” and “*al-aḍāḥīk*,” as though it were the plural of *ufʿūla* from *al-qawl*. The meaning is: had he claimed of Us anything We did not say, We would have killed him in cold blood, as kings do to one who lies about them, hastening with anger and vengeance. So He drew killing in cold blood in its form to make it more terrifying: which is that he is taken by his hand and his neck is struck. He singled out the right over the left because when the executioner means to land the blow on the nape he takes him by his left; and when he means to land it on his throat and strike him in the face with the sword, which is harder on the one bound, for his looking at the sword, he takes him by his right. The meaning of “*We would have punished him*” (*la-akhadhnā minhu bi-l-yamīn*, “We would have seized him by the right hand”) is: We would have taken his right hand; as His saying “*We would have stopped the revelations to him*” (*la-qaṭaʿnā minhu l-watīn*, “then We would have cut his aorta”) is: We would have cut his aorta. And this is clear. *Al-watīn* is the heart’s cord, which is the jugular vein: when it is cut, its owner dies. *Wa-law tuqūwila*, built for the passive, is also read.

It was said “*holding back*” in describing “*anyone*,” because it is in the sense of a group; and it is a noun that falls in general negation, the singular and the plural, the masculine and the feminine being equal in it. From it is the saying of God Most High “*We make no distinction among any of His messengers*” (Q 2:285), “*you are not the same as any other women*” (Q 33:32). The pronoun in “*from him*” is for the killing, that is: none of you is able to hold it back from him and push it away from him. Or for the Messenger of God, that is: you are not able to hold the killer back from him and come between the two. The address is to people; and likewise in the saying of God Most High “*We know; some of you are rejectors*,” which is a threat for denying. It is also said: the address is to the Muslims, and the meaning is that among them are people who will disbelieve in the Qurʾān. “*It is*”: the pronoun is for the Qurʾān; “*but sorrow*” to the unbelievers in it, the deniers of it, when they see the reward of those who believe it. Or for the denying. And that the Qurʾān is certainty, the truth of certainty, as in your saying, “He is the learned man, the true learned man,” and “the real learned man.” And the meaning is: the very eye of certainty and pure certainty. “*Therefore, you shall glorify*” God by mentioning His great name, which is His saying, “Glory be to God”; and worship Him in thanks for what He made you fit for, of His revealing to you.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-Ḥāqqa, God reckons with him an easy reckoning*.”
