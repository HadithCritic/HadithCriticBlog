::: passage 66:1-2 | 4:562-565

It is related that the Messenger of God ﷺ was alone with Māriya on ʿĀʾisha’s day, and Ḥafṣa learned of it. He said to her, “*Keep it hidden for me. I have made Māriya unlawful to myself; and I give you the good news that Abū Bakr and ʿUmar will rule {p|563}the affair of my community after me*.” She told ʿĀʾisha of it, and the two were close friends. It is also said: he was alone with her on Ḥafṣa’s day, and he pleased her with that and asked her to keep it hidden, and she did not. So he divorced her, kept apart from his wives, and stayed twenty-nine nights in Māriya’s house. It is related that ʿUmar said to her, “*Had there been any good in the family of al-Khaṭṭāb, he would not have divorced you*.” Then Gabriel ﵇ came down and said, “*Take her back, for she fasts much and prays much at night, and she is among your wives in Paradise*.” It is also related that he drank honey in the house of Zaynab bint Jaḥsh, and ʿĀʾisha and Ḥafṣa agreed together and said to him, “*We smell on you the smell of* maghāfīr,” and the Messenger of God ﷺ disliked a bad smell; so he made the honey unlawful. So the meaning is: “*why do you prohibit what GOD has made lawful for you*,” of the slave woman or the honey? “*Just to please*” is either an explanation of “*do you prohibit*,” or a circumstantial clause, or {p|564}a new sentence. This was a slip on his part, because no one may make unlawful what God has made lawful; for God, mighty and majestic is He, made lawful what He made lawful only for a wisdom and a benefit that He knew in making it lawful, and if one makes it unlawful, that turns the benefit into harm. “*GOD is Forgiver*”: He has forgiven you what you slipped in; “*Merciful*”: He has had mercy on you and not taken you to task for it.

{L|GOD has decreed for you the laws dealing with your oaths|qad faraḍa llāhu lakum taḥillata aymānikum} there are two meanings in it. One of them: God has laid down for you the exception in your oaths, from your saying “So-and-so released himself in his oath,” when he made an exception in it; from this is “*ḥillan*, may you refuse the curse,” meaning: make an exception in your oath if you have made it unrestricted. That is, that he says “if God wills” right after it, so that he does not break it. The second: God has laid down for you its release by expiation. From this is his saying ﵇, “*No man has three children die and the Fire touch him, except for the release of the oath*”; and the saying of Dhū l-Rumma:

> Brief, like the release of oaths.

{Q} What is the ruling on making the lawful unlawful? {A} There is disagreement about it. Abū Ḥanīfa holds it an oath in everything, and looks to the benefit intended in what he makes unlawful. If he makes a food unlawful, he has sworn on eating it; or a slave woman, on lying with her; or a wife, on the vow of abstinence from her, if he had no intention. If he intended *ẓihār*, it is *ẓihār*; if he intended divorce, it is an irrevocable divorce; likewise if he intended two. If he intended three, it is as he intended. If he says, “I intended a lie,” he is believed between himself and God Most High, but he is not believed in court so as to cancel the vow of abstinence. If he says, “Everything lawful is unlawful to me,” it is on food and drink if he had no intention, and otherwise on what he intended. Al-Shāfiʿī does not hold it an oath, but a cause of expiation in women alone; and if he intended divorce, it is revocable according to him. From Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd and Zayd ﵃: making unlawful is an oath. From ʿUmar: if he intended divorce, it is revocable. From ʿAlī ﵁: three. From Zayd: one, irrevocable. From ʿUthmān: *ẓihār*. {p|565}Masrūq held it nothing, and said, “*I do not care whether I made her unlawful or a bowl of tharīd*.” Likewise from al-Shaʿbī; he said it is nothing, taking as argument the saying of God Most High “*You shall not utter lies with your own tongues stating: ‘This is lawful, and this is unlawful’*” (Q 16:116), and the saying of God Most High “*do not prohibit good things that are made lawful by GOD*” (Q 5:87); and what God Most High has not made unlawful, no one may make unlawful, nor does it become unlawful by his making it so. It is not established that the Messenger of God ﷺ said of what God made lawful, “It is unlawful to me.” He only kept away from Māriya because of an oath that came first from him, which is his saying ﵇, “*By God, I will not come near her after today*.” So it was said to him, “*why do you prohibit what GOD has made lawful for you*” that is, why do you keep from it because of the oath? He means: go ahead with what you swore on, and expiate your oath. Like it is the saying of God Most High “*We forbade him from accepting all the nursing mothers*” (Q 28:12), that is, We kept him from them. The plain sense of the saying of God Most High “*GOD has decreed for you the laws dealing with your oaths*” is that there was an oath from him.

{Q} Did the Messenger of God ﷺ make expiation for it? {A} From al-Ḥasan: he did not expiate, because his past and later sins were forgiven him; it is only a teaching for the believers. From Muqātil: the Messenger of God ﷺ freed a slave for making Māriya unlawful. “*GOD is your Lord*”: your lord and the one who takes charge of your affairs; “*and He is the Knowing*” what sets you right, so He lays it down for you; “*Most Wise*,” so He commands and forbids you only what wisdom requires. It is also said: your Master is nearer to you than yourselves, so His counsel is more profitable to you than your counsel to yourselves.

{L|Some of his wives|baʿḍi azwājihi} Ḥafṣa. The talk he confided to her is the talk of Māriya and the leadership of the two elders. “*One of them spread it*”: she spread it to ʿĀʾisha. *Anbaʾat bihi* is also read. “*GOD let him know about it*”: He let the Prophet ﵇ know of the talk, that is, of its being spread, on the tongue of Gabriel. It is also said: God made the talk plain to the Prophet ﷺ, from *ẓuhūr*. “*He then informed his wife of part of the issue*”: he let her know part of the talk, out of generosity. Sufyān said: “*Overlooking has always been the act of the generous*.” *ʿArafa baʿḍahu* is also read, that is, he repaid her for it; from your saying to one who has done ill, “I will know that for you,” and “I have known what you did.” From this is “*GOD is fully aware of their innermost intentions*” (Q 4:63), and it is frequent in the Qurʾān. His repayment was his divorcing her. It is also said: what was made known was the talk of the leadership, and what was passed over was the talk of Māriya. It is related {p|566}that he ﷺ said to her, “*Did I not tell you to keep it hidden for me?*” She said, “*By Him who sent you with the truth, I could not hold myself back for joy at the honor God had given her father*.”

{Q} Why was it not said, “and when she told it to some of them, and he made part of it known to her”? {A} The aim is not to make plain to whom it was spread and to whom it was made known, but only to mention Ḥafṣa’s offense in the telling and spreading of it coming from her, and that from the Messenger of God ﷺ, by his generosity and forbearance, there came nothing but letting her know part of it, which was the talk of the leadership. Do you not see how, when the aim in His saying “*He then informed his wife … She asked him, ‘Who informed you of this?’*” was to mention the one told, He brought her pronoun?

{L|If the two of you repent|in tatūbā} an address to Ḥafṣa and ʿĀʾisha by way of turning, so as to be more telling in reproaching them. From Ibn ʿAbbās: “*I was always eager to ask ʿUmar about the two, until he made the pilgrimage and I made it with him. When we were on part of the road he turned aside, and I turned aside with him with the water-skin, and poured the water on his hand, and he made ablution. I said, ‘Who are the two?’ He said, ‘I wonder at you, Ibn ʿAbbās*,’ as though he disliked what I asked him about. *Then he said, ‘They are Ḥafṣa and ʿĀʾisha.’*” “*your hearts have listened*” (*ṣaghat qulūbukumā*, “your hearts have inclined”): there has come from you two what requires repentance, which is your hearts’ swerving from what is due in being sincere toward the Messenger of God ﷺ, loving what he loves and disliking what he dislikes. Ibn Masʿūd read *fa-qad zāghat*. “*But if you band together*”: if you help each other “*against him*” in what grieves him, of excessive jealousy and spreading his secret, he will not lack one who backs him. And how could one who backs him not be, when God is “*his Master*,” that is, his friend and helper? Adding “*He*” makes known that His helping him is one of His firm resolves, and that He takes charge of it Himself. “*And so is Gabriel*,” the head of the cherubim: He joined his mention to His own, singling him out from among the angels, to make him great and show his place with Him. “*And the righteous believers*”: whoever is righteous among the believers, meaning everyone who believed and did righteous work. From Saʿīd ibn Jubayr: whoever of them is free of hypocrisy. It is also said: the prophets; it is also said: the Companions; it is also said: the caliphs among them.

{Q} Is *ṣāliḥ al-muʾminīn* one or many? {A} It is one by which many are meant, as in your saying, “The righteous among people do not do this,” by which you mean the kind, as in your saying, “Whoever of them is righteous does not do it.” Like it is your saying, “I was among the night-talkers and the settled.” Its origin may also be *ṣāliḥū l-muʾminīn*, with *wāw*, and it was written without *wāw* by the sound, because the sound of the singular and the plural is the same in it; as things have come in the codex in which the rule of sound is followed and not the convention of writing. “*Also, the angels*,” with their great number and the heavens filled with their throngs, “*after that*,” after the help of God, His confidant and the righteous of the believers, “*are his helpers*” (*ẓahīr*): a troop backing him, as though they were one hand against whoever is hostile to him. So what can two women’s backing each other come to against one whose {p|567}backers are these?

{Q} His saying “*after that*” makes great the angels and their backing, when the help of God, Gabriel and the righteous of the believers came before, and the help of God Most High is far greater. {A} The backing of the angels is part of God’s help. So it is as though He set His help, mighty is He, by them and their backing above the other ways of His help, because they are above all His creatures. *Taẓẓāharā*, *tataẓāharā* and *taẓẓahharā* are also read.

*Yubdilahu* is read light, and heavy for multiplicity. “*Muslims, believers*”: confessing, sincere. “*Pious*” (*sāʾiḥāt*): fasting. *Sayyiḥāt* is also read, and it is more telling. The one fasting was called *sāʾiḥ* because the wanderer has no provision with him, so he keeps holding back until he finds something to eat; and the one fasting was likened to him in his holding back until the time of breaking the fast comes. It is also said: *sāʾiḥāt* are emigrants. From Zayd ibn Aslam: there was no wandering in this community except emigration.

{Q} How could the wives given in exchange be better than them, when there were no women on the face of the earth better than the mothers of the believers? {A} If the Messenger of God divorced them for disobeying him and hurting him, they would not remain in that description; and others described with these descriptions, together with obedience to the Messenger of God ﷺ and yielding to his wish and his pleasure, would be better than they. He alluded to that in His saying “*devout*,” because devotion is standing in obedience to God, and obedience to God lies in obedience to His Messenger.

{Q} Why were all the descriptions left without the coordinating particle, and why was it put between “*previously married*” and “*virgins*”? {A} Because they are two descriptions that exclude each other; they cannot meet in them as the other descriptions meet in them, {p|568}so the *wāw* could not be done without.

::: passage 66:6-7 | 4:568-569

{L|Protect yourselves|qū anfusakum} by leaving sins and doing acts of obedience, “*and your families*,” by holding them to what you hold yourselves to. In the ḥadīth: “*God have mercy on a man who says, ‘My family! Your prayer, your fasting, your alms, your poor, your orphan, your neighbors!’ Perhaps God will gather them with him in Paradise*.” It is also said: the people most severely punished on the Day of Resurrection are those who left their families ignorant. *Wa-ahlūkum* is also read, coordinated on the *wāw* of “*guard*,” and the coordination is good because of what comes between.

{Q} Is not the implied form: guard yourselves, and let your families guard themselves? {A} No. But the coordinated word is, in the implied form, alongside the *wāw*, and “*yourselves*” comes after it; so it is as though it were said: guard, you and your families, yourselves. When you put the absent together with the one addressed, you let the one addressed prevail over it, and made their pronoun together in the wording of the one addressed. “*The Hellfire whose fuel is people and rocks*”: a kind of fire that is kindled only by people and stones, as other fires are kindled by firewood. From Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄: they are stones of sulfur, which is the hottest of things when fire is lit on it. *Wuqūduhā*, with *ḍamma*, is also read, that is, what has its kindling. “*Over it*,” in charge of its affairs and the punishing of its people, “*are stern and powerful angels*,” meaning the nineteen guards of Hell and their helpers, “*stern and powerful*”: in their bodies is harshness and severity, that is, roughness and strength; or in their acts is roughness and coarseness. No pity takes them in carrying out God’s commands, in being angry for Him and taking vengeance on His enemies. “*What He commands them*” is in the place of the accusative as a substitute, that is: they do not disobey what God commands, that is, His command, as in the saying of God Most High “*from following my orders? Have you rebelled against me?*” (Q 20:93); or they do not disobey Him in what He commands them.

{Q} Are the two clauses not of one meaning? {A} No. The meaning of the first is that they accept His commands and hold to them, and do not refuse them or reject them. The meaning of the second is that they carry out what they are commanded, {p|569}not dragging their feet over it nor being slow in it.

{Q} God addressed the associators who denied the revelation with this very thing in the saying of God Most High “*then beware of the Hellfire, whose fuel is people and rocks*,” and He said, “*it awaits the disbelievers*” (Q 2:24), making it prepared for the unbelievers. So what is the meaning of His addressing the believers with it? {A} The grave sinners, even if their levels are above the levels of the unbelievers, dwell with the unbelievers in one abode. So it was said to those who believe: guard yourselves, by keeping away from grave sin, from dwelling with the unbelievers for whom this fire so described is prepared. He may also command them to guard against apostasy and regret at entering Islam; and it may be an address to those who believed with their tongues, who are the hypocrites. What supports that is the saying of God Most High right after it: “*O you who disbelieved, do not apologize today. You are being requited only for what you did*”: that is, that is said to them when they enter the Fire: make no excuse, because you have no excuse, or because making excuses does not profit you.

::: passage 66:8-8 | 4:569-570

{L|A firm repentance|tawbatan naṣūḥan} repentance was described by sincerity by a figurative attribution. Sincerity is a description of the repenters: that they counsel themselves sincerely by repentance, and bring it in its right way, making good what they let slip and wiping out evil deeds. That is, that they repent of ugly acts for their ugliness, regretting them, grieved with the deepest grief for having committed them, resolved that they will not go back to any ugly act until milk goes back into the udder, and setting their souls firmly on that. From ʿAlī ﵁: he heard a desert Arab say, “*O God, I ask Your forgiveness and repent to You*.” He said, “*You there, quickness of tongue in repentance is the repentance of liars*.” He said, “*And what is repentance?*” He said, “*Six things gather it: for past sins, regret; for obligations, making them up; giving back what was wrongfully taken; seeking the pardon of opponents; that you resolve not to go back; and that you melt your soul in obedience to God as you reared it in disobedience, and make it taste the bitterness of acts of obedience as you made it taste the sweetness of sins*.” From Ḥudhayfa: “*It is enough evil for a man that he repent of a sin and then go back to it*.” From Shahr ibn Ḥawshab: that he not go back to it, even if he is cut with the sword and burned with fire. From Ibn al-Sammāk: that you set the sin in which you had little shame before God in front of your eyes, and make ready for what awaits you. It is also said: a repentance that is not repented of. From al-Suddī: repentance is sound only with sincere counsel to oneself and to the believers, because one whose repentance is sound loves that people be like him. It is also said: *naṣūḥ* is from the sewing (*naṣāḥa*) of a garment, that is, a repentance that darns {p|570}the rents in your religion and patches your worn cloth. It is also said: pure, from their saying *ʿasal nāṣiḥ*, honey that is clear of wax. It may also be meant: a repentance that counsels people, that is, calls them to the like of it, because its trace shows in the one who has it and in his using earnestness and resolve in acting on what it requires. Zayd ibn ʿAlī read *tawban naṣūḥan*. *Nuṣūḥan*, with *ḍamma*, is also read; it is the verbal noun of *naṣaḥa*: *al-nuṣḥ* and *al-nuṣūḥ* are like *al-shukr* and *al-shukūr*, *al-kufr* and *al-kufūr*. That is: having sincerity; or it counsels sincerely; or repent for the sincere counsel of yourselves, on the view that it is an object of purpose.

{L|Your Lord will then|ʿasā rabbukum} a giving of hope from God to His servants. There are two ways in it. One of them: that it is after the custom of tyrants, of answering with “perhaps” and “it may be,” and that coming from them in place of certainty and decision. The second: that He brings it to teach the servants that one must keep between fear and hope. What points to the first meaning, and that it has the meaning of decision, is the reading of Ibn Abī ʿAbla, *wa-yudkhilkum*, in the jussive, coordinated on the place of “*Your Lord will then remit*” (*ʿasā rabbukum an yukaffira*, “perhaps your Lord will expiate”), as though it were said: repent, and it will make binding for you the expiation of your evil deeds, and He will bring you in. “*On that day, GOD will not disappoint*” is put in the accusative by “*admit you*.” “*will not disappoint*” is an allusion to those of the people of unbelief and grave sin whom God disgraced, and a calling on the believers to praise Him for having kept them from the like of their state. “*Their light will radiate*” on the bridge. “*Perfect our light for us*”: Ibn ʿAbbās said: they say that when the light of the hypocrites is put out, out of fear. From al-Ḥasan: God will complete it for them, but they pray to draw near to God, as in the saying of God Most High “*ask forgiveness of your sins*” (Q 47:19), when he is forgiven. It is also said: the lowest of them in rank say it, because they are given as much light as lets them see where to put their feet, since the light is in proportion to the works; so they ask Him to complete it as a favor. It is also said: those who are foremost to Paradise pass over the bridge like lightning, some of them like the wind, and some of them crawling and creeping; and those are the ones who say, “*Our Lord, perfect our light for us*.”

{Q} How do they fear, when the believers are safe: “*or one who comes secure on the Day of Resurrection?*” (Q 41:40), “*will have no fear*” (Q 2:38), “*The great horror will not worry them*” (Q 21:103)? And how do they seek to draw near, when the abode is not an abode of drawing near? {A} As for the fear, it may be after the custom of humans, even while they hold firmly to safety. As for drawing near, since their state is like the state of those who seek to draw near, in that they ask for the mercy that is already theirs, He called it drawing near.

{L|Struggle against the disbelievers|jāhidi l-kuffāra} with the sword, “*and the hypocrites*,” with argument; and use harshness and roughness against {p|571}both parties in what you strive against them with, of fighting and arguing. From Qatāda: striving against the hypocrites is carrying out the prescribed punishments on them. From Mujāhid: with the threat. It is also said: by spreading their secrets.

::: passage 66:10-10 | 4:571-572

God, mighty and majestic is He, likened the state of the unbelievers (in that they are punished for their unbelief and their enmity to the believers with the punishment of the likes of them, without sparing or favor, and that the bond of lineage or tie of marriage between them and the believers does not profit them along with their enmity to them, because their enmity to them and their unbelief in God and His Messenger cut the ties and severed the bonds and made them further away than strangers, and further still, even if the believer to whom the unbeliever is joined is a prophet of God’s prophets) to the state of the wife of Noah and the wife of Lot. When they played the hypocrite and betrayed the two messengers, the two messengers did not avail them at all against God’s punishment by right of the tie of marriage between them. “*The two of them were told*” to the two of them at their death, or on the Day of Resurrection: “*Enter the Hellfire with*” the rest of “*those who deserved it*” (*al-dākhilīn*, “those who enter”), who have no tie with the prophets; or with those of your brothers who enter it, of the people of Noah and the people of Lot. And He likened the state of the believers (in that the tie of the unbelievers does not harm them nor lessen anything of their reward and nearness with God) to the state of Pharaoh’s wife and her rank with God Most High, though she was the wife of the worst of God’s enemies, the one who uttered the greatest word; and to Mary the daughter of ʿImrān and what she was given of honor in this world and the Hereafter and being chosen above the women of the worlds, though her people were unbelievers.

In the folds of these two likenings is an allusion to the two mothers of the believers mentioned at the start of the sūra, and to what they let slip of backing each other against the Messenger of God ﷺ in what he disliked; and a warning to them in the harshest and severest way, because of the mention of unbelief in the likening. Like it in harshness is the saying of God Most High “*As for those who disbelieve, GOD does not need anyone*” (Q 3:97). And a pointing to this: that their due is to be, in sincerity and perfection in it, like these two believing women, and not to rely on their being the wives of the Messenger of God; for that excellence profits them only if they are sincere. The allusion to Ḥafṣa is the weightier, because Lot’s wife spread his secrets as Ḥafṣa spread the secret of the Messenger of God. The secrets and hints of the revelation in every chapter reach such a degree of subtlety and hiddenness that they are too fine for the learned man to grasp and slip from his insight.

{p|572}{Q} What is the use of His saying “*of our*” (*min ʿibādinā*, “of Our servants”)? {A} Since the likening is built on righteousness being present in a human being, whoever he may be, and that it alone is what brings one to winning and attaining what is with God, He said “*two of our righteous servants*.” So He mentioned the two famous prophets, the well-known, as two servants who were only like the rest of Our servants, with no difference between them and the others except righteousness alone, to show it and make it plain: because a servant of the servants does not outweigh others with Him except by righteousness and nothing else, and what else people outweigh one another by in the eyes of people is no cause of outweighing with Him.

{Q} What was their betrayal? {A} Their hypocrisy, their hiding unbelief, and their backing others against the two messengers. Noah’s wife said to his people, “He is mad,” and Lot’s wife pointed out his guests. It cannot be that by betrayal debauchery is meant, because that is repulsive to natures and a disgrace to everyone; unlike unbelief, for the unbelievers do not find it repulsive but find it good and call it truth. From Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄: “*No prophet’s wife ever played the harlot*.”

::: passage 66:11-12 | 4:572-574

Pharaoh’s wife is Āsiya bint Muzāḥim. It is also said: she was the paternal aunt of Moses ﵇. She believed when she heard of Moses’s staff swallowing the deceit, and Pharaoh tortured her. From Abū Hurayra: Pharaoh pinned his wife down with four stakes, set her facing the sun, laid her on her back, and put a millstone on her breast. It is also said: he commanded that a great rock be thrown on her; she prayed to God, and He raised up her spirit, and the rock was thrown on a body with no spirit in it. From al-Ḥasan: God saved her with the noblest saving and raised her to Paradise, and she eats and drinks and enjoys herself in it. It is also said: when she said, “*My Lord, build a home for me at You in Paradise*,” she was shown her house in Paradise being built. It is also said: it is of pearl. It is also said: she was being tortured in the sun, and the angels shaded her.

{Q} What is the meaning of joining “*with You*” and “*in Paradise*”? {A} She asked for nearness to God’s mercy and distance from the punishment of His enemies; then she made plain the place of nearness by her saying “*in Paradise*.” Or she wanted a high degree in Paradise, and that her garden should be among the gardens nearest the Throne, which are the Gardens of Refuge; so she expressed nearness to the Throne by her saying “*with You*.” “*from Pharaoh and his works*”: from Pharaoh’s work; or from Pharaoh’s foul self and his tyrannous power, and especially {p|573}from his work, which is unbelief, worshiping idols, wrongdoing and torture without a crime. “*Save me from the transgressing people*”: from all the Copts. In it is a proof that seeking refuge in God, taking shelter with Him and asking Him for deliverance in trials and misfortunes are of the ways of the righteous and the practices of the prophets and messengers: “*Grant me victory against them, and deliver me and my company of believers*” (Q 26:118); “*Our Lord, save us from the persecution of these oppressive people. Deliver us, with Your mercy, from the disbelieving people*” (Q 10:85–86).

{L|Into her|fīhi} into the private part. Ibn Masʿūd read *fīhā*, as it is read in Sūrat al-Anbiyāʾ (Q 21:91), and the pronoun is for the whole of her. I have already said something about this adverbial. Of the oddities of the commentaries is that the private part is the neck opening of the shift, and the meaning of “guarded it” is that she kept Gabriel from it; and that in the likening He joined one who had a husband and one who had none, to console widows and comfort them. “*She believed*” (*wa-ṣaddaqat*): it is read heavy and light, on the view that she made the words and the books truthful, meaning she described them as truthful, which is the very meaning of confirming.

{Q} What are God’s words and His books? {A} By His words may be meant His scrolls that He sent down to Idrīs and others, which He called words because they were short; and by His books, the four books. Or all that God spoke with to His angels and others may be meant, and all that He wrote in the Tablet and elsewhere. *Bi-kalimati llāhi wa-kitābihi* is also read, that is, in Jesus and in the book sent down to him, which is the Gospel.

{Q} Why was “*of the devout*” said in the masculine? {A} Because devotion is a description that takes in whoever is devout of both sexes, so its males were let prevail over its females. *Min* is partitive. It may also be for the beginning of the extent, on the view that she was born of the devout, because she was of the descendants of Aaron, the brother of Moses ﵉.

From the Prophet ﷺ: “*Many among men have been perfect, and among women only four have been perfect: Āsiya bint Muzāḥim, the wife of Pharaoh; Mary the daughter of ʿImrān; Khadīja bint Khuwaylid; and Fāṭima bint Muḥammad. And the excellence of ʿĀʾisha over women is like the excellence of tharīd over other food*.” As for what is related, that ʿĀʾisha asked {p|574}the Messenger of God ﷺ how it was that God named the Muslim woman, meaning Mary, and did not name the unbelieving women, and he said, “*Out of hatred for them*”; she said, “*And what are their names?*” He said, “*The name of Noah’s wife is Wāʿila, and the name of Lot’s wife is Wāhila*”: this is a report on which the trace of fabrication is plain and clear. God Most High named a number of unbelievers by their names and by-names; and were naming for love and leaving it for hatred, He would have named Āsiya, whom He joined with Mary in the likening for the believers. God refuses but to put on what is made up a sign that gives it away, and the speech of the Messenger of God ﷺ is wiser and sounder than that.
