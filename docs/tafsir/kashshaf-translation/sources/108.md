surah: 108
title: Sūrat al-Kawthar (108)
revelation: Meccan. Three verses. [Revealed after al-ʿĀdiyāt (100).]

::: passage 108:1-3 | 4:806-808

In the reading of the Messenger of God ﷺ: *innā anṭaynāka*, with *nūn*; and in his ḥadīth ﷺ: “*and give (anṭū) the middling ones*.” *Al-kawthar* is the pattern *fawʿal* from *kathra* (“abundance”), and it means what is abundant beyond measure. A Bedouin woman whose son had come back from a journey was asked, “*What did your son come back with?*” She said, “*He came back with a kawthar*.” And he said: “*You are abundant (kathīr), O son of Marwān, and good; and your father, son of noble women, was a kawthar*.”

{p|807}It is also said that *al-kawthar* is a river in Paradise. From the Prophet ﷺ: he recited it when it was revealed to him and said, “*Do you know what al-Kawthar is? It is a river in Paradise that my Lord has promised me, with much good in it*.” In describing it, it is related: sweeter than honey, whiter than milk, colder than ice, softer than butter; its banks are chrysolite and its vessels are silver, as many as the stars of the sky. It is related: “*Whoever drinks from it will never thirst. The first to come to it will be the poor among the Emigrants, with soiled clothes and disheveled heads, who are not given women of comfort in marriage and for whom the doors are not opened. One of them dies with his need still stirring in his chest; were he to swear by God, He would fulfill his oath*.” From Ibn ʿAbbās: he explained al-Kawthar as abundant good. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr said to him, “*People say it is a river in Paradise*.” He said, “*It is part of the abundant good*.”

*Al-naḥr* is slaughtering camels. From ʿAṭiyya: it is the dawn prayer at Jamʿ and the slaughter at Minā. It is also said: the prayer of the feast and the sacrifice. It is also said that it is prayer in general, and *al-naḥr* is placing the right hand on the left.

The meaning: you have been given, of the good of both worlds, what has no limit for its abundance, which no one but you has been given, and the giver of all of it is I, the God of the worlds. So two splendid joys have come together for you: receiving the noblest and fullest gift, from the most generous giver and the greatest benefactor. So worship your Lord, who has made you mighty by His giving, honored you, and kept you from the favors of creatures, in defiance of your people who worship other than God; and slaughter for His sake and in His name when you slaughter, in opposition to them in their slaughtering to idols.

{L|Your opponent|inna shāniʾaka} from among your people, for your opposing them, “*will be the loser*” (*huwa l-abtar*, “is the one cut off”), not you. For everyone who is born among the believers until the Day of Resurrection is your children and your offspring; your mention is raised on the pulpits and the minarets and on the tongue of every scholar and everyone who remembers God until the end of time, beginning with the mention of God and following it with the mention of you; and in the Hereafter you will have what cannot be described. One like you is not called cut off. The one cut off is the one who hates you, forgotten in {p|808}this world and the next, and if he is mentioned at all, he is mentioned with a curse. They used to say, “*Muḥammad is a ṣunbūr* (a lone man with no heirs); *when he dies, his mention dies*.” It is also said that it was revealed about al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil, who had called him *al-abtar*. *Al-abtar* is one who has no offspring; from it comes *al-ḥimār al-abtar*, the donkey with no tail.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-Kawthar, God will give him drink from every river in Paradise, and ten good deeds will be written for him for every offering that God’s servants offer, or will offer, on the day of sacrifice*.”
