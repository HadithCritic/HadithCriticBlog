surah: 96
title: Sūrat al-ʿAlaq (96)
revelation: Meccan. Nineteen verses. [It is the first of the Qurʾān to be revealed.]

::: passage 96:1-5 | 4:775-777

From Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid: it is the first sūra revealed. Most of the commentators hold that al-Fātiḥa was the first revealed, then Sūrat al-Qalam.

{L|In the name of your Lord|bi-smi rabbika} its position is accusative, as a circumstantial clause: that is, recite, opening with the name of your Lord; say “In the name of God,” then recite.

{Q} How did He say “*created*” without mentioning an object for it, then say “*created man*”? {A} It is in one of two ways. Either no object is supplied for it, and what is meant is that He is the one from whom creation came and who keeps it to Himself, there being no creator besides Him; or one is supplied, and what is meant is “created everything,” so that it takes in every created thing, because it is unrestricted, and no created thing has a better claim to be supplied than any other. His saying “*created man*” singles out man for mention among all that the creating takes in, because the revelation is sent down to him and he is the noblest of what is on the earth. It may also mean “who created man,” as He said “*The Most Gracious. Teacher of the Quran. Creator of the human beings*” (Q 55:1–3): “*who created*” was said vaguely, then explained by His saying “*created man*,” to make the creation of man great and to point to the wonder of his making.

{Q} Why did He say “*from an embryo*” (*min ʿalaq*, “from clots”), in the plural, when man was created only from one clot, as in His saying “*from a tiny drop, which turns into a hanging (embryo)*” (Q 22:5)? {A} Because {p|776}“*man*” has the sense of a plural, as in His saying “*The human being is utterly lost*” (*inna l-insāna la-fī khusr*, “man is in loss,” Q 103:2).

{L|Most Exalted|al-akram} who has perfection in going beyond every generosity. He gives His servants favors beyond counting, and is forbearing with them, not hastening punishment on them despite their unbelief, their denial of His favors, their committing what is forbidden and their casting off of His commands; and He accepts their repentance and passes over them after they have committed enormities. So His generosity has no end or limit. It is as if there were no generosity beyond the generosity of giving the benefits of knowledge, since He said “*Most Exalted. Teaches by means of the pen. He teaches man what he never knew*.” So He pointed to the perfection of His generosity by His having taught His servants what they did not know and brought them out of the darkness of ignorance into the light of knowledge; and He drew attention to the merit of the knowledge of writing, for the great benefits in it that none but He can encompass. The sciences were not recorded, nor wisdom bound, nor the reports and sayings of the ancients kept, nor God’s sent-down books, except by writing; and were it not for writing, the affairs of religion and of the world would not have been set straight. Were there no proof of God’s subtle wisdom and fine planning but the pen and the script, that would be enough. One of them has, describing the pen: “*Many a writer, speckled like spotted snakes, short in step, reaching the farthest goal; black-legged, they never go in earnest except when white blades play with them*.”

{p|777}Ibn al-Zubayr read *ʿallama l-khaṭṭa bi-l-qalam* (“taught writing by the pen”).

::: passage 96:6-19 | 4:777-779

{L|Indeed|kallā} a rebuke to the one who was ungrateful for God’s favor to him through his transgression, even though he is not mentioned, because the speech points to him.

{L|When he becomes rich|an raʾāhu} With verbs of the heart one says *raʾaytunī* and *ʿalimtunī* (“I saw myself,” “I knew myself”), and that is one of their particular features. Seeing here means knowing; were it in the sense of seeing with the eyes, joining the two pronouns in its verb would not be allowed. “*Rich*” (*istaghnā*, “free of need”) (*istaghnā*) is the second object.

{L|To your Lord is the ultimate destiny|inna ilā rabbika l-rujʿā} it comes by way of turning to man, threatening him and warning him of the end of transgression. *Al-rujʿā* is a verbal noun like *al-bushrā*, in the sense of *al-rujūʿ* (“returning”). It is said that it was revealed about Abū Jahl, and so too “*Have you seen the one who enjoins*.” It is related that he said to the Messenger of God ﷺ: “*Do you claim that whoever is free of need transgresses? Then turn the mountains of Mecca into silver and gold for us; perhaps we will take from them and transgress, and leave our religion and follow yours*.” Gabriel came down and said: “*If you wish, we will do it; then if they do not believe, we will do to them what we did to the people of the Table*.” So the Messenger of God ﷺ held back from praying for it, to spare them. It is related of him, God curse him, that he said: “*Does Muḥammad rub his face in the dust among you?*” They said, “*Yes*.” He said: “*By the one sworn by, if I see him I will trample his neck*.” He came to him, then turned back on his heels. They said to him, “*What is wrong with you, Abū l-Ḥakam?*” He said, “*Between me and him there is a trench of fire, and terror, and wings*.” And “*Have you seen the one who enjoins*” was revealed.

{L|Have you seen the one who enjoins|a-raʾayta lladhī yanhā} its meaning: tell me about the one who forbids one of God’s servants his prayer, whether that forbidder is on a sound way in what he forbids, which is the worship of God; or is commanding what is right and pious in what he commands, which is the worship of idols, as he believes; and so too if he is denying the truth and turning away from the true religion, as we say. “*Does he not realize that GOD sees*” {p|778}and is aware of his states, his guidance and his straying, and will repay him accordingly? This is a threat.

{Q} What does *a-raʾayta* attach to? {A} “*the one who enjoins*,” together with the conditional sentence; they stand in the place of the two objects.

{Q} Then where is the answer to the condition? {A} It is left out. The implied sense is: “if he is on guidance or commands piety, does he not know that God sees?” It was left out only because its mention in the answer to the second condition points to it.

{Q} How is it sound for “*Does he not realize*” to be the answer to the condition? {A} As it is sound in your saying, “If I honor you, will you honor me?” and “If Zayd does you good, will you do him good?”

{Q} Then what of the second *a-raʾayta*, standing between the objects of *a-raʾayta*? {A} It is extra, repeated for emphasis. From al-Ḥasan: it is Umayya ibn Khalaf, who used to forbid Salmān his prayer.

{L|Indeed|kallā} a rebuke to Abū Jahl, driving him off from forbidding the worship of God Most High and commanding the worship of al-Lāt. Then He said: “*unless he refrains*” from what he is about, “*we will take him by the forelock*”: We will surely take him by his forelock and drag him by it to the Fire. *Al-safʿ* is grasping a thing and pulling it hard. ʿAmr ibn Maʿdīkarib said: “*A people who, when the cry for help sounds, you see either bridling his colt or seizing it by the forelock*.” *La-nasfaʿanna* is also read, with the doubled *nūn*; and Ibn Masʿūd read *la-asfaʿan*. It was written in the copy with *alif*, following the rule of the pause. Since it was known that it is the forelock of the one mentioned, the article of the known was enough in place of annexation.

{L|A forelock|nāṣiyatin} a substitute for “*the forelock*.” Its standing as substitute for the definite, though it is indefinite, is allowed because it is described and so carries a meaning of its own. *Nāṣiyatun* is also read, as in “it is a forelock,” and *nāṣiyatan*, in the accusative, both as terms of reviling. Describing it as lying and sinning is by figurative attribution; in reality they belong to its owner. There is a beauty and force in it that is not in your saying “the forelock of a lying sinner.”

{L|His helpers|nādiyahu} *al-nādī* is the meeting place where people gather (*yantadī*), and what is meant is the people of the assembly. Jarīr said: “*They have an assembly, tawny-whiskered, humble*”; {p|779}and Zuhayr said: “*Among them are gatherings (maqāmāt) of fair faces*,” *al-maqāma* being the sitting place. It is related that Abū Jahl passed by the Messenger of God ﷺ while he was praying and said, “*Did I not forbid you?*” The Messenger of God ﷺ spoke harshly to him, and he said, “*Do you threaten me, when I have the largest assembly in the valley?*” And it was revealed.

{L|We will call the guardians of Hell|sa-nadʿu l-zabāniya} Ibn Abī ʿAbla read *sa-yudʿā l-zabāniyatu*, in the passive. *Al-zabāniya* in the speech of the Arabs are the police; the singular is *zibniya*, like *ʿifriya*, from *al-zabn*, which is pushing. It is also said that it is *zibnī*, as if it were relational to *al-zabn* and then changed for the relation, as they say *imsī*; its root is *zabānī*, and *zabāniya* was said with the *tāʾ* in compensation. What is meant is the angels of punishment. From the Prophet ﷺ: “*Had he called his assembly, the guards of Hell would have seized him in plain sight*.”

{L|No|kallā} a rebuke to Abū Jahl. “*You shall not obey him*”: that is, stand firm in what you are about, disobeying him, as in His saying “*Do not obey the rejectors*” (Q 68:8). “*You shall fall prostrate*”: keep up your prostrating; He means the prayer. “*And draw nearer*”: and come near to your Lord. In the ḥadīth: “*The nearest the servant is to his Lord is when he prostrates*.”

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-ʿAlaq is given a reward as though he had recited all of the Mufaṣṣal*.”
