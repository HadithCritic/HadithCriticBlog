::: passage 55:1-13 | 4:442-445

{p|443}God, mighty and exalted is He, counted up His favors, and He wanted to put first the one that comes earliest in rank among the kinds of His favors and the sorts of His blessings, which is the blessing of religion. So of the blessing of religion He put first what is in its highest rank and at its farthest height, which is His favor of the Qurʾān, its sending down and its teaching; because it is the highest in rank of God’s revelation, the loftiest in standing and the finest in its effect in the matters of religion, and it is the summit of the heavenly books, their confirmation and their standard. He put the mention of creating man after the mention of it, and then had it follow, so that it might be known that He created him only for religion, and to take in knowledge of His revelation and His books and what man was created for; it is as though the aim in making him came before him and went ahead of him. Then He mentioned the clear speech by which man is set apart from the other animals, which is eloquent speech that makes plain what is in the mind.

*Al-Raḥmān* (“the All-Merciful”) is a subject, and these verbs with their pronouns are predicates following one on another. They are left without a conjunction because they come in the manner of counting up, as you say: “Zayd made you rich after poverty, mighty after abasement, many after few; he did for you what no one did for anyone; so what do you deny of his kindness?” “*perfectly calculated*” (*bi-ḥusbān*): by a known reckoning and an even measuring; they run in their towers and stations, and in that are great benefits for people, among them the knowledge of years and reckoning. “*The stars*” (*al-najm*): the plants that come up (*yanjum*) from the earth and have no stem, such as herbs; “*and the trees*,” which have a stem. Their prostration is their yielding to God in what they were created for, and that they do not hold back, likened to one under obligation who prostrates in yielding.

{Q} How are these two sentences joined to *al-Raḥmān*? {A} In them there was no need for a joining in the wording, because of the joining in sense, since it is known that the reckoning is His reckoning and the prostration is to Him, not to another; as though it were said: the sun and the moon by His reckoning, and the stars and the trees prostrate to Him.

{Q} Why did He leave out the conjunction in the first sentences and then bring it afterward? {A} He reproached with those first sentences, which came in the way of threatening, so that each sentence might stand alone in rebuking those who denied the All-Merciful and His favors, as one among people who denies the kindnesses of his benefactor is reproached by having them counted up to him, in the example I gave before. Then He brought the speech back to its course after the reproach, in joining what must be joined, for fitness and nearness, {p|444}with the conjunction.

{Q} What fitness is there between these two sentences that the conjunction was set between them? {A} The sun and the moon are heavenly and the stars and the trees earthly, so between the two classes there is fitness by way of contrast; and the sky and the earth are always mentioned as a pair; and the running of the sun and the moon by a reckoning is of the kind of yielding to God’s command, so it fits the prostration of the plants and the trees.

It is also said: “*Teacher of the Quran*”: He made it a mark and a sign. From Ibn ʿAbbās ﵁: man is Adam; and also from him: Muḥammad, the Messenger of God ﷺ. From Mujāhid: *al-najm* is the stars of the sky.

{L|He constructed the sky|wa-l-samāʾa rafaʿahā} He created it raised up and lofty, since He made it the source of His rulings, the issuing place of His decrees, the place from which His commands and prohibitions come down, and the dwelling of His angels who come down with the revelation to His prophets. By that He drew attention to the greatness of His state, His kingship and His authority. “*And established the law*” (*wa-waḍaʿa l-mīzān*): in the reading of ʿAbd Allāh, *wa-khafaḍa l-mīzāna* (“and He lowered the balance”). By it He meant everything with which things are weighed and their amounts known, scales, steelyard, measure and gauge: that is, He created it set down, lowered on the earth, since He tied to it the rulings of His servants and their judgments and the evenness and justice in their taking and giving that He made them worship Him by. “*You shall not transgress*” (*allā taṭghaw*): so that you not transgress; or it is the *an* of explanation. ʿAbd Allāh read *lā taṭghaw*, without *an*, with “saying” intended. “*You shall establish justice*” (*wa-aqīmū l-wazna bi-l-qisṭ*): and make your weighing straight with justice. “*Do not violate the law*” (*wa-lā tukhsirū l-mīzān*): and do not lessen it. He commanded evenness and forbade transgression, which is overstepping and adding, and loss, which is skimping and lessening. He repeated the word “*balance*” to make the charge about it stronger, and to strengthen the command to use it and the urging to it. *Wa-l-samāʾu*, in the nominative, is also read; and *wa-lā takhsarū*, with *fatḥa* on the *tāʾ* and with *ḍamma*, *kasra* and *fatḥa* on the *sīn*. One says *khasara l-mīzāna yakhsiruhu* and *yakhsuruhu*; as for the *fatḥa*, it is on the view that the origin is *wa-lā takhsarū fī l-mīzāni*, with the preposition dropped and the verb joined directly.

{L|He created the earth|waḍaʿahā} He lowered it, spread out on the water, “*for all creatures*”: for created beings, which is every beast on the face of the earth. From al-Ḥasan: humans and jinn. So it is like a bed for them on which they move about. “*Fruit*”: kinds of what is eaten for pleasure. *Al-akmām* is everything that sheathes, that is, covers, of its fiber, fronds and spathes; and all of it is of use, as what it sheathes is of use, its fruit, its pith and its trunks. It is also said: *al-akmām* are the casings of the dates; the singular is *kimm*, with *kasra* on the *kāf*. *Al-ʿaṣf* is the leaves of the crops; it is also said, straw. “*And the spices*” (*al-rayḥān*): provision, which is the kernel. He meant: in it is what gives pleasure, the fruits; what joins pleasure and nourishment, the fruit of the palm; and what nourishes, the grain. *Wa-l-rayḥāni*, with *kasra*, is also read; its meaning is: and the grain, with the husk, which is the cattle’s fodder, and the kernel, which is people’s food. And with *ḍamma*, as in “and the one with kernels,” with the annexed word dropped and the word annexed to it put in its place. {p|445}It is also said: its meaning is that in it is the fragrant herb that is smelled. In the codices of the people of Syria: *wa-l-ḥabba dhā l-ʿaṣfi wa-l-rayḥāna*, that is, and He created the grain and the fragrant herb; or, and I single out the grain and the fragrant herb. It may also be meant: and the one with kernels, with the annexed word dropped and the word annexed to it put in its place. The address in “*(O humans and jinns,) which of your Lord’s marvels can you deny?*” is to the two weighty ones, since “*all creatures*” points to them, and His saying “*We will attend to you, O two weighty ones*.”

*Al-ṣalṣāl* is dry clay that has a ringing sound. *Al-fakhkhār* is clay baked in fire, which is earthenware.

{Q} The revelation differs on this, for He said, mighty and majestic is He: “*from aged mud*” (Q 15:26), “*from wet mud*” (Q 37:11), and “*from dust*” (Q 3:59). {A} It agrees in meaning, and conveys that He created him from dust: He made it clay, then molded black mud, then ringing clay.

*Al-jānn* is the father of the jinn; it is also said, it is Iblīs. *Al-mārij* is the pure flame that has no smoke. It is also said: the flame mixed with the black of fire, from *maraja l-shayʾu* when it was unsettled and mixed.

{Q} Then what is the meaning of His saying “*of fire*”? {A} It explains *mārij*, as though it were said: of a pure thing of fire, or of a mixed thing of fire. Or He meant of a particular fire, as in the saying of God Most High “*I have warned you about the blazing Hellfire*” (Q 92:14).

{L|He separates the two seas|maraja l-baḥrayni} He sent the salt sea and the sweet sea side by side, meeting, with no division between the two waters to the eye. “*A barrier is placed between them*”: a partition of God Most High’s power. “*To prevent them from transgressing*”: they do not pass their bounds, and one does not encroach on the other by mixing. *Yukhraju* and *yakhruju* are read, from *akhraja* and *kharaja*; and *yukhriju*, that is, God, mighty and majestic is He, brings out the pearls and the coral, in the accusative; and *nukhriju*, with *nūn*. *Al-luʾluʾ* is the pearl, and *al-marjān* is these red beads, which is coral. It is also said: *al-luʾluʾ* is the large pearls, and *al-marjān* the small ones.

{Q} Why did He say {p|446}“*Out of both of them*,” when they come only from the salt sea? {A} When the two met and became like one thing, it was allowed to say that they come out of both, as one says that they come out of the sea, though they do not come out of all the sea but of part of it. And you say, “I went out of the town,” when you only went out of one of its quarters, or rather of one of its houses. It is also said: they come out only where the salt and the sweet meet.

{L|Everyone on earth perishes|ʿalayhā} on the earth. “*The presence of your Lord*” (*wajhu rabbika*, “the face of your Lord”): His essence. Face is used to express the whole and the essence; the poor of Mecca say, “Where is a noble Arab face that will save me from abasement?” “*Possessor of majesty and honor*” is a description of the face. ʿAbd Allāh read *dhī*, as a description of “*your Lord*.” Its meaning is: the One whom those who affirm His oneness exalt above likeness to His creatures and above their acts; {p|447}or the One to whom it is said, “How majestic You are, how noble!”; or the One who has majesty and honor for the sincere among His servants. {Q} What is the favor in that? {A} The greatest of favors, which is the coming of the time of repayment after it.

::: passage 55:29-30 | 4:447-448

Everyone among the people of the heavens and the earth is in need of Him: the people of the heavens ask Him for what concerns their religion, and the people of the earth for what concerns their religion and their world. “*Every day He is in full control*” (*kulla yawmin huwa fī shaʾn*): that is, at every time and moment He brings about things and renews states; as is related of the Messenger of God ﷺ, that he recited it and was asked, “What is that affair?” He said: “*Of His affair is that He forgives a sin, lifts a distress, raises a people and lowers others*.” From Ibn ʿUyayna: time with God Most High is two days. One of them is the day that is the length of the world’s life, and His affair in it is commanding and forbidding, making die and giving life, giving and withholding. The other is the Day of Resurrection, and His affair in it is repayment and reckoning. It is also said that it was revealed about the Jews when they said that God decrees nothing on the Sabbath.

One of the kings asked his vizier about it, and he asked for respite until the next day and went off downcast, thinking about it. A black slave boy of his said, “My master, tell me what has befallen you; perhaps God will make it easy for you at my hand.” He told him, and he said, “I will explain it to the king; let him know.” He said: “O king, God’s affair is that He makes the night enter into the day and the day enter into the night, brings out the living {p|448}from the dead and the dead from the living, heals the sick and makes sick the sound, afflicts the healthy and makes healthy the afflicted, makes mighty the lowly and lowly the mighty, makes poor the rich and rich the poor.” The emir said, “Well done,” and ordered the vizier to put on him the robes of the vizierate. He said, “My master, this is of God’s affair.”

From ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ṭāhir: he summoned al-Ḥusayn ibn al-Faḍl and said to him, “Three verses have puzzled me, and I have summoned you to clear them up for me: the saying of God Most High ‘*and he became one of the remorseful*’ (Q 5:31), when it is sound that remorse is repentance; the saying of God Most High ‘*Every day He is in some affair*,’ when it is sound that the pen has dried with what is to be until the Day of Resurrection; and the saying of God Most High ‘*and that man has only what he strove for*’ (Q 53:39), so what of the multiplying?” Al-Ḥusayn said: “It may be that remorse was not repentance in that community and is repentance in this community, because God Most High has singled out this community with special things in which the communities do not share. It is also said that Cain’s remorse was not over killing Abel but over carrying him. As for His saying ‘*and that man has only what he strove for*,’ its meaning is: he has only what he strove for, in justice, and it is Mine to repay him a thousand for one, in grace. As for His saying ‘*Every day He is in some affair*,’ they are affairs He shows, not affairs He begins.” ʿAbd Allāh stood up, kissed his head and remitted his land tax.

{L|We will call you to account|sa-nafrughu lakum} borrowed from a man’s saying to one he threatens, “I will be free for you,” meaning: I will strip myself, for falling on you, of everything that keeps me busy from you, so that I have no other business. In the reading of Ubayy: *sa-nafrughu ilaykum*, in the sense “We will make for you.” *Al-thaqalān* are humans and jinn; they are so called because they are the two weights of the earth.

{L|Rose colored|wardatan} red. “*Like paint*” (*ka-l-dihān*): like the dregs of olive oil, as He said, “*like molten rocks*” (Q 70:8), which is the dregs of olive oil. It is the plural of *duhn*, or the name of what one anoints with, like *al-ḥizām* and *al-idām*. He said: “*As though they were the two water-skins of one in haste, both full, not yet greased with oil*.” {p|450}It is also said: *al-dihān* is red leather. ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd read *wardatun*, in the nominative, in the sense “and there came to be a rose-red sky”; it is of the speech called abstraction, as in his saying: “*If I live, I will set out on a raid that gathers the spoils, or a noble man will die*.”

{L|No human|insun} some of humankind; “*nor a jinn*” (*jānn*): by it is meant “nor jinn,” that is, nor some of the jinn. He put *al-jānn*, who is the father of the jinn, in the place of the jinn, as one says “Hāshim” and means his descendants. He made the pronoun of “*human*” singular in His saying “*about his sins*” only because it is in the sense of “some.” The meaning: they will not be asked, because they will be known by the mark of the guilty, which is blackness of faces and blueness of eyes.

{Q} This goes against the saying of God Most High “*By your Lord, we will question them all*” (Q 15:92), and His saying “*Stop them, and ask them*” (Q 37:24). {A} That is a long day with stations in it; they will be asked at one station and not asked at another. Qatāda said: there was questioning, then the people’s mouths were sealed, and their hands and feet spoke of what they used to do. It is also said: he is not asked about his sin so that it may be known from him, but he is asked a question of rebuke. Al-Ḥasan and ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd read *wa-lā jaʾnun*, fleeing the meeting of two quiescent letters, even though it is within its limit.

{Q} Why did He say “*two gardens*”? {A} The address is to the two weighty ones, as though it were said: for every two who fear, of you two, are two gardens, a garden for the human who fears and a garden for the jinn who fears. It may also be said: a garden for doing acts of obedience and a garden for leaving acts of disobedience, because obligation turns on the two. And it may be said: a garden with which he is rewarded, and another joined to it by way of grace, as in the saying of God Most High “*For the righteous, the reward will be multiplied manifold*” (Q 10:26).

{L|Full of provisions|dhawātā afnānin} He singled out the branches for mention, which are the boughs that spread from the limbs of the tree, because they are what put forth leaves and fruit; from them the shades stretch and from them the fruits are picked. It is also said: *al-afnān* are the kinds of favors, whatever souls desire and eyes delight in. He said: “*With every kind of delight and youth I amused myself, when life was green and fresh*.” “*Two springs are in them, flowing*”: wherever they wish, above and below. It is also said: they flow from a mountain of musk. From al-Ḥasan: they flow with clear water, one of them Tasnīm and the other Salsabīl. “*Two kinds*”: two sorts; it is said, a known sort and a strange sort.

{L|While relaxing|muttakiʾīna} accusative of praise for those who fear; or a circumstantial clause to them, because “*whoever fears*” is in the sense of a plural. “*On furnishings lined with satin*” (*baṭāʾinuhā min istabraq*): of thick silk; and when the linings are of brocade, what do you think of the outsides? It is said: their outsides are of fine silk; it is also said, of light. “*Within reach*” (*dān*): close, so that the one standing, sitting and lying reaches it. *Wa-jinā*, with *kasra* on the *jīm*, is also read.

{p|454}{L|Beautiful mates|khayrātun} *khayyirāt*, lightened, as in his saying ﵇, “*easy, gentle*” (*haynūna laynūna*). As for *khayr* in the sense of *akhyar* (“better”), neither *khayrūn* nor *khayrāt* is said of it. *Khayyirātun*, in the original form, is also read. The meaning: excellent in character, beautiful in form. “*Confined in the tents*”: kept in their curtained rooms. One says *imraʾatun qaṣīra*, *qaṣūra* and *maqṣūra*, a woman kept behind the curtain. It is said that each tent of their tents is a hollowed pearl. “*No human ever touched them*” (*lam yaṭmithhunna insun qablahum*): before the people of the two gardens, to whom the mention of the two gardens points.

{L|They relax|muttakiʾīna} accusative of specification. *Al-rafraf* is a kind of carpet; it is also said, carpets; it is also said, cushions; and it is also said that every broad cloth is a *rafraf*. The edges of carpets and the loose ends of a tent are called *rafārif*, and the *rafraf* of the clouds is their fringe. *Al-ʿabqarī* is related to ʿAbqar, which the Arabs claim is the land of the jinn, and they relate to it everything wondrous. *Rafārifa khuḍurin*, with two *ḍamma*s, is also read; and *ʿabāqariyyin*, like *madāʾiniyy*, related to ʿAbāqar as a name of the land. Abū Ḥātim related *ʿabāqariyya*, with *fatḥa* on the *qāf* and not declined; and this has no ground to be sound.

{Q} How do the descriptions of these two gardens fall short of the first two, so that it was said “*Below them are two gardens*”? {A} “*Side by side*” (*mudhāmmatān*) is below “*Full of provisions*” (*dhawātā afnān*), “*wells to be pumped*” (*naḍḍākhatān*) below “*flowing*,” and “*fruits*” below “*Of every fruit*”; and likewise the description of the houris and of the reclining.

*Dhū l-jalāli*, as a description of “*the name*,” is also read.
