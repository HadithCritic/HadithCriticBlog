surah: 112
title: Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ (112)
revelation: Meccan; it is also said Medinan. Four verses. [Revealed after al-Nās (114).]

::: passage 112:1-4 | 4:817-819

{L|He|huwa} the pronoun of the matter (*ḍamīr al-shaʾn*), and *Allāhu aḥad* (“God is One”) is the matter itself, as in your saying “*huwa Zayd munṭaliq*.” It is as if it were said: the matter is this, namely that God is one, with no second.

{Q} What is the grammatical position of *huwa*? {A} Nominative, as inchoative, with the sentence as predicate.

{Q} A sentence serving as predicate must contain a pronoun returning to the subject, so where is it? {A} This sentence has the ruling of a single word, as in “*Zayd ghulāmuk*,” in that it is itself the subject in meaning. For His saying *Allāhu aḥad* is the very matter that *huwa* stands for. This is unlike “*Zayd abūhu munṭaliq*,” where Zayd and the sentence indicate two different meanings, so a link between them is required.

{From|Ibn ʿAbbās} Quraysh said, “*Muḥammad, describe to us your Lord to whom you call us*,” so this was revealed. The meaning: the one whose description you asked of me is God. “*One*” (*aḥad*) is a substitute (*badal*) for “*God*”; or else it is [the predicate of] “*he is one*,” and *aḥad* has the sense of *wāḥid*, its root being *waḥad*.

ʿAbdallāh [ibn Masʿūd] and Ubayy read *Huwa Allāhu aḥad* without *qul*. In the Prophet’s reading it was *Allāhu aḥad* without *qul huwa*, and he said: “*Whoever {p|818}recites Allāhu aḥad [has recited what] equals the Qurʾān*.” Al-Aʿmash read *Qul huwa Allāhu l-wāḥid*. It is also read *aḥadu llāh* without *tanwīn*, dropped because it meets the definite *lām*, like the poet’s *wa-lā dhākiri llāha illā qalīlā*, “and not remembering God except a little.” The good reading is with *tanwīn*, with the *kasra* for the meeting of two quiescents.

{L|The Absolute|al-ṣamad} *faʿal* in the sense of *mafʿūl*, from *ṣamada ilayhi* when one aims at him. It is the master to whom resort is made in needs. The meaning: He is God whom you know and acknowledge to be Creator of the heavens and the earth and your Creator. He is one, singular in divinity, with none sharing in it, and He is the one to whom every creature resorts, none being independent of Him, while He is independent of them.

{L|Never did He beget|lam yalid} because He has no genus, so that a consort of His genus might exist for them to procreate. He pointed to this meaning in His saying, “*How can He have a son, when He never had a mate?*” (Q 6:101).

{L|Nor was He begotten|wa-lam yūlad} because everything begotten is originated and a body, while He is eternal with no beginning to His existence and is not a body.

{L|None equals Him|wa-lam yakun lahu kufuwan aḥad} none is equal to Him or resembles Him. It may also be from *kafāʾa* in marriage, a denial of a consort.

They asked him to describe Him to them, so there was revealed to him that which contains His attributes. His saying *huwa llāhu* (“He is God”) points them to the One who is the Creator and Originator of things, and folded within that is a description of Him as powerful and knowing, because creation requires power and knowledge, since it is realized in the utmost precision, coherence and order. Also within that is a description of Him as living, hearing, seeing.

His saying “*One*” describes Him with oneness and denies partners. His saying “*the Absolute*” describes Him as only ever being needed [by others], and one who is only needed is independent. And in His being independent while knowing is [the fact] that He is just and does not do evils, because He knows the ugliness of the ugly and knows His own independence from it. His saying “*Nor was He begotten*” describes Him with eternity and primacy. His saying “*Never did He beget*” denies similarity and congeneric likeness. His saying “*None equals Him*” is a confirmation of that and a decisive judgment upon it.

{Q} The eloquent Arabic idiom is to place the non-predicative adverbial (*ẓarf laghw*, not *mustaqarr*) later and not to put it first, and Sībawayh stated this explicitly in his Book. So why is it placed first in the most eloquent and most articulate speech? {A} This speech was conveyed only to deny equivalence to the essence of the Maker, glorified is He, and the focus and center of this meaning is this {p|819}adverbial, so it was the most important and the most concerning, the most deserving and apt to come first.

*Kufuʾan* is also read with *ḍamma* on the *kāf* and *fāʾ*, and with *ḍamma* or *kasra* on the *kāf* and *sukūn* on the *fāʾ*.

{Q} Why was this sūra made equal to the whole Qurʾān despite the shortness of its body and the nearness of its two ends? {A} “*For some reason is the master mastered*,” and that is only because it comprises the attributes of God Most High, His justice and His oneness. It suffices as proof for whoever acknowledges its excellence and affirms the saying of the Messenger of God ﷺ about it. The knowledge of *tawḥīd* holds a station with God Most High, and how could it not, since knowledge follows its object: it is honored by its honor and lowered by its lowliness, and the object of this knowledge is God Most High and His attributes and what is permissible and impermissible for Him. So what do you think of the nobility of its rank, the grandeur of its place, its eminence over every knowledge, and its seizing the prize of precedence over it? Whoever disdains it does so from weakness of knowledge of its object, little esteem for it, lack of fear of Him, and distance from looking to the outcome.

O God, gather us among those who know You and work for You, who profess Your justice and Your unity, who fear Your threat.

It is called Sūrat al-Asās (“the Foundation”) for it comprises the fundamentals of religion. Ubayy and Anas related from the Prophet ﷺ: “*The seven heavens and the seven earths were founded upon Qul huwa Allāhu aḥad*,” meaning they were created only to be proofs of God’s oneness and of knowledge of His attributes, which this sūra voiced.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} he heard a man reciting *Qul huwa Allāhu aḥad* and said, “*It is due*” (*wajabat*). It was asked, “*O Messenger of God, what is due?*” He said, “*Paradise is due for him*.”
