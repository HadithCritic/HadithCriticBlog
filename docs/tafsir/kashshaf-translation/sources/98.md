surah: 98
title: Sūrat al-Bayyina (98)
revelation: Meccan; it is also said Medinan. Eight verses. [Revealed after al-Ṭalāq (65).]

::: passage 98:1-8 | 4:781-783

{p|782}The unbelievers of the two parties, the people of the Book and the worshippers of idols, used to say before the sending of the Prophet ﷺ: “*We will not leave what we hold of our religion, or abandon it, until the promised Prophet is sent, who is written of in the Torah and the Gospel*,” and he is Muḥammad ﷺ. So God Most High reported what they used to say, then said “*In fact, those who received the scripture did not dispute*.” He means that they used to promise agreement and unity on the truth when the Messenger came to them; then nothing parted them from the truth and kept them in unbelief but the coming of the Messenger ﷺ. Its like in speech is that a poor, wicked man says to one who admonishes him, “I will not leave what I am in until God gives me wealth.” God gives him wealth and he grows more wicked, and his admonisher says: “You were not going to leave wickedness until you were well off, and you only plunged your head into wickedness after you grew rich,” reminding him of what he used to say, to reproach him and hold him to it. A thing’s *infikāk* from a thing is its coming apart from it after being joined to it, as a bone when it comes out of its joint. The meaning: they cling to their religion and will not leave it except at the coming of the clear proof.

{L|The proof|al-bayyina} the plain argument. “*A messenger*” is a substitute for “*the proof*.” In the reading of ʿAbd Allāh: *rasūlan*, as a circumstantial clause to “*the proof*.” “*sacred instructions*” (*ṣuḥufan*, “pages”): sheets. “*Sacred*” (*muṭahhara*, “purified”): of falsehood. “*In them there are valuable teachings*” (*fīhā kutub*, “in which are writings”): things written. “*Valuable*” (*qayyima*, “upright”): straight, speaking truth and justice.

{L|Did not dispute|wa-mā tafarraqa} what is meant by their dividing is their parting from the truth and scattering away from it; or their splitting into parties, so that some believed, some denied and said “It is not he,” and some knew and were obstinate.

{Q} Why did He join the people of the Book and the idolaters at first, then name the people of the Book alone in His saying “*In fact, those who received the scripture did not dispute*”? {A} Because they had knowledge of him, since he was found in their books. If they are described as parting from him, then those who have no book fit this description all the more.

{L|All that was asked of them|wa-mā umirū} He means: in the Torah and the Gospel they were commanded only the upright religion, but they altered and changed it. “*Such is the perfect religion*” (*wa-dhālika dīnu l-qayyima*, “and that is the religion of the upright”): that is, the religion of the upright community. *Wa-dhālika l-dīnu l-qayyimatu* is also read, taking “religion” in the sense of “community.”

{Q} What is the point of His saying “*All that was asked of them was to worship GOD*”? {A} Its meaning: they were commanded what is in the two books only so that they should worship God in this way. Ibn Masʿūd read *illā an yaʿbudū*, in the sense of “to worship.”

{L|Creatures|al-bariyya} Nāfiʿ read *al-barīʾa* {p|783}with *hamza*, and the readers have the lightened form. *Al-nabī* and *al-bariyya* are among the words whose use has kept to the lightened form and dropped the original. *Khiyāru l-bariyya* is also read, the plural of *khayr*, as *jiyād* and *ṭiyāb* are the plurals of *jayyid* and *ṭayyib*.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites ‘lam yakun’ will be on the Day of Resurrection with the best of creation, going and coming*.”
