::: passage 81:1-14 | 4:706-710

{L|Is rolled|kuwwirat} there are two ways of taking *takwīr*. One is that it comes from *kawwartu l-ʿimāma*, when you wind a turban, that is, its light will be wound up, and so its {p|707}spreading and diffusion across the horizons will go. This expresses its removal and its being taken away, because as long as it remains its light is spread out, not wound up. Or its winding up expresses its being lifted away and hidden, because when a garment is to be put away it is rolled and folded; like this is His saying “*On that day, we will fold the heaven*” (Q 21:104). The other is that it comes from *ṭaʿanahu fa-jawwarahu wa-kawwarahu*, “he stabbed him and threw him down,” that is, it will be thrown and cast out of its orbit, as the stars are described as falling.

{Q} Is “*the sun*” nominative as a subject or as an agent? {A} As an agent. What makes it nominative is an implied verb that “*is wound up*” explains, because *idhā* calls for a verb, since it has a conditional sense.

{L|Are crashed into each other|inkadarat} swoop down. He said: “*It saw bustards in the open and swooped down*.” It is related of the sun and the stars that they will be thrown into Hell so that those who worshipped them may see them, as He said: “*You and the idols you worship besides GOD will be fuel for Hell*” (Q 21:98).

{L|Are wiped out|suyyirat} that is, over the face of the earth, and taken far away; or made to move through the air as the clouds are moved, as in His saying “*But they are moving, like the clouds*” (Q 27:88).

{L|The reproduction|al-ʿishār} the plural of *ʿusharāʾ*, as *nifās* is the plural of *nufasāʾ*. She is a camel ten months into her pregnancy, and the name stays with her until she gives birth at the end of the year. She is then at her most precious and dearest to her owners. “*Is halted*” (*ʿuṭṭilat*): left to roam untended. It is also said that their owners left off milking them and binding their udders, being taken up with themselves. *ʿUṭilat* is also read, without doubling.

{L|Are summoned|ḥushirat} brought together from every side. Qatāda said: everything will be gathered, even the flies, for retribution. It is also said that once judgment has been given among them they will be turned back into dust, and none of them will remain except what gives the children of Adam delight and pleasure in its form, like the peacock and the like. From Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄: their gathering is their death. When a year of drought falls hard on people and their wealth, one says, “The year has gathered them.” *Ḥushshirat* is also read, with doubling.

{L|Are set aflame|sujjirat} read without doubling and with doubling, from *sajara l-tannūr*, when one fills the oven with firewood. That is, they will be filled and burst into one another until they become one body of water. It is also said that they will be filled with blazing fires to torment the people of the Fire. From al-Ḥasan: their water will go, and not a drop will remain in them.

{L|Are restored to their bodies|zuwwijat} each soul is joined to its like. It is also said that the spirits are joined {p|708}to the bodies; it is also said, to their records and their deeds. From al-Ḥasan: it is like His saying “*You will be stratified into three kinds*” (Q 56:7). It is also said: the souls of the believers to the houris, and the souls of the unbelievers to the devils.

{L|The girl who was buried alive|al-mawʾūda} *waʾada yaʾidu* is a transposition of *āda yaʾūdu*, “to weigh down.” God Most High said: “*ruling them never burdens Him*” (*wa-lā yaʾūduhu ḥifẓuhumā*, Q 2:255). It is called so because it weighs her down with earth. When a daughter was born to a man and he wanted to let her live, he dressed her in a gown of wool or hair and she herded his camels and sheep in the desert. If he wanted to kill her, he left her until she was six, then said to her mother, “Perfume her and adorn her, so that I can take her to her in-laws.” He had dug a well for her in the desert. He brought her to the well and said, “Look into it,” then pushed her from behind and heaped earth on her until the well was level with the ground. It is also said that when a pregnant woman’s time drew near she dug a pit and labored at its edge; if she bore a girl she threw her into the pit, and if she bore a son she kept him.

{Q} What drove them to bury their daughters alive? {A} Fear that shame would come on them through the daughters, or fear of want, as God Most High said: “*You shall not kill your children due to fear of poverty*” (Q 17:31). They also used to say that the angels are the daughters of God, so they sent the daughters to join Him, since He has the better claim to them. Ṣaʿṣaʿa ibn Nājiya was one of those who stopped the burying of girls, and al-Farazdaq took pride in him when he said: “*Among us is the one who stopped the women who buried their daughters, and gave life to the buried girl, so that she was not buried*.”

{Q} What does it mean that the buried girl is asked for what sin she was killed? Why is the one who buried her not asked what made him kill her? {A} Her being asked, and her answer, is a rebuke to her killer, like the rebuke in His saying to Jesus “*did you say to the people*…” up to “*Be You glorified. I could not utter what was not right*” (Q 5:116).

*Saʾalat* (“she asks”) is also read, that is, she pleads her own case and asks God, or asks her killer. It says *qutilat* (“she was killed”) because the speech reports about her. Had it quoted what was said to her when she was asked, it would have said *qutilti* (“you were killed”); or had it quoted her words when she was asked, it would have said *qutiltu* (“I was killed”). Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄ read *qutiltu*, quoting her. *Qutilat*, with doubling, is also read.

In this is clear proof that the children of those who associate partners with God are not punished, and that punishment is deserved only for sin. If God rebukes the unbeliever with the buried girl’s innocence of any sin, how vile it would be of Him, who does not wrong by so much as an atom’s weight, to turn {p|709}on her after this rebuke and do to her what would make the rebuked man’s deed forgotten: a harsh and endless punishment. From Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄: he was asked about this, and he cited this verse as his proof.

{L|Are made known|nushirat} read without doubling and with doubling. He means the records of deeds: a person’s record is folded at his death, then spread open when he is called to account. From Qatāda: “*Your record, son of Adam, is folded over your deeds, then spread open on the Day of Resurrection; so let a man look to what he dictates into his record*.” From ʿUmar ﵁: when he recited it he would say, “*It is to you that the matter is being driven, son of Adam*.” From the Prophet ﷺ: he said, “*People will be gathered naked and barefoot*.” Umm Salama said, “*What of the women?*” He said, “*People will be too busy, Umm Salama*.” She said, “*What will keep them busy?*” He said, “*The spreading open of the records, in which are the weights of atoms and the weights of mustard seeds*.” It may also mean that they are spread out among their owners, that is, handed out among them. From Marthad ibn Wadāʿa: “*When the Day of Resurrection comes, the records will fly from beneath the Throne. The believer’s record will fall into his hand saying ‘in a lofty garden,’ and the unbeliever’s record will fall into his hand saying ‘in scorching wind and boiling water’*”; that is, this is what is written in it. These are records other than the records of deeds.

{L|Is removed|kushiṭat} uncovered and removed, as the hide is stripped from a slaughtered animal and the cover from a thing. Ibn Masʿūd read *qushiṭat*. *Kāf* and *qāf* often take each other’s place: one says *labaktu l-tharīd* and *labaqtuhu* (“I mixed the broth”), and *al-kāfūr* and *al-qāfūr* (“camphor”).

{L|Is ignited|suʿʿirat} kindled fiercely. *Suʿʿirat* is read with doubling, for intensity. It is said that God’s anger and the sins of the children of Adam kindle it.

{L|Is presented|uzlifat} brought close to the God-fearing, as in His saying “*Paradise will be offered to the righteous, readily*” (Q 50:31). It is said that these are twelve matters, six of them in this world and six in the Hereafter.

{L|Every soul will know|ʿalimat nafsun} “*will know*” is what puts “*When the sun is rolled*,” and what is joined to it, in the accusative.

{Q} Every soul will know what it has brought, as in His saying “*each soul will find all the good works it had done brought forth*” (Q 3:30), not one soul only. So what does His saying *ʿalimat nafsun* (“a soul will know”) mean? {A} It is one of their reversals of speech, by which they mean the extreme of the opposite of what they say. Of this is the saying of God the Mighty and Majestic “*those who disbelieved will wish they were submitters*” (*rubamā yawaddu lladhīna kafarū*, “perhaps those who disbelieve will wish,” Q 15:2), where the meaning is the meaning of “how many” (*kam*), and more forceful. And the poet’s saying: “*At times I leave the champion with his fingertips gone yellow*.” You say to some commander of troops, “How many horsemen do you have?” and he says, “I may have a horseman,” or “You will not find me without a horseman,” when he has whole squadrons. His aim is to go to the limit in making his horsemen many, but he means {p|710}to show that he is free of exaggeration and is one who makes little of the much he has, let alone adding to it. So he uses a word of fewness, and from it the sense of great number is understood, soundly and with certainty.

From Ibn Masʿūd ﵁: a reciter recited it in his presence, and when he reached “*Every soul will know everything it brought*,” he said, “*Alas, my back is broken!*”

::: passage 81:15-18 | 4:710-711

{L|By the galaxies|bi-l-khunnas} those that turn back: just as you see a star at the end of its sign, it turns and goes back to its beginning. *Al-jawārī* are those that travel, and *al-kunnas* are those that hide, from *kanasa l-waḥshī*, when a wild animal goes into its lair (*kināsihi*). It is said that they are the five bright planets: {p|711}Bahrām, Saturn, Mercury, Venus and Jupiter. They run with the sun and the moon and turn back until they are hidden beneath the light of the sun: their receding is their turning back, and their hiding is their disappearing beneath the sun’s light. It is also said that they are all the stars: they recede by day and are gone from sight, and they hide by night, that is, they come out in their places, like wild animals in their lairs.

{L|As it falls|idhā ʿasʿasa} *ʿasʿasa l-layl* and *saʿsaʿa* are said when the night departs. Al-ʿAjjāj said: “*Until, when the dawn breathed for it, and its night lifted from it and departed (ʿasʿasa)*.” It is also said that *ʿasʿasa* is when its darkness comes on.

{Q} What is meant by the dawn breathing? {A} When the dawn comes, a breath and a breeze come with it, and this is made its breathing by figure of speech. It is also said: the dawn breathed [when its light spread].

{L|This is|innahu} the pronoun is for the Qurʾān. “*The utterance of an honorable messenger*”: he is Gabriel ﵊. “*Fully supported*” (*dhī quwwatin*, “of power”), {p|712}as in His saying “*Dictated by the Most Powerful. Possessor of all authority*” (*shadīdu l-quwā dhū mirratin*, “great in power, of strength,” Q 53:5–6). Since standing depends on the one who holds it, He said “*Authorized by the Possessor of the Throne*” (*ʿinda dhī l-ʿarshi*, “with the Lord of the Throne”) to show the greatness of his rank and standing.

{L|There|thamma} points to the place mentioned, I mean *ʿinda dhī l-ʿarshi* (“with the Lord of the Throne”): he is obeyed with God among His angels brought near, who act on his command and turn to his judgment. *Thumma* (“then”) is also read, to magnify trustworthiness and show that it is the best of the attributes listed for him.

{L|Your friend|wa-mā ṣāḥibukum} He means Muḥammad ﷺ, “*is not crazy*,” as the unbelievers slander him. Let this be proof enough of the greatness of Gabriel’s ﵇ standing and his superiority over the angels, and of how far his rank stands from that of the best of humankind, Muḥammad ﷺ, when you weigh the two mentions where He sets them side by side, and compare His saying “*This is the utterance of an honorable messenger. ::: passage 81:23-25 | 4:713-713

{L|He saw him|wa-laqad raʾāhu} the Messenger of God ﷺ did see Gabriel “*at the high horizon*,” at the highest point where the sun rises.

“*He is not*”: Muḥammad is not, regarding what he reports of the unseen (his seeing Gabriel, the revelation to him and so on), *bi-ẓanīn*, suspect, from *al-ẓinna*, which is suspicion. *Bi-ḍanīn* is also read, from *al-ḍann*, which is stinginess: he does not hold back the revelation, keeping some of it back and not delivering it, or refuse to teach it when he is asked to. In the codex of ʿAbd Allāh it is with *ẓāʾ*, and in the codex of Ubayy with *ḍād*, and the Messenger of God ﷺ used to recite it both ways.

Mastering the distinction between *ḍād* and *ẓāʾ* is a duty, and knowing where each is produced is something the reciter cannot do without. Most non-Arabs do not tell the two letters apart, and when they do, they do it wrongly, though there is a wide gap between them. The *ḍād* is produced from the root of the edge of the tongue and the molars next to it, on the right side of the tongue or the left. ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb ﵁ was ambidextrous, working with both hands, and he used to produce the *ḍād* from both sides of his tongue. It is one of the letters from the middle of the mouth (*al-shajariyya*), sister of *jīm* and *shīn*. The *ẓāʾ* is produced from the tip of the tongue and the roots of the upper front teeth, and it is one of the letters of the tongue’s tip (*al-dhawlaqiyya*), sister of *dhāl* and *thāʾ*. Were the two letters the same, two readings of this word would not have been established, with two of the mountains of knowledge and recitation differing over it, and the meaning, the derivation and the construction would not differ.

{Q} What if someone praying puts one of the two letters in place of the other? {A} He is like one who puts *dhāl* in place of *jīm* and *thāʾ* in place of *shīn*, because the gap between *ḍād* and *ẓāʾ* is like the gap between their sisters.

“*It is not*”: the Qurʾān is not “*the talk of a rejected devil*,” that is, the word of those who steal a hearing, and what they whisper to their friends among the soothsayers.

::: passage 81:26-29 | 4:713-714

{L|Now then, where will you go?|fa-ayna tadhhabūn} declaring them astray, as one says “Where are you going?” to someone who has left the highway and wanders aimlessly or goes off down the byways. He likened their state to his, in their leaving the truth and turning from it to falsehood.

{L|For those who wish|li-man shāʾa minkum} a substitute for “*all the people*.” They were singled out from them because those who willed to go straight by entering Islam are the ones who benefit from the reminder, as if no one else were admonished by it, though all were admonished.

{L|Whatever you will|wa-mā tashāʾūn} will to go straight, you who will it, {p|714}except by God’s granting you success and His grace. Or: and you will not will it, you who do not will it, except by God’s compelling and forcing you.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat idhā l-shamsu kuwwirat, God will protect him from being disgraced when his record is spread open*.”
