title: Sūrat al-Mulk (67)
revelation: Meccan. Thirty verses. [Revealed after al-Ṭūr (52).] It is called al-Wāqiya and al-Munjiya, because it guards and saves its reciter from the punishment of the grave.

::: passage 67:1-4 | 4:574-577

{L|Most exalted|tabāraka} exalted and made great above the attributes of creatures; “*the One in whose hands is all kingship*” over every existing thing; {p|575}“*and He is able*” over everything not yet existing that comes under power. Mentioning the hand is a figure for taking in the kingdom and holding mastery over it. Life is that by whose existence feeling becomes possible. It is also said: what makes a thing alive, which is that from which knowing and being able become possible. Death is the absence of that in it. The meaning of creating death and life is bringing that enabling thing into being and making it cease. The meaning is: He created your death and your life, you who are charged with obligation, “*to test you*.” He called His knowledge of what happens from them by their choice a “test,” which is trying, borrowed from the act of one who tries. Like it is the saying of God Most High “*We will certainly put you to the test, in order to distinguish those among you who strive*” (Q 47:31).

{Q} From where is His saying “*which of you is best in work*” attached to the verb of testing? {A} In that it contains the meaning of knowing, as though it were said: to know you, which of you is best in work. When you say, “I knew of him whether Zayd is better in work or he,” this clause falls in the place of the second of its two objects, as you say, “I knew him to be better in work.”

{Q} Do you call this suspension? {A} No. Suspension is only that you put after it what stands in place of both objects together, as in your saying, “I knew which of them is ʿAmr,” and “I knew whether Zayd is going.” Do you not see that, after one of the two objects has come first, there is no difference between what follows opening with an interrogative particle or not opening with one? Were it suspension, the two cases would differ, as they differ in your saying, “I knew whether Zayd is going,” and “I knew Zayd to be going.”

{L|Would do better|aḥsanu ʿamalan} it is said: the purest and most right; because if it is pure and not right it is not accepted, and likewise if it is right and not pure. The pure is that it be for the sake of God Most High, and the right is that it be according to the Sunna. From the Prophet ﷺ: he recited it, and when he reached His saying “*which of you is best in work*,” he said, “*which of you is best in understanding, most scrupulous about what God has forbidden, and quickest in obeying God*.” He means: which of you is most complete in understanding from God and comprehending His purposes. What is meant is that He gave you life, by which you are able to work and are given power for it, and set death over you, which calls you to choose good work over ugly, because behind it is the raising and the repayment that must come. He put death before life because the person with the strongest motive to work is the one who sets his death before his eyes; so it was put first because it is the more important in what goes back to the aim the verse was brought for. “*And He is the Mighty*,” {p|576}the overcoming, whom the one who does ill work cannot escape; “*the Forgiving*” to whoever repents of the people of ill-doing.

{L|In layers|ṭibāqan} matched, one above another; from *ṭābaqa l-naʿla*, when he sewed it layer on layer. This is describing by the verbal noun; or on: having layers; or on: they were layered in layers. “*Any imperfection*” (*min tafāwut*): *min tafawwut* is also read. The meaning of the two forms is one, as they say *taẓāharū min nisāʾihim* and *taẓahharū*, and “I attended to him” (*taʿāhadtuhu* and *taʿahhadtuhu*). That is: of difference and disorder in the creation, and no contradiction; they are only even and straight. The reality of disparity is lack of proportion, as though one part of a thing escapes another and does not suit it. From this is their saying “a disparate creature,” and for its opposite, “well matched.”

{Q} How does this clause stand to what is before it? {A} It is a description that goes along with His saying “*in layers*.” Its origin is “you see no disparity in them”; and in place of the pronoun He put His saying “*in the creation by the Most Gracious*,” to make their creation great and to draw attention to the cause of their being free of disparity, which is that it is the creation of the Merciful, and that by His dazzling power He is the one who creates such a well-proportioned creation. The address in “*You do not see*” is to the Messenger, or to anyone addressed. The saying of God Most High “*Keep looking*” is attached to it in the sense of causing: He told him that there is no disparity in their creation, then said, “*Keep looking*,” so that what you were told becomes sound to you by seeing it, and no doubt about it remains with you. “*Do you see any flaw?*” (*hal tarā min fuṭūr*): any cracks and splits; plural of *faṭr*, which is a split. One says *faṭarahu fa-nfaṭara*. From this is *faṭara nābu l-baʿīri*, the camel’s tusk split through, as one says *shaqqa* and *bazala*; and its meaning is: it split the flesh and came out. He commanded him to repeat the sight over them, scanning and following up, seeking a fault and a flaw. “*Your eyes will come back*”: that is, if you turn back your sight and repeat the look, your sight will not come back to you with what you sought, of seeing a flaw and finding a fault; rather it will come back to you with humiliation and weariness, that is, far from hitting what it sought, as though it were driven from that with smallness and abasement, and with fatigue and tiredness from the long roving and repeating.

{Q} How does sight return humbled and weary by its being turned back twice, two times only? {A} The meaning of the dual is repetition many times, as in your saying *labbayka wa-saʿdayka*, by which you mean many answers, one after another. Their saying in the proverb, “*Dahdurrayn*, Saʿd the smith!” is of that kind, that is: falsehood after falsehood.

{Q} Then what is the meaning of “*Look again*”? {A} He commanded him to turn back {p|577}the sight; then He commanded him not to be content with the first turning back and the foolish glance, but to stop after it and let his sight rest, then go back to it again and again, until his sight grows weary from the long going back; for he will not light on any rift.

{L|The lowest universe|al-dunyā} the closest, because it is the closest of the heavens to people; its meaning is: the heaven nearest to you. *Al-maṣābīḥ* are lamps; the stars were named by them. People adorn their mosques and houses by kindling lamps; so it was said: and We have adorned the roof of the house in which you are gathered “*with lamps*,” that is, with such lamps as your lamps do not equal in light; and We joined to that other benefits: that “*We made them missiles*” against your enemies, “*against the devils*,” who bring you out of light into darkness; and by them you are guided in the darknesses of land and sea. Qatāda said: God created the stars for three things: adornment for the heaven, missiles for the devils, and signs by which one is guided; whoever interprets them otherwise has taken on what he has no knowledge of. From Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb: “*By God, no one of the people of the earth has a star in the heaven; but they seek soothsaying and take the stars as a pretext*.” *Al-rujūm* is the plural of *rajm*, a verbal noun by which what is thrown is named. The meaning of their being things thrown at the devils is that the shooting stars that dart down to strike those of them who steal a hearing are split off from the fire of the stars, not that they are pelted with the stars themselves, because these stay fixed in the sphere as they are. That is only like a firebrand taken from a fire, while the fire stays fixed and whole and does not lessen. It is also said: among the devils that are pelted are some whom the meteor kills and some whom it drives mad. It is also said: its meaning is, and We made them guesses and shots in the dark for the devils of humankind, who are the astrologers. “*We prepared for them a retribution in Hell*” in the Hereafter, after the punishment of burning by meteors in this world.

{p|578}{L|For those who disbelieved in their Lord|wa-li-lladhīna kafarū bi-rabbihim} that is: and for everyone who disbelieves in God, of the devils and others, “*the retribution of Gehenna*”; the devils that are pelted are not singled out for it. *ʿAdhāba jahannama* is also read, in the accusative, coordinated on “*the punishment of the Blaze*.” “*When they get thrown therein*”: that is, flung as firewood is flung into a great fire and cast into it. Like it is the saying of God Most High “*fuel for Hell*” (Q 21:98). “*They hear its furor*” (*samiʿū lahā shahīqan*): either of its people who were flung into it earlier, or from themselves, as in His saying “*they sigh and wail*” (Q 11:106); or of the fire, likening its dreadful, fearful sound to gasping. “*As it fumes*”: it boils with them as a cauldron boils with what is in it. It was made like one raging at them because of how fiercely it boils with them. They say, “So-and-so is bursting with rage and cracking with anger,” and “he grew angry and a piece of him flew to the earth and a piece to the heaven,” when they describe him as going to excess in it. The rage of the guards of Hell may also be meant. “*Did you not receive a warner?*”: a rebuke by which they are given more punishment on their punishment and more grief on their grief. Its keepers are Mālik and his helpers among the guards. “*They would answer, ‘Yes indeed’*”: a confession from them of God’s justice, and an acknowledgment that God, mighty and exalted is He, took away their excuses by sending the messengers and their warning of what they fell into; and that they were not brought down by His decree, as the Compulsionists claim, but were brought down from the side of their own selves, and by their choosing the opposite of what God chose and commanded, and threatened against its contrary.

{Q} “*You are totally astray*”: who are addressed by it? {A} It is part of the saying of the unbelievers and their address to those who warned them, on the view that *nadhīr* has the sense of warning. The meaning is: did no people of warning come to you? Or those who warned them were described so, for their going to the utmost in warning, as though they were nothing but warning. Likewise “*a warner did come to us*.” Like it is the saying of God Most High “*We are messengers from the Lord of the universe*” (Q 26:16), where the Arabic has the singular *rasūl*, that is, bearing His message. It may also be of the speech of the keepers to the unbelievers, with a saying implied: they meant to relate what they were on of their error in this world; or by error they meant destruction; or they called the punishment of error by its name. Or it is of the speech of the messengers to them, which they related to the keepers, that is: they said this to us and we did not accept it. “*If we heard*” to {p|579}the warning with the listening of those who seek the truth, “*or understood*” it with the understanding of those who reflect. It is also said: He joined hearing and understanding only because the turning point of obligation is the proofs of hearing and of reason. Of the oddities of the commentaries is that what is meant is: had we been of the school of the people of ḥadīth, or of the school of the people of opinion. As though this verse were revealed after these two schools appeared; as though God had sent down His threat for all the other people of the schools and those who reason independently; as though whoever was of these two were surely of the saved; when the number of the Companions given good news is ten, to whom no eleventh was added; and as though most of those who pass over the bridge had not heard the name of these two parties. “*Their sins*”: their unbelief in denying the messengers. “*Woe*” is read light and heavy, that is: far be it from them, whether they confess or deny, for that does not profit them.

Its plain sense is a command to one of two things, hiding and openness. Its meaning is: let your hiding and your openness be equal with you in God’s knowledge of both. Then He gave the reason for it by “*He is fully aware of the innermost thoughts*,” that is, their secret thoughts before the tongues translate them; so how should He not know what is spoken? Then He rejected the idea that “*what He created*” (*man khalaqa*, “He who created”) things should not take in, with knowledge, what is kept in mind, what is hidden and what is open, while His state is that He is “*the Sublime, Most Cognizant*,” whose knowledge reaches to what appears of His creation {p|580}and what is hidden. “*He know what He created*” may also be in the accusative, meaning: does He not know what He created, while this is His state? It is related that the associators used to speak among themselves of things, and God would let His Messenger know of them, and they would say, “*Keep your talk hidden, so that the god of Muḥammad does not hear it*.” So God drew attention to their ignorance.

{Q} You implied an object in “*Should He not know*,” on the sense: does He who created not know that which was mentioned, of what is kept in the heart and shown by the tongue? Why did you not make it like their saying, “He gives and withholds,” and why was the meaning not: is He who creates not knowing, since creating is sound only with knowledge? {A} The circumstantial clause, which is His saying “*He is the Sublime, Most Cognizant*,” refuses that. For if you said, “Is not He who is a creator knowing, while He is the Subtle, the Aware?” it would not be a sound meaning, because “*Should He not know*” leans on the circumstantial clause, and a thing is not timed by itself. So one does not say, “Does He not know, while He is knowing?” but rather, “Does He not know such-and-such, while He knows everything?”

Walking on its shoulders is a likeness for making it utterly submissive, past the limit; because the two shoulders, and where they meet at the withers, are the most delicate part of a camel and the least fit for the rider to tread on with his foot and lean on. So when He made it so submissive that one walks on its shoulders, He did not leave [any of it unsubdued]. It is also said: its shoulders are its mountains. Al-Zajjāj said: its meaning is, He made travel in its mountains easy for you; and when travel in its mountains is possible for you, it is the utmost of making it submissive. It is also said: its sides. The meaning is: and to Him is your raising, and He will ask you about thanks for what He has blessed you with.

{L|The One in heaven|man fī l-samāʾi} there are two ways in it. One of them: the one whose kingdom is in the heaven, because it is the dwelling of His angels, and there are His Throne, His Footstool and the Guarded Tablet, and from it His decrees, His books, His commands and His prohibitions come down. The second: that they {p|581}believed in likening Him to creatures, and that He was in the heaven, and that mercy and punishment came down from Him, and they used to call on Him from its direction. So it was said to them according to their belief: do you feel safe from the one you claim is in the heaven, while He is exalted above place, that He will punish you with a sinking or a storm of stones? As you say to one of the likeners, when you see him committing some sin, “Do you not fear the One above the Throne, that He will punish you for what you do?” “*Then you will know*” is read with *tāʾ* and with *yāʾ*. “*Will you then appreciate the value of My warning?*”: that is, when you see what you were warned of, you will know how My warning was, when knowing will not profit you.

{L|Spreading their wings|ṣāffātin} stretching their wings in the air in their flight, because when they stretch them they range their forefeathers in a row; “*and folding them*,” drawing them in when they strike their sides with them.

{Q} Why was it said “*and they fold*” and not “and folding”? {A} Because the root of flying is ranging the wings, since flying in the air is like swimming in water, and the root of swimming is stretching out the limbs and spreading them. Folding comes upon the spreading to get help from it in moving; so what comes upon it and is not the root was brought in the wording of the verb, on the sense that they are spreading their wings, and folding comes from them at times, as it comes from the swimmer. “*The Most Gracious is the One who holds them*,” by His power and by what He arranged for them of forefeathers and hind feathers, and His building their bodies in a shape and with properties from which flying through the air becomes possible. “*He is Seer of all things*”: He knows how to create and how to arrange wonders.

{L|Where are those|amman} is pointed out among the throngs, and of whom it is said, “*Where are those soldiers who can help you*” apart from God if He sends His punishment on you? “*Or who*” is pointed out, and of whom it is said, “*This is the one who will provide for you, if He holds back His provision?*” This is by way of supposing. It may also point to all the idols, because they believed that they were kept safe from misfortunes and provided for by the blessing of their gods, so it was as though they were the helping host and the provider. Like it is the saying of God Most High “*Do they have gods who can protect them from us?*” (Q 21:43). “*Indeed, they have plunged deep into transgression and aversion*”: rather, they have gone on in stubbornness and in fleeing from the truth, because it is heavy on them; so they have not followed it.

::: passage 67:22-24 | 4:581-582

{p|582}Making *akabba* the reflexive of *kabbahu*, so that one says *kababtuhu fa-akabba*, is of the odd and irregular things; like it is *qashaʿati l-rīḥu l-saḥāba fa-aqshaʿa*. It is not so; nothing of the pattern *afʿala* is reflexive, and no one masters the like of this but those who carry the Book of Sībawayh. *Akabba* is only of the kind of *anfaḍa* and *alʾama*. Its meaning is: he entered into falling face down and came to have it; and likewise *aqshaʿa l-saḥābu*: it entered into scattering. The reflexive of *kabba* and *qashaʿa* is *inkabba* and *inqashaʿa*.

{Q} What is the meaning of “*walks while slumped over on his face*”? And how did He set it against “*walks straight on the right path*”? {A} Its meaning is: he walks at random in a usual place that is not level, with dips and rises, stumbling every hour and falling on his face, flat down; so his state is the opposite of the state of one who walks upright, that is, standing, safe from stumbling and falling; or even in his direction, little given to swerving, unlike the one who walks at random and swerves this way and that, on a level road. The blind man may also be meant, who is not guided to the road and walks at random, and keeps falling on his face; and that he is not like the upright man of sound sight who walks on the road, guided to it. It is a likeness for the believer and the unbeliever. From Qatāda: the unbeliever fell face down on acts of disobedience to God Most High, so God will gather him on the Day of Resurrection on his face. From al-Kalbī: by it Abū Jahl ibn Hishām was meant, and by the upright one the Messenger of God ﷺ; it is also said, Ḥamza ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib.

{L|When they see it|fa-lammā raʾawhu} the pronoun is for the promise. *Al-zulfa* is nearness. It is in the accusative as a circumstantial clause or as an adverbial, that is: they saw it having nearness, or a place having nearness. “*The faces of those who disbelieved will turn miserable*”: that is, the sight of the promise grieved their faces, in that gloom came over them, darkness and dust covered them, and they scowled, as the face is of one led to be killed or brought before some punishment. “*It will be proclaimed*”: those who say it are the guards of Hell. “*You used to mock*” (*taddaʿūn*, “you called for”): *taddaʿūna* is *taftaʿilūna* from *duʿāʾ*, {p|583}that is: you asked for it and hurried it on. It is also said: it is from *daʿwā*, that is, because of it you used to claim that you would not be raised. *Tadʿūna* is also read. From one of the ascetics: he recited it at the start of the night in his prayer, and stayed repeating it and weeping until the call to the dawn prayer was made. By my life, it is crushing to whoever pictures that state and reflects on it.

::: passage 67:28-28 | 4:583-583

The unbelievers of Mecca used to pray for destruction on the Messenger of God ﷺ and the believers. So he was commanded to say to them: we are believers, waiting for one of the two best things: either we perish, as you wish, and go over to Paradise; or we are shown mercy by victory and by the turn going to Islam, as we hope. Then what will you do? Who will protect you, being unbelievers, from the punishment of the Fire? You cannot escape it. He means: you seek for us the destruction that is a hastening to winning and happiness, while you are in a matter that is the destruction after which there is no destruction, and you are heedless and do not seek release from it. Or: if God destroys us by death, who will protect you after the death of your guides, those who hold you back by your waist-wrappers from the Fire? And if He has mercy on us with respite and victory over you and killing you, who will protect you, since the one killed at our hands is destroyed? Or: if God destroys us in the Hereafter for our sins while we are Muslims, who will protect the unbelievers, who are more deserving of destruction for their unbelief? And if He has mercy on us for faith, who will protect one who has no faith?

{Q} Why did He put the object of “*we believe*” after, and the object of “*we rely*” first? {A} Because “*we believe*” comes as an allusion to the unbelievers, since it follows right after mentioning them, as though it were said: we believe and do not disbelieve as you have disbelieved. Then He said “*and on Him we rely*” in particular: we do not rely on what you rely on, of your men and your wealth.
