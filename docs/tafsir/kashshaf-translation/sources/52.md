::: passage 52:1-10 | 4:408-409

*Al-ṭūr* is the mountain on which God spoke to Moses, and it is in Midian. The book is written on the unrolled parchment; *al-raqq* is the sheet. It is also said: the skin on which is written the book in which deeds are written; God Most High said: “*On the Day of Resurrection we will hand him a record that is accessible*” (Q 17:13). It is also said: it is what God wrote for Moses while he heard the scratching of the pen; it is also said, the Preserved Tablet; and it is also said, the Qurʾān. It is indefinite because it is a book singled out from among the whole kind of books, as in the saying of God Most High “*The soul and Him who created it*” (Q 91:7).

{L|The frequented Shrine|wa-l-bayti l-maʿmūri} al-Ḍurāḥ, in the fourth heaven; its being frequented is the many angels who throng it. It is also said: the Kaʿba, because it is frequented by those on the pilgrimage, those on the lesser pilgrimage, and those who stay near it. “*The exalted ceiling*”: the sky. “*The sea that is set aflame*” (*al-masjūr*): the full one. It is also said: the kindled one, from the saying of God Most High “*The oceans are set aflame*” (Q 81:6). It is related that on the Day of Resurrection God Most High will make all the seas fire, with which the fire of Hell will be kindled. From ʿAlī ﵁: he asked a Jew, “*Where is the place of the Fire in your book?*” He said, “*In the sea*.” ʿAlī said, “*I can only think him truthful*,” because of the saying of God Most High “*The sea that is set aflame*.”

{L|Is unavoidable|la-wāqiʿun} will surely come down. {p|409}Jubayr ibn Muṭʿim said: “*I came to the Messenger of God ﷺ to speak to him about the captives, and found him in the dawn prayer reciting Sūrat al-Ṭūr. When he reached ‘The punishment of your Lord will surely come about,’ I became a Muslim, for fear that the punishment would come down*.” “*the sky will violently thunder*” (*tamūru*): it will shake and come and go. It is also said: *al-mawr* is a movement in waves, a thing going back and forth across a breadth, like the kneecap on the knee.

*Al-khawḍ* (“wading”) came to be used mostly of plunging into falsehood and lying. From it is the saying of God Most High “*We blundered with the blunderers*” (Q 74:45), and “*you have become preoccupied with your material possessions, just like those before you have become preoccupied*” (Q 9:69). *Al-daʿʿ* is violent pushing. That is, the keepers of the Fire chain their hands to their necks, bring their forelocks together with their feet, and push them into the Fire, shoving them on their faces and thrusting them in the napes of their necks. Zayd ibn ʿAlī read *yudʿawna*, from *al-duʿāʾ* (“calling”): that is, it will be said to them, “Come on to the Fire,” and “Enter the Fire.” “*forcibly*” (*daʿʿan*): being shoved, it will be said to them, “*This is the Fire*.”

{L|Is this magic|a-fa-siḥrun hādhā} He means: you used to say of the revelation, “This is sorcery”; is this then sorcery? He means: is this, which proves it true, sorcery too? The *fāʾ* entered for this sense. “*Or do you not see?*” as you did not see in the world. He means: or are you blind to the thing told of, as you were blind to the telling? This is rebuke and mockery. “*It will be the same for you*” is a predicate whose subject is left out: that is, the two things are all the same to you, patience and its absence.

{Q} Why did He give as the reason for patience and its absence being all the same His saying “*This is the just requital for what you did*”? {A} Because patience has an advantage over impatience only by its benefit in the outcome, in that the patient one is repaid for it with the repayment of good. As for patience under the punishment that is the repayment, which has no outcome and no benefit, it has no advantage over impatience.

{p|410}{L|Gardens and bliss|fī jannātin wa-naʿīmin} in what gardens and what bliss, in the sense of perfection in the description; or in gardens and bliss reserved for the God-fearing, created for them alone. *Fākihīna*, *fakihīna* and *fākihūna* are read. Whoever puts it in the accusative as a circumstantial clause makes the adverbial phrase a full predicate; whoever puts it in the nominative as the predicate makes the adverbial phrase dependent. That is, taking delight “*what their Lord has reserved for them*.”

{Q} On what is His saying “*their Lord has spared them*” coordinated? {A} On His saying “*gardens*,” or on “*their Lord has reserved for them*,” if you make *mā* form a verbal noun; the meaning is then: taking delight in their Lord’s giving to them and His guarding them from the punishment of Hell. The *wāw* may also be circumstantial, with *qad* implied after it.

It will be said to them: “*Eat and drink*” an eating and drinking “*with relish*,” or food and drink with relish, which is what has nothing to spoil it. It may also be like the word in his saying: “*With relish and pleasure, no lingering ailment, may ʿAzza have what she has made lawful of our honor*.” I mean a description used as a verbal noun standing in place of the verb, with “what she has made lawful” in the nominative through it as through a verb; as if it were said, “May ʿAzza relish what she has made lawful of our honor.” Likewise the meaning of *hanīʾan* here: may the eating and drinking be a relish to you; or may what you used to do be a relish to you, that is, the repayment of what you used to do. The *bāʾ* is then extra, as in “*GOD suffices*” (*kafā bi-llāhi*, Q 4:79); and the *bāʾ* is attached to “*eat and drink*” if you make the agent the eating and drinking. *Bi-ʿīsin ʿīnin* is also read.

{p|411}{L|For those who believed|wa-lladhīna āmanū} coordinated on “*with beautiful spouses*” (*bi-ḥūrin ʿīnin*, “with wide-eyed houris”): that is, We have joined them with the houris and with those who believed, that is, with companions and sitting-fellows from among them, as in the saying of God Most High “*Like one family, they will be on adjacent furnishings*” (Q 15:47). So they take their pleasure at one time in playing with the houris and at another in the company of their believing brothers.

{L|And their children also followed them|wa-ttabaʿathum dhurriyyatuhum} the Messenger of God ﷺ said: “*God raises the offspring of the believer to his rank, even if they are below him, so that his eye may be cooled by them*.” Then he recited this verse. So God brings together for them the kinds of joy: their happiness in themselves, their being married to the wide-eyed houris, the company of their believing brothers, and their children and descendants being gathered with them. Then He said: “*in belief, we will have their children join them*”: that is, because of a great faith of high standing, which is the faith of the fathers, We joined their offspring to their ranks, though they do not deserve them, as a favor to them and to their fathers, to complete their joy and perfect their bliss.

{Q} What is the meaning of making “*faith*” indefinite? {A} Its meaning is to show that it is a special faith of great standing. The faith of the offspring, of low standing, may also be meant, as though He said: for some measure of faith that does not qualify them for the rank of the fathers, We joined them to them.

*Wa-atbaʿathum dhurriyyatuhum* and *wa-ttabaʿathum dhurriyyatuhum* are read, and *dhurriyyātuhum*; *dhirriyyātuhum*, with *kasra* on the *dhāl*, is also read. Another way is that “*those who believed*” is a subject whose predicate is “*with faith We joined their offspring to them*,” and what is between them is a parenthesis.

{L|We never fail to reward them|wa-mā alatnāhum} and We did not lessen them. He means: We gave them in full all the reward and favor We mentioned, and We did not lessen them anything of the reward of their work. It is also said that its meaning is: We did not lessen them anything of their reward to give it to the children so that they might be joined to them; We joined them to them only by way of favor. *Alatnāhum* is read, and it is from two patterns, from *alata yaʾlitu* and from *alāta yulītu*, like *amāta yumītu*; *ālatnāhum*, from *ālata yuʾlitu*, like *āmana yuʾminu*; *litnāhum*, from *lāta yalītu*; and *walatnāhum*, from *walata yalitu*. Their meaning is one.

{L|Every person is paid for what he did|kullu mriʾin bi-mā kasaba rahīnun} that is, held in pledge; as though the servant’s soul were a pledge with God for the righteous work that is demanded of him, as a man pledges his slave for a debt he owes. If he does righteous work he redeems it and frees it; otherwise he ruins it. “*We will supply them*”: We gave them more, time after time. “*They will enjoy drinks*” (*yatanāzaʿūna*): they hand around and take turns, they and their sitting-fellows from among their relatives and brothers, “*a cup*,” wine. “*That are never polluted*” (*lā laghwun fīhā*): in drinking it; “*and never sinful to drink*”: that is, during the drinking they do not speak worthless talk and what has nothing behind it, as boon companions do over wine in this world in their folly and brawling, and they do not do what would make the one who does it sinful, that is, what would be counted a sin {p|412}if he did it in the abode of obligation, of lying, reviling and indecencies. They speak only words of wisdom and good speech, taking delight in that, because their minds are steady and do not go, and they are wise and knowing. *Lā laghwa fīhā wa-lā taʾthīma* is also read.

{L|Serving them will be servants|ghilmānun lahum} that is, owned by them and reserved for them. “*Protected*” (*maknūn*): in the shell, because it is finer and clearer while fresh; or stored away, because only what is precious and of high price is stored. It was said to Qatāda, “If this is the servant, what of the one served?” He said: the Messenger of God ﷺ said: “*By the One in whose hand my soul is, the excellence of the one served over the servant is like the excellence of the moon on the night it is full over the other stars*.” From him ﵇: “*The lowest in rank of the people of Paradise is one who calls a servant from among his servants, and a thousand answer him at his door: ‘At your service, at your service!’*”

{L|They will meet each other and reminisce|yatasāʾalūna} talking together, each asking the other about his states and his works and what made him deserve to attain what is with God. “*Kind and humble*” (*mushfiqīna*): tender of heart from fear of God. *Wa-waqqānā* is also read, with doubling. “*The agony of ill winds*” (*ʿadhāba l-samūm*): the punishment of the Fire, its glare and its blast. *Al-samūm* is the hot wind that enters the pores; the fire of Hell was named after it because it is of this description. “*We used to*” (*min qablu*, “before”): before meeting God Most High and coming to Him; they mean in this world. “*Implore Him*”: we worshiped Him and asked Him for protection. “*He is the Most Kind*”: the doer of good; “*Most Merciful*”: great in mercy, who when He is worshiped rewards and when He is asked answers. *Annahu* is also read, with *fatḥa*, in the sense of “because He is.”

{L|You shall remind the people|fa-dhakkir} so hold firm to reminding people and admonishing them, and do not let their saying “soothsayer” or “madman” hold you back; pay it no heed, for it is false and contradictory speech, because the soothsayer needs for his soothsaying keenness and subtlety of view, while the madman’s mind is covered over. By God’s praise and His favor to you, in the truth of prophethood and the soundness of mind, you are neither of these two.

{p|413}*Yutarabbaṣu bihi raybu l-manūn* is also read, in the passive. *Raybu l-manūn* is the events of time that disturb souls and carry them off. He said: “*Is it at Fate and its blows that I grieve?*” It is also said: *al-manūn* is death; it is in origin on the pattern of *faʿūl*, from *manahu* when he cut it, because death is a great cutter; and for that it was called *Shaʿūb* (“the Divider”). They said: we will wait for the calamities of time to strike him, so that he perishes as the poets before him perished, Zuhayr and al-Nābigha. “*I will wait along with you*”: I wait for your ruin as you wait for mine.

{L|Their dreams|aḥlāmuhum} their minds and understandings. From this is their saying “the dreams of ʿĀd” (that is, their minds). The meaning: do their minds command them to this contradiction in speech, which is their saying “soothsayer” and “poet” together with their saying “madman”? Quraysh used to be called the people of minds and intellects. “*Or are they naturally wicked?*”: overstepping the bound in obstinacy, though the truth has been made plain to them.

{Q} What is the meaning of minds being ones that command? {A} {p|414}It is a figure, for their leading to that, as in the saying of God Most High “*does your religion dictate upon you that we must abandon our parents’ religion*” (Q 11:87). *Bal hum qawmun ṭāghūna* is also read.

{L|He made it all up|taqawwalahu} he invented it of himself. “*Instead, they are simply disbelievers*”: because of their unbelief and obstinacy they cast these slanders, though they know their saying is false and that it is not made up, since the Arabs cannot do it, and Muḥammad is only one of the Arabs. *Bi-ḥadīthi mithlihi* is also read, in annexation, the pronoun being for the Messenger of God ﷺ; its meaning is that one like Muḥammad in eloquence is not hard to find among the Arabs; if Muḥammad were able to compose it, one like him would be able to, so let them bring the speech of that like.

{L|Were they created|am khuliqū} or were they brought into being and shaped with the shaping on which their nature is made “*from nothing*,” without one who shaped them? “*Or are they*” the ones who created themselves, since they do not worship the Creator? “*Indeed, they have no certainty*”: that is, when they are asked, “Who created you and created the heavens and the earth?” they say, “God,” and they are in doubt about what they say; they are not certain. It is also said: were they created for nothing, no repayment and no reckoning? It is also said: were they created with no father and mother?

{L|Do they possess the treasures|am ʿindahum khazāʾinu} of provision, so that they might provide prophethood to whom they will? Or have they the treasuries of His knowledge, so that they might choose for it one whose choosing is wisdom and benefit? “*Are they in control?*”: the lords who prevail, so that they govern the affair of lordship and build things on their will and wishes? *Al-muṣayṭirūna* is read, with *ṣād*. “*Do they climb a ladder*” set up to the sky, on which they climb and listen to the speech of the angels and the knowledge of the unseen revealed to them, so that they know what is to be, that his ruin will come before theirs and that they will prevail in the end and not he, as they claim? “*Show their proof*” (*bi-sulṭānin mubīn*): with a plain proof that confirms the listening of their listener. *Al-maghram* is a man’s taking on what is not due from him: that is, has a heavy debt fallen on them that weighs them down, so that it makes them unwilling to follow you? “*Do they know the future*” (*am ʿindahumu l-ghayb*): that is, the Preserved Tablet, “*so they write*” what is in it, so that they may say, “We will not be raised, and if we are raised we will not be punished”?

{L|Are they plotting and scheming?|am yurīdūna kaydan} it is their plotting in the House of Assembly against the Messenger of God ﷺ and the believers. “*Those who disbelieve*” points to them; or everyone who disbelieves in God is meant by it. “*The disbelievers’ schemes backfire against them*” (*humu l-makīdūn*): they are the ones on whom the harm of their plot comes back and whom their scheming surrounds; for they were killed on the day of Badr. Or the ones beaten in plotting, from *kāyadtuhu fa-kidtuhu* (“I plotted against him and outplotted him”).

{p|415}*Al-kisf* is the piece. It is the answer to their saying “*Or unless you cause masses from the sky, as you claimed, to fall on us*” (Q 17:92). He means: so great is their transgression and obstinacy that were We to make it fall on them, they would say, “This is heaped cloud, piled one part on another, that will rain on us,” and would not believe that it was a piece falling for punishment. *Ḥattā yalqaw* and *yulqaw* are also read. “*They are struck*” (*yuṣʿaqūna*): they will die. *Yuṣʿaqūna* is also read; one says *ṣaʿiqahu fa-ṣaʿiqa*. That is at the first blast, the blast of the swoon.

{L|Those who transgress|wa-inna li-lladhīna ẓalamū} and for these wrongdoers there is “*suffer retribution here*” (*ʿadhāban dūna dhālika*): short of the Day of Resurrection, which is the killing at Badr, the drought of seven years, and the punishment of the grave. In the codex of ʿAbd Allāh: *dūna dhālika qarīban* (“short of that, near at hand”).

{L|In carrying out your Lord’s command|li-ḥukmi rabbika} in giving them respite, and the hardship and trouble that comes to you in it. “*You are in our eyes*”: a figure; that is, where We see you and watch over you. The eye is made plural because the pronoun has the form of the pronoun of a group. Do you not see the saying of God Most High “*I had you made before My watchful eye*” (Q 20:39)? *Bi-aʿyunnā* is also read, with assimilation. “*When you get up*”: from whatever place you rise; it is also said, from your sleep. “*At dawn as the stars fade away*” (*idbār*): and when the stars set at the end of the night. *Adbār* is also read, with *fatḥa*, in the sense of following the stars and their traces when they have gone down. What is meant is the command to say “Glory and praise be to God” at these times. It is also said that the glorifying is the prayer, when he rises from his sleep; “*during the night*” is the two night prayers; and “*as the stars fade away*” is the dawn prayer.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-Ṭūr, it is a right on God to keep him safe from His punishment and to grant him bliss in His Garden*.”
