{p|472}In some openings *sabbaḥa* (“glorified”) comes in the form of the past, and in some in the form of the present; and the meaning of each is that it is the way of whatever glorifying is ascribed to to glorify Him: that is its constant habit and practice. This verb is made transitive with the *lām* at one time and by itself at another, in the saying of God Most High “*and glorify Him*” (Q 48:9). Its origin is to be transitive by itself, because the meaning of *sabbaḥtuhu* is “I set Him far from evil,” carried over from *sabaḥa* when he went away and was far. So the *lām* must either be like the *lām* in *naṣaḥtuhu* and *naṣaḥtu lahu* (“I advised him”), or by *sabbaḥa li-llāhi* it is meant: brought about glorifying for God’s sake and for His face alone. “*Everything in the heavens and the earth*”: whatever glorifying can come from and is sound from.

{Q} What is the place of “*He controls life and death*”? {A} It may have no place, being a sentence on its own, like His saying “*To Him belongs the kingship of the heavens*.” It may be nominative, as in “He gives life and makes die”; or accusative, as a circumstantial clause to the genitive in “*To Him*,” with the preposition governing it. Its meaning: He gives life to sperm-drops, to eggs and to the dead on the Day of Resurrection, and makes the living die.

{L|He is the Alpha|huwa l-awwalu} He is the Eternal who was before everything; “*and the Last*,” who remains after everything perishes; “*and the Manifest*,” by the proofs that point to Him; “*and the Hidden*,” because He is not perceived by the senses.

{Q} Then what is the meaning of the *wāw*? {A} The first *wāw* means to show that He joins the two qualities of firstness and lastness, and the third that He joins manifestness and hiddenness. As for the middle one, it shows that He joins the sum of the first two qualities and the sum of the other two. So He is the One whose being continues through all times past and to come, and in all of them He is manifest and hidden, joining manifestness by the proofs and hiddenness, so that He is not perceived by the senses. In this is an argument against those who allow that He is perceived by a sense in the Hereafter. It is also said: the Manifest is the One high over everything, prevailing over it, from *ẓahara ʿalayhi*, when he rose over him and prevailed over him; and the Hidden is the One who knows the inside of everything, that is, knows its hidden part. That is not good, since it turns away from the plain sense that is understood.

{L|What He has bestowed upon you|mustakhlafīna fīhi} He means that the wealth in your hands is only God’s wealth, through His creating it and bringing it into being. He only gave it to you as wealth, granted you the enjoyment of it, and made you successors in disposing of it; so it is not really your wealth, and you are in it only like agents and deputies. So spend of it on God’s dues, and let spending of it be light for you, as spending from another man’s wealth is light for a man when he has given him leave in it. Or: He has made you successors in what is in your hands to those who were before you, by making you inherit it. So take warning from their state, since it passed from them to you, and it will pass from you to those after you. So do not be miserly with it, and profit yourselves by spending from it.

{L|Why should you not believe|lā tuʾminūna} a circumstantial clause to the sense of the verb in *mā lakum*, as you say *mā laka qāʾiman*, in the sense “what are you doing, standing?”: that is, what is with you, disbelieving in God? The *wāw* in “*when the messenger is inviting you*” is the *wāw* of the circumstantial clause, so they are two circumstantial clauses, one inside the other. “*Why should you not believe in GOD when the messenger is inviting you*” is read. The meaning: what excuse have you for leaving faith, when the Messenger calls you to it, draws your attention to it and recites to you the book that speaks with proofs and arguments; and before that God took your covenant to faith, since He set minds in you, set up proofs for you, made you able to reflect and removed your excuses? Since no excuse is left you after the proofs of minds and the Messenger’s drawing your attention, why do you not believe, “*if you are believers*” for any cause at all? For this cause has nothing beyond it. *Akhadha mīthāqakum* is also read, in the active, the agent being God, mighty and majestic is He.

{L|Why do you not spend|wa-mā lakum allā tunfiqū} in not spending. “*When GOD possesses all wealth in the heavens and the earth*” (*wa-li-llāhi mīrāthu l-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ*): He inherits everything in them; nothing of it remains to anyone, wealth or anything else. He means: what aim have you in leaving off spending in the way of God and striving with His Messenger, when God will destroy you and so inherit your wealth? This is among the most forceful urgings to spend in the way of God. Then He made plain the difference between those of them who spend, and said: “*Not equal among you is the one who spent*” before the conquest of Mecca, before the might of Islam and the strength of its people and the entering of people into God’s religion in crowds and the lessening need for fighting and spending in it, and the one who spent after the conquest; this was dropped because what points to it is clear. “*Those*,” who spent before the conquest, who are the foremost, the first of the Emigrants and the Helpers, of whom the Prophet ﷺ said, “*If one of you spent the like of Uḥud in gold, it would not reach the handful of one of them, nor half of it*,” “*They attain a greater rank*.” *Qabla l-fatḥi* is also read.

{L|For each|wa-kullan} to each one of the two parties “*GOD promises salvation*” (*waʿada llāhu l-ḥusnā*): that is, the best reward, which is Paradise, with differences in ranks. It is also read in the nominative, as in “and each, God has promised him.” It is said that it was revealed about Abū Bakr ﵁, because he was the first to become a Muslim and the first to spend in the way of God.

{L|A loan of righteousness|qarḍan ḥasanan} is spending in His way. That is likened to a loan by way of figure, because when he gives his wealth for His face, it is as though he lent it to Him. “*To have it multiplied for him manifold*”: that is, He gives him his reward for his spending multiplied many times out of His grace. “*And end up with a generous recompense*”: He means that that reward, with the multiples joined to it, is noble in itself. *Fa-yuḍaʿʿifahu* is also read; both are read in the accusative, as the answer of the question; and the nominative is by coordination on “*lends*,” or on “*multiplies*.”

{L|It is your lord|hiya mawlākum} it is said: it is nearer to you. Like it is their saying, “So-and-so was struck by such-and-such, and he called on impatience for help”; and from it is the saying of God Most High “*they will be given a liquid like concentrated acid*” (Q 18:29). It is also said: it will take charge of you, as you took up the works of the people of the Fire in the world.

{L|Race|sābiqū} hasten with the hastening of those who race their peers on the course, to a Garden “*whose width encompasses the heaven and the earth*.” Al-Suddī said: as the breadth of the seven heavens and the seven earths. Misfortune in the earth is such as drought and the blights of crops and fruits; and in the souls, such as diseases and death. “*Has already been recorded*” (*fī kitābin*, “in a book”): in the Tablet. “*Even before the creation*” (*min qabli an nabraʾahā*): He means the souls, or the misfortunes. “*This*”: that the decreeing of that and setting it down in a book “*is easy for GOD to do*,” though it be hard for servants. Then He gave the reason for that and made plain the wisdom in it, and said: “*you should not grieve over anything you miss, nor be proud*.” He means that when you know that everything is decreed and written with God, your grief over what you miss and your joy at what comes are lessened, because whoever knows that what he has will surely be lost does not let his anguish grow at its loss, since he has prepared himself for that; and likewise whoever knows that some good will reach him, and that its reaching will not pass him by in any way, does not let his joy grow great at gaining it. “*GOD does not love those who are boastful*” {p|480}“*proud*,” because whoever exults at a share of the world and grows great in his own eyes swaggers, boasts of it and is proud toward people. *Bi-mā ātākum* and *atākum* are read, from giving and from coming. In the reading of Ibn Masʿūd: *bi-mā ūtītum*.

{Q} No one can keep himself, at a harm that comes down on him or a benefit he gains, from grieving or rejoicing. {A} What is meant is grief that drives one to what distracts one from patience and submission to God’s command and hope for the reward of the patient, and exultation that makes one transgress and diverts from thanks. As for the grief that man can hardly be free of, along with submission, and joy at God’s favor and setting store by it, along with thanks, there is no harm in them.

{L|They are stingy|alladhīna yabkhalūna} a substitute for His saying “*every arrogant boaster*,” as though He said: He does not love those who are miserly. He means those who exult with the exultation that makes one transgress when they are given wealth and a share of the world; then because of their love for it, its preciousness to them and its greatness in their eyes, they keep it back from God’s dues and are miserly with it. Nor is it enough for them to be miserly, until they make people miserly, make them desire holding back and make it fair to them. All that is the fruit of their exulting in it and their insolence on gaining it. “*If one turns away*” from God’s commands and prohibitions, and does not stop at what He forbade of grieving over what is missed and exulting at what comes, then God has no need of him. *Bi-l-bakhali* is also read. Nāfiʿ read *fa-inna llāha l-ghaniyyu* (without *huwa*), and it is so in the codices of the people of Medina and Syria.

Al-Ḥasan read *al-anjīl*, with *fatḥa* on the *hamza*. Its matter is easier than that of *barṭīl* and *sakkīna* for those who related them with *fatḥa* on the first letter, because the word is foreign, and one need not keep to the Arab patterns in it. *Raʾāfatan*, on the pattern of *faʿāla*, is also read: that is, We granted them mercy and fellow feeling among themselves; like it is, in the description of the companions of the Messenger of God ﷺ, “*kind and compassionate amongst themselves*” (Q 48:29).

{L|Hermitism|wa-rahbāniyyatan} their becoming monks in the mountains, fleeing from being tried in religion, giving themselves wholly to worship. That was because the tyrants prevailed over the believers after the death of Jesus and fought them three times, and they were killed until only a few of them remained; so they feared that they would be tried in their religion, and chose monasticism. Its meaning is the deed ascribed to the *rahbān*, who is the one who fears, *faʿlān* from *rahiba*, like *khashyān* from *khashiya*. *Wa-ruhbāniyyatan*, with *ḍamma*, is also read, as though it were related to *ruhbān*, the plural of *rāhib*, like *rākib* and *rukbān*. It is put in the accusative through an implied verb explained by {p|482}the plain one; its supposing is: and they invented a monasticism, they invented it.

{L|They invented|ibtadaʿūhā} He means: and they brought it about of themselves and vowed it. “*Which we never decreed for them*”: We did not impose it on them. “*All we asked them to do was to uphold the commandments approved by GOD*” (*illā btighāʾa riḍwāni llāh*, “only to seek the good pleasure of God”): an exception that stands apart: that is, but they invented it to seek the good pleasure of God. “*But they did not observe it with its due observance*,” as one who vows must observe his vow, because it is a pledge with God that may not be broken. “*We gave those who believed among them*”: He means the people of mercy and kindness who followed Jesus; “*while many of them were wicked*,” those who did not keep their vow.

“*Hermitism*” may also be coordinated on what is before it, with “*they invented*” as its description in the place of the accusative: that is, and We put in their hearts kindness, mercy and a monasticism invented by them; in the sense: and We granted them mercy among themselves and the inventing and bringing about of monasticism. “*Which we never decreed for them*” that they might seek God’s good pleasure by it and deserve the reward by it; on the view that He prescribed it for them and bound them to it, so that they might free themselves from trials and seek God’s good pleasure and reward by that. “*But they did not observe it*,” all of them, “*with its due observance*,” but some of them did; so We gave those believers among them who observed monasticism their reward, “*while many of them were wicked*,” and they are those who did not observe it.

It is related that the Messenger of God ﷺ sent Jaʿfar ﵁ with seventy riders to the Negus to call him. Jaʿfar came to him and called him, and he answered him. Some of those of his kingdom who believed, forty men, said, “*Give us leave to go to the Messenger of God ﷺ*.” He gave them leave, and they came with Jaʿfar while he was preparing for the battle of Uḥud. When they saw the want the Muslims were in, they asked the Messenger of God ﷺ for leave, went back, and came with wealth of theirs with which they shared with the Muslims. Then God sent down “*Those to whom We gave the Book…*” up to His saying “*…and from our provisions to them, they give*” (Q 28:52–54). When those of the people of the Book who had not believed heard His saying “*they will be given their reward twice*,” they boasted over the Muslims and said, “*As for those of us who believed in your book and ours, they have their reward twice; and as for those who did not believe in your book, they have a reward like yours; so what excellence have you over us?*” Then it was revealed. It is also related that the believers of the people of the Book boasted over the other believers that they would be given their reward twice, and claimed excellence over them, and it was revealed.

*Li-kay yaʿlama*, *li-kaylā yaʿlama* and *li-yaʿlama* are also read; and *li-an yaʿlama*, with the *nūn* assimilated into the *yāʾ*; and *li-yan yaʿlama*, turning the *hamza* into *yāʾ* and assimilating the *nūn* into the *yāʾ*. From al-Ḥasan: *laylā yaʿlama*, with *fatḥa* on the *lām* and *sukūn* on the *yāʾ*; Quṭrub related it with *kasra* on the *lām*. It is said of its way: the *hamza* of *an* was dropped and its *nūn* assimilated into the *lām* of *lā*, giving *lillā*; then a *yāʾ* was put in place of the assimilated *lām*, as they say *dīwān* and *qīrāṭ*. Whoever gives the *lām* a *fatḥa* does so on the view that the origin of the *lām* of the genitive is *fatḥa*, as he recited: “*I want to forget her mention…*” {p|484}*An lā yaqdirū* is also read. “*In GOD’s hand*”: in His kingship and His disposing; and the hand is a figure. “*He gives it to whom He will*,” and He wills to give only to one who deserves it.
