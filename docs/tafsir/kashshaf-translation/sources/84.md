surah: 84
title: Sūrat al-Inshiqāq (84)
revelation: Meccan. Twenty-five verses. [Revealed after al-Infiṭār (82).]

::: passage 84:1-5 | 4:725-726

The answer to “*when*” is left out so that the hearer may imagine it in every way; or because what is known from the like of it in Sūrat al-Takwīr and Sūrat al-Infiṭār is enough. It is also said that its answer is what *fa-mulāqīhi* (“and you will meet it”) points to, that is: when the sky is split, man will meet his toil. Its meaning: when it is split by the clouds, as in the saying of God Most High “*The heaven will break apart, into masses of clouds*” (Q 25:25). From ʿAlī ﵁: it will split at the Milky Way.

{L|It will submit|wa-adhinat} *adhina lahu* is “he listened to him.” From this is his saying ﵇: “*God has not listened to anything as He listens to a prophet chanting the Qurʾān*”; and the saying of Jaḥḥāf ibn Ḥakīm: “*I lent you my ear when I heard your snarling*.” The meaning is that in yielding to God when He willed it to split, it acted as the willing servant acts: when the order comes to him from the one he obeys, he gives it his ear and submits, and does not refuse or hold back, as in His saying “*We come willingly*” (Q 41:11). “*And expire*” (*wa-ḥuqqat*, “and it is bound to”): from your saying “he is *maḥqūq* for such a thing” and “*ḥaqīq* for it”; He means: and it is bound to yield and not hold back. Its meaning is to declare that the One who has power by His essence must be able to bring about everything within power, and that this is His due.

{L|Will be leveled|muddat} from *madda l-shayʾa fa-mtadda*, “he stretched it and it stretched”: {p|726}its mountains and hills and every rise in it will be taken away, so that it stretches out and spreads and its surface is level, as God Most High said: “*a barren, flat land. Not even the slightest hill will you see therein, nor a dip*” (Q 20:106–107). From Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄: it will be stretched as an ʿUkāẓ hide is stretched, because when a hide is stretched every fold and rise in it goes and it becomes even. Or it is from *maddahu* in the sense of *amaddahu*, that is, it will be given more breadth and expanse.

{L|It will eject its contents|wa-alqat mā fīhā} it throws out what is in its belly, of the dead and the treasures buried in it. “*As it erupts*” (*wa-takhallat*, “and empties itself”) (*wa-takhallat*): it becomes as empty as can be, until nothing remains inside it, as if it strained to the utmost to be empty, as one says *takarrama l-karīm* and *taraḥḥama l-raḥīm* when the generous and the merciful go to their utmost in generosity and mercy and force themselves beyond what is in their nature. “*It will submit to its Lord*”: in casting out what is in its belly and emptying itself.

::: passage 84:6-15 | 4:726-727

{L|Irreversibly heading|kādiḥ} *al-kadḥ* is the self’s straining at work and laboring at it until it leaves its mark on it, from *kadaḥa jildahu*, when he scratched his skin. The meaning of “*irreversibly heading for a meeting with your Lord*” (*kādiḥun ilā rabbika kadḥan*, “toiling toward your Lord”) is: striving toward meeting your Lord, which is death and the state after it, pictured as a meeting. *fa-mulāqīhi* (“and you will meet it”): you will surely meet it, and there is no escape from it for you. It is also said that the pronoun in *mulāqīhi* is for the toil.

{L|Easy|yasīran} light and simple: he is not examined closely in it, or confronted with what would grieve him and weigh on him, as the people of the left are examined. From ʿĀʾisha ﵂: it is that his sins are made known to him, and then he is pardoned. From the Prophet ﷺ: he said, “*Whoever is called to account is punished*.” He was asked, “*Messenger of God, ‘he will be called to an easy account’?*” He said, “*That is the presentation. Whoever is examined closely at the reckoning is punished*.”

{L|To his people|ilā ahlihi} to his kin, if they are believers; or to the company of the believers; or to his family in Paradise, among the houris.

{L|Behind his back|warāʾa ẓahrihi} it is said that his right hand will be chained to his neck and his left hand put behind his back, so that he is given his record in his left hand from behind his back. It is also said that his left hand will be wrenched out through his back. “*He will be ridden with remorse*” (*yadʿū thubūran*, “he will call for ruin”): he will say, “*O my ruin!*” (*yā thubūrāh*); *al-thubūr* is perdition. *Wa-yuṣallā saʿīran* is also read, like His saying “*and burning in Hell*” (*wa-taṣliyatu jaḥīm*, Q 56:94); and *yuṣlā*, with *ḍamma* on the *yāʾ* and without doubling, like His saying “*and commit him to Hell*” (*wa-nuṣlihi jahannam*, Q 4:115).

{L|Among his people|fī ahlihi} in their midst; or with them, as they were all happy. He means that in this world he was pampered, insolent and pleased with himself, as is the way of {p|727}the wicked, who are not troubled by the matter of the Hereafter and give no thought to outcomes. He was not downcast, sad and thoughtful, as is the way of the righteous and God-fearing, whose words God reports: “*We used to be kind and humble among our people*” (*innā kunnā qablu fī ahlinā mushfiqīn*, “before, among our families, we were in fear,” Q 52:26).

{L|He thought that he will never be called to account|ẓanna an lan yaḥūra} that he would not go back to God Most High, denying the return. One says *lā yaḥūru wa-lā yaḥūlu*, “he does not go back and does not change.” Labīd said: “*It returns to ashes after it was blazing*.” From Ibn ʿAbbās: “*I did not know what yaḥūru meant until I heard a Bedouin woman say to a little daughter of hers, ‘Ḥūrī,’ that is, ‘Come back.’*”

{L|Yes indeed|balā} it affirms what comes after the negation in *lan yaḥūra* (“he would not return”), that is: yes, he will surely return. “*His Lord was Seer of him*” and his deeds: He does not forget them and they are not hidden from Him, so He must bring him back and repay him for them. It is said that the two verses were revealed about Abū Salama ibn ʿAbd al-Asad and his brother al-Aswad ibn ʿAbd al-Asad.

::: passage 84:16-19 | 4:727-728

{L|By the rosy dusk|bi-l-shafaq} the redness seen in the west after the sun has set. When it sets, the time of the sunset prayer ends and the time of the night prayer begins, according to most scholars, except for what is related from Abū Ḥanīfa ﵁ in one of two reports, that it is the whiteness; and Asad ibn ʿAmr related that he went back on it. It is named for its thinness; from this is *al-shafaqa* toward a person, which is softness of heart toward him.

{L|As it spreads|wa-mā wasaqa} what it collects and draws together. One says *wasaqahu fa-ttasaqa wa-stawsaqa*, “he gathered it and it was gathered.” He said: “*Gathered together, if only they found a driver*.” Like it, in that *iftaʿala* and *istafʿala* both serve as its intransitive forms, is *ittasaʿa* and *istawsaʿa* (“it widened”). Its meaning: what it gathers, covers and gives shelter to, of animals and other things. “*And its phases*” (*idhā ttasaqa*, “when it is full”): when it is gathered and complete, on the fourteenth night.

{L|You will move|la-tarkabunna} *la-tarkabanna* is read, addressing man in “*O humans*”; *la-tarkabunna*, with *ḍamma*, addressing the kind, because the call is to the kind; *la-tarkabinna*, with *kasra*, addressing the soul; and *la-yarkabanna*, with *yāʾ*, in the sense of: man will ride. {p|728}*Al-ṭabaq* is what matches something else. One says *mā hādhā bi-ṭabaqin li-dhā*, “this does not match that”; and from it the cover is called *ṭabaq*, and *iṭbāq al-tharā* is the earth where it lies layer on layer. Then a state that matches another was called *ṭabaq*. From this is the saying of God, mighty and exalted, “*ṭabaqan ʿan ṭabaq*,” that is, state after state, each one matching its sister in hardship and terror. It may also be the plural of *ṭabaqa*, which is a rank, from their saying “it is in *ṭabaqāt*” (“layers”); from this the vertebrae of the back are called *ṭabaq*, one of them being *ṭabaqa*. The sense is then: you will ride state after state, which are layers of hardship, some above others. They are death and what comes after it, the stations of the Resurrection and its terrors.

{Q} What is the position of “*ʿan ṭabaq*”? {A} Accusative, as a description of “*ṭabaqan*,” that is, a stage passing beyond a stage; or as a circumstantial clause to the pronoun in “*You will move*” (*la-tarkabunna*), that is, you will ride a stage, passing beyond a stage, as *mujāwizīn*, *mujāwizan* or *mujāwizatan*, according to the reading. From Makḥūl: “*Every twenty years you will find yourselves in a state you were not in before*.”

::: passage 84:20-25 | 4:728-728

{L|They do not fall prostrate|lā yasjudūn} they do not humble themselves or submit. It is said that one day the Messenger of God ﷺ recited “*you shall fall prostrate and draw nearer*” (Q 96:19) and prostrated, he and the believers with him, while Quraysh clapped and whistled over their heads; and it was revealed. Abū Ḥanīfa ﵁ argued from it that the prostration is obligatory. From Ibn ʿAbbās: there is no prostration in the Mufaṣṣal. From Abū Hurayra ﵁: he prostrated at it and said, “*By God, I prostrated at it only after I saw the Messenger of God ﷺ prostrate at it*.” From Anas: “*I prayed behind Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and ʿUthmān, and they prostrated*.” From al-Ḥasan: it is not obligatory.

{L|Those who disbelieved|alladhīna kafarū} this points to those mentioned. “*Their innermost thoughts*” (*bi-mā yūʿūn*, “what they store up”): the unbelief, envy, wrongdoing and hatred they gather and hide in their breasts; or the evil deeds they gather in their records, laying up for themselves all kinds of punishment.

{L|As for those who believed|illā lladhīna āmanū} an exception that stands apart from what precedes it.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat inshaqqat, God will protect him from being given his record behind his back*.”
