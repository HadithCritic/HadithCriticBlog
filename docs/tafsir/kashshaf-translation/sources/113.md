surah: 113
title: Sūrat al-Falaq (113)
revelation: Meccan; it is also said Medinan. Five verses. [Revealed after al-Fīl (105).]

::: passage 113:1-5 | 4:820-822

{L|Daybreak|al-falaq} *Al-falaq* and *al-faraq* are the dawn, because night is split (*yuflaq*) and parted (*yufraq*) from it. It is *faʿal* in the sense of *mafʿūl*. It is said in a proverb: “*clearer than the falaq of dawn*,” and “*than the faraq of dawn*.” From it comes their saying “*saṭaʿa l-furqān*” when the dawn rises. It is also said that it is everything God splits open: the earth from plants, mountains from springs, clouds from rain, wombs from offspring, grain and date-stones, and so on. It is also said to be a valley in Hell, from their calling low-lying ground *falaq*; the plural is *fulqān*.

{From|one of the Companions} he came to Syria and saw the houses of the protected non-Muslims and the ease of life they enjoyed and the worldly provision spread out for them, and said, “*I do not care: is not the Falaq behind them?*” He was asked, “*What is the Falaq?*” He said: “*A house in Hell which, when opened, all the people of the Fire cry out from the severity of its heat*.”

{L|From the evils among His creations|min sharri mā khalaq} from the evil of His creation. Their evil is what the responsible beings among living creatures do of disobedience and sins, and their harming one another through injustice, transgression, killing, beating, reviling and the like. It is also what non-responsible creatures do from [their natures], such as eating, biting, stinging and snapping, as with predators and vermin; and what God placed in inanimate things of kinds of harm, such as burning in fire and killing in poison.

{L|Darkness|al-ghāsiq} the night {p|821}when its darkness thickens, from His saying “*as it moves towards sunset*” (*ilā ghasaqi l-layl*, “until the darkness of the night,” Q 17:78). Hence *ghasaqat al-ʿayn*, the eye filled with tears, and *ghasaqat al-jirāḥa*, the wound filled with blood. “*As it falls*” (*waqab*, “when it settles”): its darkness entering into everything. One says *waqabat al-shams* when it has set. In the ḥadīth: when he saw that the sun had set (*waqabat*), he said, “*This is the time when it [the prayer] becomes permissible*,” meaning the Maghrib prayer. It is also said it is the moon when full. From ʿĀʾisha ﵂: the Messenger of God ﷺ took me by the hand and pointed to the moon and said, “*Seek refuge in God from the evil of this, for it is the ghāsiq when it waqaba*.” Its *waqūb* is its entering eclipse and blackening. It may also be that by *ghāsiq* is meant the black among snakes, and its *waqb* is its striking and piercing. *Al-waqb* is a hollow; hence *waqbat al-tharīd* [the hollow in a dish of *tharīd*].

Seeking refuge from the evil of night is because the spreading of [evil things] in it is greater and guarding against it harder. Hence their sayings “*the night is more concealing of woe*” and “*the night has acted treacherously*,” because treachery increases when it grows dark. The evil is ascribed to it because of its association with it, arising in it.

{L|The troublemakers|al-naffāthāt} women, or souls, or groups of sorceresses who tie knots in strings and blow on them and recite incantations. *Nafth* is blowing with spittle, and it has no effect, except where something harmful is fed, or given to drink, or smelled, or where the bewitched is touched in some way. But God the Mighty and Majestic may do something at that moment, as a trial by which the one firm in the truth is distinguished from the *ḥashwiyya* and the ignorant commoners. So the rabble and rubbish ascribe it to the women and their blowing, while those firm in the firm word pay no heed and take no account of it.

{Q} What is the meaning of seeking refuge from their evil? {A} There are three aspects. First, that one seek refuge from their work, which is the craft of sorcery, and from their sin in it. Second, that one seek refuge from their tempting people with their sorcery and from what they deceive them with of their falsehood. Third, that one seek refuge from the evil with which God afflicts [people] at their blowing. It is also possible that what is meant by {p|822}them is scheming women, from His saying “*Indeed, your scheming is formidable*” (Q 12:28), likening their scheming to sorcery and blowing on knots; or those who tempt men by exposing themselves to them and displaying their charms, as if they were bewitching them thereby.

{L|When they envy|idhā ḥasad} when his envy appears and he acts upon it, seeking mischief against the envied. For if the effect of what he has concealed does not show, no harm returns from it upon the one he envies; rather he harms himself, through his grief at another’s joy. From ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz: “*I have not seen a wrongdoer more like the wronged than the envier*.” It may also be that the evil of the envier means his sin, and the ugliness of his state at the time of his envy and the showing of its effect.

{Q} “*From the evils among His creations*” is a generalization of everything one seeks refuge from, so what is the meaning of seeking refuge afterwards from the darkness, the blowers and the envier? {A} He singled out the evil of these from every evil because of its hiddenness, and because it reaches a person from where he does not know, as though he were assassinated by it. They said: “*The worst enemy is the dissembler who plots against you from where you do not perceive*.”

{Q} Why was some of what is sought refuge from made definite and some indefinite? {A} “*The troublemakers*” (*al-naffāthāt*, “the blowers”) were made definite because every blower is evil, and “*darkness*” indefinite because not every darkness contains evil: it is in some and not in others. Likewise not every envier does harm, and many an envy is praiseworthy, namely envy in good things. Of this is his saying ﵊: “*There is no envy except in two*.” Abū Tammām said: “*and no envier in noble deeds is an envier*,” and he said: “*Indeed, lofty rank: envy of the like of it is good*.”

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites the two Muʿawwidhatayn, it is as if he had recited all the Books God Most High sent down*.”
