surah: 64
title: Sūrat al-Taghābun (64)
revelation: Disputed whether Meccan or Medinan. Eighteen verses. [Revealed after al-Taḥrīm (66).]

::: passage 64:1-4 | 4:545-547

{L|To Him belongs all kingship, and to Him belongs all praise|lahu l-mulku wa-lahu l-ḥamdu} the two adverbials were put first so as to point, by putting them first, to the meaning that the kingdom and the praise belong to God, mighty and majestic is He, alone. That is because the kingdom in reality is His, because He is the beginner and originator of everything, the one who sustains it and watches over it; and likewise the praise, because the roots and branches of blessings are from Him. As for the kingdom of anyone else, it is a power given by Him and a charge entrusted; and praising him is a recognition that God’s blessing has run through his hand. “*He is the One who created you, then among you there is the disbeliever, and the believer*”: He means that of you is one who brings unbelief and does it, {p|546}and of you one who brings faith and does it, as in the saying of God Most High “*we granted their descendants prophethood and the scripture. Some of them were guided, while many were wicked*” (Q 57:26). The proof of it is the saying of God Most High “*GOD is fully Seer of everything you do*,” that is, He knows your unbelief and your faith, which are both of your work. The meaning is: He is the one who favored you with the root of blessings, which is creation and bringing into being from nothing; so you ought to have looked rightly and all been thankful servants. You did not do so, though you were able; rather, you split into branches and scattered into communities, so of you is an unbeliever and of you a believer. He put unbelief first because it prevails over them and is the more frequent among them. It is also said: it is He who created you; then of you is one who disbelieves in creation, and they are the materialists, and of you one who believes in it.

{Q} Yes, the servants are the doers of unbelief. But it went before in the knowledge of the Wise that if He created them they would do nothing but unbelief and choose nothing else; so what called Him to create them, knowing what would come from them? Are creating the ugly and creating the doer of the ugly not one and the same? And is the like of it anything but the like of one who gave a cutting sword to a man known for highway robbery and killing a soul that is forbidden, and he killed a believer with it? Would not people of sense agree on blaming the giver, reproaching him and beating on his scalp, as they blame the killer? Indeed, would not their turning blame on the giver be harsher? {A} We know that God is wise, knowing the ugliness of the ugly and knowing that He has no need of it. So we know that His acts are all good. Creating the doer of the ugly is His act, so it must be good and must have a good aspect; and that the good aspect is hidden from us does not damage its goodness, as our ignorance of what wisdom called for in creating most of His creatures does not damage their goodness.

{L|For a specific purpose|bi-l-ḥaqqi} with a sound purpose and far-reaching wisdom, which is that He made them the dwellings of those charged with obligation, so that they would work and He would repay them. “*Designed you and perfected your design*”: *ṣiwarakum*, with *kasra*, is also read; so that you would give thanks; “*and to Him is the return*,” and He will repay you for thanksgiving and for neglecting it.

{Q} How did He make their shapes good? {A} He made them the best and most splendid of all animals, by the proof that a human being does not wish his shape were different from what he sees of all the other shapes. Of the goodness of his shape is {p|547}that he was created upright, not bent forward, as He, mighty and majestic is He, said: “*in the best design*” (Q 95:4).

{Q} How many an ugly man there is, misshapen in form, repulsive in build, whom eyes look past! {A} There is no ugliness there. But beauty, like other qualities, is in degrees and ranks. Because some shapes fall plainly below the ranks of those above them, and are set beside those that surpass them, they are not found handsome; otherwise they come within the bounds of beauty and do not go out of its limit. Do you not see that you may admire a shape and find it handsome, and think the world of it; then you see one more handsome and higher in the ranks of beauty, and your eye shrinks from the first, and you find it tedious to look at after being smitten and infatuated with it? The wise said: two things have no limit, beauty and eloquence.

By His knowing what is in the heavens and the earth, then His knowing what the servants hide and show, then His knowing what is in the breasts, He drew attention to the fact that nothing of the universals and the particulars is hidden from Him or escapes Him; so His due is to be feared and guarded against, and no one should dare anything that goes against His good pleasure. The repetition of knowledge has the meaning of a repetition of the threat. Everything He mentioned after the saying of God Most High “*then among you there is the disbeliever, and the believer*,” as you see, has the meaning of a threat for unbelief, and a rejection of the Creator’s being disobeyed and His blessing not being thanked. How ignorant, then, is one who mixes unbelief with creation and makes it part of it, when creation is the greatest blessing from God to His servants, and unbelief the greatest ingratitude from the servants to their Lord!

::: passage 64:5-6 | 4:547-547

{L|Have you noted|a-lam yaʾtikum} the address is to the unbelievers of Mecca. “*That*” points to what was mentioned of the ill consequence they tasted in this world and the punishment prepared for them in the Hereafter. “*Because*”: because the matter and the tale is that “*their messengers went to them*.” “*Shall we follow humans like us?*” (*a-basharun yahdūnanā*, “shall human beings guide us?”): they denied that the messengers should be human beings, and did not deny that their god should be a stone. “*GOD does not need them*”: He left it unrestricted so that it takes in everything, and among it their faith and their obedience.

{Q} His saying “*and turned away. GOD does not need them*” makes one imagine that the turning away and the being free of need existed together, and God Most High has never ceased to be free of need. {A} Its meaning is: and God’s freedom from need appeared, since He did not drive them to faith nor force them to it, though He was able to do that.

{L|Claim|zaʿama} *al-zaʿm* is claiming knowledge. From this is his saying ﵇: “*‘They claimed’ is the mount of lying*.” From Shurayḥ: “*Everything has a by-name, and the by-name of lying is ‘they claimed.’*” It passes to two objects as “knowing” does. He said:

> … and I did not think you were apart from that.

*Anna*, with what is within its range, stands in place of the two. “*Those who disbelieved*”: the people of Mecca. “*Yes*” affirms what comes after *lan*, which is the raising. “*This is easy for GOD to do*”: that is, nothing turns Him from it. By His messenger and the light He meant Muḥammad ﷺ and the Qurʾān.

::: passage 64:9-10 | 4:548-549

*Najmaʿukum*, *nukaffir* and *nudkhilhu* are also read, with *yāʾ* and with *nūn*.

{Q} By what is the adverbial put in the accusative? {A} By His saying “*you will surely be told*,” or by “*aware*,” because of what is in it of the meaning of threat, as though it were said: and God will punish you on the day He gathers you; or by an implied “remember.” “*to the Day of Summoning*” (*li-yawmi l-jamʿ*, “for the day of gathering”): for a day on which the first and the last are gathered. *Al-taghābun* is borrowed from people’s cheating one another (*taghābun*) in trade, which is that some of them cheat others: because the blessed take the places of the wretched, which they would have taken had they been blessed; and the wretched take the places of the blessed, which they would have taken had they been wretched. In it is mockery of the wretched, because their taking those places is not cheating. In the ḥadīth of the Messenger of God ﷺ: “*No servant enters Paradise but he is shown his seat in the Fire had he done evil, so that he may grow in thanks; and no servant enters the Fire but he is shown his seat in {p|549}Paradise had he done good, so that he may grow in grief*.” The meaning of “*That is the Day of Mutual Blaming*” (*yawmu l-taghābun*, “the day of mutual cheating”), when people cheat one another on other than that day, is to make it great, and that its cheating is cheating in reality, not cheating in the matters of this world, however weighty and great. “*Righteous*” describes the verbal noun, that is, a righteous work.

::: passage 64:11-11 | 4:549-549

{L|Except in accordance with GOD’s will|illā bi-idhni llāhi} except by His decree and His will, as though He gave the misfortune leave to strike him. “*He will guide his heart*”: He shows it favor and opens it to grow in obedience and good. It is also said: it is saying “To God we belong and to Him we return” at the misfortune. From al-Ḍaḥḥāk: He guides his heart until he knows that what struck him was not going to miss him, and what missed him was not going to strike him. From Mujāhid: if he is tried he is patient, if he is given he is thankful, and if he is wronged he forgives. *Yuhda qalbuhu* is also read, built for the passive, with “heart” in the nominative or in the accusative. The way of the accusative is that it is like *safiha nafsahu* (Q 2:130), that is: he is guided in his heart. The meaning may also be that the unbeliever is astray from his heart, far from it, while the believer finds it and is guided to it, as in the saying of God Most High “*for everyone who possesses a mind*” (Q 50:37). *Nahdi qalbahu* is also read, with *nūn*; and *yahdi qalbuhu*, in the sense of “is guided”; and *yahdaʾ qalbuhu*, is at rest; and *yahda* and *yahdā*, lightened. “*GOD is fully aware of all things*”: He knows which hearts favor has an effect on and which it has none on, and grants it or withholds it.

::: passage 64:12-13 | 4:549-549

{L|If you turn away|fa-in tawallaytum} then it is not against him if you turn away, because obeying you was not written for him; only conveying and making plain was written for him, nothing more. “*In GOD the believers shall trust*” urges the Messenger of God ﵌ to rely on Him and draw strength from Him in his affair, so that He helps him against whoever denied him and turned away from him.

::: passage 64:14-15 | 4:549-550

{p|550}Among wives are wives who are hostile to their husbands, quarrel with them and stir up trouble against them; and among children are children who are hostile to their fathers, are undutiful to them, and make them swallow bitterness and hurt. “*Beware*”: the pronoun is for the enemy, or for the wives and children together. That is: since you know that these are not free of an enemy, be on your guard against them and do not feel safe from their mischief and their evil. “*If you pardon*” them, when you find enmity in them and do not meet them with the like of it, God will forgive you your sins and expiate them for you. It is said that some people wanted to emigrate from Mecca, and their wives and children held them back and said, “*You will go and leave us to be lost*.” They felt pity for them and stayed. When they emigrated after that and saw that those who had gone before them had gained understanding in religion, they wanted to punish their wives and children, and pardon was made fair to them. It is also said: they said to them, “*Where are you going, leaving your land, your clan and your wealth?*” They grew angry with them and said, “*If God gathers us in the abode of emigration, we will do you no good*.” When they emigrated they kept good from them, and they were urged to pardon them and give back goodness and ties to them. It is also said: ʿAwf ibn Mālik al-Ashjaʿī had a family and children, and when he wanted to go on a raid they clung to him, wept to him and softened him; and he was about to hurt them, and it was revealed.

{L|A test|fitnatun} a test and an ordeal, because they bring one into sin and punishment, and there is no test greater than these two. Do you not see His saying “*and GOD possesses a great recompense*”? In the ḥadīth: “*A man will be brought on the Day of Resurrection, and it will be said: his household ate up his good deeds*.” From one of the early generations: “*A household is the woodworm of acts of obedience*.” From the Prophet ﷺ: he was preaching, and al-Ḥasan and al-Ḥusayn came wearing two red shirts, stumbling and getting up. He came down to them, took them, and put them in his lap on the pulpit, and said, “*God spoke the truth: ‘Your wealth and your children are only a trial.’ I saw these two boys and could not keep away from them*.” Then he went on with his sermon. It is also said: when striving and emigrating are possible for you, let inclination to wealth and children not tempt you away from them.

::: passage 64:16-16 | 4:550-551

{L|As much as you can|mā staṭaʿtum} your effort and your capacity, that is: spend your ability on it. “*And hear*” what you are admonished with, “*and obey*” in what you are commanded and forbidden, “*and spend*” on the ways in which spending is binding on you.

{p|551}{L|For your own good|khayran li-anfusikum} put in the accusative by something dropped, whose implied form is: bring good for yourselves, and do what is better and more profitable for them. This is an emphasis on the urging to obey these commands, and a making plain that these things are better for yourselves than wealth and children and the love of desires and the finery of this world to which you are devoted.

::: passage 64:17-18 | 4:551-551

Mentioning the loan is gentleness in asking. “*He will multiply the reward for you manifold*”: He writes for you ten for one, or seven hundred, up to whatever increase He wills. *Yuḍaʿʿifhu* is also read. “*Thankful*” is figurative, that is: He does with you what one who goes to the utmost in thanks does, of great reward. Likewise “*Clement*”: He does with you what one does who bears patiently with the one who does wrong, so He does not hurry punishment on you despite the many sins you have.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-Taghābun, sudden death is lifted from him*.”
