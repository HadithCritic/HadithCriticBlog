{L|And its galaxies|dhāti l-burūj} they are the twelve signs, which are the towers of heaven, by way of likeness. It is also said that *al-burūj* are the stars that are the mansions of the moon; it is also said, the great stars, called *burūj* because they stand out; and it is also said, the gates of heaven. “*The promised day*” is the Day of Resurrection.

{L|The witness and the witnessed|wa-shāhidin wa-mashhūd} He means: a witness on that Day and what is witnessed on it. By the witness is meant everyone of all creatures who will be present on it, and by what is witnessed, the wonders of that Day. The reason they are indefinite is either what I said on His saying *ʿalimat nafsun mā aḥḍarat* (“a soul will know what it has brought,” Q 81:14), as if it were said: and witnesses and witnessed things beyond all number; or vagueness in the description, as if it were said: and a witness and a witnessed whose nature cannot be grasped.

The commentators’ opinions about them are confused. It is said that the witness and the witnessed are Muḥammad ﷺ and the Day of Resurrection. It is said: Jesus and his community, because of His saying “*I was a witness among them for as long as I lived with them*” (Q 5:117). It is said: the community of Muḥammad and the other communities. It is said: the Day of Tarwiya and the Day of ʿArafa. It is said: the Day of ʿArafa and Friday. It is said: the Black Stone and the pilgrims. It is said: the days and nights, and the children of Adam. From al-Ḥasan: “*There is no day that does not call out: ‘I am a new day, and I am a witness over what is done in me. So make the most of me, for once my sun has set you will not find me again until the Day of Resurrection.’*” It is said: the guardian angels and the children of Adam. It is said: the prophets and Muḥammad ﵇.

{Q} Where is the answer to the oath? {A} It is left out, and His saying “*Woe to the people of the canyon*” points to it, as if it were said: I swear by these things that they are accursed, meaning the unbelievers of Quraysh, as the people of the trench were cursed. That is {p|730}because the sūra came to steady the believers and make them patient under the harm done them by the people of Mecca, and to remind them of what befell those before them: punishment for their faith and every kind of harm, and their patience and steadfastness. So they would take comfort from them and bear patiently what they met from their own people, and know that their unbelievers are, with God, in the place of those who punished and burned others with fire: accursed, and deserving that it be said of them “Perish Quraysh,” as it was said “*Woe to the people of the canyon*.” *Qutila* (“perish”) is a curse on them, as in His saying “*Woe to the human being; he is so unappreciative!*” (Q 80:17). *Quttila* is also read, with doubling.

*Al-ukhdūd* is a furrow (*khadd*) in the ground, that is, a cleft. Like the two in form and meaning are *al-khaqq* and *al-ukhqūq*, from which is: “*and its legs sank into the burrows of the rats*.”

It is related that the Prophet ﷺ said: “*A certain king had a sorcerer. When the sorcerer grew old he took a boy to teach him sorcery. On the boy’s way there was a monk, and he listened to him. One day he saw on his way a beast that had blocked the people. He took a stone and said, ‘O God, if the monk is dearer to You than the sorcerer, kill it,’ and it killed it. After that the boy healed those born blind and the lepers and cured illnesses. One of the king’s companions went blind, and he healed him. The king saw him and asked him, ‘Who gave you back your sight?’ He said, ‘My Lord.’ The king was angry and tortured him, and he pointed him to the boy. He tortured the boy, and he pointed him to the monk. The monk would not turn from his religion, so he was cut apart with a saw. The boy refused, so he was taken to a mountain to be thrown from its top. He prayed, and the mountain shook with the men, and they fell off, and he was saved. He was taken out on a long ship, and they carried him to deep water to drown him. He prayed, and the ship overturned with them, and they drowned, and he was saved. He said to the king: ‘You will not kill me until you gather the people on one open plain, crucify me on a trunk, take an arrow from my quiver and say, “In the name of God, Lord of the boy,” and then shoot me with it.’ He shot him, and the arrow struck his temple. He put his hand on it and died. The people said, ‘We believe in the Lord of the boy.’ The king was told, ‘What you feared has come upon you.’ So he ordered trenches dug at the mouths of the streets and fires lit in them, and whoever of them would not turn back he threw into them. At last a woman came with a child, and she shrank back from falling into it. The child said, ‘Mother, be patient, for you are on the truth,’ and she went in*.” It is also said that he said to her, “*Go in, and do not be a hypocrite*”; and it is also said that he said to her, “*It is only a closing of the eyes*,” and she bore it patiently.

From ʿAlī ﵁: when they disagreed over the rulings on the Magians, he said: “*They are a people of scripture, and they held fast to their scripture. Wine had been made lawful for them. One of their kings drank it and became drunk and lay with his sister. When he sobered up he regretted it and looked for a way out. She said to him, ‘The way out is to preach to the people and say, “People, God has made marriage to sisters lawful,” and then to preach to them afterward and say, “God has forbidden it.”’ He preached, and they would not accept it from him. {p|731}She said to him, ‘Turn the whip on them,’ and they would not accept it. She said to him, ‘Turn the sword on them,’ and they would not accept it. Then she told him to dig the trenches and light the fires and throw in whoever refused. They are the ones God meant by His saying ‘Perish the people of the trench.’*”

It is also said that a man who followed the religion of Jesus ﵇ came to Najrān and called them, and they answered him. Dhū Nuwās the Jew marched against them with troops from Ḥimyar and gave them the choice between the fire and Judaism. They refused, and he burned twelve thousand of them in the trenches; it is also said, seventy thousand. It is mentioned that the trench was forty cubits long and twelve cubits wide. From the Prophet ﷺ: when the people of the trench were mentioned, he would seek refuge from the strain of trial.

{L|A blazing fire|al-nār} a substitute of inclusion for “*the canyon*.” “*blazing*” (*dhāti l-waqūd*, “full of fuel”): describing it as a great fire with what makes its flames rise high, of much firewood and the bodies of people. *Al-wuqūd*, with *ḍamma*, is also read.

{L|When|idh} an adverb to *qutila*, that is, they were cursed when they ringed the fire, sitting around it. The meaning of “*around it*” (*ʿalayhā*, “over it”) is: over the edges of the trench that lie close to it, as in the line “*and Generosity and al-Muḥalliq spent the night by the fire*,” and as you say “I passed over him,” meaning higher than a place close to him.

{L|To watch|wa-hum … shuhūd} the meaning of their witnessing the burning of the believers is that they were put in charge of it and made witnesses, each bearing witness for the others before the king that none of them had fallen short in the punishment he had been ordered and entrusted to carry out. It may also mean that they are witnesses to what they are doing to the believers and will give their testimony on the Day of Resurrection, “*The day will come when their own tongues, hands, and feet will bear witness to everything they had done*” (Q 24:24).

{L|They hated them for no other reason|wa-mā naqamū minhum} they found no fault in them and took exception to nothing but faith, as in the line: “*There is no fault in them, except that their swords…*” Ibn Qays al-Ruqayyāt said: “*They held nothing against the Banū Umayya except that they are forbearing when they are angry*.” {p|732}Abū Ḥaywa read *naqimū*, with *kasra*; the more correct form is with *fatḥa*.

He mentioned the attributes for which He deserves to be believed in and worshipped: that He is mighty, overpowering and able, whose punishment is feared; and praiseworthy, giving favors, to whom praise is due for His favor and whose reward is hoped for; “*To Him belongs the kingship of the heavens and the earth*,” so that everyone in them owes Him worship and humility. He mentions this to establish that what they held against the believers is the truth, which no one holds against them except one devoted to falsehood and sunk in error, and that those who held it against them deserve God’s vengeance on them with a punishment like no other. “*And GOD witnesses all things*” is a threat to them; He means that He knows what they did and will repay them for it.

{L|Those who persecute|alladhīna fatanū} He may mean by those who persecuted the people of the trench in particular, and by those who believed, those who were thrown into the trench. The meaning of their persecuting them is that they punished them with fire and burned them. *fa-lahum* (“for them”) in the Hereafter “*the retribution of Gehenna*” for their unbelief, “*they have incurred the retribution of burning*,” which is another great fire that spreads as a blaze spreads, for their burning the believers. Or: for them is the punishment of Hell in the Hereafter, and for them is the punishment of the burning in this world, because it is related that the fire turned on them and burned them. He may also mean those who persecuted the believers, that is, tried them with harm, in general, and by the believers those who were persecuted; and that the persecutors have two punishments in the Hereafter, for their unbelief and for their persecution.

{L|Your Lord’s blow|baṭsha rabbika} *al-baṭsh* is seizing with violence; when it is described as severe, it is doubled and made graver. It is His seizing of tyrants and wrongdoers and His taking them with punishment and vengeance.

{L|He is the One who initiates and repeats|yubdiʾu wa-yuʿīdu} that is, He begins the seizing and repeats it, meaning that He seizes them in this world and in the Hereafter. Or by His power to begin and to repeat He points to how severe His seizing is, and threatens the unbelievers that He will bring them back as He began them, to seize them, since they did not give thanks for the favor of the beginning and denied the bringing back. {p|733}*Yabdaʾu* is also read.

{L|Most Kind|al-wadūd} who does for those who obey Him what one who loves does, giving them what they want. *Dhī l-ʿarshi* is also read, as a description of “*your Lord*,” and *al-majīdi*, in the genitive, as a description of the Throne. God’s glory is His greatness; the glory of the Throne is its height and greatness.

{L|Doer of whatever He wills|faʿʿālun} the predicate of a subject left out. It says *faʿʿāl* (“ever doing”) only because what He wills and does is beyond all number.

{L|Pharaoh and Thamûd|firʿawna wa-thamūd} a substitute for “*the troops*.” By Pharaoh He means him and his people, as in His saying “*of Pharaoh and his elders*” (Q 10:83). The meaning: you know how those hosts denied the messengers and what befell them for their denial. “*Those who disbelieve*” (*bal alladhīna kafarū*) among your people “*are plagued with denial*,” and what a denial, earning the punishment; and God knows their states and has power over them, and they cannot escape Him. His encompassing them from behind is an image of their not getting away from Him, as what is surrounded does not get away from what surrounds it. The sense of *bal* (“rather”) is that their case is stranger than the case of those others, because they have heard their stories and what happened to them, and seen the traces of their destruction, and they took no lesson and denied more fiercely than those did.

{L|Indeed, it is|bal huwa} that is, rather, this that they deny “*it is a glorious Quran*”: noble and of high rank among the scriptures, in its composition and in its inimitability. *Qurʾānu majīdin* is also read, with annexation, that is, the Qurʾān of a glorious Lord. Yaḥyā ibn Yaʿmar read *fī lūḥin*; *al-lūḥ* is the air, meaning the air above the seventh heaven in which the Tablet is, “*preserved*” from the devils reaching it. *Maḥfūẓun* is also read, in the nominative, as a description of the Qurʾān.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-Burūj, God will give him ten good deeds for every Friday and every Day of ʿArafa there is in this world*.”
