{L|Why|li-ma} it is the *lām* of annexation coming in on the interrogative *mā*, as other prepositions come in on it in your saying *bima*, *fīma*, *mimma*, *ʿamma*, *ilāma* and *ʿalāma*. The *alif* was dropped only because *mā* and the particle are like one thing, and they are used together a great deal in the speech of one who asks. Using the original form has come, but rarely. The pause is made by adding the *hāʾ* of silence, or by making it quiescent; whoever makes it quiescent when joining does so by treating it as a pause, as was heard: “*thalāthah, arbaʿah*,” with the *hāʾ*, and the vowel of the *hamza* cast onto it, the *hamza* dropped. This speech takes in lying and breaking a promise. It is related that the believers said, before they were commanded to fight: “*If we knew the works most beloved to God Most High, we would do them and spend our wealth and our lives in them*.” So God Most High showed them striving in His way; they turned their backs on the day of Uḥud, and He reproached them. It is also said: when God told of the reward of the martyrs of Badr, they said, “*If we meet fighting, we will pour our whole strength into it*”; then they fled on the day of Uḥud and did not keep faith. It is also said: a man would say, “I killed,” when he had not killed; “I thrust,” when he had not thrust; “I struck,” when he had not struck; and “I stood firm,” when he had not stood firm. It is also said: a man had harmed the Muslims and done them damage, and Ṣuhayb killed him, and another claimed the killing. ʿUmar said to Ṣuhayb, “*Tell the Prophet ﵇ that you killed him*.” He said, “*I killed him only for God and His Messenger*.” ʿUmar said, “*Messenger of God, Ṣuhayb killed him*.” He said, “*Is that so, Abū Yaḥyā?*” He said, “*Yes*.” It was revealed about the one who claimed it. From al-Ḥasan: it was revealed about the hypocrites; and addressing them by faith is mockery of them {p|523}and of their faith.

{L|Most abominable|kabura maqtan} this is among the most eloquent of speech and the most telling in its meaning. In “*kabura*” wonder is meant, without its own wording, as in his saying:

> Dear was the blood-price of Kulayb’s she-camel.

The meaning of wonder is to make the matter great in the hearts of the hearers, because wonder is only at a thing that goes beyond its likes and its kind. He rested it on “*that you say*,” and put “*hatred*” in the accusative as its specification, to point out that their saying what they do not do is pure hatred with no mixture in it, because of how firmly hatred has taken hold of it. The word *maqt* was chosen because it is the most severe and most telling hatred; from this the marriage of hatred was so called, for the contract with a stepmother. He was not content to make the hatred great, but made it the most severe and foulest of it. “*In the sight of GOD*” is more telling than that, because when the greatness of its hatefulness with God is established, its greatness and severity are complete and doubts about it are removed. It is related of one of the early generations that he was told, “Tell us ḥadīth,” and he kept silent; then he was told, “Tell us ḥadīth,” and he said, “*Do you command me to say what I do not do, and so hasten God’s hatred?*” In His saying “*GOD loves those who fight in His cause*,” right after mentioning hatred for the one who breaks his promise, there is a proof that the hatred attached to the saying of those who promised to stand firm in fighting the unbelievers and did not keep faith. Zayd ibn ʿAlī read *yuqātalūna*, with *fatḥa* on the *tāʾ*; and *yuqtalūna* is also read. “*United in one column*” (*ṣaffan*): ranging themselves, or ranged. “*Like the bricks in one wall*” (*ka-annahum bunyānun marṣūṣ*), in their pressing close together without gap or break, “*were a building*” whose parts are packed close and set in rows. It is also said: He may mean that their intentions are equal in standing firm, so that in their unity of word they are like a compact building. From one of them: in it is a proof of the excellence of fighting on foot, because horsemen do not range themselves in this way. His saying “*in ranks, as though they were a building*” is two circumstantial clauses, one within the other.

::: passage 61:5-5 | 4:524-524

{L|Recall that|wa-idh} put in the accusative by an implied “remember”; or: and when he said to them what he said, such-and-such came about. “*Do you hurt me*”: they used to hurt him with kinds of hurt: belittling him and finding fault with his person, denying his signs, disobeying him in what would bring them benefit, their worshiping the calf, their asking to see God openly, and the denial that is the wasting of God’s due and his. “*Though you know*” is in the place of a circumstantial clause, that is: you hurt me while knowing with certain knowledge “*that I am God’s messenger to you*.” What your knowing that requires and calls for is honoring and revering me, not hurting me and making light of me; because whoever knows God and His greatness honors His messenger, knowing that honoring Him lies in honoring His messenger; and because whoever hurts him, God’s threat reaches him. “*When they deviated*” from the truth, “*GOD diverted their hearts*,” by holding back His favors from them; “*For GOD does not guide the wicked people*”: He does not show them favor, because they are not people fit for favor.

{Q} What is the meaning of *qad* in His saying “*though you know*”? {A} Its meaning is emphasis, as though He said: and you know with a certain knowledge in which you have no doubt.

{p|525}It is said that he said “*Children of Israel*” and did not say “*my people*,” as Moses said, only because he had no lineage among them, so that they would be his people. The meaning is: I have been sent to you in the state of confirming what came before me “*the Torah*,” and in the state of bringing good news “*of a messenger to come after me*.” He means that my religion is confirming all the books of God and His prophets, those who came before and those who came after. *Min baʿdī* is read with the *yāʾ* quiescent and with *fatḥa*; al-Khalīl and Sībawayh choose the *fatḥa*. From Kaʿb: the disciples said to Jesus, “*Spirit of God, is there a community after us?*” He said, “*Yes, the community of Aḥmad: wise, learned, righteous, God-fearing, as though in their understanding they were prophets. They are content with a little provision from God, and God is content with a little work from them*.”

{Q} By what are “*confirming*” and “*bringing good news*” put in the accusative: by the meaning of sending that is in “*messenger*,” or by “*to you*”? {A} Rather, by the meaning of sending; because “*to you*” is linked to “*messenger*,” so it cannot govern anything. Prepositions do not govern by themselves, but by what is in them of the meaning of the verb; so when they are linked words that do not contain the meaning of a verb, from where would they govern? *Hādhā sāḥirun mubīn* is also read.

What person is more wrongful than one whom his Lord calls, on the tongue of His prophet, to Islam, in which is his happiness in the two abodes, and who, in place of answering Him, invents a lie against God by saying of His speech, which is His call to His servants to the truth, “This is magic”? Because magic is a lie and a false show. Ṭalḥa ibn Muṣarrif read *wa-huwa yaddaʿī*, in the sense of *yudʿā*: *daʿāhu* and *iddaʿāhu*, like *lamasahu* and *iltamasahu*. And from him: *yaddaʿī*, in the sense of *yadʿū*, and that is God, mighty and majestic is He.

::: passage 61:10-13 | 4:526-528

{L|That will save you|tunjīkum} is read light and heavy. “*Believe in GOD*” (*tuʾminūna*, “you believe”) is a new sentence, as though they said, “How shall we act?” and He said, “You believe.” It is a report with the meaning of a command; for this reason it was answered by His saying “*He will forgive you*,” and the reading of Ibn Masʿūd points to it: *āminū bi-llāhi wa-rasūlihi wa-jāhidū*.

{Q} Why did it come in the wording of a report? {A} To make known that obeying is binding, as though it had been obeyed and He were reporting of faith and striving that exist. Like it is the saying of {p|527}one who prays, “God forgave you,” and “God forgives you”: forgiveness is made, for the strength of the hope, as though it had already been and come about.

{Q} Is there a way for the saying of al-Farrāʾ that it is the answer to “*shall I show you*”? {A} Its way is that what the showing attaches to is the trade, and the trade is explained by faith and striving; so it is as though it were said: will you trade in faith and striving, He will forgive you?

{Q} Then what is the way of the reading of Zayd ibn ʿAlī ﵄, *tuʾminū … wa-tujāhidū*? {A} Its way is that it is on an implied *lām* of command, as in his saying:

> Muḥammad, may every soul ransom your soul / whenever you fear ruin from some matter.

From Ibn ʿAbbās: they said, “*If we knew the works most beloved to God, we would do them*”; and this verse was revealed. They stayed as long as God willed, saying, “*Would that we knew what it is*.” Then God showed it to them by His saying “*Believe in GOD*.” This is a proof that “*Believe in GOD*” is a new sentence, and that a command that comes to souls after they have been looking out and longing for it falls deeper in them and is nearer to their accepting it than one they are taken by surprise with. “*This*”: He means what was mentioned of faith and striving, “*is the best deal for you*” than your wealth and your lives.

{Q} What is the meaning of His saying “*if you know*”? {A} Its meaning is: if you know that it is better for you, then it is better for you, because when you know that and hold it firmly, you love faith and striving above what you love of your lives and your wealth, and so you are saved and succeed.

{L|Additionally, you get something you truly love|wa-ukhrā tuḥibbūnahā} and you have, besides this favor that was mentioned, of forgiveness and reward in the later life, another favor, near at hand, beloved to you. Then He explained it by His saying “*support from GOD and guaranteed victory*,” that is, near at hand, which is the conquest of Mecca. Al-Ḥasan said: the conquest of Persia and Rome. In “*you truly love*” there is something of rebuke for loving what is near at hand.

{Q} On what is His saying “*Give good news to the believers*” coordinated? {A} On “*Believe in GOD*,” because it has the meaning of a command, as though it were said: believe and strive, God will reward you and help you; and give the believers good news of that, Messenger of God.

{Q} Why did the one who read *naṣran mina {p|528}llāhi wa-fatḥan qarīban* put it in the accusative? {A} It may be put in the accusative of specification; or on: you are helped with help, and victory is opened for you; or on: He forgives you and brings you into gardens, and gives you another, help from God and victory.

::: passage 61:14-14 | 4:528-529

{L|Be GOD’s supporters|kūnū anṣāra llāhi} *kūnū anṣāra llāhi* and *anṣāran li-llāhi* are read. Ibn Masʿūd read: *kūnū antum anṣāra llāhi*; and in it there is more insistence on their helping.

{Q} What makes the likening sound, when on its face it likens their being helpers to the saying of Jesus ﵊, “*Who are my supporters towards GOD*” {A} The likening is taken by the meaning, and on that it is sound. What is meant is: be God’s helpers as the disciples were the helpers of Jesus when he said to them, “*Who are my supporters towards GOD*”

{Q} What is the meaning of his saying “*Who are my supporters towards GOD*” {A} Its meaning must match the answer of the disciples, “*We are GOD’s supporters*.” What matches it is that the meaning is: who are my troops, turned toward helping God? The annexation in “*my helpers*” is unlike the annexation in “*GOD’s supporters*.” The meaning of “*We are GOD’s supporters*” is: we are those who help God. The meaning of “*who are my helpers*” is: who of the helpers belong to me and are with me in helping God. It cannot be that its meaning is “who will help me along with God,” because that does not match the answer; and the proof of it is the reading of whoever read *man anṣāru llāhi*. The disciples were his chosen ones; they were the first to believe in him, and they were twelve men. A man’s disciple (*ḥawārī*) is his chosen and sincere friend, from *ḥawar*, which is pure whiteness; and *al-ḥuwwārā* is fine white flour. From this is his saying ﵊: “*Al-Zubayr is my cousin and my disciple from my community*.” It is also said: they were fullers who whitened clothes. Like *ḥawārī* in its pattern is *ḥawālī*, one of many wiles. “*Then a party*” of them “*believed*” in Jesus, “*and another group disbelieved*” in him; “*We helped*” their believers against their unbelievers, and they prevailed {p|529}over them. From Zayd ibn ʿAlī: their prevailing was by argument.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-Ṣaff, Jesus will pray for him and ask forgiveness for him as long as he is in this world, and on the Day of Resurrection he will be his companion*.”
