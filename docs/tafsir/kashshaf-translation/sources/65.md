surah: 65
title: Sūrat al-Ṭalāq (65)
revelation: Medinan. Twelve verses; it is also said eleven or thirteen. [Revealed after al-Insān (76).]

::: passage 65:1-3 | 4:551-556

{p|552}He singled out the Prophet ﷺ in the call and was general in the address, because the Prophet is the leader of his community and their model; as one says to the chief of a people and their elder, “So-and-so, do (all of you) such-and-such,” to show his precedence and take account of his headship, and that he is his people’s seat and their tongue, the one from whose judgment they act, and who decide nothing apart from him. So he alone was as all of them, and stood in the place of them all. The meaning of “*when you people divorce the women*” is: when you want to divorce them and are about to; treating the one who is facing a matter and close to it as one who has begun it, as in his saying ﵇, “*Whoever kills a slain man has his spoils*.” From this is that the one walking to prayer and the one waiting for it are as one praying. “*You shall ensure that a divorce interim is fulfilled*” (*fa-ṭalliqūhunna li-ʿiddatihinna*, “divorce them for their waiting period”): divorce them facing their waiting period, as in your saying, “I came to him for a night that remained of al-Muḥarram,” that is, facing it. In the reading of the Messenger of God ﷺ: *fī qubuli ʿiddatihinna*, at the facing of their waiting period. When a woman is divorced in the clean period that comes before the first of her menstrual cycles, she has been divorced facing her waiting period. What is meant is that they be divorced in a clean period in which there has been no intercourse with them, then left alone until their waiting period ends. This is the best divorce, the most within the Sunna and the furthest {p|553}from regret. What is related from Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī points to it: the Companions of the Messenger of God ﷺ liked not to divorce their wives by the Sunna except once, and then not to divorce more than that until the waiting period ended; and this was better with them than that a man divorce three times in three clean periods. Mālik ibn Anas ﵁ said: “*I know no divorce of the Sunna but once*”; and he disliked the three, whether together or apart. As for Abū Ḥanīfa and his followers, they disliked only what is more than one in a single clean period; apart, over the clean periods, they did not, because of what is related of the Messenger of God ﷺ, that he said to Ibn ʿUmar when he divorced his wife while she was menstruating: “*This is not how God commanded you. The Sunna is only that you await the clean period and divorce her with one divorce for each cycle*.” It is related that he said to ʿUmar: “*Command your son to take her back, then let him leave her until she menstruates and then becomes clean; then let him divorce her if he wishes. That is the waiting period for which God commanded that women be divorced*.” According to al-Shāfiʿī ﵁, there is no harm in sending out the three at once; he said: “*I know of no Sunna or innovation in the number of divorces; it is permitted*.” So Mālik takes account, in the divorce of the Sunna, of the once and the time; Abū Ḥanīfa takes account of the spreading out and the time; and al-Shāfiʿī takes account of the time alone.

{Q} Does a divorce that goes against the Sunna take effect? {A} Yes, and the man is a sinner, because of what is related from the Prophet ﷺ: a man divorced his wife three times in his presence, and he said, “*Do you play with the Book of God while I am among you?*” In the ḥadīth of Ibn ʿUmar: he said, “*Messenger of God, what if I had divorced her three times?*” He said to him, “*Then you would have disobeyed, and your wife would have been separated from you*.” From ʿUmar ﵁: no man who had divorced his wife {p|554}three times was brought to him but he beat him painfully, and he let it take effect against him. From Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyab and a group of the Followers: whoever goes against the Sunna in divorce, giving it during menstruation or as three, it does not take effect. They likened him to one who appointed another to divorce by the Sunna, and he went against it.

{Q} How is a woman divorced by the Sunna who does not menstruate, from youth, age or pregnancy, and one with whom marriage has not been consummated? {A} The young girl, the woman past menstruation and the pregnant woman: with all of them, according to Abū Ḥanīfa and Abū Yūsuf, the three are spread out over the months. Muḥammad and Zufar differed from them about the pregnant woman and said: she is divorced by the Sunna only once. As for the one with whom marriage has not been consummated, she is divorced by the Sunna only once, and the time is not taken into account.

{Q} Is it disliked to divorce the one with whom marriage has been consummated with one irrevocable divorce? {A} The report from our companions differs about it; the plain one is that it is disliked.

{Q} His saying “*when you people divorce the women*” is general, taking in those with whom marriage has been consummated and those with whom it has not, those who have menstrual cycles, those past menstruation, the young and the pregnant; so how was it sound to restrict it to those with menstrual cycles with whom marriage has been consummated? {A} There is neither generality nor restriction there. But “women” is a generic noun for the females of humankind, and this generic sense is a meaning present in all of them and in some of them; so it was allowed that by “women” this or that be meant. When it was said “*you shall ensure that a divorce interim is fulfilled*,” it was known that it was applied to some of them, and they are those with whom marriage has been consummated, of those who keep the waiting period by menstruation. “*You shall measure such an interim precisely*”: keep exact track of it and complete it as three full cycles to come, with nothing lacking in them. “*Do not evict them*” until their waiting period ends “*from their homes*,” from their dwellings that they lived in before the waiting period, which are the husbands’ houses. They were annexed to them because they belong to them in the matter of dwelling.

{Q} What is the meaning of joining their driving out and their going out? {A} The meaning of the driving out is that the husbands should not drive them out in anger at them, or dislike of living with them, or because they need the dwellings; and that they should not give them leave to go out if they ask for it, to make known that their leave has no effect in lifting the ban. And the women should not go out of themselves if they want to. “*Unless they commit a proven adultery*” (*illā an yaʾtīna bi-fāḥishatin mubayyina*): it is read with *fatḥa* and *kasra* on the *yāʾ*. It is said: it is adultery, meaning: unless they commit adultery, and they are taken out for the prescribed punishment to be carried out on them. It is also said: unless they are divorced for rebelliousness, and rebelliousness drops their right to a dwelling. It is also said: unless they speak obscenely, {p|555}and then driving them out is lawful for their obscenity; and the reading of Ubayy supports it: *illā an yafḥushna ʿalaykum*. It is also said: her going out before the end of the waiting period is an indecency in itself. The matter that God brings about is that He turns his heart from hating her to loving her, from turning away from her to desiring her, and from resolving on divorce to regretting it, so that he takes her back. The meaning is: divorce them for their waiting period and count the waiting period; perhaps you will desire and regret and take them back.

{L|Once the interim is fulfilled|fa-idhā balaghna ajalahunna} which is the end of the waiting period, and they come near it, you have the choice. If you wish, then taking back and keeping with what is right and with kindness; and if you wish, then leaving the taking back, parting, and guarding against harm, which is that he takes her back at the end of her waiting period and then divorces her, to lengthen the waiting period on her and torment her. “*You shall have two equitable witnesses witness*”: He means at the taking back and the parting both. This calling to witness is recommended according to Abū Ḥanīfa, like His saying “*Two men shall serve as witnesses*” (Q 2:282). According to al-Shāfiʿī it is obligatory in taking back and recommended in parting. It is also said: the use of the calling to witness is that they not deny one another, that he not be suspected in keeping her, and lest one of them die and the one remaining claim that the marriage stood, so as to inherit. “*Equitable*” (*dhaway ʿadlin minkum*, “two just men of you”): al-Ḥasan said: of the Muslims. From Qatāda: of your free men. “*Before GOD*” (*li-llāh*): for His sake alone; that is, that you give it not for the one witnessed for nor for the one witnessed against, nor for any aim but establishing the right and warding off wrong, as in the saying of God Most High “*you shall be absolutely equitable, and observe GOD, when you serve as witnesses, even against yourselves*” (Q 4:135). That is: “*that*,” the urging to give witness for God’s sake and for upholding justice, “*is an admonition*.”

{L|Anyone who reverences GOD|wa-man yattaqi llāha} it may be a parenthesis stressing what went before, of carrying out the matter of divorce by the Sunna and its best way, the furthest from regret. The meaning is then: whoever fears God, and divorces by the Sunna, does not harm the woman in her waiting period nor drive her from her dwelling, and is careful and calls witnesses, “*He will create an exit for him*” of the griefs and straits that come in the matter of wives, relieve him, give him breathing room and grant him release; “*And will provide for him*” from a direction that does not cross his mind nor does he reckon on, if he pays the bride-gift in full and discharges the rights and the maintenance, and his wealth grows small. From the Prophet ﷺ: he was asked about one who divorced three times, or a thousand, whether there was a way out for him; and he recited it. From Ibn ʿAbbās: he was asked about that and said, “*You did not fear God, so He did not make a way out for you. She is separated from you by three, and the rest is a sin on your neck*.” It may also have been brought by way of digression at His saying “*that is an admonition*,” meaning: whoever fears God, He will make for him a way out and an escape from the griefs of this world and the Hereafter. From the Prophet ﷺ: he recited it and said, “*A way out from the doubtful things of this world, from the throes of death, and from the hardships {p|556}of the Day of Resurrection*.” He ﵇ said: “*I know a verse which, if people held to it, would suffice them: ‘Anyone who reverences GOD …’*”; and he kept reciting it and repeating it. It is related of ʿAwf ibn Mālik al-Ashjaʿī that the associators captured a son of his named Sālim. He came to the Messenger of God and said, “*My son has been captured*,” and complained to him of want. He said, “*The family of Muḥammad has nothing this evening but a* mudd. *So fear God, be patient, and say often, ‘There is no power and no strength except in God.’*” He did so. While he was in his house, his son knocked at the door, and with him were a hundred camels whose guard the enemy had let slip, and he had driven them off; and this verse was revealed.

{L|GOD’s commands are done|bāligh amrihi} that is, He attains what He wants; no aim escapes Him and nothing sought is beyond Him. *Bālighu amrihi*, with annexation, is also read; and *bālighun amruhu*, in the nominative, that is, His command is carried out. Al-Mufaḍḍal read *bālighan amrahu*, on the view that His saying “*GOD has decreed*” is the predicate of *inna* and *bālighan* is a circumstantial clause. “*Its fate*” (*qadran*): a determining and a setting of times. This makes plain that relying on God and leaving the matter to Him is binding; because when one knows that everything, of provision and the like, comes about only by His determining and His setting of its time, nothing remains but submitting to the decree, and reliance.

It is related that some people said, “*We know the waiting period of those who have menstrual cycles; what is the waiting period of those who do not menstruate?*” and it was revealed. So the meaning of “*if you are in doubt*” is: if their ruling is unclear to you and you do not know how they keep the waiting period, then this is their ruling. It is also said: if you are in doubt about the blood of those who have reached the age of despair, which they set at sixty years, or at fifty-five, whether it is menstrual blood or bleeding outside the period, “*their interim shall be three months*.” If this is the waiting period of the one about whom there is doubt, the one about whom there is no doubt has more right to it. “*As for those who do not menstruate*”: they are the young girls. The meaning is: their waiting period is three months; it was dropped because what is mentioned points to it. The wording is unrestricted for “*discover that they are pregnant*,” so it takes in the divorced and those whose husbands have died. Ibn Masʿūd, Ubayy, Abū Hurayra and others did not distinguish. From ʿAlī and Ibn ʿAbbās: the waiting period of the pregnant widow is the later of the two terms. From ʿAbd Allāh: “*Whoever wishes, I will invoke a curse with him that the shorter Sūrat al-Nisāʾ was revealed after the one in al-Baqara*”; he means that this wording is unrestricted for pregnant women. Umm Salama related that Subayʿa al-Aslamiyya gave birth some nights after her husband’s death and mentioned that to the Messenger of God ﷺ, and he said to her, “*You have become lawful, so marry*.” “*He makes everything easy for him*”: He will make his affair easy for him and loose his knots for him because of his fearing God. “*This is GOD’s command*”: He means what is known of the ruling on these women in their waiting period. The meaning is: whoever fears God in acting on these rulings that God sent down, and keeps the rights binding on him that were mentioned, of giving a dwelling, leaving harm, maintaining pregnant women, giving wet nurses their wage and other things, has earned the expiation of his evil deeds and the great reward.

{p|558}{L|You shall allow them to live|askinūhunna} this and what comes after explain the fear of God made a condition in His saying “*Anyone who reverences GOD*,” as though it were said, “How do we act with fear of God in the matter of women in their waiting period?” and it was said, “Lodge them.”

{Q} What is *min* in “*where you dwell*”? {A} It is the partitive *min*, and the part it takes is dropped. Its meaning is: lodge them in a place where you dwell, that is, part of the place of your dwelling, as in the saying of God Most High “*they shall subdue their eyes*” (Q 24:30), that is, part of their gaze. Qatāda said: if there is only one house, lodge her in one of its sides.

{Q} Then His saying *min wujdikum* (“according to your means”) [where does it stand]? {A} It is a clarifying apposition to His saying “*in the same home in which they lived with you*” (*min ḥaythu sakantum*, “where you dwell”) and explains it, as though it were said: lodge them in a place of your dwelling that you can afford. *Al-wujd* is capacity and ability. It is read with all three vowels. The dwelling and the maintenance are both binding for every divorced woman. According to Mālik and al-Shāfiʿī, the irrevocably divorced woman has only the dwelling and no maintenance. From al-Ḥasan and Ḥammād: she has neither maintenance nor dwelling, because of the ḥadīth of Fāṭima bint Qays that her husband divorced her irrevocably, and the Messenger of God ﷺ said to her, “*You have no dwelling and no maintenance*.” From ʿUmar ﵁: “*We will not leave the Book of our Lord and the Sunna of our Prophet for the saying of a woman who perhaps forgot or was confused. I heard the Prophet ﷺ say, ‘She has the dwelling and the maintenance.’*”

{L|Do not make life so miserable for them|wa-lā tuḍārrūhunna} do not use harm with them, “*to straiten them*” in the dwelling by some means, by lodging someone who does not suit them, or by occupying their place, or something else, until you force them to leave. It is also said: it is that he takes her back when two days of her waiting period remain, to straiten {p|559}her affair for her. It is also said: it is that he drives her to buy her release from him.

{Q} If, according to you, maintenance is binding for every divorced woman, what is the use of the condition in His saying “*If they are pregnant, you shall spend on them*”? {A} Its use is that the period of pregnancy may grow long, and someone might think that the maintenance drops when the measure of the waiting period of one not pregnant has passed; so He removed that false idea.

{Q} Then what do you say about the pregnant widow? {A} It is disputed. Most of them hold that she has no maintenance, because there is agreement that whoever a man is forced to maintain, of a wife or a young child, need not be maintained from his wealth after his death; and so the pregnant woman. From ʿAlī, ʿAbd Allāh and a group: they made her maintenance binding.

{L|If they nurse the infant|fa-in arḍaʿna lakum} He means: these divorced women, if they suckle for you a child by another woman or by them, after the tie of marriage has been cut, “*you shall pay them for this service*”: their ruling in that is the ruling of wet nurses. According to Abū Ḥanīfa and his followers ﵃, hiring is not allowed when the child is theirs, so long as they are not irrevocably separated; according to al-Shāfiʿī it is allowed. *Al-iʾtimār* has the meaning of *al-taʾāmur*, consulting together, as *al-ishtiwār* has the meaning of *al-tashāwur*. One says *iʾtamara l-qawmu* and *taʾāmarū* when they command one another. The meaning is: let some of you command others; and the address is to fathers and mothers. “*The amicable relations*” (*bi-maʿrūf*): with what is seemly, which is being easy with one another, that the father not haggle and the mother not be difficult, because it is their child together, and they are partners in it and in the duty of tender care for it. “*If you disagree, you may hire another woman to nurse the child*”: a wet nurse other than the mother will be found and will not be lacking, to suckle him. In it is a touch of reproach to the mother for being difficult; as you say to one you ask to see to a need and who is slow, “Someone else will see to it,” meaning: it will not be left undone, and you are to blame. His saying “*for him*” means for the father, that is: the father will find another, not difficult, to suckle his child for him, if its mother makes difficulties for him.

{L|Shall provide support|li-yunfiq} each of {p|560}the well-off and the hard-pressed what his capacity reaches. He means what He commanded of spending on divorced women and wet nurses, as He said: “*the rich as he can afford and the poor as he can afford*” (Q 2:236). *Li-yunfiqa* is also read, in the accusative, that is: We laid that down so that he spend. Ibn Abī ʿAbla read *quddira*. “*GOD will provide*” is a promise to the poor of that time to open the gates of provision to them; or to the poor husbands, if they spend what they are able and do not fall short.

{L|Rebelled against the commands of its Lord|ʿatat ʿan amri rabbihā} turned away from it in insolence and stubbornness. “*We held them strictly accountable*”: by going into everything and disputing every point. “*A terrible requital*” (*ʿadhāban nukran*): *nukuran* is also read; dreadful and great. What is meant is the reckoning of the Hereafter and its punishment, and the ill consequence they taste in it and the loss they meet. It came in the wording of the past, like the saying of God Most High “*The dwellers of Paradise will call*” (Q 7:44), “*The dwellers of Hell will call*” (Q 7:50), and the like, because what is awaited of God’s promise and threat is in reality already met, and what is to be is as though it had been. His saying “*GOD has prepared for them severe retribution*” repeats the threat and makes plain that it is awaited, as though He said: God has prepared this punishment for them; so let that be for you, “*you who have understanding*” among the believers, a favor moving you to fear God and beware of His punishment. It may also be meant: the counting of evil deeds and going into them against them in this world, recording them in the sheets of the recording angels, and the punishment that struck them in the near term; and that “*rebelled*” and what is coordinated with it are a description of the town, and “*God has prepared for them*” is the answer to *kaʾayyin*.

{L|A messenger|rasūlan} he is Gabriel ﵊. It is put as a substitute for “*a reminder*,” because he is described by the reciting of God’s signs, so his being sent down has the meaning of the sending down of the reminder, and the substitution from it is sound. Or by *dhikr* is meant honor, from His saying “*This is a message for you and your people*” (Q 43:44), and he was put as a substitute {p|561}for it, as though he were in himself an honor: either because he is an honor to the one he is sent down to, or because he has glory and honor with God, as in the saying of God Most High “*Authorized by the Possessor of the Throne*” (Q 81:20). Or, for his much remembrance of God and his worship, he was made as though he were remembrance. Or “one of remembrance” is meant, that is, an angel remembered in the heavens and among all nations. Or His saying “*GOD has sent down to you a message*” points to “He sent,” as though it were said: He sent a messenger. Or He made *dhikr* govern “*a messenger*” as a verbal noun governs objects, that is: God sent down that He remembered a messenger, or his remembering a messenger. *Rasūlun* is also read, on: he is a messenger. He sent him down “*to lead those who believe*” after he was sent down, that is, so that what they are now on, of faith and righteous work, might come about for them; because at the time he was sent down they were not believers, and they believed only after the sending down and the conveying. Or: to bring out those of whom it was known that they would believe. *Yudkhilhu* is read with *yāʾ* and with *nūn*. “*GOD will generously reward him*”: in it is the meaning of wonder and of making great what the believer is provided with of reward.

{L|GOD created|allāhu lladhī khalaqa} a subject and a predicate. *Mithlahunna* is read in the accusative, coordinated on “*seven universes*”; and in the nominative, as a subject, its predicate being “*of the earth*.” It is said: there is no verse in the Qurʾān that points to the earths being seven except this one. It is also said: between every two heavens is a journey of five hundred years, and the thickness of each heaven is the same, and the earths are like the heavens. “*The commands flow among them*”: that is, God’s command and judgment run among them, and His kingdom is carried out in them. From Qatāda: in every heaven and every earth is a creature of His creatures, a command of His commands and a decree of His decrees. It is also said: it is the wonders of His governing that He arranges in them. *Yanzilu l-amru* is also read. From Ibn ʿAbbās: Nāfiʿ ibn al-Azraq asked him whether there were creatures beneath the earths. He said, “*Yes*.” He said, “*What creatures?*” He said, “*Either angels or jinn*.” “*to let you know*” is read with *tāʾ* and with *yāʾ*.

{From|the Messenger of God ﵌} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-Ṭalāq dies on the Sunna of the Messenger of God ﷺ*.”
