surah: 102
title: Sūrat al-Takāthur (102)
revelation: Meccan. Eight verses. [Revealed after al-Kawthar (108).]

::: passage 102:1-8 | 4:791-793

One says *alhāhu ʿan kadhā* and *aqhāhu* when something occupied him so that he turned from a thing. “*Hoarding*” (*al-takāthur*, “rivalry in abundance”) is competing in abundance and boasting of it, so that these say, “We are more,” and those say, “We are more.” It is related that Banū ʿAbd Manāf and Banū Sahm boasted over which of them was more numerous, and Banū ʿAbd Manāf outnumbered them. Banū Sahm said, “Wrongdoing wiped us out in the Jāhiliyya, so count us against you with the living and the dead,” and then Banū Sahm outnumbered them. The meaning: you competed in numbers with the living until, when you had counted them all, you went on to the graves and competed in numbers with the dead. He expressed their reaching the mention of the dead as “visiting the graves” in mockery of them. It is also said that they used to visit the graves, saying as they boasted, “This is the grave of so-and-so, and this is the grave of so-and-so.”

The meaning: that distracted you, though it is no concern of yours and does you no good in this world or the next, from what does concern you in the matter of religion, which is more important and more pressing than anything that matters. Or He meant: rivalry in wealth and children distracted you until {p|792}you died and were buried, spending your lives in seeking this world, racing toward it and throwing yourselves upon it, until death came to you with no concern but it, away from what is more fitting for you: striving for your final end and working for your Hereafter. Visiting the graves is an expression for death. He said: “*This year no friend will stay true ten [nights] who has tasted ḍimād, or else he visits the grave*.” And he said: “*Abū Mālik visited the graves, and became the meanest of their visitors*.” Ibn ʿAbbās read *a-alhākum?*, a question meant to make them admit it.

{L|Indeed|kallā} a rebuke, and a warning that one who looks to his own good should not make this world his whole concern and give no thought to his religion. “*You will find out*” (*sawfa taʿlamūn*) is a warning, so that they will fear and wake from their heedlessness. The repetition strengthens the rebuke and the warning against them, and *thumma* (“then”) shows that the second warning is more forceful and more severe than the first, as you say to someone you are advising, “I tell you, and then I tell you: do not do it.” The meaning: you will come to know the error you are in when you see before you the terror of meeting God, and that this warning was good counsel to you and a mercy toward you.

{L|If only you could find out|law taʿlamūn} then He repeated the warning again and said “*If only you could find out*,” its answer left out. He means: if you knew what lies before you with the knowledge of a certain matter, that is, as you know the things you are certain of, to which your ambitions are devoted, you would do what cannot be described or fathomed; but you are astray and ignorant. Then He said “*You would envision Hell*,” and so made clear to them what He had warned them of and threatened them with. We have already discussed how making a thing plain after leaving it vague magnifies it and gives it weight. It is the answer to an oath left unstated, and the oath confirms the threat, and that what they are threatened with leaves no room for doubt. He repeated it, joined with *thumma*, to harden the threat and add to the terror.

It is also read *la-taraʾunna*, with *hamza*, which is disliked.

{Q} Why is it disliked, when turning a *wāw* that carries *ḍamma* into a *hamza* is a regular rule? {A} That applies to a *wāw* whose *ḍamma* is permanent, and this one is incidental, arising from the meeting of two quiescent letters.

*La-turawunna* and *la-turawunnahā* are also read, in the passive.

{L|The eye of certainty|ʿayn al-yaqīn} that is, the seeing that is certainty itself, in its pure form. By the seeing may also be meant {p|793}knowledge and sight.

{L|About the blessings|ʿan al-naʿīm} about the amusement and luxury whose enjoyment distracted you from religion and its duties.

{Q} What is the bliss a person will be asked about and rebuked for, when there is no one who has no bliss? {A} It is the bliss of one who has bent all his ambition on taking his fill of pleasures, who lives only to eat good food and wear soft clothes and passes his time in amusement and merriment, caring nothing for knowledge and deeds and not bearing their hardships. As for one who enjoys God’s favor and the provisions He created only for His servants, strengthens himself by them to study knowledge and carry out deeds, and rises to give thanks, he is far from that. The Messenger of God ﷺ pointed to this in what is related: he and his Companions ate dates and drank water after them, and he said, “*Praise be to God, who fed us, gave us drink, and made us Muslims*.”

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Alhākum al-takāthur, God will not call him to account for the bliss He granted him in the abode of this world, and he will be given a reward as if he had recited a thousand verses*.”
