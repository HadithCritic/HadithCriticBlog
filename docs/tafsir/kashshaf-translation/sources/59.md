::: passage 59:1-2 | 4:498-500

The Banū l-Naḍīr made peace with the Messenger of God ﷺ on the terms that they would be neither against him nor for him. When he prevailed on the day of Badr they said, “*He is the prophet whose description is in the Torah; no banner of his is turned back*.” When the Muslims were routed on the day of Uḥud they doubted and broke faith. Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf went out with forty riders to Mecca, and they made an alliance with Quraysh against him at the Kaʿba. So he ﵇ commanded Muḥammad ibn Maslama al-Anṣārī, and he killed Kaʿb by stealth; he was his foster brother. Then he came upon them in the morning with the troops, riding an ass with a halter of palm fiber, and said to them, “*Leave Medina*.” They said, “*Death is dearer to us than that*,” and they called one another to war. It is also said that they asked the Messenger of God for ten days’ delay to make ready to leave, and ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ubayy the hypocrite and his companions secretly sent word to them: “*Do not leave the fortress. If they fight you, we are with you and will not forsake you; and if you are driven out, we will go out with you*.” So they narrowed the mouths of the lanes and fortified them, and he besieged them for twenty-one nights. When God cast terror into their hearts and they despaired of help from the hypocrites, they asked for terms. He refused them anything but leaving the land, on the terms that every three households might load on a camel whatever of their goods they wished. So they left for Syria, to Jericho and Adhriʿāt, except the people of two households of them, the family of {p|499}Abū l-Ḥuqayq and the family of Ḥuyayy ibn Akhṭab, who joined Khaybar; and a party joined al-Ḥīra.

{L|In a mass exodus|li-awwali l-ḥashri} the *lām* is connected with “*evicted*.” It is the *lām* in the saying of God Most High “*Oh, I wish I prepared for my (eternal) life*” (Q 89:24), and in your saying, “I came to him for such-and-such a time.” The meaning is: He evicted those who disbelieved at the first gathering. The meaning of the first gathering is that this was the first time they were gathered to Syria. They were of a tribe that exile had never touched, and they were the first of the People of the Book to be driven out of the Arabian peninsula to Syria. Or: this was the first of their gathering, and the last of their gathering was ʿUmar’s driving them out of Khaybar to Syria. It is also said: the last of their gathering is the gathering of the Day of Resurrection, because the place of gathering will be in Syria. From ʿIkrima: “*Whoever doubts that the place of gathering is here*,” meaning Syria, “*let him recite this verse*.” It is also said that its meaning is: He drove them from their homes at the first that was gathered to fight them, because it was the first fighting in which the Messenger of God ﷺ fought them. “*You never thought that they would leave*,” because of the force of their might and their strength, the firmness of their fortresses, and their great number and equipment; “*and they thought*” that their fortresses would keep them from God’s might. “*Then*” God’s command “*came upon them from where they did not reckon*,” from where they did not think and it did not cross their minds: the killing of their chief Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf unawares at the hand of his brother. That was among what weakened their strength and blunted their sting. He took from their hearts safety and calm by the terror He cast into them, inspired them to join the believers in ruining their houses and to help against themselves, and held back the hypocrites who took them as friends from backing them. None of this was in their reckoning, and from it destruction came upon them.

{Q} What difference is there between your saying “they thought that their fortresses would keep them,” or “would be keeping them,” and the arrangement in which it came? {A} In putting the predicate before the subject there is a proof of their great trust in the fortresses’ strength and their keeping them safe; and in making their pronoun the noun of *anna* and resting the clause on it there is a proof that they believed of themselves that they were in might and strength, with which no one who came against them or hoped to overcome them was to be minded. That is not in your saying “they thought that their fortresses would keep them.”

*Fa-ātāhum*, that is, “He brought them destruction,” is also read. Terror is the fear that fills the breast, that is, fills it up; and casting it is setting it firm and planting it. From this they said in describing the lion, *muqadhdhaf*, as though he were packed with flesh, because of his being compact and his parts running into one another. *Yukharribūna* and *yukhribūna* are read, heavy and light. *Takhrīb* and *ikhrāb* are spoiling by tearing down and demolishing. *Al-kharba* is ruin. They were ruining the insides and the Muslims the outsides, for what God wanted of tearing them out by the root, and that they should have no dwelling left in Medina and none of them left in it. What called them to the ruining was their need of the wood and the stones {p|500}to block the mouths of the lanes with them; and so that after their leaving they should not grieve over the houses’ remaining as dwellings for the Muslims; and so that they might carry with them what was in their buildings of fine wood and handsome teak. As for the believers, what called them was removing their place of defense and refuge, and widening the field of war for themselves.

{Q} What is the meaning of their ruining them by the hands of the believers? {A} Since they exposed them to that and were the cause of it, it was as though they had commanded them to do it and charged them with it. “*So take heed*” from what God planned and made easy of the matter of driving them out and giving the Muslims power over them without fighting. It is also said: the Messenger of God ﷺ promised the Muslims that God would make them inherit their land and their wealth without fighting, and it was as he said.

{L|You chop a tree|min līnatin} explains “*what you cut*.” The place of *mā* is the accusative through “*you cut*,” as though He said: whatever thing you cut. He made the pronoun that refers to *mā* feminine in His saying “*or left*” because it has the meaning of *līna*. *Al-līna* is a palm of the common kinds, the sorts of palm other than the *ʿajwa* and the *barniyya*, which are the best of palms. Its *yāʾ* comes from a *wāw*, changed because of the *kasra* before it, as in *dīma*. It is also said: *al-līna* is the noble palm, as though they derived it from softness (*līn*). Dhū l-Rumma said:

> As though my saddle-frames upon her were a bird’s nest / {p|501}on a long-trunked palm whose sides sway in the wind.

Its plural is *līn*. *Quwwaman ʿalā uṣulihā* is also read. In it are two ways: that *uṣul* is the plural of *aṣl*, like *rahn* and *ruhun*; or that the *ḍamma* was taken as enough in place of the *wāw*. *Qāʾiman ʿalā uṣūlihi* is also read, going by the wording of *mā*. “*Is in accordance with GOD’s will*”: their cutting was by God’s leave and His command. “*He will surely humiliate the wicked*” (*wa-li-yukhziya l-fāsiqīn*): and to abase the Jews and enrage them He gave leave to cut them. That is because, when the Messenger of God ﷺ commanded that their palms be cut and burned, they said, “*Muḥammad, you used to forbid corruption in the earth; what is this cutting and burning of palms?*” There was something of that in the minds of the believers, and it was revealed. He means: God gave them leave to cut them so as to add to your rage and double your grief when you see them ruling over your wealth as they please and handling it as they wish. The learned are agreed that there is no harm in tearing down the unbelievers’ fortresses and homes, burning them, flooding them and striking them with catapults; and likewise there is no harm in uprooting their trees, fruit-bearing or not. From Ibn Masʿūd: they cut those of them that stood in a place of fighting.

{Q} Why were the *līna* palms singled out for cutting? {A} If they were of the common kinds, it was so that they might keep the *ʿajwa* and the *barniyya* for themselves; and if they were of the noble palms, it was so that the rage of the Jews would be stronger and harder. It is related that two men were cutting, one of them the *ʿajwa* and the other the common kind. The Messenger of God ﷺ asked them, and this one said, “*I left it for the Messenger of God*,” and that one said, “*I cut it to enrage the unbelievers*.” It has been used as proof that independent reasoning is allowed, and that it is allowed in the presence of the Messenger ﷺ, because they did that by independent reasoning; {p|502}and it was used as an argument by whoever says that everyone who reasons independently is right.

{L|God restored to His Messenger|afāʾa llāhu ʿalā rasūlihi} made it spoils (*fayʾ*) for him alone. *Al-ījāf* is from *wajīf*, which is going fast; from this is his saying ﵊ in the setting out from ʿArafāt: “*Piety is not in spurring on horses nor in speeding camels; go at your ease*.” The meaning of “*you did not spur on toward it*” is: you did not spur on horses or riding camels to get it and take it as plunder, and you did not tire yourselves fighting for it; you only walked to it on your feet. The meaning is that what God gave His Messenger of the wealth of the Banū l-Naḍīr is a thing you did not get by fighting and overcoming. Rather, God gave him power over them and over what was in their hands, as He gave His messengers power over their enemies; so the matter of it is left to him, to put where he wills. He means that it is not divided as spoils that were fought for and taken by force and conquest are divided. That is because they asked for it to be divided, and it was revealed.

The coordinating particle did not come in on this clause because it explains the first, so it is part of it and not foreign to it. He made plain to the Messenger of God ﷺ what to do with what God restored to him, and commanded him to put it where he puts the fifth of the spoils, divided into the five shares. *Dawla* and *dūla*, with *fatḥa* and *ḍamma*, and both have been read, is the good fortune that turns (*yadūlu*) to a person, that is, comes round to him. One says, “Fortune turned to him,” and “So-and-so was given the turn.” The meaning of the saying of God Most High “*Thus, it will not remain monopolized by the strong among you*” is: so that the spoils, whose due is to be given to the poor so that they have a sufficiency to live on, do not become a fortune among the rich with which they vie in wealth. Or: so that it does not become a Jāhiliyya fortune among them. The meaning of the Jāhiliyya fortune is that their chiefs used to take the spoils for themselves, because they were the people of headship, fortune and dominance, and they used to say, “Whoever prevails, plunders.” The meaning is: so that taking it is not by dominance and Jāhiliyya self-preference. From this is the saying of al-Ḥasan: “*They took God’s servants as {p|503}slaves and God’s wealth as a fortune passed around*,” meaning: whoever of them prevailed took it and kept it for himself. It is also said: *al-dūla* is what is passed around, like *ghurfa*, the name of what is scooped up. He means: so that the spoils are not a thing the rich pass around among themselves and take turns with, so that it does not reach the poor. *Al-dawla*, with *fatḥa*, means passing around: that is, so that it is not a thing passed around among them; or so that keeping it is not a passing around among them in which they do not bring it out to the poor. *Dūlatun* is also read, in the nominative, with the complete *kāna*, as in the saying of God Most High “*If the debtor is unable to pay*” (Q 2:280). He means: so that a Jāhiliyya fortune does not happen and so that its trace is cut off; or so that there is no passing around of it among them; or so that there is not a thing taken in turns among them and not brought out to the poor.

{L|You may keep the spoils given to you by the messenger|wa-mā ātākumu l-rasūlu} of a share of spoils or *fayʾ*, “*but do not take what he enjoins you from taking*” to take of it, “*do not take*” from it, and do not let your souls follow it. “*You shall reverence GOD*” lest you go against him and make light of His commands and prohibitions. “*GOD is strict in enforcing retribution*” to whoever goes against His Messenger. The better view is that it is general, in everything the Messenger of God ﷺ brought and forbade, and the matter of the spoils comes under its generality. From Ibn Masʿūd ﵁: he met a man in the pilgrim’s state of consecration wearing his clothes and said to him, “*Take this off*.” The man said, “*Recite to me a verse of God’s Book about this*.” He said, “*Yes*,” and recited it to him.

{L|Those who provided them with a home|wa-lladhīna tabawwaʾū} is coordinated with “*the emigrants*”; they are the Anṣār.

{Q} What is the meaning of coordinating faith with the abode, when one does not say, “they settled faith”? {A} Its meaning is: they settled in the abode and made faith sincere, as in his saying:

> I fed her straw and cool water.

Or: they made faith a place of rest and a home for themselves, because of their firm hold on it and their keeping straight on it, as they made Medina so. Or He meant the abode of the emigration and the abode of faith, and put the article in “the abode” in place of the second term of annexation, dropped the first term from “the abode of faith,” and put the second in its place. Or He named Medina “faith” because it is the abode of the emigration {p|505}and the place where faith appeared. “*Before them*”: before the emigrants, because they went before them in settling the abode of the emigration and faith. It is also said: before their emigrating. “*Find no hesitation*” (*wa-lā yajidūna*, “and they do not find”): and they do not know in themselves “*hesitation in their hearts*” (*ḥājatan*, “any want”) “*of what they were given*,” that is, any seeking of a thing needed from what the emigrants were given of the spoils and other things. What is needed is called a want; one says, “Take of it what you want,” and “He gave him of his wealth what he wanted.” He means that their souls did not follow what they were given and did not aspire to anything of it that they needed. “*Even when they themselves need what they give away*” (*wa-law kāna bihim khaṣāṣa*): that is, want. Its origin is the *khaṣāṣ* of a house, which are its gaps. The clause is in the place of a circumstantial clause, that is: their need being supposed. The Messenger of God ﷺ divided the wealth of the Banū l-Naḍīr among the emigrants and gave the Anṣār none but three needy men: Abū Dujāna Simāk ibn Kharasha, Sahl ibn Ḥunayf, and al-Ḥārith ibn al-Ṣimma. He said to them, “*If you wish, you may divide some of your wealth and homes among the emigrants and share with them in these spoils; and if you wish, your homes and your wealth are yours and nothing of the spoils is divided for you*.” The Anṣār said, “*No, we will divide some of our wealth and homes for them, prefer them in the spoils, and not share with them in it*”; and it was revealed.

*Al-shuḥḥ*, with *ḍamma* and *kasra*, and both have been read, is meanness, and that a man’s soul be tight and eager to withhold, as he said:

> He wrestles with a tight soul between his ribs; / whenever he thinks of a kindness, it says to him, “Slowly!”

It was annexed to the soul because it is an inborn trait in it. As for avarice, it is the withholding itself; from this is the saying of God Most High “*Selfishness is a human trait*” (Q 4:128). “*Those who overcome their natural stinginess*”: whoever overcomes what his soul commands him of it and goes against its desire with God’s help and the success He grants, “*are the successful ones*,” who win what they wanted. *Wa-man yuwaqqa* is also read.

{p|507}{L|Fear|rahbatan} the verbal noun of *ruhiba*, built for the passive, as though it were said: more feared. His saying “*in their breasts*” points to their hypocrisy. He means that openly they show you fear of God, while you are more dreaded in their breasts than God.

{Q} As though they feared God, so that their fear of you was greater? {A} Its meaning is that their fear of you in secret is greater than their fear of God that they show you; and they used to show them great fear of God. He may also mean that the Jews fear you in their breasts more than they fear God, because they were a people of might and courage, and they made a show of boldness to them while hiding fear in their breasts. “*People who do not comprehend*”: they do not know God and His greatness, so as to fear Him as He should be feared. “*They do not get together to fight you*” (*lā yuqātilūnakum jamīʿan*): they are not able to fight you, *jamīʿan* (“together”), gathered and backing one another, meaning the Jews and the hypocrites, “*except*” while they are “*in fortified towns*,” with trenches and narrow passes, “*or behind walls*,” without coming out into the open to meet you in combat, because God has cast terror into their hearts and because God’s support and help are with you. *Judr*, lightened, is also read, and *jidār*, and *jadr* and *judur*, which mean the wall. “*Their might appears formidable among themselves*”: He means that the severe might with which they are described is only among themselves when they fight one another; were they to fight you, that might and strength would not remain to them, because the brave man turns coward and the mighty is abased when he makes war on God and His Messenger. “*You would think that they are united*,” gathered, with fellowship and union, “*when in fact their hearts are divided*,” scattered, with no fellowship among them. He means that among them are grudges and enmities, so they do not support one another as they should and do not shoot from one bow. This is to embolden the believers and give their hearts courage to fight them. “*People who do not understand*” that scattered hearts are among what weakens their strength and helps against their lives.

{L|Their fate is the same as their counterparts who preceded them|ka-mathali lladhīna min qablihim} that is, their example is like the example of the people of Badr, in a near time.

{Q} By what is “*shortly*” put in the accusative? {A} By *mathal*, on the sense: like the being of the example of the people of Badr shortly before. “*They suffered the consequences of their decisions*”: the evil outcome of their unbelief and their enmity to the Messenger of God ﷺ; from their saying “*kalaʾun wabīl*,” unwholesome pasture of evil outcome. He means that they tasted the punishment of killing in this world, “*and for them*” in the Hereafter is the punishment of the Fire.

The example of the hypocrites, in their stirring up the Jews to fight and promising them help, then leaving them and breaking their word, is “*They are like the devil*” when he leads a human being astray by his cunning and then disowns him in the end. What is meant is his leading Quraysh astray on the day of Badr, and his saying to them, “*No one among the people will overcome you today, and I am your protector*,” up to his saying, “*I disown you*” (Q 8:48). Ibn Masʿūd read *khālidāni fīhā*, as the predicate of *anna*, with “*in the Fire*” a non-predicative adverbial (*laghw*); on the well-known reading the adverbial is predicative (*mustaqarr*) and “*abiding in it*” is a circumstantial clause. *Anā barīʾun* is also read, and *ʿāqibatuhumā* in the nominative.

He repeated the command to fear God for emphasis. Or: fear God in carrying out obligations, because it is joined to something that is a work; and fear God in leaving sins, because it is joined to something that runs as a threat. “Tomorrow” is the Day of Resurrection. He named it by the day that follows your day, to bring it near. From al-Ḥasan: He kept bringing it near until He made it like tomorrow. Like it is the saying of God Most High “*as if nothing existed the previous day*” (Q 10:24), meaning to bring near past time. It is also said: He expressed the Hereafter by tomorrow as though this world and the Hereafter were two days, a day and a tomorrow.

{Q} What is the meaning of leaving “soul” and “tomorrow” indefinite? {A} As for leaving “soul” indefinite, it is to make few the souls that look into what they have sent ahead for the Hereafter, as though He said: let one soul look into that. As for leaving “tomorrow” indefinite, it is to make it great and leave its matter vague, as though it were said: for a tomorrow whose nature is not known for its greatness. From Mālik ibn Dīnār: written on the gate of Paradise is: “*We found what we did; we gained what we sent ahead; we lost what we left behind*.”

{L|Forgot God|nasū llāha} forgot what is due to Him; so He made them forget what is due to themselves, by forsaking them, so that they did not strive for themselves with what would profit them with Him. Or: He showed them on the Day of Resurrection such terrors that in them they forgot themselves, as in the saying of God Most High “*their eyes will not even blink*” (Q 14:43).
