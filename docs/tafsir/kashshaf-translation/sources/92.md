surah: 92
title: Sūrat al-Layl (92)
revelation: Meccan. Twenty-one verses. [Revealed after al-Aʿlā (87).]

::: passage 92:1-4 | 4:761-762

{L|As it covers|idhā yaghshā} what is covered is either the sun, from His saying “*The night that covers*” (*wa-l-layli idhā yaghshāhā*, Q 91:4); or the day, from His saying “*The night overtakes the day*” (*yughshī l-layla l-nahāra*, Q 7:54); or everything it hides with its darkness, from His saying “*darkness as it falls*” (*idhā waqab*, “when it settles,” Q 113:3). “*As it reveals*” (*idhā tajallā*, “when it shines forth”) (*tajallā*): it appears as the darkness of night departs; or it becomes clear and uncovered as the sun rises.

{L|And Him who created|wa-mā khalaqa} and the Able One, great in power, who was able to create the male and the female from one water. It is also said that they are Adam ﵇ and Eve. In the reading of the Prophet ﷺ: *wa-l-dhakari wa-l-unthā*. Ibn Masʿūd read *wa-lladhī khalaqa l-dhakara wa-l-unthā*. {p|762}From al-Kisāʾī: *wa-mā khalaqa l-dhakari wa-l-unthā*, in the genitive, as a substitute for the place of *mā khalaqa* (“what He created”), in the sense “and what God created,” that is, and God’s creation, the male and the female. Leaving the name of God implied is allowed because it is known, since He alone creates and there is no creator but Him. It is also said that God created no creature with a spirit that is neither male nor female. The hermaphrodite, even if his case is doubtful to us, is not doubtful with God, but known to be male or female. So if a man swore by divorce that he had not met a male or a female that day, and he had met a doubtful hermaphrodite, he would have broken his oath, because in truth it is either male or female, even if it is doubtful to us.

{L|Of various kinds|la-shattā} the plural of *shatīt*: that is, your strivings are scattered and different, and how they differ is made clear in what is set out after it.

::: passage 92:5-7 | 4:762-762

{L|Gives to charity|aʿṭā} He means the dues on his wealth. “*And maintains righteousness*” (*wa-ttaqā*, “and fears”) God, and so does not disobey Him. “*And upholds the scripture*” (*wa-ṣaddaqa bi-l-ḥusnā*, “and confirms the best”): the best trait, which is faith; or the best religion, which is the religion of Islam; or the best reward, which is Paradise.

{L|We will direct him towards happiness|fa-sa-nuyassiruhu li-l-yusrā} We will make him ready for it, from *yassara l-farasa li-l-rukūb*, when he saddled and bridled the horse. From this is his saying ﵇: “*Everyone is eased toward what he was created for*.” The meaning: We will be gracious to him and grant him success, until obedience is the easiest and lightest of things for him, from His saying “*Whomever GOD wills to guide, He renders his chest wide open to Submission*” (Q 6:125).

::: passage 92:8-11 | 4:762-762

{L|Though he is rich|wa-staghnā} and has no desire for what is with God, as though he had no need of it, and so did not fear Him; or he made do with the pleasures of this world in place of the bliss of Paradise; because it stands against *wa-ttaqā* (“and fears”).

{L|We will direct him towards misery|fa-sa-nuyassiruhu li-l-ʿusrā} We will forsake him and withhold the graces from him, until obedience is the hardest and most grievous thing for him, from His saying “*He renders his chest intolerant and straitened, like one who climbs towards the sky*” (Q 6:125). Or He called the way of good *al-yusrā* (“ease”) because its end is ease, and the way of evil *al-ʿusrā* (“hardship”) because its end is hardship. Or by them He meant the two ways of Paradise and the Fire: that is, We will guide the two of them in the Hereafter to the two ways. It is said that the two passages were revealed about Abū Bakr ﵁ and about Abū Sufyān ibn Ḥarb.

{L|His money cannot help him|wa-mā yughnī ʿanhu} a question in the sense of denial; or a negation. *Taraddā* is *tafaʿʿala* from *al-radā*, which is perishing; He means death. Or: he fell into the pit, when he was buried; or he fell into the depths of Hell.

::: passage 92:12-13 | 4:763-763

{L|We provide the guidance|inna ʿalaynā la-l-hudā} guiding to the truth is binding on Us, by setting up proofs and making the laws clear. “*We control the Hereafter, as well as this life*”: that is, the reward of the two abodes for the one who is guided, as in His saying “*we endowed him with his due recompense in this life, and in the Hereafter he will surely be with the righteous*” (Q 29:27).

::: passage 92:14-21 | 4:763-765

{L|The blazing Hellfire|nāran talaẓẓā} Abū l-Zubayr read *tatalaẓẓā*.

{Q} How did He say “*None burns therein except the wicked*” (*lā yaṣlāhā illā l-ashqā*, “none will burn in it but the most wretched”) … “*Avoiding it will be the righteous*” (*wa-sa-yujannabuhā l-atqā*, “and the most pious will be kept far from it”), when it is known that every wretched one will burn in it and every pious one will be kept far from it, so that burning is not reserved for the most wretched of the wretched, nor escape for {p|764}the most pious of the pious? And if you claim that He made “*Hellfire*” indefinite and meant a particular fire reserved for the most wretched, what will you do with His saying “*Avoiding it will be the righteous*,” when it is known that the most sinful of the Muslims will be kept far from that particular fire, not the most pious of them alone? {A} The verse comes in weighing the states of a great man among the idolaters and a great man among the believers against each other. So it was meant to go to the utmost in their opposite descriptions. It said *al-ashqā* (“the most wretched”) and made him alone the one who burns, as though the Fire were created only for him; and it said *al-atqā* (“the most pious”) and made him alone the one who escapes, as though Paradise were created only for him. It is said that they are Abū Jahl, or Umayya ibn Khalaf, and Abū Bakr ﵁.

{L|To charity|yatazakkā} from *al-zakāʾ*: that is, he seeks to be pure with God, wanting no show or reputation by it. Or it is *yatafaʿʿalu* from *al-zakāh* (“alms”).

{Q} What is the position of *yatazakkā*? {A} It is in one of two ways. If you make it a substitute for *yuʾtī*, it has no position, because it comes under the rule of the relative clause, and relative clauses have no position. If you make it a circumstantial clause to the pronoun in *yuʾtī*, its position is accusative.

{L|Seeking only his Lord|illā btighāʾa wajhi rabbihi} excepted from what is not of its kind, namely the favor: that is, no one has any favor with him, only the seeking of the face of his Lord; as you say “There is no one in the house except a donkey” (*ḥimāran*). Yaḥyā ibn Waththāb read *illā btighāʾu wajhi rabbihi*, in the nominative, in the dialect of those who say *mā fī l-dāri aḥadun illā ḥimārun*. Recited for both dialects are the saying of Bishr ibn Abī Khāzim, “*It became empty and desolate, with no companion in it but the wild calves and the ostriches coming and going*,” and the saying of the poet, “*And many a land with no companion in it but the gazelles and the white camels*.”

{p|765}“*Seeking only his Lord*” may also be an object of purpose by sense, because the meaning of the speech is: he gives his wealth only seeking the face of his Lord, not to repay a favor. “*He will certainly attain salvation*” (*wa-la-sawfa yarḍā*, “and he will be pleased”): a promise of the reward that will please him and gladden his eye.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat wa-l-layl, God will give him until he is pleased, keep him from hardship and make ease easy for him*.”
