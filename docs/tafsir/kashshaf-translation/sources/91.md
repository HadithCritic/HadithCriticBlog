title: Sūrat al-Shams (91)
revelation: Meccan. Fifteen verses. [Revealed after al-Qadr (97).]

::: passage 91:1-10 | 4:758-760

{L|Its brightness|ḍuḥāhā} its light when it shines and its power is established. Hence it is said “the time of *ḍuḥā*,” and “as if his face were the sun of *ḍuḥā*.” It is also said: *al-ḍaḥwa* is when the day is up, and *al-ḍuḥā* is beyond that, and *al-ḍaḥāʾ*, with *fatḥa* and a long vowel, is when the day has stretched out and is near its middle.

{L|That follows it|idhā talāhā} rising as it sets, taking from its light; that is in the first half of the month. It is also said: when it becomes round and follows it in brightness and light.

{L|That reveals|idhā jallāhā} when the day has risen high and spread out, because the sun shows itself fully at that time. It is also said that the pronoun is for the darkness, or the world, or the earth, even though there has been no mention of it, as in their saying *aṣbaḥat bāridatan* (“it became cold”), meaning the morning, and *arsalat* (“it let loose”), meaning the sky. “*That covers*” (*idhā yaghshāhā*, “when it covers it”): so that it disappears and the horizons grow dark.

{Q} What puts *idhā* in the accusative is a hard knot. You must either make the *wāw*s coordinating, putting the nouns in the accusative and the genitive through them, and then you fall into coordinating on two governors, as in your saying “I passed yesterday by Zayd, and today ʿAmr”; or make them *wāw*s of oath, and then you fall into what al-Khalīl and Sībawayh agreed in disliking. {A} The answer is that with the *wāw* of oath, expressing the verb is dropped entirely. So it has a standing unlike that of the *bāʾ*, with which the verb may be expressed or implied: the *wāw* stands in the place of the verb and the *bāʾ* together, filling the place of both. The coordinating *wāw*s stand in for this *wāw*, and so properly do the work of both the verb and the preposition; as you say, “Zayd struck ʿAmr, {p|759}and Bakr Khālid,” putting one in the nominative and the other in the accusative through the *wāw*, because it stands in the place of “struck,” which governs both.

{L|And Him who built it|wa-mā banāhā} *mā* has been taken as a verbal-noun particle in His saying “*and Him who built it*” (*wa-mā banāhā*, “and what built it”), “*and Him who sustains it*” (*wa-mā ṭaḥāhā*, “and what spread it”), and “*and Him who created it*” (*wa-mā sawwāhā*, “and what proportioned it”); but that is not the right way, because of His saying “*Then showed it*” (*fa-alhamahā*, “and inspired it”), and the disorder of construction it would lead to. The right way is that it be a relative. It was preferred to *man* (“who”) only for the sake of the sense of description, as if it were said: by the sky and the Mighty, Able One who built it; and by a soul and the Wise One of dazzling wisdom who proportioned it. In their speech is: “Glory be to what (*mā*) subjected you to us.”

{Q} Why is “*soul*” indefinite? {A} There are two ways. One: that He means a particular soul among souls, which is the soul of Adam, as if He said: and by one of the souls. The second: that He means every soul, and it is indefinite to show multitude, in the way mentioned on His saying *ʿalimat nafsun* (“a soul will know,” Q 81:14).

{L|Then showed it what is evil and what is good|fa-alhamahā fujūrahā wa-taqwāhā} the meaning of inspiring wickedness and piety is making them understood and grasped by the mind, and that one of them is good and the other foul, and enabling it to choose whichever of them it wills. The proof is His saying “*Successful is one who redeems it. Failing is one who neglects it*”: He made it the doer of the purifying {p|760}and the corrupting, and the one who carries them out. *Al-tazkiya* is making grow and raising up through piety; *al-tadsiya* is diminishing and hiding through wickedness. The root of *dassā* is *dassasa*, as is said *taqaḍḍā* for *taqaḍḍaḍa*. Ibn ʿAbbās was asked about it, and he said: “*Do you recite ‘He has succeeded who purifies himself’ (Q 87:14) and ‘and he has failed who carries wrongdoing’ (Q 20:111)?*” As for the saying of the one who claimed that the pronoun in *zakkā* and *dassā* is for God Most High, and that what refers back to *man* is feminine because it is in the sense of “soul”: that is of the perversity of the Qadariyya, who lay at God’s door a decree He is free of and far above, and spend their nights contriving a foul thing that they ascribe to Him.

{Q} Then where is the answer to the oath? {A} It is left out. The implied sense is: God will surely bring destruction (*la-yudamdimanna*) on them, that is, on the people of Mecca, for denying the Messenger of God ﷺ, as He brought destruction on Thamūd because they denied Ṣāliḥ. As for “*Successful is one who redeems it*,” it is speech that follows on His saying “*Then showed it what is evil and what is good*” by way of digression, and is no part of the answer to the oath.

{L|Disbelief caused them to transgress|bi-ṭaghwāhā} the *bāʾ* in *bi-ṭaghwāhā* is like that in “I wrote with the pen.” *Al-ṭaghwā* is from *al-ṭughyān*. In the *faʿlā* form of roots ending in *yāʾ* they distinguished between noun and adjective by turning the *yāʾ* into *wāw* in the noun and leaving it unchanged in the adjective, so they said *imraʾa khazyā* and *ṣadyā*. He means: they committed denial through their transgression, as you say, “He wronged me through his daring against God.” It is also said: they denied the punishment they were threatened with, which came with transgressing force, as in His saying “*they were annihilated by the devastating (quake)*” (*bi-l-ṭāghiya*, Q 69:5). Al-Ḥasan read *bi-ṭughwāhā*, with *ḍamma* on the *ṭāʾ*, like *al-ḥusnā* and *al-rujʿā* among verbal nouns.

{L|They followed|idhi nbaʿatha} in the accusative through *kadhdhabat* (“denied”), or through *al-ṭaghwā*. “*The worst among them*” (*ashqāhā*, “its most wretched”) is Qudār ibn Sālif. They may also have been a group, and it is singular because in the annexed elative you treat singular and plural, masculine and feminine alike; it would also have been possible to say *ashqawhā*, as you say *afāḍiluhum*. The pronoun in “*to them*” may be for the most wretched ones. The elative in wretchedness is because whoever took charge of the hamstringing and did it himself, his wretchedness was the plainer and the greater.

{L|GOD’s camel|nāqata llāh} in the accusative of warning, as you say “The lion, the lion!” and “The boy, the boy!”, with “leave” or “beware of hamstringing” implied. “*Let her drink*” (*wa-suqyāhā*, “and her drinking”): so do not keep it from her, and do not {p|761}take it for yourselves instead of her.

{L|They disbelieved him|fa-kadhdhabūhu} in what he warned them of, that punishment would come down if they did it. “*Their Lord then requited them*” (*fa-damdama ʿalayhim rabbuhum*) (*fa-damdama*): He let the punishment loose on them. It is from doubling their saying *nāqa madmūma*, when fat has covered her. “*For their sin*”: because of their sin. In it is a great warning of the end of sin, so every sinner should take a lesson and beware.

{L|And annihilated them|fa-sawwāhā} the pronoun is for the destruction: that is, He leveled it among them, so that neither small nor great of them escaped it. “*Yet, those who came after them remain heedless*” (*wa-lā yakhāfu ʿuqbāhā*, “and He does not fear its consequence”): its outcome and what follows from it, as every king who punishes fears, and so spares somewhat. The pronoun may also be for Thamūd, in the sense: He leveled it with the ground, or in destruction, and does not fear the consequence of destroying it. In the copies of the people of Medina and Syria: *fa-lā yakhāfu*; and in the reading of the Prophet ﵌: *wa-lam yakhaf*.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-Shams, it is as if he gave in charity everything the sun and the moon rise over*.”
