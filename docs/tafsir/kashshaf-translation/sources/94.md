::: passage 94:1-4 | 4:770-771

{L|Did we not cool|a-lam nashraḥ} He asked about the absence of the expanding by way of denial, and so it gives the affirming and establishing of the expanding, as if it were said “We expanded your breast for you.” That is why “*And we unloaded*” (*wa-waḍaʿnā*, “and We removed”) is coordinated with it, following the sense. The meaning of “We expanded your breast” is: We widened it until it held the whole of prophethood and the call to both races, jinn and humans; or until it bore the hardships that the unbelievers of your people and others put you through. Or: We widened it with the knowledge and wisdom We placed in it, and took from it the narrowness and constraint that go with blindness and ignorance. From al-Ḥasan: it was filled with wisdom and knowledge. From Abū Jaʿfar al-Manṣūr: he read *a-lam nashraḥa laka*, with *fatḥa* on the *ḥāʾ*; and they said that perhaps he made the *ḥāʾ* clear and gave it full value at its point of articulation, and the hearer thought he had given it *fatḥa*.

{L|One that burdened your back|al-wizra lladhī anqaḍa ẓahraka} that is, made it creak from its weight (*al-naqīḍ* is the sound of cracking and coming apart). It is a likeness of what weighed on the Messenger of God ﷺ and grieved him: his lapses before prophethood; or his ignorance of the rulings and laws; or his wearing himself out over the Islam of the obstinate among his people, and his longing for it. Removing it from him is that He forgave him; or taught him the laws; or made his excuse plain after he had delivered [the message] and done his utmost. Anas read *wa-ḥalalnā* and *wa-ḥaṭaṭnā*; Ibn Masʿūd read *wa-ḥalalnā ʿanka wiqraka*.

{L|We exalted you to an honorable position|wa-rafaʿnā laka dhikrak} raising his mention is that it is joined with the mention of God in the word of witness, the call to prayer, the *iqāma*, the *tashahhud* and the sermons, and in more than one place in the Qurʾān: “*GOD and His messenger are more worthy of pleasing*” (Q 9:62), “*Those who obey GOD and His messenger*” (Q 4:13), “*You shall obey GOD, and you shall obey the messenger*” (Q 5:92); and in his being named the Messenger of God and the Prophet of God. Part of it is his mention in the books of the ancients, and the covenant taken from the prophets and their communities that they would believe in him.

{Q} What benefit is there in adding *laka* (“for you”), when the meaning stands without it? {A} Adding *laka* has what is in the way of {p|771}vagueness and then making clear. It is as if it were said “Did We not expand for you?”, and it is understood that something was expanded; then it was said *ṣadraka* (“your breast”), making clear what was known vaguely. So too *laka dhikrak* (“for you your mention”) and *ʿanka wizrak* (“from you your burden”).

{Q} How does His saying “*With pain there is gain*” (*fa-inna maʿa l-ʿusri yusrā*, “for with hardship is ease”) connect with what comes before? {A} The idolaters used to taunt the Messenger of God ﷺ and the believers with poverty and want, until it crossed his mind that they turned away from Islam because its people were poor and despised. So He reminded him of the great favors He had given him, then said “*With pain there is gain*,” as if He said: We have given you what We have given you, so do not despair of God’s bounty, for with the hardship you are in there is ease.

{Q} *Maʿa* (“with”) is for accompanying; so what does it mean for ease to accompany hardship? {A} He meant that God would bring them ease after the hardship they were in, within a short time. So He brought the awaited ease near, until He made it as though joined to the hardship, to add to the consolation and strengthen hearts.

{Q} What is the meaning of the saying of Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Masʿūd ﵄, “*One hardship will not overcome two eases*,” when it is also related as the Prophet’s own words, that he ﷺ went out one day laughing and saying, “*One hardship will not overcome two eases*”? {A} This is acting on the outward sense and building on strong hope, and on the fact that God’s promise is to be taken only in the fullest and furthest sense the wording will bear. The account of it is this. The second sentence may be a repetition of the first, as He repeated “*Woe on that day to the rejectors*” (Q 77:15) to fix its meaning in souls and settle it in hearts, and as the single word is repeated in your saying “Zayd came to me, Zayd.” Or the first may be a promise that hardship is followed by ease without fail, and the second a fresh promise that hardship is followed by ease; then they are two eases, on the reading of a fresh start. The hardship is one only because either its article is that of the known, the hardship they were in, so it is the same one, its case being that of Zayd in your saying “With Zayd is wealth; with Zayd is wealth”; or it is that of the kind that everyone knows, so it is the same one too. But “*ease*” is indefinite and takes in a part of the kind; so if the second statement is a fresh start and not a repetition, it takes in a part other than the first, without doubt.

{Q} Then what is meant by the two eases? {A} They may mean {p|772}the conquests made easy for them in the days of the Messenger of God ﷺ and those made easy for them in the days of the caliphs; or ease in this world and ease in the Hereafter, as in the saying of God Most High “*You can only expect for us one of two good things*” (Q 9:52), which are the best of victory and the best of reward.

{Q} Then what does this indefiniteness mean? {A} Making great: as if it were said, “with hardship is a great ease, and what an ease!” In the copy of Ibn Masʿūd it is there only once.

{Q} If it is established that in his reading it is not repeated, why did he say, “*By Him in whose hand is my soul, if hardship were in a burrow, ease would seek it out until it came in on it; one hardship will not overcome two eases*”? {A} It is as if by the two eases he meant the sense of greatness in His saying “*ease*,” and took it as the ease of the two abodes, and that is two eases in truth.

{Q} How does His saying “*Whenever possible you shall strive*” (*fa-idhā faraghta fa-nṣab*, “so when you are free, toil”) connect with what comes before? {A} When He had counted over to him His past favors and His promise of what is to come, He urged him to give thanks and to strive in worship and toil at it, joining one act to the next, following on and taking care not to leave any of his time empty of it: when he finishes one act of worship, he should follow it with another. From Ibn ʿAbbās: when you have finished your prayer, strive in supplication. From al-Ḥasan: when you have finished fighting, strive in worship. From Mujāhid: when you have finished with your worldly affairs, toil in your prayer. From al-Shaʿbī: he saw a man lifting a stone and said, “*That is not what the one who is free was commanded*.” A man’s sitting idle with no occupation, or busying himself with what does not concern him in his religion or his world, comes of foolish judgment, a feeble mind and the mastery of heedlessness. ʿUmar ﵁ said: “*I hate to see one of you idle and slack, at work neither for this world nor for the Hereafter*.” Abū l-Sammāl read *farighta*, with *kasra* on the *rāʾ*, and it is not eloquent. Among the innovations is what is related of one of the Rāfiḍa, that he read *fa-nṣib*, with *kasra* on the *ṣād*, that is, “then appoint ʿAlī to the imamate.” Were this sound for the Rāfiḍī, it would be sound for the Nāṣibī to read it so and make it a command to *naṣb*, which is hating ʿAlī and being his enemy.

{L|Seeking only your Lord|wa-ilā rabbika fa-rghab} make your longing for Him alone, and ask for nothing but His bounty, relying on Him. *Fa-rghib* is also read: that is, make people long to seek what is with Him.

{From|the Prophet ﷺ} “*Whoever recites ‘Did We not expand,’ it is as if he came to me while I was grieved and relieved me*.”
