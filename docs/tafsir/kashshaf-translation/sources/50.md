What is to be said on “*Q., and the glorious Quran. They found it strange*” is like what is said on “*S. (Saad), and the Quran that contains the proof. Those who disbelieve*” (Q 38:1–2), exactly alike, since the two meet in one manner of speech. *Al-majīd* is the one with glory and honor above the other books. Whoever has encompassed its meanings in knowledge and acts on what is in it is glorious with God and with people; and it comes from God the Glorious, so it may properly be described with His attribute.

{L|They found it strange|bal ʿajibū} His saying “*that a warner from among them came to them*” disowns their wondering at what is no wonder: that a man from among them, whose standing among them and whose uprightness and trustworthiness they knew, should warn them of what is to be feared. One of his kind can only be a sincere counselor to his people, hovering over them in care, afraid that harm might reach them and something hateful befall them; and when he knows that something to be feared hangs over them, he is bound to {p|380}warn them and caution them, so how much more with what is the utmost of fears and the last of dangers. It also disowns their wondering at what he warned them of, the raising, despite their knowing God Most High’s power to create the heavens and the earth and what is between them, and to originate and devise everything, and their acknowledging the first making, and despite the witness of reason that there must be a repayment. Then He leaned on one of the two disownings with the saying of God Most High “*The disbelievers said, ‘This is really strange. After we die…’*”, to show that their wondering at the raising goes further in deeming it unlikely and more deserves to be disowned. He put “*the unbelievers*” in place of the pronoun to bear witness that in this saying of theirs they are venturing on great unbelief. “*This*” points to the return.

*Idhā* (“when”) is in the accusative through an implied verb, meaning: when we die and decay, shall we return? “*This is impossible*” (*dhālika rajʿun baʿīd*): unlikely and rejected, as you say “This is a far-fetched saying” and “So-and-so has gone far in his saying”; its meaning is far from imagination and custom. *Al-rajʿ* may also be in the sense of what is returned, which is the answer; then it is the speech of God Most High, deeming unlikely their denial of the raising they were warned of, and on this explanation the pause before it is good. *Idhā mitnā* is also read in the form of a statement; its meaning is “when we have died we shall return,” and what points to it is “*this is impossible*.”

{Q} Then what governs the adverb, if *al-rajʿ* is in the sense of what is returned? {A} What the warner points to of what he warns of, which is the raising.

What is meant may also be: and the people of Mecca roamed, in their journeys and travels, the lands of those generations; {p|391}and did they see any escape for them, that they might hope for the like for themselves? The proof that this is sound is the reading of those who read *fa-naqqibū*, as a command, as in the saying of God Most High “*roam the earth freely*” (Q 9:2). *Fa-naqibū* is also read, with *kasra* on the *qāf* and without doubling, from *al-naqab*, which is when the camel’s pad wears through. He said: “*No worn pad has touched her, nor any galled back*.” The meaning: the pads of their camels wore through; or their feet grew sore and worn through, as camels’ pads wear through, from their much going about in the lands. “*Did they find an escape?*”: from God, or from death.

::: passage 50:37-37 | 4:391-392

{L|For everyone who possesses a mind|li-man kāna lahu qalbun} that is, a heart that takes in, because one whose heart does not take in is as if he had no heart. Giving ear is listening closely. “*Witness*” (*shahīd*): that is, present with his understanding, because one whose mind is not present is as if absent. The imam ʿAbd al-Qāhir put it wittily to one of those who learned from him: “*All the humming you like, while the young man is at Muṣqalābādh watering the crops*.” {p|392}Or: and he is a believer bearing witness that it is sound and that it is revelation from God. Or: and he is one of the witnesses in the saying of God Most High “*that you may serve as witnesses among the people*” (Q 2:143). From Qatāda: and he is one of the people of the Book bearing witness to its truth, because he finds its description with him. Al-Suddī and a group read *ulqiya l-samʿu*, in the passive; its meaning is: for one to whom another lent his hearing and opened his ear, no more, and who did not bring his mind, while he is present in mind and alert. It is also said: he gave his hearing, or the hearing was given by him.

::: passage 50:38-38 | 4:392-392

*Al-lughūb* is weariness. It is also read with *fatḥa* (*laghūb*), on the pattern of *al-qabūl* and *al-walūʿ*. It is said that it was revealed about the Jews, may they be cursed, giving the lie to their saying: God created the heavens and the earth in six days, the first of them Sunday and the last Friday, and rested on the Sabbath and lay back on the Throne. They have said that the likening [of God to creatures] that occurred in this community came only from the Jews and was taken from them.

::: passage 50:39-43 | 4:392-393

{L|Therefore, be patient in the face of their utterances|fa-ṣbir ʿalā mā yaqūlūna} that is, the Jews, and the unbelief and likening they bring. It is also said: so bear patiently what the idolaters say in denying the raising, for whoever had power to create the world has power to raise them and take revenge on them. It is also said that it is abrogated by the verse of the sword; it is also said that patience is commanded in every state. “*Praise and glorify your Lord*” (*sabbiḥ bi-ḥamdi rabbika*): glorify, praising your Lord. The glorifying is taken in its plain sense, or as the prayer. So the prayer “*before sunrise*” is the dawn prayer; “*and before sunset*,” the noon and afternoon prayers; “*During the night*,” the two night prayers; it is also said, the night vigil. “*And after prostrating*”: glorifying after the prayers; prostration and bowing are both used to mean the prayer. It is also said: the supererogatory prayers after the prescribed ones. From ʿAlī ﵁: the two *rakʿa*s after the sunset prayer. It is related from the Prophet ﷺ: “*Whoever prays after the sunset prayer before he speaks, his prayer is written in ʿIlliyyūn*.” From Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄: {p|393}the *witr* after the night prayer. *Al-adbār* is the plural of *dubur*. *Wa-idbāra* is also read, from *adbarati l-ṣalātu*, when the prayer is over and done; its meaning is “and at the time the prostrations end,” as in their saying “I will come to you at the setting of the star.”

{L|Prepare|wa-stamiʿ} literally, “and listen.” He means: and listen to what I tell you of the state of the Day of Resurrection. In that is a making terrible and great of what is told and spoken of, as it is related of the Prophet ﷺ that he said to Muʿādh ibn Jabal for seven days, “*Muʿādh, listen to what I say to you*,” and then told him after that.

{Q} What puts “*the day*” in the accusative? {A} What “*that is the day you come out*” points to: that is, on the day the caller calls, they come forth from the graves. “*When they hear*” is a substitute for “*the day when the caller calls*.” The caller is Isrāfīl, who blows the Trumpet and calls: “*Decayed bones, severed joints, torn flesh and scattered hairs: God commands you to gather for the passing of judgment*.” It is also said that Isrāfīl blows and Gabriel calls to the Gathering. “*From a place that is near*”: from the Rock of Jerusalem, which is the nearest of the earth to the sky by twelve miles, and is the middle of the earth. It is also said: from under their feet; and also: from the roots of their hair, heard from every hair: “*Decayed bones…*” “*the inevitable cry*” is the second blast. *Bi-l-ḥaqq* (“with the truth”) is attached to “*the inevitable cry*,” and what is meant by it is the raising and the gathering for the repayment.

::: passage 50:44-44 | 4:393-393

*Tashshaqqaqu* is also read, with the *tāʾ* assimilated to the *shīn*; and *tushaqqaqu*, in the passive; and *tanshaqqu*. “*Hastening*” is a circumstantial clause to the governed pronoun. “*Easy for Us*”: putting the adverbial phrase first shows that it is reserved; He means that so great a thing is easy only for the One able in His essence, whom no affair distracts from another, as God Most High said: “*The creation and resurrection of all of you is the same as that of one person*” (Q 31:28).

{L|We are fully aware of everything they utter|naḥnu aʿlamu bi-mā yaqūlūna} a threat to them and consolation for the Messenger of God ﷺ. “*You have no power over them*” (*bi-jabbār*): as in the saying of God Most High “*You have no power over them*” (Q 88:22), where the word is *muṣayṭir*, that you should force them into faith; you are only one who calls and urges. It is also said: what is meant is forbearing with them and leaving harshness toward them. It may also be from *jabarahu ʿalā l-amr* in the sense of *ajbarahu ʿalayhi* (“he forced him to it”): that is, you are not {p|394}a governor over them, forcing them into faith. *ʿAlā* is as in your saying “he is over them,” when he is their governor and holds their affairs. “*Those who reverence My warnings*”: as in the saying of God Most High “*Your mission is to warn those who expect it*” (Q 79:45), because it profits only him, not the one who persists in unbelief.
