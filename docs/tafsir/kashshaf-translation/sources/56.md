{L|When the inevitable comes to pass|idhā waqaʿati l-wāqiʿatu} like your saying, “The thing that was came to be,” and “The happening happened.” What is meant is the Resurrection. It is described as befalling because it will befall without fail; as though it were said, “When that which must befall befalls.” The befalling of a matter is its coming down; one says, “What I was expecting has befallen,” that is, what I was watching for has come down.

{Q} What puts *idhā* in the accusative? {A} *Laysa*, as in your saying, “On Friday I have no business.” Or something left out: He means, when it befalls, such-and-such will be. Or an implied “remember.”

{L|Nothing can stop it|kādhibatun} a soul that belies: that is, when it befalls there will be no soul that lies against God, or lies in giving the lie to the unseen, because every soul then will be believing, truthful and confirming, while most souls today are lying and giving the lie; as in the saying of God Most High “*when they saw our retribution they said, ‘Now we believe in GOD ALONE’*” (Q 40:84), “*they cannot believe in it; not until they see the painful retribution*” (Q 26:201), and “*As for those who disbelieve, they will continue to harbor doubts until the Hour comes to them suddenly*” (Q 22:55). The *lām* is like that in the saying of God Most High “*Oh, I wish I prepared for my (eternal) life*” (Q 89:24). Or: it has no soul that gives it the lie and says to it, “You will not be,” as today it has many souls that give it the lie and say to it, “You will never be.” Or it is from their saying *kadhabat fulānan nafsuhu fī l-khaṭbi l-ʿaẓīm*, when his soul encourages him to take on a great matter and says to him, “You can bear it and more, so face it and pay it no heed”; in the sense that it is an event that cannot be borne for its severity and horror, and no soul then will tell its owner what it tells him at great affairs, making fair to him that he can carry and bear them, because on that day they will be weaker and more abased than that. Do you not see the saying of God Most High “*like swarms of butterflies*” (Q 101:4)? And moths are a byword for weakness. It is also said that *kādhiba* is a verbal noun like *al-ʿāqiba*, in the sense of giving the lie, from your saying *ḥamala ʿalā qirnihi fa-mā kadhaba*, that is, he charged at his match and did not shrink or hold back. Its real sense is: {p|456}he did not belie himself in what it told him of his being able for it and going forward to it. Zuhayr said: “*… when the lion belies [itself] before its matches, he holds true*.” That is: when it befalls it has no turning back and no going back.

{L|It will lower some, and raise others|khāfiḍatun rāfiʿatun} as in “it is lowering, raising,” raising some peoples and putting down others. This is either describing it as severe, because great events are like that, some people rising in them to ranks and some people being put down; or because the wretched will be brought down to the lower levels and the happy raised to the high ranks; or because it will shake things and move them from their places, lowering some and raising others, since the sky will fall in pieces, the stars will be scattered and dim, and the mountains will move and pass through the air as clouds pass. *Khāfiḍatan rāfiʿatan* is also read, in the accusative as a circumstantial clause.

{L|The earth will be shaken up|rujjat} is moved with a violent moving, until everything on it, mountain and building, is thrown down. “*The mountains will be wiped out*” (*wa-bussati l-jibālu bassan*): they are broken to pieces until they become like barley meal; or they are driven, from *bassa l-ghanama* when he drove the sheep, as in His saying “*The mountains will be removed*” (Q 78:20). “*As if they never existed*” (*habāʾan munbaththan*, “scattered dust”): dispersed. It is also read with *tāʾ* (*munbattan*), that is, cut off. *Rajjat* and *bassat*, in the active, are also read, that is, it shook and went away; and in the speech of the daughter of al-Khuss: “*Her eye is sunken, her haunches shake, and she walks with her legs wide apart*.”

{Q} What puts “*The earth will be shaken up*” in the accusative? {A} It is a substitute for “*When the inevitable comes to pass*.” It may also be in the accusative through “*It will lower some, and raise others*”: that is, it lowers and raises at the time of the shaking of the earth and the crumbling of the mountains, because at that what is high is lowered and what is low is raised. “*Three kinds*” (*azwājan*, “pairs”): kinds. Kinds that are with one another, or that are mentioned with one another, are called pairs.

{L|Those who deserved bliss|fa-aṣḥābu l-maymanati} those who are given their records in their right hands; “*Those who deserved misery*” (*al-mashʾama*), those who are given them in their left hands. Or the people of the high rank and the people of the low rank, from your saying, “So-and-so is on my right,” “So-and-so is on my left,” when you describe them as high and low with you. That is because they took the right as lucky and the left as unlucky, {p|457}and took good omen from what passes from left to right and evil omen from what passes from right to left. For that they derived the name for the right hand from luck (*al-yumn*) and called the left hands *al-shuʾmā*. It is also said: the companions of the right and the companions of the left are the people of good fortune and of ill fortune, because the happy bring good fortune on themselves by their obedience, and the wretched bring ill fortune on themselves by their disobedience. It is also said: the people of Paradise will be taken to the right and the people of the Fire to the left. “*Those who deserved bliss will be in bliss. Those who deserved misery will be in misery*” (*mā aṣḥābu l-maymana … mā aṣḥābu l-mashʾama*, “what are the companions of the right … of the left?”): making wonder at the state of the two parties in happiness and wretchedness.

{L|The elite of the elite|wa-l-sābiqūna} the sincere, who went ahead to what God called them to and pressed through the dust in seeking the pleasure of God, mighty and majestic is He. It is also said: people are three. A man who began good early, in his youth, then kept at it until he left the world: this is the foremost, the one brought near. A man who began his life with sin and long heedlessness, then turned back in repentance: this is the companion of the right. And a man who began evil early, in his youth, then did not cease in it until he left the world: this is the companion of the left. {p|458}The meaning is: what are they?

“*Then there is the elite of the elite*” (*wa-l-sābiqūna l-sābiqūn*): He means, and the foremost are those whose state you know and whose description has reached you; as in his saying, “And ʿAbd Allāh is ʿAbd Allāh,” and the saying of Abū l-Najm, “*and my poetry is my poetry*,” as though he said: and my poetry is what has reached you and whose eloquence and excellence you have heard of. The second “*foremost*” has been made an emphasis and “*those are the ones brought near*” the predicate, but that is not good. Some paused at “*and the foremost*” and began, “*The foremost, those are the ones brought near*”; what is right is to pause at the second, because it completes the sentence, and it stands against “*What are the companions of the right?*” and “*What are the companions of the left?*”

{L|They are those who will be closest (to GOD). In the gardens of bliss|al-muqarrabūna fī jannāti l-naʿīmi} those whose ranks in Paradise have been brought near to the Throne and whose stations have been raised. *Fī jannati l-naʿīmi* is also read. *Al-thulla* is a large group of people. He said: “*And a Khindifī throng came to them with an army like a foaming torrent*.” The saying of God, mighty and majestic is He, “*Few from the later generations*” is enough to show that it means many. It is from *al-thall*, which is breaking, as *al-umma* is from *al-amm*, which is wounding the head; as though it were a company broken off from people and cut from them. The meaning is that the foremost of the earlier are many, and they are the communities from the time of Adam ﵇ to Muḥammad ﷺ; “*Few from the later generations*,” and they are the community of Muḥammad ﷺ. It is also said: “*of the earlier*,” of the early ones of this community, and “*from the later generations*,” of its later ones. From the Prophet ﷺ: “*The two throngs are both of my community*.”

{Q} How did He say “*Few from the later generations*,” and then say “*Many from the later generations*” (Q 56:40)? {A} {p|459}This is about the foremost, and that about the companions of the right, and that they will be many from the earlier and the later together.

{Q} It is related that when it was revealed it was hard on the Muslims, and the Messenger of God ﷺ kept going back to his Lord until “*a throng of the earlier and a throng of the later*” was revealed. {A} This is not sound, for two reasons. One: this verse plainly comes about the foremost, and the second likewise about the companions of the right; do you not see how He coordinated the companions of the right and their promise on the foremost and their promise? The second: abrogation in reports is not allowed. From al-Ḥasan ﵁: the foremost of the communities are more than the foremost of our community, and the followers of the communities are like the followers of this community. *Thulla* is the predicate of a left-out subject: that is, they are a throng.

{L|On luxurious furnishings|mawḍūnatin} woven with gold, latticed with pearls and rubies, worked into one another as the rings of a coat of mail are woven. Al-Aʿshā said: “*And of David’s weaving, close-linked*.” It is also said: joined, set close to one another. “*Reclining*” is a circumstantial clause to the pronoun in “*on*,” which is what governs it: that is, they are settled on them, reclining. “*They will be neighbors*” (*mutaqābilīn*, “facing one another”): none looks at the back of another’s head. They are described with good fellowship and refined character and manners.

{L|Immortal|mukhalladūna} kept forever in the form of boys, at the age of service, not changing from it. It is also said: wearing earrings, *al-khalada* being the earring. It is also said: they are the children of the people of this world, who had no good deeds to be rewarded for and no evil deeds to be punished for; this is related from ʿAlī ﵁ and from al-Ḥasan. In the ḥadīth: “*The children of the unbelievers are the servants of the people of Paradise*.” *Al-akwāb* are vessels without handles or spouts, {p|460}and *al-abārīq* are those with spouts.

{L|They never run out, nor do they get bored|lā yuṣaddaʿūna ʿanhā} that is, because of it; its real sense is that their headache does not issue from it. Or: they are not parted from it. Mujāhid read *lā yaṣṣaddaʿūna*, in the sense of *lā yataṣaddaʿūna*, they do not part, as in His saying “*On that day, they will be shocked*” (*yaṣṣaddaʿūna*, Q 30:43); and *yuṣdiʿūna*, that is, they do not part one another, do not split them up. “*Of their choice*”: they take its best and finest. “*They desire*”: they wish for. *Wa-luḥūmi ṭayrin* is also read.

*Wa-ḥūrun ʿīnun* is read in the nominative, as in “and in it are wide-eyed houris,” like the verse of *the Book*: “*… only the firm-set [hearthstones], their embers dust, and a [tent peg] driven in*…”; or by coordination on “*boys*.” And in the genitive, by coordination on “*the gardens of bliss*,” as though He said: they are in the gardens of bliss, and fruit, and meat, and houris; or on “*cups*,” because the meaning of “*Serving them will be immortal servants. With cups*” is that they are given the bliss of cups. And in the accusative, as in “and they are given houris.” “*As repayment*” is an object of purpose: that is, all that is done to them as repayment for their works.

{L|Peace, peace|salāman salāman} either a substitute for “*a saying*,” as shown by His saying “*They will not hear any nonsense therein; only peace*” (Q 19:62), or an object of “*a saying*,” in the sense: they hear in it only that they say “Peace, peace.” The meaning is that they spread peace among themselves, greeting with peace after peace. *Salāmun salāmun* is also read, as a report of the words.

{L|Extended shade|wa-ẓillin mamdūdin} stretched and spread, not shrinking, like the shade between the break of dawn and the rising of the sun. “*Abundant water*” (*māʾin maskūb*, “poured water”): poured for them wherever they wish and however they wish, not tiring themselves over it. It is also said: flowing always, not cut off. It is also said: poured out, running over the ground with no channel. “*Never ending*”: it lasts, not cut off at some times like the fruits of this world; “*never forbidden*”: not kept from the one who reaches for it in any way, and not fenced off as the orchards of this world are fenced off. *Wa-fākihatun kathīratun* is also read, in the nominative, as in “and there is fruit,” like “*and wide-eyed houris*.”

{L|Luxurious furnishings|wa-furushin} plural of *firāsh*. *Wa-furshin*, light, is also read. “*Luxurious*” (*marfūʿa*, “raised”): layered until they were raised high; or raised on the thrones. {L|We create for them mates|innā anshaʾnāhunna inshāʾan} that is, We began their creation with a new beginning, without birth. Either those whose making was begun are meant, or those whose making was made anew. From the Messenger of God ﷺ: Umm Salama ﵂ asked him about the saying of God Most High “*We create for them mates*,” and he said: “*Umm Salama, {p|462}they are those who were taken in the world old women, grey-haired and bleary-eyed; God made them [virgins] after old age*.” “*Perfectly matched*” (*atrāban*): as though of one birth in evenness of form. Whenever their husbands come to them they find them virgins. When ʿĀʾisha ﵂ heard that from the Messenger of God ﷺ she said, “*Oh, the pain!*” and the Messenger of God ﷺ said, “*There is no pain there*.” An old woman said to the Messenger of God ﷺ, “*Pray to God to bring me into Paradise*.” He said, “*Old women do not enter Paradise*.” She turned away weeping, and he ﵊ said, “*Tell her that on that day she will not be an old woman*,” and he recited the verse.

{L|Perfectly matched|ʿuruban} *ʿurban*, light, is also read; plural of *ʿarūb*, which is the woman who makes herself loved by her husband, good at being a wife. *Atrāban* (“*Perfectly matched*”): equal in age, thirty-three years old, and their husbands likewise. From the Messenger of God ﷺ: “*The people of Paradise will enter Paradise hairless, beardless, white, curly-haired, their eyes lined with kohl, thirty-three years old*.” The *lām* in “*For those on the right side*” belongs to “*We create*” and to *fa-jaʿalnāhunna*.

{p|463}{L|In misery and inferno|fī samūmin} in the heat of a fire that gets into the pores; “*and boiling water*,” hot water at the end of heat. “*Even their shade is hot*” (*wa-ẓillin min yaḥmūm*): of black, dark smoke. “*Never cool, never tolerable*”: a denial to it of the two descriptions of shade. He means that it is shade, but not like other shades. He called it shade, then denied it the coolness of shade and its refreshment and its benefit to one who takes refuge in it from the hurt of heat, which is its generosity, so as to wipe out what is in the sense of shade of taking rest in it. The meaning is that it is a hot, harmful shade; only, denying has a force in such cases that affirming does not. In it is mockery of the companions of the left, and that they do not deserve the cool, generous shade that belongs to their opposites in Paradise. *Lā bāridun wa-lā karīmun* is also read, in the nominative, that is, it is not so.

{L|The great blasphemy|al-ḥinthi} the great sin. From it is their saying “the boy has reached *al-ḥinth*,” that is, puberty and the time of being taken to account for sins. From it is *ḥanitha fī yamīnihi* (“he broke his oath”), the opposite of *barra fīhā* (“he kept it”). And one says *taḥannatha* when one keeps from sin and holds back.

{L|Does this include our forefathers?|a-wa-ābāʾunā} the *hamza* of the question has entered on the particle of coordination.

{L|To a meeting on a predetermined day|ilā mīqāti yawmin maʿlūmin} to what the world has been timed by, of a known day. The annexation is in the sense of *min* (“of”), like “a ring of silver.” *Al-mīqāt* is that by which a thing is timed, that is, bounded. From it are the stations of consecration, the bounds that one who wants to enter Mecca may not pass except in consecration. “*You who stray*” from guidance, “*who give the lie*” to the raising: they are the people of Mecca and those in their state.

{L|From the trees of bitterness|min shajarin min zaqqūmin} the first *min* is for the start of the reach, and the second is to explain the trees. He made the pronoun of “*trees*” feminine following the sense, and masculine following the wording in His saying “*from it*” and “*on it*.” Whoever reads *min shajaratin min zaqqūmin* has made both pronouns for the tree, and made the second masculine only by taking it as *al-zaqqūm*, because that explains it and it has its sense.

{L|Drinks of sand|shurba l-hīmi} read with all three vowels. The *fatḥa* and the *ḍamma* are verbal nouns; from Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq ﵁: “*Days of eating and drinking*” (*shurb*), with *fatḥa* on the *shīn*. The one with *kasra* is in the sense of what is drunk, that is, what the *hīm* drink. They are the camels that have *huyām*, which is a disease from which they drink and are not quenched; plural of *ahyam* and *haymāʾ*. Dhū l-Rumma said: {p|464}“*So I became like the thirsting she-camel: the water does not cool her thirst, nor does her thirst-sickness finish her off*.” It is also said: *al-hīm* is sands. Its way is that it is the plural of *al-hayām*, with *fatḥa* on the *hāʾ*, which is sand that does not hold together, gathered on the pattern of *fuʿul*, like *saḥāb* and *suḥub*; then it was lightened, and treated as the plural of *abyaḍ* is treated. The meaning is that hunger will be set on them that forces them to eat the Zaqqūm, which is like molten metal; and when they have filled their bellies with it, thirst will be set on them that forces them to drink the boiling water that cuts their bowels, and they will drink it as the thirsting camels drink.

{Q} How was it sound to coordinate the drinkers on the drinkers, when they are the same persons and two like descriptions, so that it would be coordinating a thing on itself? {A} They are not alike, in that their being drinkers of boiling water as it is, at the end of heat and cutting the bowels, is a wondrous thing; and their drinking it, as it is, as thirsting camels drink water, is a wondrous thing too. So they are two different descriptions.

{L|Their share|nuzuluhum} *al-nuzul* is the provision made ready for the guest, to honor him. In it is mockery, as in the saying of God Most High “*promise them a painful retribution*” (Q 3:21), and as in the saying of Abū l-Shiʿr al-Ḍabbī: “*When the tyrant came to us as a guest with his army, we made the spears and the keen blades his hospitality*.” *Nuzluhum*, light, is also read.

{L|The water you drink|al-māʾa lladhī tashrabūna} He means fresh water fit for drinking. *Al-muzn* is the clouds, the singular being *muzna*; it is also said, the white clouds in particular, which are the freshest water. “*Salty*” (*ujājan*): salt and bitter, which cannot be drunk.

{Q} Why did you bring the *lām* into the answer of *law* in His saying “*we can turn it into hay*” and take it out of it here? {A} Since *law* comes in over two sentences, the second of them hanging on the first as the repayment hangs on the condition, and it is not purely for condition as *in* is, nor governing as it does, but the sense of condition passed into it by chance, in that it conveys in what the two sentences contain that the second was held back because the first was held back, it needed in its answer something set up as a sign of this hanging. So this *lām* was added to be a sign of that. Then if it is dropped after it has become a well-known sign of its place, it is because when a thing is known and its place is well known and it has become familiar and accustomed, one does not care if it is dropped from the wording, the hearer’s knowing it being enough. Do you not see what is told of Ruʾba, that he used to say “Well” to one who said to him, “How are you this morning?” He dropped the preposition because everyone knows its place, and dropping it and keeping it were alike because its matter was well known. And enough for you is the saying of Aws: “*Until, when the master of the hounds said to it, ‘Like today, neither a sought one nor a seeker*,’” {p|467}and his dropping “I have not seen.” So dropping it is a shortening in the wording, while it stands in the meaning, and the two places are alike with no difference between them; besides, its mention just before, at a short distance, makes mentioning it a second time unnecessary and stands in for it.

It may also be said that this *lām* certainly conveys the sense of emphasis, so it was brought into the verse on food and not into the verse on drink, to show that the matter of food comes before the matter of drink, and that the threat of losing it is harder and more severe, because drink is needed only following food. Do you not see that you give your guest drink only after you have fed him? And if you reversed it you would fall under the saying of Abū l-ʿAlāʾ: “*When people’s guests are given pure milk to drink, they give their guests cold, clear water*.” One of the Arabs was given drink and said, “I drink only on top of some food.” For this the verse on food was put before the verse on drink.

{L|The fire you ignite|tūrūna} you strike it and draw it out of the fire-sticks. The Arabs strike fire with two sticks, rubbing one on the other; they call the upper one *al-zand* and the lower one *al-zanda*, likening them to the stallion and the mare. “*Its tree*”: the one from which the fire-sticks come. “*A reminder*”: a reminding of the fire of Hell, since We tied to it all the means of living and made the need for it general, so that it might be present to people, who look at it and remember what they were threatened with. Or: We made it a reminder and a sample of Hell, because of what is related from the Messenger of God ﷺ: “*This fire of yours that the children of Adam kindle is one part of seventy parts of the heat of Hell*.” “*a useful tool*” (*matāʿan*): a benefit “*for the users*” (*al-muqwīna*), those who stop in the *qawāʾ*, which is the empty desert; or those whose bellies or provision bags are empty of food. One says, “I have been empty for {p|468}days,” that is, I have eaten nothing.

{L|You shall glorify the name of your Lord|fa-sabbiḥ bi-smi rabbika} so bring about glorifying by mentioning the name of your Lord; or by “the name” He meant the mention: that is, by mentioning your Lord. “*The Mighty*” is a description of the annexed word or of the word annexed to. The meaning is that when He had mentioned what points to His power and His favor to His servants, He said: so bring about glorifying, which is to say “Glory be to God,” either to exalt Him above what the wrongdoers say who deny His oneness and are ungrateful for His favor, or in wonder at their matter in belittling His favors and His plain kindnesses, or in thanks to God for the favors He counted up and drew attention to.

{L|I swear|fa-lā uqsimu} its meaning is “so I swear,” and *lā* is extra and emphatic, like that in His saying “*the followers of previous scripture should know*” (Q 57:29). Al-Ḥasan read *fa-la-uqsimu*, and its meaning is “so surely I swear.” The *lām* is the *lām* of the subject, entering on a sentence of subject and predicate, which is “I swear,” as in your saying *la-zaydun munṭaliqun* (“surely Zayd is leaving”); then the subject was dropped. The *lām* cannot be the *lām* of the oath, for two reasons. One: its due is to have the emphatic *nūn* joined with it, and leaving it out is weak and ugly. The second: *la-afʿalanna* in the answer of an oath is for the future, while the verb of swearing must be for the present.

{L|By the positions of the stars|bi-mawāqiʿi l-nujūmi} by their falling places and their setting places. Perhaps at the end of the night, when the stars sink to the west, God Most High has particular, great acts, or the angels have described acts of worship; or because it is the time when those of His righteous servants who keep vigil and humbly pray to Him rise, and mercy and good pleasure come down on them. For that He swore by their settings, and made it great by His saying “*This is an oath, if you only knew, that is awesome*.” Or by their settings He meant their stations and their courses, and in that God Most High has proof of great power and wisdom that description cannot take in. His saying “*This is an oath, if you only knew, that is awesome*” is a parenthesis within a parenthesis, because He put it as a parenthesis between the oath and what is sworn, which is His saying “*This is an honorable Quran*,” and put “*if you only knew*” as a parenthesis between the described and its description. {p|469}It is also said: the settings of the stars are the times of the falling of the installments of the Qurʾān, that is, the times of their coming down.

{L|Honorable|karīmun} good, approved in its kind among books; or useful, of many uses; or noble with God. “*In a protected book*”: kept from all but the angels brought near; none but they look at it, and they are the purified from all defilements, the defilements of sins and others; this if you make the sentence a description of “*a protected book*,” which is the Tablet. If you make it a description of the Qurʾān, the meaning is that it is not fitting that any but those of people who are in a state of purity should touch it: He means touching what is written of it. Some people have taken it to apply to reciting as well. From Ibn ʿUmar: I prefer that one not recite except in a state of purity. From Ibn ʿAbbās, in one report, that he allowed reciting to one in major impurity. Like it is the saying of the Messenger of God ﷺ: “*The Muslim is the brother of the Muslim; he does not wrong him or betray him*”: that is, it is not fitting that he wrong him or betray him. *Al-mutaṭahhirūna* is also read; *al-muṭṭahhirūna*, with assimilation; *al-muṭhirūna*, from *aṭharahu* in the sense of *ṭahharahu*; and *al-muṭahhirūna*, in the sense of those who purify themselves, or others by asking forgiveness for them and by the revelation they bring down.

{L|A revelation|tanzīlun} a fourth description of the Qurʾān: that is, sent down from the Lord of the worlds. Or it is described with the verbal noun because it came down in installments among all of God Most High’s books, so it is as though it were in itself a sending down; and for that it has come to be used like one of its names, and it is said, “It has come in the Sending Down,” and “the Sending Down has spoken of it.” Or it is “it is a sending down,” with the subject dropped. *Tanzīlan* is also read, as in “it was sent down a sending down.”

{L|Are you disregarding this narration?|a-fa-bi-hādhā l-ḥadīthi} He means the Qurʾān. “*Are you disregarding*” (*mudhinūna*): that is, do you take it lightly, like one who is pliant in a matter, softening his side and not standing firm in it, from taking it lightly. “*Do you make it your business that you disbelieve?*” (*wa-tajʿalūna rizqakum annakum tukadhdhibūn*, “and you make your provision that you give the lie”): with an annexed word dropped. He means: and you make the thanks for your provision the giving of the lie; that is, you have put the giving of the lie in the place of thanks. ʿAlī ﵁ read: *wa-tajʿalūna shukrakum annakum tukadhdhibūna* (“and you make your thanks that you give the lie”); it is also said that it is the reading of the Messenger of God ﷺ. The meaning: and you make your thanks for the favor of the Qurʾān that you give it the lie. It is also said that it was revealed about the rain stars and their ascribing the rain to them. Provision is the rain. He means: and you make your thanks for the rain God provides you that you deny that it is from God, since you ascribe it to the stars. *Takdhibūna* is also read, which is their saying of the Qurʾān, “poetry, sorcery and invention,” and of the rain, “it is from the rain stars”; and because everyone who gives the lie to the truth is a liar.

{p|470}The order of the verse is: then why do you not bring it back, when it reaches the throat, if you are not to be judged? The second *fa-lawlā* is repeated for emphasis. The pronoun in “*restore (your soul)*” is for the soul, which is the spirit, and in “*closer to it*” for the dying man. “*You do not owe any accounting*” (*ghayra madīnīn*): not ruled as subjects, from *dāna l-sulṭānu l-raʿiyyata* when he governed the subjects. “*We are closer to it than you are*,” people of the dead man, by Our power and Our knowledge, or by the angels of death. The meaning: in your denial of the acts of God Most High and His signs in everything, if He sends down to you a miraculous book you say, “Sorcery and invention”; if He sends you a messenger you say, “A lying sorcerer”; and if He provides you with rain by which He gives you life you say, “Such-and-such a rain star was true,” on a doctrine that leads to leaving things unattended and to denying the Maker. So why do you not bring the spirit back to the body after it has reached the throat, if there is no one there who takes it, and if you are truthful in your denial and your unbelief in the One who gives life and death, who begins and brings back?

{L|And if he is|fa-ammā in kāna} the one who dies, “*one of those close to Me*”: of the foremost, of the three pairs mentioned at the start of the sūra, “*then joy*” (*fa-rawḥun*): he will have ease. ʿĀʾisha ﵂ related from the Messenger of God ﷺ: *fa-rūḥun*, with *ḍamma*; al-Ḥasan read it so and said: *al-rūḥ* is mercy, because it is like life to the one shown mercy. It is also said: lasting; that is, these two are his together, which are everlastingness with provision and bliss. *Al-rayḥān* is provision.

{L|Peace is the lot of those on the right|fa-salāmun laka min aṣḥābi l-yamīni} that is, peace to you, companion of the right, from your brothers, the companions of the right; that is, they greet you with peace, as in the saying of God Most High “*Only the utterance: ‘Peace, peace’*” (Q 56:26). “*Then an abode of inferno*” (*fa-nuzulun min ḥamīm*): as in the saying of God Most High “*Such is their share on the Day of Judgment*” (Q 56:56); it is also read light. “*And burning in Hell*”: read in the nominative and in the genitive, coordinated on “*hospitality*” and on “*boiling water*.” “*This*,” which was sent down in this sūra, “*the absolute truth*” (*ḥaqqu l-yaqīn*): that is, the firm truth that is of certainty.
