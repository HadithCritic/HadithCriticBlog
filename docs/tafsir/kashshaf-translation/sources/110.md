surah: 110
title: Sūrat al-Naṣr (110)
revelation: Revealed at Minā during the Farewell Pilgrimage, and so counted Medinan; it is the last sūra to be revealed. Three verses. [Revealed after al-Tawba (9).]

::: passage 110:1-3 | 4:810-813

{L|When triumph comes|idhā jāʾa} accusative through “*You shall glorify*,” and it refers to the future. Announcing this before it came about is one of the signs of prophethood. It is related that it was revealed during the days of Tashrīq at Minā in the Farewell Pilgrimage.

{Q} What is the difference between help (*naṣr*) and victory (*fatḥ*), that one is joined to the other? {A} *Al-naṣr* is relief and giving the upper hand over the enemy; from it comes *naṣara llāhu l-arḍ*, “God relieved the land,” that is, sent it rain. *Al-fatḥ* is the conquest of lands. The meaning is the help given to the Messenger of God ﷺ against the Arabs, or against Quraysh, and the conquest of Mecca. It is also said that it is God’s help to the believers in general and the opening of the lands of idolatry to them.

The conquest of Mecca was when ten days of Ramaḍān had passed in the year eight. With the Messenger of God ﷺ were ten thousand of the Emigrants, the Helpers and the Arab tribes. He stayed there fifteen nights and then went out against Hawāzin. When he entered it he stood at the door of the Kaʿba and said, “*There is no god but God alone, who has no partner. He has kept His promise, helped His servant, and routed the Confederates alone*.” Then he said, “*People of Mecca, what do you think I will do with you?*” They said, “*Good: a noble brother and the son of a noble brother*.” He said, “*Go, for you are free (al-ṭulaqāʾ)*.” So the Messenger of God ﷺ set them free, though God Most High had put their necks in his power by force and they were his spoils; and for that reason the people of Mecca were called al-Ṭulaqāʾ. Then they pledged allegiance to him on Islam.

{L|GOD’s religion|fī dīni llāh} into the community of Islam, beside which He has no religion to which His name is attached: “*Anyone who accepts other than Submission as his religion, it will not be accepted from him*” (Q 3:85).

{L|In throngs|afwājan} in thick throngs: a whole tribe would enter it at once, {p|811}after they had been entering it one by one and two by two. From Jābir ibn ʿAbd Allāh ﵁: he wept one day and was asked [about it]. He said, “*I heard the Messenger of God ﷺ say: ‘People have entered God’s religion in crowds, and they will leave it in crowds.’*” It is also said that by “*the people*” He meant the people of Yemen. Abū Hurayra said: when it was revealed, the Messenger of God ﷺ said, “*God is greatest! ‘God’s help and the victory have come,’ and the people of Yemen have come, a people whose hearts are tender. Faith is Yemeni, understanding is Yemeni, and wisdom is Yemeni*.” And he said, “*I find the relief of your Lord coming from the direction of Yemen*.” From al-Ḥasan: when the Messenger of God ﷺ conquered Mecca, the Arabs turned to one another and said, “*Now that he has overcome the people of the Sanctuary, no one has the strength to face him; God had protected them from the People of the Elephant and from everyone who meant them harm*.” So they entered Islam in crowds, without fighting.

Ibn ʿAbbās read *fatḥu llāhi wa-l-naṣr* (“God’s victory and the help”). *Yudkhalūna* (“they are brought in”) is also read, in the passive.

{Q} What is the position of “*embracing*” (*yadkhulūna*, “entering”)? {A} Accusative: either as a circumstantial clause, if *raʾayta* means “you saw” or “you recognized,” or as a second object, if it means “you knew.”

{L|You shall glorify and praise your Lord|fa-sabbiḥ bi-ḥamdi rabbika} say “Glory be to God,” praising Him. That is: marvel at God’s making easy what had never crossed your mind or anyone’s, that anyone should overcome the people of the Sanctuary, and praise Him for what He has done. Or: remember Him, glorifying and praising Him, adding to your worship of Him and your praise of Him for His added favor to you. Or: pray to Him. Umm Hāniʾ related that when the door of the Kaʿba was opened he prayed the forenoon prayer, eight *rakʿa*s. From ʿĀʾisha: before his death he ﵊ used often to say, “*Glory be to You, O God, and praise. I seek Your forgiveness and turn to You in repentance*.”

The command to seek forgiveness along with glorifying Him completes the command to what is the mainstay of the religion: joining obedience with guarding against {p|812}disobedience. It was also so that his being commanded to do it, though he was protected from sin, might be a kindness to his community; and because seeking forgiveness is humility before God and lowering the self, so it is an act of worship in itself. From the Prophet ﷺ: “*I seek forgiveness a hundred times in a day and a night*.”

It is related that when the Messenger of God ﵌ recited it to his Companions they rejoiced, but al-ʿAbbās wept. The Messenger of God ﷺ said, “*What makes you weep, uncle?*” He said, “*Your death has been announced to you*.” He said, “*It is as you say*.” He lived two years after it and was not seen laughing or rejoicing in them. It is also said that it was Ibn ʿAbbās who said that, and the Messenger of God ﷺ said, “*This boy has been given much knowledge*.”

It is related that when it was revealed the Messenger of God ﷺ preached and said, “*A servant was given the choice by God between this world and meeting Him, and he chose to meet God*.” Abū Bakr ﵁ understood and said, “*We would ransom you with ourselves, our wealth, our fathers and our children*.”

From Ibn ʿAbbās: ʿUmar ﵄ used to keep him close and admit him with the people of Badr. ʿAbd al-Raḥmān said, “*Do you admit this youth with us, when among our sons are those as old as he?*” He said, “*He is one whose standing you know*.” Ibn ʿAbbās said: one day he admitted them and admitted me with them, and he asked them about the saying of God Most High “*When God’s help comes*,” and I think he asked them only on my account. Some of them said, “*God commanded His Prophet, when victory was given him, to seek His forgiveness and turn to Him in repentance*.” I said, “*It is not so; rather, his death was announced to him*.” ʿUmar said, “*I know nothing of it but what you know*.” Then he said, “*How can you blame me for him, after what you see?*”

From the Prophet ﷺ: he called Fāṭima ﵂ and said, “*My daughter, my death has been announced to me*.” She wept, and he said, “*Do not weep, for you will be the first of my family to join me*.” From Ibn Masʿūd: this sūra is called the Sūra of Farewell (*al-Tawdīʿ*).

{L|He is the Redeemer|innahu kāna tawwāban} that is, in times past, ever since He created those under obligation, He has turned to them in forgiveness whenever they sought it; so everyone who seeks forgiveness should expect the same.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} {p|813}“*Whoever recites Sūrat idhā jāʾa naṣru llāh will be given a reward like one who was present with Muḥammad on the day of the conquest of Mecca*.”
