::: passage 87:1-5 | 4:737-738

The glorifying of His name, mighty and exalted is He, is declaring it free of meanings that are not sound in it and that are a deviation (*ilḥād*) in His names, such as compulsion {p|738}and likening Him to creatures and the like: for example, that *al-aʿlā* (“the Most High”) be explained as the highness that is overpowering and power, not as highness in place and actually settling on the Throne; and that it be kept from being cheapened and from being mentioned other than with humility and reverence. *Al-aʿlā* may be a description of the Lord, or of the name. ʿAlī ﵁ read: *subḥāna rabbiya l-aʿlā*. In the ḥadīth: when “*You shall glorify the name of your Lord, the Great*” (Q 56:74) was revealed, the Messenger of God ﷺ said, “*Put it in your bowing*”; and when “*Glorify the name of your Lord, the Most High*” was revealed, he said, “*Put it in your prostration*.” In bowing they used to say, “*O God, to You I have bowed*,” and in prostration, “*O God, to You I have prostrated*.”

{L|He creates and shapes|khalaqa fa-sawwā} that is, He created everything and proportioned its making, and did not bring it out uneven and ill-fitting, but firm and orderly, and as a sign that it comes from One who knows and is the work of One who is wise.

{L|He designs and guides|qaddara fa-hadā} He determined for every animal what is good for it, and guided it to it and taught it how to profit by it. It is told that when a viper has lived a thousand years it goes blind, and God has inspired it that rubbing its eyes on the tender leaves of fennel gives it back its sight. It may be in a wilderness with days of travel between it and the cultivated land, and it crosses all that distance, long as it is and blind as it is, until it comes upon a fennel plant in some garden without missing it, rubs its eyes on it, and goes back seeing, by God’s leave. God’s guiding of man to his countless benefits and needs, in his food and his medicines and in the affairs of his world and his religion, and His inspiring of the beasts, the birds and the creeping things of the earth, is a wide door and a long course that no describer can encompass. So glory be to my Lord, the Most High. *Qadara* is also read, without doubling.

{L|Light hay|aḥwā} a description of *ghuthāʾ* (“stubble”); that is, “*produces the pasture*,” made it grow, “*Then turns it*,” after its greenness and freshness, “*light hay*” (*ghuthāʾan aḥwā*, “dark stubble”): dry, old (*darīn*) and black. *Aḥwā* may also be a circumstantial clause to “*the pasture*”: He brought it out dark, black from the depth of its green and its moisture, then made it stubble after its darkness.

::: passage 87:6-7 | 4:738-739

God gave him the good news that he would be given a clear sign: that Gabriel would recite to him what he recites of the revelation, while he was unlettered, neither writing nor reading, and he would keep it and not forget it.

{L|Everything is in accordance with GOD’s will|illā mā shāʾa llāh} so that He takes it from his memory by lifting its ruling and its recitation, as in His saying “*or cause it to be forgotten*” (Q 2:106). It is also said: he used to hurry the recitation when Gabriel taught it to him, and he was told: Do not hurry, for Gabriel is commanded to recite it to you again and again until you have it by heart; then you will not forget it, except what God wills, and then you will remember it after forgetting. Or He said *illā mā shāʾa llāh* (“except what God wills”) meaning the little and the rare, as it is related that he left out a verse in {p|739}his recitation in prayer, and Ubayy thought it had been abrogated; he asked him, and he said, “*I forgot it*.” Or He said *illā mā shāʾa llāh* with the aim of denying forgetting altogether, as a man says to his companion, “You share with me in what I own, except what God wills,” without meaning to except anything; this is using “little” in the sense of “none.” It is also said that His saying *fa-lā tansā* is a prohibition (“so do not forget”), the *alif* being added for the verse ending, as in *al-sabīlā* (Q 33:67); that is: do not neglect reciting and repeating it so that you forget it, except what God wills to make you forget by lifting its recitation for some benefit.

{L|He knows what is declared|innahu yaʿlamu l-jahra} He means: you recite aloud along with the recitation of Gabriel ﵇ for fear that it will slip away, and God knows your reciting aloud with him and what is in yourself that moves you to it; so do not, for I will spare you what you fear. Or: He knows what you keep secret and what you make known of your words and deeds, what is outward and inward in your states, and what serves your religion and what harms it; so He causes to be forgotten of the revelation what He wills, and leaves kept what He wills.

::: passage 87:8-13 | 4:739-740

{L|We will direct you to the easiest path|wa-nuyassiruka li-l-yusrā} coordinated with “*We will recite to you*,” and His saying “*He knows what is declared, and what is hidden*” is a parenthesis. Its meaning: We will make you succeed in the way that is easiest and simplest, meaning [in] keeping the revelation. It is also said: toward the tolerant law, which is the easiest of the laws and the simplest to take up. It is also said: We will make you succeed in the work of Paradise.

{Q} The Messenger ﷺ was commanded to remind, whether the reminder benefited or not. So what is the meaning of making benefit a condition? {A} It is in one of two ways. One: the Messenger of God ﷺ had spent all his effort in reminding them, and with more reminding they only grew in insolence and transgression, while the Prophet ﷺ burned with grief and longing and grew ever more earnest and eager in reminding them. So he was told “*while you have no power over them. Therefore, remind with this Quran, those who reverence My warnings*” (Q 50:45), and “*You shall disregard them and say, ‘Peace’*” (Q 43:89), and “*you shall remind; perhaps the reminder will benefit*” (*fa-dhakkir in nafaʿati l-dhikrā*, “so remind, if the reminder benefits”); and that comes after the proof has been made binding by repeated reminding. The second: it is outwardly a condition, but its meaning is blame of those who are reminded, a report of their state, a judgment that the reminder is unlikely to work on them, and a record against them that their hearts are sealed; as you say to a preacher, “Preach to the tax-takers, if they will listen to you,” meaning by the condition that it is unlikely and will not happen.

{L|The reverent will take heed|sa-yadhdhakkaru} and accept the reminder {p|740}and profit by it, “*The reverent*” (*man yakhshā*, “whoever fears”) God and the evil end, and so looks and reflects until his looking leads him to follow the truth. As for these, they neither fear nor look, so do not hope that they will accept it from you.

{L|The wicked will avoid it|wa-yatajannabuhā} avoids the reminder and keeps clear of it, “*The wicked*” (*al-ashqā*, “the most wretched”): the unbeliever, because he is more wretched than the sinner; or the one who is the most wretched of the unbelievers, for how deep he went in enmity to the Messenger of God ﷺ. It is said that it was revealed about al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra and ʿUtba ibn Rabīʿa.

{L|The great Hellfire|al-nāra l-kubrā} the lowest of the levels of the Fire. It is also said: “*great*” (*al-kubrā*, “the greatest”) is the fire of Hell, and the lesser is the fire of this world.

{L|Then|thumma} He said “*then*” because wavering between life and death is more terrible than the burning, so it lies beyond it in degrees of severity. The meaning: he will not die and be at rest, nor live a life that profits him.

::: passage 87:14-17 | 4:740-741

{L|Redeems his soul|tazakkā} purifies himself of idolatry and sins; or purifies himself for prayer; or does much of piety, from *zakāʾ*, which is growth; or it is *tafaʿʿala* from *zakāh* (“alms”), as *taṣaddaqa* is from *ṣadaqa*. “*And observing the contact prayers (Salat)*”: that is, the five prayers, like His saying “*and they observe the Contact Prayers (Salat) and give the obligatory charity (Zakat)*” (Q 2:177). From Ibn Masʿūd: “*God’s mercy on a man who gave alms and prayed!*” From ʿAlī ﵁: it is giving the alms of the breaking of the fast; and he said, “*I do not mind if I find nothing else in my record*,” because of His saying “*Successful indeed is the one who redeems his soul*,” that is, gives the alms of the breaking of the fast, then goes to the place of prayer and prays the feast prayer; and “*remembering the name of his Lord*,” that is, says the opening *takbīr*. It is used as a proof that the opening *takbīr* is obligatory, that it is not part of the prayer, because the prayer is coordinated with it, and that the prayer may be opened with any of His names, mighty and majestic is He. From Ibn ʿAbbās ﵁: he remembered his return and his standing before his Lord, and prayed to Him. From al-Ḍaḥḥāk: he mentioned the name of his Lord on the way to the place of prayer, and prayed the feast prayer.

{L|Indeed, you are preoccupied with this first life|bal tuʾthirūna l-ḥayāta l-dunyā} and so do not do what you would succeed by. It is also read {p|741}*yuʾthirūna*, in the third person. The reading of Ibn Masʿūd, *bal antum tuʾthirūna*, supports the first. “*Far better and everlasting*”: finer in itself, more blissful and more enduring. From ʿUmar ﵁: “*This world beside the Hereafter is only like a hare’s starting up*.”

::: passage 87:18-19 | 4:741-741

{L|This|hādhā} points to His saying from “*Successful indeed*” to “*everlasting*”; He means that the sense of these words is found in those scrolls. It is also said: to what is in the whole sūra. It is related from Abū Dharr ﵁ that he asked the Messenger of God ﷺ, “*How many books did God send down?*” He said: “*A hundred and four books. Of them, on Adam ten scrolls, on Seth fifty scrolls, on Enoch, who is Idrīs, thirty scrolls, on Abraham ten scrolls; and the Torah, the Gospel, the Psalms and the Furqān*.” It is said that in the scrolls of Abraham is: “*The wise man ought to guard his tongue, know his time and attend to his own affairs*.”

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-Aʿlā, God will give him ten good deeds for every letter God sent down on Abraham, Moses and Muḥammad*.” When he recited it he would say, “*Glory be to my Lord, the Most High*,” and ʿAlī and Ibn ʿAbbās used to say it. He loved it, and he said: “*The first to say ‘Glory be to my Lord, the Most High’ was Michael*.”
