*Uḥiya* is also read; its origin is *wuḥiya*. One says *awḥā ilayhi* and *waḥā ilayhi*; then the *wāw* was turned to *hamza*, as one says *uʿida* and *uzina*, and “*The messengers are summoned*” (*uqqitat*, Q 77:11). It is of the turning that is allowed without restriction in every *wāw* with *ḍamma*; and al-Māzinī allowed it without restriction in the one with *kasra* too, as in *ishāḥ*, *isāda* and *iʿāʾ akhīhi*. Ibn Abī ʿAbla read *wuḥiya*, on the origin. “*That a group of jinns listened*” is with *fatḥa*, because it is the subject of “*I was inspired*”; and “*We have heard*” is with *kasra*, because it is a fresh start related after the saying. Then the rest are carried on these two: what is of the revelation takes *fatḥa*, and what is of the saying of the jinn takes *kasra*. All of them are of their saying except the last two, “*The places of worship*” and “*When GOD’s servant advocated Him*.” Whoever gives them all *fatḥa* joins them {p|623}to the place of the preposition and its object in “*we have believed in it*,” as though it were said: we affirmed it, and we affirmed that exalted is the majesty of our Lord, and that our fool used to say; and likewise the rest.

{L|A group of jinns|nafarun mina l-jinni} a group of them, between three and ten. It is also said: they were of al-Shayṣabān, who are the most numerous of the jinn, and most of Iblīs’s troops are from them. “*Then said, ‘We have heard’*”: that is, they said to their people when they went back to them; as in His saying “*As soon as it was over, they rushed to their people, warning. They said, ‘O our people, we have heard a book’*” (Q 46:29–30). “*Wonderful*”: original, unlike all other books in the beauty of its arrangement and the soundness of its meanings, the proofs of inimitability standing in it. *ʿAjab* is a verbal noun put in the place of *ʿajīb*, and there is emphasis in it; and it is what goes beyond the bounds of its kinds and likes. “*It guides to righteousness*”: it calls to what is right. It is also said: to the oneness of God and faith. The pronoun in “*in it*” is for the Qurʾān; and since faith in it is faith in God and His oneness and freedom from associating, they said “*we will never set up any idols besides our Lord*”: that is, we will not go back to the associating with Him that we were in, in obeying Satan. It may also be that the pronoun is for God, Mighty and Majestic, because His saying “*besides our Lord*” explains it.

{L|The Most High is our only Lord|jaddu rabbinā} His greatness; from your saying, “So-and-so became great (*jadda*) in my eye,” that is, grew great. In the ḥadīth of ʿUmar ﵁: “*When a man of us had recited al-Baqara and Āl ʿImrān he became great among us*”; and it is related, “*in our eyes*.” Or His rule and His authority; or His riches; a borrowing from *al-jadd* that is fortune and luck, because kings and the rich are the fortunate. And the meaning is: He is described as exalted above a consort and a child for His greatness; or for His authority and His dominion; or for His riches. His saying “*He never had a mate, nor a son*” makes that clear. *Jaddan rabbunā* is also read, as specification; and *jiddu rabbinā*, with *kasra*, that is: the truth of His lordship and the reality of His godhood is exalted above taking a consort and a child. That is because when they heard the Qurʾān and were brought to the oneness of God and faith, they took notice of the error in what the unbelievers of the jinn believed, of likening God to His creation and His taking a consort and a child; so they held it monstrous and declared Him far above it.

Their fool is Iblīs, God curse him, or another of the rebels of the jinn. *Al-shaṭaṭ* is going past the limit in wrong and other things; from it is *ashaṭṭa fī l-sawm*, when he went too far in the price. That is: he says a saying that is in itself an outrage, for how far he went in it; and it is attributing a consort and a child to God. And it was our thought that none of the two weighty races would lie about God or forge against Him what is not true; so we believed them in what they attributed to Him of that, until the Qurʾān made their lying and their forging clear to us. “*Such nonsense*” (*shaṭaṭan*): a lying saying, that is, one lied in. Or it is put in the accusative as a verbal noun is, because lying is a kind of saying. Whoever reads *an lan taqawwala* put “*lies*” in the place of “a fabricating,” and did not make it a description, because fabricating can only be lying.

{p|624}*Al-rahaq* is going into forbidden things. And the meaning is that the humans, by their seeking refuge with them, increased them in pride and unbelief. That is because when a man of the Arabs came at evening into a desolate valley on one of his journeys and feared for himself, he would say, “I seek refuge with the lord of this valley from the fools of his people,” meaning the jinn and their chief. When they heard that, they grew proud and said, “We have become lords of the jinn and the humans”; and that is their *rahaq*. Or the jinn increased the humans in *rahaq* by leading them on and leading them astray, because they sought refuge with them. “*They*,” and that the humans, “*thought, just like you thought*”: and it is of the speech of the jinn, some of them saying it to others. It is also said: the two verses are part of the revelation; the pronoun in “*They thought*” is for the jinn, and the address in “*you thought*” is to the unbelievers of Quraysh.

*Al-lams* is touching; it was borrowed for seeking, because one who touches is seeking and trying to learn. He said:

> We have touched something of our fathers, and each of us / goes back to a lineage among his people that is not low.

One says *lamasahu*, *iltamasahu* and *talammasahu*, like *ṭalabahu*, *aṭlabahu* and *taṭallabahu*. Like it is *al-jass*, and their saying, “They felt him with their eyes,” and “they spied on him.” And the meaning is: we sought to reach the heaven and listen to the speech of its people. *Al-ḥaras* is a singular noun in the sense of *al-ḥurrās*, the guards, like *al-khadam* in the sense of *al-khuddām*; for that reason it was described by *shadīd*; had its meaning been followed, it would have been said *shidād*. Like it is “*I fear a band on foot* (rujayl) *or a band riding* (rukayb) *out early*,” because *al-rajl* and *al-rakb* are singulars in the sense of *al-rijāl* and *al-rikāb*. *Al-raṣad* is like *al-ḥaras*: a collective noun {p|625}for one who lies in wait, on the meaning: possessors of a flame, lying in wait to stone; and they are the angels who stone them with flames and keep them from listening. It may also be a description of the flame, in the sense of lying in wait; or as in his saying, “*… and a gut hungry*” [a singular described by a plural]. He means: he finds a flame lying in wait for him and for his sake.

{Q} It is as though there was no stoning in the Jāhiliyya; yet God Most High has said, “*We adorned the lowest universe with lamps, and guarded its borders with projectiles against the devils*” (Q 67:5), and mentioned two uses in the creation of the stars: adornment, and the stoning of the devils. {A} Some said it came about after the sending of the Messenger of God ﷺ, and is one of his signs. What is right is that it was before the sending, and mention of it has come in the poetry of the people of the Jāhiliyya. Bishr ibn Abī Khāzim said:

> And the wild ass, the dust pressing on her, and her foal / swooping behind the two as a star swoops.

{p|626}And Aws ibn Ḥajar said:

> And he swooped like a glittering star, followed / by a dust cloud rising that you would take for a tent rope.

And ʿAwf ibn al-Khariʿ said:

> It brings back to us the wild ass short of its mate, / or the bull, like a glittering star followed by blood.

But the devils used to steal a hearing in some states; and when the Messenger of God ﷺ was sent, the stoning increased and grew by a plain increase, until the humans and the jinn took notice of it, and stealing was stopped altogether. From Maʿmar: I said to al-Zuhrī, “*Were stars thrown in the Jāhiliyya?*” He said, “*Yes*.” I said, “*Have you considered the saying of God Most High, ‘And we used to sit’?*” He said, “*It was made harsher and its matter made harder when the Prophet ﷺ was sent*.” Al-Zuhrī related from ʿAlī ibn al-Ḥusayn, from Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄: “*While the Messenger of God ﷺ was sitting among a band of the Anṣār, a star was thrown and lit up, and he said, ‘What did you use to say about the like of this in the Jāhiliyya?’ They said, ‘We used to say: a great man dies, or a great man is born.’*” In His saying “*filled*” is proof that what came about new was the filling and the increase. Likewise His saying “*We used to sit there*,” that is: we used to find some of the places in it empty of guards and flames, and now all the places are filled. And this is the mention of what drove them to travel through the lands until they came upon the Messenger of God ﷺ and listened to his recitation.

{L|Some of us are righteous|minnā l-ṣāliḥūna} among us are the pious, the God-fearing; “*and some are less than righteous*”: and among us are a people below that. The thing described was dropped, as in His saying “*Each one of us has a specific job*” (Q 37:164). And they are the ones moderate in righteousness, not complete in it. Or they meant the wicked. “*We follow various paths*” makes clear the division mentioned, that is: we were possessed of split and differing doctrines. Or: we were, in the difference of our states, like differing ways. Or: we were in differing ways; as in his saying, “*as the fox ran swiftly the road*.” Or: our ways were ways of divided paths, on the dropping of the annexed, which is the ways, and putting the pronoun it is annexed to in its place. *Al-qidda* is from *qadda*, cut, like *al-qiṭʿa* from *qaṭaʿa*. The ways were described as *qidad* because it points to the meaning of being cut up and split apart.

::: passage 72:12-12 | 4:627-627

“*On earth*” and “*run away and escape*” are two circumstantial clauses, that is: we will not frustrate Him, being in the earth wherever we are in it; and we will not frustrate Him, fleeing from it to the heaven. It is also said: we will not frustrate Him in the earth if He wills a thing for us, and we will not frustrate Him by flight if He seeks us. The thinking is in the sense of certainty. This is a description of the states of the jinn and what they are in of their states and beliefs: among them are the good, the evil and the moderate; and that they believe that God, Mighty and Majestic, is mighty, overcoming, no aim escapes Him and no flight saves from Him.

::: passage 72:13-13 | 4:627-628

{L|When we heard the guidance|lammā samiʿnā l-hudā} is their hearing the Qurʾān and their believing in it. “*Will never fear*”: then he does not fear, that is, then he is one not fearing. And because the speech is on the estimate of a subject and predicate, the *fāʾ* came in; were it not for that, it would have been said, “let him not fear.”

{Q} {p|628}What is the benefit in putting the verb in the nominative, implying a subject before it so that it falls as its predicate, and the necessity of bringing in the *fāʾ*, when all that could be done without by saying “let him not fear”? {A} The benefit in it is that when that is done, it is as though it were said “then he does not fear”; so it points to making it certain that the believer is saved without doubt, and that he is the one singled out for that and no other. Al-Aʿmash read *fa-lā yakhaf*, as a prohibition. “*Any injustice, nor any affliction*” (*bakhsan wa-lā rahaqan*): that is, the repayment of loss and oppression; because he did not cheat anyone of a right, nor bring wrong on anyone, so he does not fear the repayment of these two. In it is proof that it is the due of one who believes in God to avoid wrongs; and from it is his saying ﵊: “*The believer is the one whom people trust with their lives and their wealth*.” It may also be meant: he does not fear being cheated, but is repaid in full, nor that abasement should come on him; from the saying of the Mighty and Majestic “*Humiliation is their lot*” (Q 10:27).

{L|The compromisers|al-qāsiṭūna} the unbelievers who swerve from the way of the truth. From Saʿīd ibn Jubayr ﵁: when al-Ḥajjāj meant to kill him, he said to him, “*What do you say about me?*” He said, “*Qāsiṭ ʿādil*.” The people said, “*How well he spoke!*” They reckoned that he was describing him by justice and fairness (*qisṭ* and *ʿadl*). Al-Ḥajjāj said, “*Ignorant ones! He has called me a wrongdoer and an associater*”; and he recited to them the saying of God Most High “*As for the unjust*” and the saying of God Most High “*Then those who disbelieve set up equals* (yaʿdilūna) *to their Lord*” (Q 6:1). One who sees no reward for the jinn has claimed that God Most High threatened their unjust and did not promise their Muslims. It is enough of a promise that He said “*they are on the right path*”: He mentioned the cause of reward and what requires it; and God is too just to punish the unjust and not reward the rightly guided.

{L|If they remain on the right path|wa-an law istaqāmū} *an* is lightened from the heavy one, and it is part of what was revealed. And the meaning is: and it was revealed to me that the matter and the account is: had the jinn kept straight on the best way; that is, had their father, al-Jānn, held firm on what he was {p|629}in, of the worship of God and obedience, and not been too proud to prostrate to Adam, and not disbelieved, and his children followed him in Islam, We would have blessed them and widened their provision. He mentioned abundant water, which is plentiful, *ghadaq* with *fatḥa* and *kasra* on the *dāl*, and both are read, because it is the root of living and of wide provision. “*We will surely test them all*” (*li-naftinahum fīhi*, “that We might try them in it”): that We might test them in it, how they give thanks for what they were granted of it. It may also be that its meaning is: and had the jinn who listened kept straight on their way that they were on before the listening, and not moved from it to Islam, We would have widened their provision for them, drawing them on, “*We will surely test them*”: that the blessing might be a cause of their following their desires, falling into trial and increasing in sin; or that We might punish them for denying the blessing.

{L|Disregards the message of his Lord|ʿan dhikri rabbihi} from His worship, or from His admonition, or from His revelation. “*He will make him go*” is also read with *nūn*, with *ḍamma* and *fatḥa*, that is: We will make him enter “*a punishment*.” The origin is “We will make him go into a punishment,” as in His saying “*What brought you to this retribution?*” (Q 74:42). So it was given two objects: either by dropping the preposition and joining the verb, as in His saying “*Moses then selected seventy men from among his people*” (Q 7:155); or by its containing the meaning of “We make him enter.” One says *salakahu* and *aslakahu*. He said: “*Until, when they made them go* (aslakūhum) *into Qatāʾida*.” *Al-ṣaʿad* is the verbal noun of *ṣaʿida*; one says *ṣaʿida ṣaʿadan* and *ṣuʿūdan*. The punishment was described by it because it climbs over the one punished, that is, it rises over him and overcomes him, and he cannot bear it. From it is the saying of ʿUmar ﵁: “*Nothing has climbed over me as the marriage sermon has climbed over me*,” meaning: nothing has been as hard on me or overcome me.

{L|The places of worship|wa-anna l-masājida} is part of what was revealed. It is also said that its meaning is: and because the places of prostration belong to God, “*do not call*”; on the view that the *lām* is connected with “*do not call*,” that is: do not call on anyone along with God in the places of prostration, because they are God’s alone and for His worship. From al-Ḥasan: he means the whole earth, because it was made a place of prostration for the Prophet ﷺ. It is also said: what is meant by it is the Sacred Mosque, because it is the qibla of the mosques; and from it is the saying of God Most High “*Who are more evil than those who boycott GOD’s masjids, where His name is commemorated*” (Q 2:114). From Qatāda: when the Jews and the Christians entered their synagogues and churches they associated others with God, so we were commanded to make the call to God pure when we enter the mosques. It is also said: the *masājid* are the seven limbs of prostration. The Messenger of God ﷺ said: “*I have been commanded to prostrate on seven limbs: the forehead, the nose, the two hands, {p|630}the two knees and the two feet*.” It is also said: it is the plural of *masjad*, which is prostration.

{L|GOD’s servant|ʿabdu llāhi} is the Prophet ﷺ.

{Q} Why was it not said “the Messenger of God” or “the Prophet”? {A} Because its estimate is: and it was revealed to me that when the servant of God stood. Since it fell in the speech of the Messenger of God ﷺ about himself, it was brought as humility and lowliness require. Or because the meaning is that the servant of God’s worship of God is not a thing far from reason, nor to be denied, so that they should be crowding on him.

The meaning of “*the servant of God stood calling on Him*” is: he stood worshiping Him; He means his standing for the dawn prayer at Nakhla, when the jinn came to him and listened to his recitation ﷺ. “*Almost all of them banded together to oppose him*” (*kādū yakūnūna ʿalayhi libadan*): that is, they crowd on him, piled on one another, in wonder at what they saw of his worship and his companions following him, standing, bowing and prostrating; and in delight at what he recited of the Qurʾān, because they saw what they had not seen the like of and heard what they had not heard the equal of. It is also said: its meaning is, when he stood as a messenger worshiping God alone, going against the idolaters in their worship of gods besides Him, the idolaters, for their backing one another against him and helping one another in their enmity to him, almost crowded on him, piled up. *Libad* is the plural of *libda*, which is what is matted one part on another; and from it is “the lion’s mane” (*libdat al-asad*). *Lubadan* is also read, and *al-lubda* is in the sense of *al-libda*; and *lubbadan*, plural of *lābid*, like *sājid* and *sujjad*; and *lubudan*, with two *ḍamma*s, plural of *labūd*, like *ṣabūr* and *ṣubur*. From Qatāda: the humans and the jinn massed together against this matter to put it out, and God would have nothing but to help it and make it prevail over whoever opposed it. Whoever reads *wa-innahu*, with *kasra*, made it of the speech of the jinn, which they said to their people when they went back to them, relating what they saw of his prayer and his companions crowding on him in following him in prayer.

{p|631}{L|Say|qul} to those who backed one another against him: “*I worship only my Lord*.” He means: I have not brought you a thing to be denied; I only worship my Lord alone, “*I never set up any idols besides Him*”; and that is not of what requires you to agree on hating me and being hostile to me. Or he said to the jinn when they crowded, wondering: what you see of my worshiping God and refusing to associate others with Him is not a thing to wonder at; one only wonders at whoever calls on other than God and makes a partner for Him. Or the jinn said that to their people, relating from the Messenger of God ﷺ. “*Nor to guide you*” (*wa-lā rashadan*): nor benefit. Or by harm he meant error, and the reading of Ubayy points to it, *ghayyan wa-lā rashadan*. And the meaning is: I am not able to harm you or benefit you; the one who harms and benefits is only God. Or: I am not able to force you to error or to right conduct; the one able for that is only God, Mighty and Majestic. And “*except a conveying*” is an exception from it, that is: I have power over nothing except a conveying from God. “*Say, ‘No one can protect me*” is a parenthesis put in to stress his denying ability to himself and to make his helplessness clear; on the meaning that if God wills evil for him, of sickness or death or something else, it is not possible that anyone should protect him from it or that he should find, besides Him, a refuge to shelter in. *Al-multaḥad* is the place of refuge; its origin is the place one goes into, from *al-laḥd*, the niche of the grave. It is also said: an escape and a turning aside. *Qāla lā amliku* is also read, {p|632}that is: the servant of God said to the idolaters, or to the jinn. It may also be of the jinn’s relating to their people.

It is also said: “*I deliver GOD’s proclamations*” is a substitute for “*refuge*,” that is: I will find no escape besides Him except that I convey from Him what He sent me with. It is also said: *illā* is *in lā*, and its meaning is: if I do not convey a conveying; as in your saying, “If not standing, then sitting.” “*and messages*” is joined to “*I deliver GOD’s proclamations*,” as though it were said: I have power for you over nothing but the conveying and the messages. And the meaning is: except that I convey from God, and say, “God said such-and-such,” attributing His saying to Him; and that I convey His messages that He sent me with, without adding or taking away.

{Q} Is it not said “he conveyed from him” (*ʿanhu*)? And from it is his saying ﵊: “*Convey from me, convey from me*.” {A} *Min* is not a complement of the conveying; it is only in the position of *min* in His saying “*An ultimatum is herein issued from GOD*” (Q 9:1), in the sense of a conveying that is from God. *Fa-anna lahu nāra jahannama* is also read, on: then his repayment is that he has the fire of Hell; as in His saying “*one-fifth shall go to GOD*” (Q 8:41), that is, then its ruling is that to God belongs a fifth of it. And He said “*abiding*,” carrying it on the plural meaning in “*whoever*.”

{Q} With what is “*until*” connected, with what is after it made its limit? {A} With His saying “*they were almost on him in crowds*,” on the view that they back one another against him in enmity, think his helpers weak and reckon their number few, “*Once they see what is awaiting them*,” of the day of Badr and God’s making him prevail over them, or of the Day of Resurrection, “*they will find out*” at that time that they are “*who is really weaker in power, and fewer in number*.” It may also be connected with something dropped that the state points to, of the unbelievers’ thinking him weak and reckoning his number few, as though He said: they will go on as they are “*Once they see what is awaiting them*.” The idolaters said, “*When will that prophecy come to pass?*” in denial of it; and it was said: “*Say*” it is coming, there is no doubt of it, so do not deny it, for God has promised that, and He does not break the promise. As for its time, I do not know when it will be, because God has not made it clear, for what He saw of benefit in hiding its time.

{Q} What is the meaning of His saying “*or if my Lord will delay it for awhile*,” when a term can be near and far? Do you not see His saying “*it will wish that they were far, far removed*” (Q 3:30)? {A} The Messenger of God ﷺ used to think the promised time near, so it is as though he said: I do not know whether it is a state expected at every hour, or put off, with a limit set for it. That is: He is “*the Knower of the future; He does not reveal*”: and He does not let anyone see. “*To a messenger*” makes clear “*that He chooses*.” He means that He lets none see the unseen but the approved one who is chosen for prophethood in particular, not every approved one. In this is the voiding of saints’ wonders, because those to whom they are attributed, even if they are friends of God and approved, are not messengers. {p|633}God has singled out the messengers, among the approved, for seeing the unseen; and there is the voiding of soothsaying and astrology, because their people are the farthest thing from approval and the most deeply in displeasure.

{L|Does He reveal from the past and the future|fa-innahu yasluku min bayni yadayhi} before the one He approved for the message, “*specific news*” (*min khalfihi raṣadan*, “and behind him watchers”): guardians of the angels who guard him from the devils, drive them from him, and keep him from their whisperings and their muddlings, until he conveys what was revealed to him. From al-Ḍaḥḥāk: no prophet was sent but there were angels with him guarding him from the devils’ taking the likeness of the angel’s form. “*This is to ascertain*,” God, “*that they have delivered their Lord’s messages*”: He means the prophets. He made it singular first, following the word, in His saying “*before him and behind him*,” then plural following the meaning; as in His saying “*incur the fire of Hell, wherein they abide forever*.” And the meaning is: that they may convey the messages of their Lord as they are, guarded from adding and taking away. Mentioning knowing is like mentioning it in the saying of God Most High “*in order to distinguish those among you who strive*” (Q 47:31). *Li-yuʿlama* is also read, built for the passive. “*He is fully aware of what they have*”: what is with the messengers, of rulings and laws; nothing of them escapes Him and He does not forget a letter of them; so He watches over them and guards them. “*He has counted the numbers of all things*,” of the drops of rain, the sand, the leaves of the trees and the foam of the seas; so how should He not encompass what is with the messengers of His revelation and His speech? “*In number*” is a circumstantial clause, that is: and He has fixed everything, counted and reckoned; or a verbal noun in the sense of counting.
