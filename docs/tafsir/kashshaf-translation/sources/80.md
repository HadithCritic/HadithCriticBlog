surah: 80
title: Sūrat ʿAbasa (80)
revelation: Meccan. Forty-two verses; it is also said forty-one. [Revealed after al-Najm (53).]

::: passage 80:1-10 | 4:700-702

Ibn Umm Maktūm came to the Messenger of God ﷺ. Umm Maktūm was his father’s mother, and his name was ʿAbd Allāh ibn Shurayḥ ibn Mālik ibn Rabīʿa al-Fihrī, of Banū ʿĀmir ibn Luʾayy. With him were the leading men of Quraysh, ʿUtba and Shayba the sons of Rabīʿa, Abū Jahl ibn Hishām, al-ʿAbbās ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, Umayya ibn Khalaf and al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra, and he was calling them to Islam in the hope that others would become Muslims through their becoming Muslims. He said, “*Messenger of God, recite to me and teach me some of what {p|701}God has taught you*,” and he said it again and again, not knowing that he was busy with the men. The Messenger of God ﷺ disliked his breaking in on his speech, frowned, and turned away from him, and it was revealed. After that the Messenger of God ﷺ would treat him with honor, and when he saw him he would say, “*Welcome to the one on whose account my Lord reproached me*,” and he would ask him, “*Is there anything you need?*” He made him his deputy over Medina twice. Anas said: “*I saw him on the day of al-Qādisiyya wearing a coat of mail, with a black banner*.”

*ʿAbbasa* is also read, with doubling, for intensity; likewise *kallaḥa* for *kalaḥa* (“he scowled”). “*When the blind man came to him*” (*an jāʾahu*) is accusative through “*turned away*” or through “*frowned*,” according to the two schools. Its meaning: he frowned because the blind man came to him, or turned away for that reason. *Ā-an jāʾahu* is also read, with two *hamza*s and an *alif* between them, pausing at “*He frowned and turned away*” and then beginning again, in the sense: was it because the blind man came to him that he did this? This is said in reproach. It is related that after this he never frowned in a poor man’s face, nor gave his attention to a rich one.

Telling what he had done and then turning to address him shows a stronger reproach, like one who complains to people of a wrongdoer who has wronged him, and then, when the complaint has grown heated, turns on the wrongdoer, faces him with rebuke, and holds him to the argument. There is something of the same in the mention of the blind man, as if He were saying: in his eyes the man deserved frowning and turning away because he was blind, when his blindness ought to have made him more tender, gentler, closer and more welcoming to him. People have taken God’s instruction in this to heart and been well taught by it: it is related of Sufyān al-Thawrī ﵀ that the poor were princes in his gatherings.

{L|How do you know|wa-mā yudrīka} what makes you aware of this blind man’s state? “*He may purify himself*” (*laʿallahu yazzakkā*): be cleansed of some of the stains of sin by the laws he is taught. “*Or he may take heed*”: or take heed, “*and benefit from the message*,” that is, your reminder, your admonition, and it would be a grace to him toward some acts of obedience. The meaning: you do not know what may be awaited from him, purification or heeding, and had you known, this would not have come from you. It is also said that the pronoun in “*perhaps he*” is for the unbeliever. He means: you hoped that he would be purified by Islam, or be reminded, and that the reminder would bring him near to accepting the truth; and what makes you know that what you hoped for will come about? *Fa-tanfaʿuhu* (“and would benefit him”) is read in the nominative, joined to *yadhdhakkaru*, and *fa-tanfaʿahu* in the accusative, as an answer to *laʿalla*, as in His saying “*and take a look at the god of Moses*” (*fa-aṭṭaliʿa*, Q 40:37).

{L|You gave him your attention|taṣaddā} you put yourself in his way by turning to him; *al-muṣādāh* is facing someone. *Taṣṣaddā* is also read, with doubling, by assimilating {p|702}the *tāʾ* into the *ṣād*. Abū Jaʿfar read *tuṣaddā*, with *ḍamma* on the *tāʾ*, that is, you are made to face him; its meaning is that something prompts you to give him your attention, out of your eagerness and longing for his Islam. And it is no fault of yours if he is not purified by Islam: “*Your sole mission is delivering the message*” (Q 42:48).

{L|The one who came to you eagerly|yasʿā} hastening in seeking good, “*And is really reverent*” God; or fears the unbelievers and their harm for coming to you. It is also said that he came with no one to lead him, so he feared stumbling.

{L|You ignored him|talahhā} you busy yourself elsewhere; from *lahiya ʿanhu*, and *iltahā* and *talahhā*. Ṭalḥa ibn Muṣarrif read *tatalahhā*, and Abū Jaʿfar read *tulhā*, that is, the affair of the leading men distracts you.

{Q} In His saying “*You gave him your attention*” (*fa-anta lahu taṣaddā*) and “*You ignored him*” (*fa-anta ʿanhu talahhā*) there seems to be a restriction. {A} Yes, and its sense is a reproach to him for giving his attention and being distracted: one like you, of all people, should not give his attention to the rich and be distracted from the poor.

{L|Indeed|kallā} a rebuke for what he was reproached for, and against doing the like again. “*This is a reminder*”: that is, an admonition that must be heeded and acted upon. “*Whoever wills shall take heed*”: that is, keep it and not forget it. The pronoun is masculine because *tadhkira* (“reminder”) has the sense of *dhikr* and admonition.

{L|In honorable scriptures|fī ṣuḥuf} a description of “*a reminder*.” He means: it is set down in scriptures copied from the Tablet, “*honorable*” with God, “*Exalted*” in heaven, or raised in worth, “*pure*,” kept from the hands of the devils, touched only by the hands of purified angels. *Safara* are scribes who copy the books from the Tablet; *barara* are the God-fearing. It is also said that they are the scriptures of the prophets, as in His saying “*This is recorded in the earlier teachings*” (Q 87:18). It is also said that the *safara* are the reciters; and it is also said that they are the Companions of the Messenger of God ﷺ.

::: passage 80:17-23 | 4:702-703

{L|Woe to the human being|qutila l-insān} a curse upon him, and one of the most hideous of their curses, because being killed is the utmost of {p|703}this world’s hardships and horrors. “*He is so unappreciative!*” (*mā akfarahu*) is an exclamation of wonder at his excess in denying God’s favor. You will see no style harsher than this, rougher to the touch, more telling of anger, reaching further in censure though its two ends lie so close together, or gathering more reproach into so short a text.

Then He set about describing his state, from the start of his coming into being to its end, and the favors he is steeped in, root and branch, and the ingratitude, the scorn of favor and the heedlessness in which he has buried his head, paying little attention to what he moves about in and to the thanks he owes. “*What did He create him from?*”: from what lowly, contemptible thing did He create him? Then He made that thing plain with His saying “*From a tiny drop, He creates him and designs him*,” that is, fitted him for what suits him and belongs to him. Like it is “*He created everything in exact measure*” (Q 25:2).

“*The path*” is accusative through an implied *yassara*, explained by “*He points out*” (*yassarahu*, “He made easy”). The meaning: then He made his way easy, which is his coming out of his mother’s womb; or the way he chooses to follow, of the two paths of good and evil, by His giving him the power and the means, as in His saying “*We showed him the two paths*” (*innā hadaynāhu l-sabīla*, “We guided him to the way”) (Q 76:3). From Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄: He made clear to him the way of good and evil.

{L|And into the grave|fa-aqbarahu} He made him one with a grave in which he is hidden, as an honor to him, and did not leave him thrown on the face of the earth as prey for beasts and birds, like other animals. One says *qabara l-mayyit* when one buries the dead, and *aqbarahu l-mayyit* when one has him buried and lets him do it. From this is the saying of the one who said to al-Ḥajjāj, “*Give us Ṣāliḥ to bury (aqbirnā Ṣāliḥan)*.”

{L|He resurrects him|ansharahu} brought him into being in the second creation. *Nasharahu* is also read.

{L|No|kallā} a rebuke to man for the state he is in. “*He shall uphold*” (*lammā yaqḍi*, “he has not yet done”): he has not done, for all the length and stretch of time from Adam until now, “*His commandments*,” that is, what God commanded him, so as to have fulfilled all His commands. He means that no human being has ever been free of falling short.

::: passage 80:24-32 | 4:703-705

{p|704}Having counted the favors within man himself, He followed it with the favors in what he needs, and said “*Let the human consider his food!*”: at what he eats and lives by, and how We arranged it. “*We pour the water generously*” (*annā ṣababnā l-māʾa*, “that We poured the water”): the rain. It is read with *kasra* (*innā*), as a new sentence, and with *fatḥa* (*annā*), as a substitute for “*his food*.” Al-Ḥusayn ibn ʿAlī ﵄ read *annā ṣababnā*, with *imāla*, in the sense: let man look how We poured the water.

{L|Then we split|wa-shaqaqnā} from the earth’s splitting with plants. It may also be from splitting it by plowing with oxen, and He ascribed the splitting to Himself as an act is ascribed to its cause. *Al-ḥabb* (“grain”) is everything that is reaped, such as wheat, barley and the like. *Al-qaḍb* is fresh fodder (*al-raṭba*), and *al-miqḍāb* is the land it grows in. It is named with the verbal noun of *qaḍabahu*, “he cut it,” because it is cut time after time.

{L|A variety of orchards|wa-ḥadāʾiqa ghulban} He may make each garden *ghulbāʾ*, meaning that it is thick, with many great trees, as you say “a massive garden”; or make its trees *ghulb*, that is, great and thick. The word is first used of necks, and was borrowed. ʿAmr ibn Maʿdīkarib said: “*Thick-necked men walk through it, as though they were full-grown camels clothed in coverings of tar*.”

{L|And pasture|wa-abban} grazing land, because it is *yuʾabbu*, that is, made for and sought out. *Al-abb* and *al-amm* are brothers. He said: “*Our root is Qays and Najd is our home; there we have the pasture and the watering place*.” From Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq ﵁: he was asked about *al-abb* and said, “*What heaven would shade me, and what earth would bear me, if I said about the Book of God what I do not know?*” From ʿUmar ﵁: he recited this verse and said, {p|705}“*All of this we know; but what is al-abb?*” Then he threw away a stick that was in his hand and said, “*By the life of God, this is going to needless lengths. What harm is it to you, son of ʿUmar’s mother, not to know what al-abb is?*” Then he said, “*Follow what has been made clear to you of this Book, and what has not, leave it*.”

{Q} This looks like forbidding the pursuit of the Qurʾān’s meanings and the study of its difficulties. {A} He did not mean that. Rather, those people gave the greatest part of their concern to action, and to occupy oneself with knowledge one would not act on was, to them, going to needless lengths. He meant that the verse is set out to remind man of the favor of his food and to call him to give thanks; and it is known from the drift of the verse that *al-abb* is something God makes grow for man as provision for him or for his livestock. So hold to what matters more, rising to thank God for those of His favors He has counted that are clear to you and not obscure, and do not busy yourself away from it with seeking the meaning of *al-abb* and identifying the particular plant it names. Be content with knowing it in general until it becomes clear to you at some other time. Then he urged people to follow this course with other difficulties of the Qurʾān like it.

::: passage 80:32-41 | 4:705-706

{L|The blow|al-ṣākhkha} one says *ṣakhkha li-ḥadīthihi*, like *aṣākha lahu*, “he listened closely to what he said.” The blast is called *al-ṣākhkha* figuratively, because people listen closely for it.

{L|Flees|yafirru} he flees from them because he is taken up with what he faces, and because he knows they will be of no use to him. He began with the brother, then the parents, because they are closer than he, then the wife and children, because they are closer and dearer, as if He said: he flees from his brother, no, from his parents, no, from his wife and his children. It is also said that he flees from them for fear that they will claim what he owes them: the brother says, “You did not share your wealth with me”; the parents, “You fell short in your duty to us”; the wife, “You fed me what was forbidden, and you did this and that”; and the children, “You did not teach us or guide us.” It is also said that the first to flee from his brother is Abel, from his parents Abraham, from his wife Noah and Lot, and from his son Noah.

{L|Worries about his own destiny|yughnīhi} will be enough for him to be concerned with. *Yaʿnīhi* (“will concern him”) is also read.

{L|Happy|musfira} shining and glad, from *asfara l-ṣubḥ*, when the dawn shines. From Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄: {p|706}from standing in prayer at night, because of what is related in the ḥadīth: “*Whoever prays much by night, his face is fair by day*.” From al-Ḍaḥḥāk: from the traces of ablution. It is also said: from how long they were covered with dust in the way of God.

{L|Covered with misery|ghabara} dust that rises over them; *qatara* is blackness like smoke. You will see nothing more forbidding than dust and blackness together on a face, as you see on the faces of the Zanj when they are dusty. It is as though God the Mighty and Majestic joins dust to the blackness of their faces, just as they joined depravity to unbelief.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat ʿAbasa wa-tawallā will come on the Day of Resurrection with his face laughing and rejoicing*.”
