{L|The blowing winds|wa-l-dhāriyāti} the winds, because they scatter dust and other things. God Most High said: “*blown away by the wind*” (Q 18:45). It is also read with the *tāʾ* assimilated into the *dhāl*. “*Bearing rain*” (*fa-l-ḥāmilāti wiqran*): the clouds, because they bear the rain. *Waqran* is also read, with *fatḥa* on the *wāw*, naming the thing borne by the verbal noun, or putting it in the place of *ḥimlan* (“a bearing”). “*Bringing provisions*” (*fa-l-jāriyāti yusran*): the ships. The meaning of *yusran* is a running with ease, that is, with smoothness. “*Distributing them as commanded*” (*fa-l-muqassimāti amran*): the angels, because they divide the affairs, the rains, the provisions and the rest; or they carry out the dividing, commanded to do so. From Mujāhid: they take charge of dividing the affair of the servants: Gabriel for severity, Michael for mercy, the Angel of Death for taking the spirits, and Isrāfīl for the blowing.

From ʿAlī ﵁: he said while on the pulpit, “*Ask me before you can no longer ask me; you will never ask anyone like me after me*.” Ibn al-Kawwāʾ stood and said, “*What are the scatterers with a scattering?*” He said, “*The blowing winds*.” He said, “*Bearing rain?*” {p|395}He said, “*The clouds*.” He said, “*And the runners with ease?*” He said, “*The ships*.” He said, “*And the dividers of a command?*” He said, “*The angels*.” The like is from Ibn ʿAbbās. From al-Ḥasan: *the dividers* are the clouds, by which God divides the servants’ provisions. They have also been taken as the seven planets. It may also be that the winds alone are meant, because they raise the clouds, carry them and turn them about, and run in the air with an easy running, and divide the rains by turning the clouds about.

{Q} What is the meaning of the *fāʾ* on the two explanations? {A} On the first, the sense of sequence in it is that He Most High swore by the winds, then by the clouds they drive, then by the ships they make run by their blowing, then by the angels who divide the provisions by God’s leave, from the rains and the trade of the sea and its benefits. On the second, it is because they begin by blowing, then scatter dust and pebbles, then carry the clouds, then run in the air spreading them out, then divide the rain.

{L|What is promised to you|inna mā tūʿadūna} the answer of the oath. *Mā* is a relative or forms a verbal noun, and what is promised is the raising. *Waʿdun ṣādiq* (“a true promise”) is like *ʿīshatun rāḍiya* (“a pleasing life,” Q 69:21). *Al-dīn* is the repayment, and *al-wāqiʿ* is what comes about.

{L|The perfectly created sky|al-ḥubuki} the tracks, like the ripples (*ḥubuk*) of sand and of water when the wind strikes it; likewise the *ḥubuk* of hair, the traces of its bending and breaking. Zuhayr said: “*Crowned with the roots of the grass, woven by a piercing wind, the open face of its water rippled*.” {p|396}A coat of mail is *maḥbūka* because its rings are laid in tracks. It is said that the sky is made in this way. From al-Ḥasan: its *ḥubuk* are its stars; the meaning is that they adorn it as the tracks of embroidery adorn the embroidered cloth. It is also said that its *ḥubuk* is its firmness and solidity, from their saying *farasun maḥbūku l-maʿāqim*, that is, a horse firm in the joints; and when the weaver weaves well they say, “How fine his *ḥabk* is!” It is the plural of *ḥibāk*, like *mithāl* and *muthul*, or of *ḥabīka*, like *ṭarīqa* and *ṭuruq*. It is also read *al-ḥubk*, on the measure of *al-qufl*; *al-ḥibk*, on the measure of *al-silk*; *al-ḥabak*, on the measure of *al-jabal*; *al-ḥubak*, on the measure of *al-buraq*; *al-ḥibak*, on the measure of *al-niʿam*; and *al-ḥibik*, on the measure of *al-ibil*.

{L|You continue to dispute the truth|innakum la-fī qawlin mukhtalifin} their saying of the Messenger, “sorcerer,” “poet” and “madman,” and of the Qurʾān, “poetry,” “sorcery” and “tales of the ancients.” From al-Ḍaḥḥāk: the speech of the unbelievers is never even; it is only contradictory and differing. From Qatāda: among you are those who believe and those who deny, those who acknowledge and those who reject.

{L|Deviating therefrom|yuʾfaku ʿanhu} the pronoun is for the Qurʾān or for the Messenger: that is, turned away from it is the one who has been turned with the turning than which there is none more severe or greater; as in the saying, “None perishes against God except one bound to perish.” It is also said: turned away from it is the one who was turned away in God’s prior knowledge, that is, of whom He knew from eternity that he would be turned from the truth and would not desist. The pronoun may also be for what you are promised or for the repayment: He swore by the scatterers that the coming of the Resurrection is true, then swore by the sky that they are in differing speech about its coming, some doubting and some denying; then He said: turned away from acknowledging the Resurrection is the one who is truly turned away.

Another way is that the pronoun returns to “*differing speech*,” and *ʿan* is like that in his saying: “*They reach their limit from eating and from drinking*,” that is, they reach the limit of fatness because of eating and drinking, its real sense being that their reaching the limit of fatness issues from the two. {p|397}So likewise their being turned away issues from the differing speech.

Saʿīd ibn Jubayr read *yuʾfaku ʿanhu man afaka*, with the second verb active: that is, the one who turned people from it, namely Quraysh. That was because the tribe used to send a man of sense and judgment to ask about the Messenger of God ﷺ, and they would say to him, “Beware of him,” and he would go back and tell them. From Zayd ibn ʿAlī: *yaʾfiku ʿanhu man ufika*, that is, the one who is himself turned away turns people from it. Also from him: *yaʾfiku ʿanhu man afaka*, that is, a great liar turns people from it. It is also read *yuʾfanu ʿanhu man ufina*, that is, deprived of it is the one who is deprived, from *afana l-ḍarʿa*, when he drains the udder by milking.

{L|Woe to the falsifiers|qutila l-kharrāṣūna} a prayer against them, as in the saying of God Most High “*Woe to the human being; he is so unappreciative*” (Q 80:17). Its origin is praying for killing and ruin; then it came to be used like “cursed be he” and “shame on him.” *Al-kharrāṣūn* are the liars who reckon what is not sound; they are the people of the differing speech, and the definite article points to them, as if it were said, “Death to these conjecturers.” *Qatala l-kharrāṣīna* is also read, that is, God has killed [them]. “*In their blundering*” (*fī ghamratin*): in ignorance that floods over them. “*Heedless*”: unmindful of what they were commanded. “*They ask*,” saying, “*They question the Day of Judgment*”, that is, when is the Day of Repayment. *Iyyāna* is also read, with *kasra* on the *hamza*, and it is a dialect form.

{Q} How does *ayyāna* serve as an adverb for the day, when times serve as adverbs only for events? {A} Its meaning is: when is the coming of the Day of Judgment.

{Q} Then what puts in the accusative the day that comes in the answer? {A} An implied verb that the question points to, that is: it comes on the day when they are tried over the Fire. It may also be built on *fatḥa* because it is annexed to what is not declinable, namely the sentence.

{Q} Then what is its place when it is built on *fatḥa*? {A} Its place may be accusative, through the implied verb “it comes,” or nominative, as in “it is the day when they are tried over the Fire.” Ibn Abī ʿAbla read it in the nominative. “*They are presented to the fire*” (*yuftanūna*): are burned and punished; from this is *al-fatīn*, which is the lava field, because its stones look as though they had been burned. “*Taste the retribution*” is in the place of a circumstantial clause, that is, with this saying said to them. “*This*” is a subject and “*what*” (*alladhī*) its predicate: that is, this punishment is what “*you were seeking to hasten*.” “*This*” may also be a substitute for “*the retribution*” (*fitnatakum*): that is, taste this punishment.

{p|398}{L|They receive their Lord’s rewards|ākhidhīna mā ātāhum rabbuhum} accepting all that He gave them, content with it. He means that in what He gave them there is only what is received with acceptance, approved and not resented, because all of it is good and fine. From this is the saying of God Most High “*and takes the charities*” (Q 9:104), that is, accepts and approves them. “*Pious*” (*muḥsinīna*): they had done their deeds well, and what follows explains their doing good.

*Mā* is extra. The meaning: they slept in a small part of the night, if you make *qalīlan* an adverb; or you may make it a description of the verbal noun: they slept a little sleep. *Mā* may also form a verbal noun or be a relative, as in “their sleep in the night was little,” or “what they slept in was little,” and the verbal noun is in the nominative through *qalīlan*, as its agent. In it are several emphases: the word *hujūʿ*, which is a snatch of sleep; he said: “*The helmet has worn my head bald, so I taste no sleep but a doze*”; His saying “*little*” and “*the whole night*,” because the night is the time of sleep and rest; and the addition of *mā*, which emphasizes that. {p|399}He described them as reviving the night in vigil, and when they reached the hour before dawn they took to asking forgiveness, as though they had committed crimes in their night. His saying “*they prayed for forgiveness*” (*hum yastaghfirūna*) carries that they are the ones asking forgiveness, the most worthy of it, not those who persist in sin; so it is as though it belonged to them alone, since they keep at it and are lavish in it.

{Q} May *mā* be a negative, as some have said, with the meaning that they do not sleep even a little of the night, and revive all of it? {A} No, because what comes after negative *mā* does not govern what comes before it. You say *zaydan lam aḍrib* (“Zayd I did not strike”), but you do not say *zaydan mā ḍarabtu*.

“*The beggar*” is the one who asks for gifts. “*And the deprived*” is the one thought rich and so deprived of alms because of his restraint. From the Prophet ﷺ: “*The poor man is not the one turned away by a mouthful or two, a morsel or two, a date or two*.” They said, “Then who is he?” He said, “*The one who does not find, and is not given alms*.” It is also said: the one whose wealth does not grow. It is also said: the luckless one who can hardly earn anything.

{L|The earth is full of signs|wa-fī l-arḍi āyātun} signs that point to the Maker, His power, His wisdom and His governing. It is spread out like a carpet for what is on it, as He said, “*made the earth habitable for you*” (Q 20:53). In it are the roads and passes for those who move about in it and walk on its shoulders. It is divided into plain and mountain, land and sea, and neighboring tracts, hard and soft, good-soiled and salty. It is like the she-camel that is covered, made to bear every color of plant and every kind of tree with fruits of different colors, tastes and scents, “*they are irrigated with the same water, we prefer some of them over others in eating*” (Q 13:4). All of it suits the needs of those who live on it, their benefits and their interests, in health and in sickness. And there are the springs that gush in it, the metals of many kinds, and the beasts spread over its land and sea, different in form, shape and act, wild and tame and creeping, and other things. “*For those who are certain*”: those who affirm His oneness, who have taken the straight path of proof that leads to knowledge, so they look with seeing eyes and piercing understanding; whenever they see a sign they know how to consider it, and so they add faith to their faith and certainty to their certainty.

{L|And within yourselves|wa-fī anfusikum} in their state at the start and their passing from state to state, and in their inward and outward parts, there are wonders of make and marvels of creation that bewilder minds. It is enough for you to consider the hearts and the minds set in them, and the kinds of meanings given to them alone; and the tongues, speech, the points where the letters are made, and their composition, order and subtleties: plain signs and decisive proofs of the wisdom of the Governor. Leave aside hearing, sight, the limbs and the other members, and how each is fitted for what it was made for, and the joints set in the members {p|400}for bending and folding: if any of them stiffens, weakness comes, and if it slackens, abasement settles. So blessed is God, the best of creators.

*Mithlu mā* is read in the nominative, as a description of *ḥaqq*: that is, a truth like your speaking; and in the accusative, as in: it is true, as true as your speaking. It may also be built on *fatḥa* because it is annexed to what is not declinable; and *mā* is extra, by the explicit statement of al-Khalīl. This is like people’s saying, “This is true, just as you see and hear,” and “just as you are here.” The pronoun points to what was mentioned of the signs, the provision and the matter of the Prophet ﷺ, or to what you are promised.

From al-Aṣmaʿī: “*I was coming from the congregational mosque of Basra when a Bedouin appeared on a young camel of his. When I made the pilgrimage with al-Rashīd I was going around the House, and there was someone calling to me in a thin voice. I turned, and there was the Bedouin, grown thin and pale. He greeted me and asked me to recite the sūra. When I reached the verse he cried out and said, ‘We have found what our Lord promised us true.’ Then he said, ‘Is there more than this?’ I recited, ‘By the Lord of the sky and the earth, it is true,’ and he cried out and said, ‘Glory be to God! Who angered the Majestic so that He swore? They would not believe Him at His word until they drove Him to an oath.’ He said it three times, and his soul went out with it*.”

{L|You may disregard them|fa-tawalla ʿanhum} turn away from those to whom you have repeated the call and who did not answer, and in whom you have come to know obstinacy and persistence. There is no blame on you for turning away after you have delivered the message and spent your effort in delivering and calling. And do not leave off reminding and admonishing with the days of God, “*for the reminder benefits the believers*”: that is, it has an effect on those of whom God knows that they will enter into faith; or it increases in faith those who have entered it. It is related that when “*So turn away from them*” was revealed, the Messenger of God ﷺ grieved and his Companions took it hard, and they thought that revelation had been cut off and that the punishment had come; then God revealed “*And remind*.”

::: passage 51:56-56 | 4:405-406

{p|406}That is: I created the jinn and humankind only for the sake of worship, and I wanted nothing from all of them but that.

{Q} If He wanted worship from them, they would all be worshipers. {A} He wanted them only to worship Him by choosing worship, not by being forced to it, because He created them able. So some of them chose to leave worship, though He wanted it; and had He wanted it by compulsion and constraint, it would have come about from all of them.

::: passage 51:57-58 | 4:406-406

He means that My affair with My servants is not like the affair of masters with their slaves. Those who own slaves own them only to have their help in getting their living and provision: one is fitted out for trade to bring in profit, one set to tilling to work the land, one put to a craft to profit from his wage, or one gathers firewood, or gathers fodder, or cooks, or bakes, and other such works and trades that are dealings in the means of living and the doors of provision. But I, the Owner of the slaves, have said to them: occupy yourselves with what makes you happy in yourselves; I do not want to employ you in getting My provision or yours. I have no need of you or of your services, and I bestow on you your provision and what sets you right and keeps you alive from Myself; there is none but I alone.

{L|The Supreme|al-matīnu} the One of great strength. It is read in the nominative as a description of *dhū*, and in the genitive as a description of *al-quwwa*, taking it as power. The meaning of describing Him with strength and firmness is that He is the One able, utterly able, to do everything. *Al-rāziq* is also read. In the reading of the Prophet ﷺ: *innī ana l-rāziq* (“I am the Provider”).

::: passage 51:59-60 | 4:407-407

*Al-dhanūb* is the great bucket. This is a figure whose origin is in water drawers who share the water, so that this one has a bucketful and that one a bucketful. He said: “*We have a bucketful and you have a bucketful; and if you refuse, the well is ours*.” When ʿAmr ibn Shās said: “*In every tribe you have dealt out your favor; so Shās has a right to a bucketful of your bounty*,” the king said, “*Yes, and buckets more*.” The meaning: those of the people of Mecca who wronged the Messenger of God ﷺ by giving him the lie have a share of God’s punishment like the share of their fellows and their likes among the generations. From Qatāda: a bucketful of God’s punishment like the bucketful of their fellows. “*From the day that is awaiting them*”: from the Day of Resurrection; it is also said, from the day of Badr.
