surah: 95
title: Sūrat al-Tīn (95)
revelation: Meccan. Eight verses. [Revealed after al-Burūj (85).]

::: passage 95:1-8 | 4:773-775

{L|By the fig and the olive|wa-l-tīni wa-l-zaytūn} He swore by them because they are wonders among the kinds of fruit-bearing trees. It is related that a dish of figs was given to the Messenger of God ﷺ; he ate from it and said to his Companions: “*Eat. If I were to say that a fruit came down from Paradise, I would say it is this, because the fruit of Paradise has no stones. So eat it, for it cuts off piles and helps against gout*.” Muʿādh ibn Jabal passed by an olive tree, took a twig from it, cleaned his teeth with it and said: “*I heard the Messenger of God ﷺ say: ‘The best tooth-stick is the olive, from the blessed tree; it sweetens the mouth and takes away decay.’ And I heard him say: ‘It is my tooth-stick and the tooth-stick of the prophets before me.’*” From Ibn ʿAbbās ﵁: it is these figs and olives of yours. It is also said: they are two mountains of the Holy Land, called in Syriac Ṭūr Tīnā and Ṭūr Zaytā, because they are where the fig and the olive grow. It is also said that “*the fig*” is the mountains between Ḥulwān and Hamadhān, and “*the olive*” the mountains of Syria, because they are where they grow; as if it were said: and by the places where the fig and the olive grow.

{L|Mount Sinai|wa-ṭūri sīnīn} *al-ṭūr*, which is the mountain, is annexed to *Sīnīn*, which is the spot. Like *Sīnūn* is *Yabrūn*, in that it may be inflected with *wāw* and *yāʾ*, or kept with *yāʾ* and the *nūn* given the vowels of inflection.

{L|And this honored town|wa-hādhā l-baladi l-amīn} the town is Mecca, may God protect it. *Al-amīn* is from *amuna l-rajulu amānatan fa-huwa amīn* (“the man was trustworthy, and he is trustworthy”); the form *ammān* is also used, as *kurrām* is used for *karīm*. Its trustworthiness is that it keeps safe whoever enters it, as the trustworthy man keeps what is entrusted to him. It may also be *faʿīl* in the sense of *mafʿūl*, from *amināhu*, because it is safe from harm, {p|774}as it is described with safety in the saying of God Most High “*a Sacred Sanctuary*” (*ḥaraman āminan*, “a secure sanctuary,” Q 28:57), in the sense of “having safety.” The meaning of swearing by these things is to make plain the honor of the blessed places and the good and blessing that appeared in them through the dwelling of prophets and the righteous there. The place where the fig and the olive grow is where Abraham emigrated, and where Jesus was born and grew up. The Mount is the place from which Moses was called. Mecca is the place of the House, which is guidance for the worlds, and the birthplace of the Messenger of God ﷺ and the place where he was sent.

{L|In the best design|fī aḥsani taqwīm} in the finest balance of his form and shape and the finest proportioning of his limbs. Then the end of his case, when he did not give thanks for the favor of that fine, upright, well-proportioned creation, was that “*Then turned him into the lowliest of the lowly*,” in make and build: that is, uglier in form and more misshapen in make than anyone ugly, and these are the people of the Fire; or lower than the lowest of the people of the depths. Or: then, after that upright making and that beauty, We returned him to the lowest of the low in beauty of form and shape, in that We turned him back in his make. So his back is bowed after it was straight, his hair is white after it was black, his skin is dry and wrinkled after it was soft, his hearing and sight are dull after they were sharp, and everything in him has changed: his walk is a shuffle, his voice a whisper, his strength weakness and his vigor dotage. ʿAbd Allāh read *asfala l-sāfilīn*.

{Q} How is the exception on each of the two views? {A} On the first it is joined, plainly joined; on the second it stands apart. He means: but those of the very old who were righteous have a lasting reward, never cut off, for their obedience and their patience under God’s trial of them with old age and decrepitude, and for bearing hardships and keeping up worship though their strength to rise fails them.

{Q} Who is addressed in “*Why do you still reject*” (*fa-mā yukadhdhibuka*, “so what makes you deny”)? {A} It is an address to man by way of turning to him: that is, what makes you a liar by reason of the Judgment and denying it, after this proof? He means that you are lying when you deny the Repayment, because everyone who denies the truth is a liar. So what thing forces you to be a liar by denying the Repayment? The *bāʾ* is like that in the saying of God Most High “*those who choose him as their master; those who choose him as their god*” (*wa-lladhīna hum bihi mushrikūn*, “and those who through him are idolaters,” Q 16:100). The meaning: man’s creation from a drop, his being made a well-shaped human, his being raised by degrees of increase until he is complete and full grown, and then his being turned back until he reaches the most abject age: you see no proof clearer than this of the Creator’s power, and that the One who had power to do all this with man is not unable to bring him back. So what is the cause of your denying the Repayment, O man, after this decisive proof? It is also said that the address is to the Messenger of God ﷺ.

{L|Is GOD not the Most Wise, of all the wise ones?|a-laysa llāhu bi-aḥkami l-ḥākimīn} a threat to the unbelievers, that He will judge against them with what they are {p|775}fit for. From the Prophet ﷺ: when he recited it, he would say, “*Yes indeed, and to that I am among those who bear witness*.”

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat wa-l-tīn, God will give him two things, well-being and certainty, as long as he is in the abode of this world; and when he dies, God will give him a reward as many times over as the number of those who recite this sūra*.”
