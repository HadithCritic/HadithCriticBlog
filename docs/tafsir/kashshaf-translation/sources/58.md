{L|God has heard|qad samiʿa llāhu} ʿĀʾisha ﵂ said: “*Praise be to God, whose hearing takes in all voices! The woman who disputed spoke to the Messenger of God ﷺ at the side of the house while I was with him, and I could not hear, yet He heard her*.” From ʿUmar: when she came in to him he would honor her and say, “*GOD has heard the woman*.” *Tuḥāwiruka*, that is, she goes back and forth with you in speech, is also read; and *tuḥāwiluka*, that is, she asks of you. She is Khawla bint Thaʿlaba, the wife of Aws ibn al-Ṣāmit, the brother of ʿUbāda. He saw her while she was praying, and she was of fine figure; when she had ended the prayer he sought her, and she refused. He grew angry, and there was some lightness and a touch of madness in him, and he declared *ẓihār* against her. She came to the Messenger of God ﷺ and said, “*Aws married me when I was young and sought after; and when my age had passed and my belly had scattered*,” that is, my children had grown many, “*he made me to him like his mother*.” It is related that she said to him: “{p|485}*I have small children; if I leave them with him they will be lost, and if I keep them with me they will go hungry*.” He said, “*I have nothing concerning your affair*.” It is related that he said to her, “*You have become unlawful to him*.” She said, “*Messenger of God, he did not mention divorce; he is the father of my children and the dearest of people to me*.” He said, “*You have become unlawful to him*.” She said, “*I complain to God of my want and my grief*.” Every time the Messenger of God ﷺ said, “You have become unlawful to him,” she cried out and complained to God; then it was revealed.

{L|About her husband|fī zawjihā} concerning his affair and its meaning. “*GOD is Hearer, Seer*”: it is sound for Him to hear everything that can be heard and see everything that can be seen.

{Q} What is the meaning of *qad* in His saying “*God has heard*”? {A} Its meaning is expectation, because the Messenger of God ﷺ and the woman who disputed were expecting God to hear her disputing and her complaint and to send down about it what would relieve her.

::: passage 58:2-4 | 4:485-489

{L|Those among you who estrange their wives|alladhīna yuẓāhirūna minkum} in “*of you*” is a rebuke to the Arabs and a shaming of their custom of *ẓihār*, because it was one of the oaths of the people of their Jāhiliyya alone, not of the other nations. “*They are not their mothers*”: it is also read in the nominative, on the two dialects, that of the Ḥijāz and that of Tamīm. In the reading of Ibn Masʿūd: *bi-ummahātihim*, and adding the *bāʾ* belongs to the dialect of those who use the accusative. The meaning is that whoever says to his wife, “You are to me like my mother’s back,” in this speech of his joins the wife to the mother and makes her like her; and this is a false likening, because the two states are wholly different.

{L|Their mothers are the women who gave birth to them|in ummahātuhum illā llāʾī waladnahum} He means that mothers in the real sense are only those who give birth, and others are joined to them by entering under their rule. So foster mothers are mothers because, {p|486}having suckled, they have entered by suckling under the rule of mothers; and likewise the wives of the Messenger of God ﷺ are the mothers of the believers, because God has forbidden the community to marry them, and so they have entered under the rule of mothers. As for wives, they are the furthest thing from motherhood, because they are not mothers in the real sense and do not enter under the rule of mothers. So the saying of the one who declares *ẓihār* is “*a hateful saying*,” which reality rejects and the rulings of the law reject, “*and a falsehood*,” a false lie turned from the truth. “*GOD is Pardoner, Forgiver*” for what is past of it, when it is repented of and not gone back to.

{L|Those who estrange their wives in this manner, then reconcile thereafter|wa-lladhīna yuẓāhirūna min nisāʾihim thumma yaʿūdūna li-mā qālū} He means: those whose custom it was to say this hateful saying, then cut it off by Islam, and then go back to the like of it: the expiation of the one who goes back is to free a slave and then touch the woman against whom he declared *ẓihār*; touching her is not lawful to him until he has first made the expiation. Another way: “*then go back on what they said*” means: then make amends for what they said, because the one who makes amends for a matter goes back to it; from this is the proverb, “Rain went back over what it had spoiled,” that is, it made amends for it by setting it right. The meaning is that the making of amends for this saying and putting it right is by expiating, so that their state goes back to what it was before the *ẓihār*. A third way is that by “*what they said*” is meant what they have made unlawful {p|487}for themselves by the wording of *ẓihār*, putting the saying in the place of what it was said of, like what we mentioned on the saying of God Most High “*Then we inherit everything he possessed*” (Q 19:80); the meaning is then: then they want to go back to touching. Touching is taking pleasure in her by intercourse, or touching with desire, or looking at her private part with desire.

{L|That|dhālikum} the ruling, “*you are admonished by it*,” because the ruling of expiation is a proof that an offense was committed; so you must take admonition from this ruling, so that you do not go back to *ẓihār*, and fear God’s punishment for it.

{Q} Is *ẓihār* sound with other than this wording? {A} Yes, when in place of “you” one puts a part of her by which the whole is expressed, such as the head, the face, the neck or the private part; or in place of the back another part of the mother that is unlawful to look at, such as the belly or the thigh; and in place of the mother a woman of the womb unlawful to him, by descent, by suckling, by marriage or by intercourse. Such as saying, “You are to me like the back of my sister by suckling, or {p|488}of my paternal aunt by descent, or of my son’s wife, or my father’s, or my wife’s mother, or her daughter”: then he has declared *ẓihār*. That is the doctrine of Abū Ḥanīfa and his followers, and the like is from al-Ḥasan, al-Nakhaʿī, al-Zuhrī, al-Awzāʿī, al-Thawrī and others. Al-Shāfiʿī said: *ẓihār* is only by the mother alone; that is the view of Qatāda and al-Shaʿbī. From al-Shaʿbī: God did not forget to mention daughters, sisters, paternal aunts and maternal aunts, since He told that *ẓihār* is only by mothers who give birth, not by foster mothers. From some: the back must be mentioned for it to be *ẓihār*.

{Q} If the one who declares *ẓihār* refuses to expiate, may the wife take him to court? {A} She may. The judge must force him to expiate and imprison him. There is no expiation that one is forced to and imprisoned for except the expiation of *ẓihār* alone, because he harms her by leaving the expiation and holding back from enjoyment, so he is bound to give her her due.

{Q} And if he touches before he expiates? {A} He must ask forgiveness and not do it again until he expiates; because it is related that Salama ibn Ṣakhr al-Bayāḍī said to the Messenger of God ﷺ, “*I declared* ẓihār *against my wife, then I saw her anklet on a moonlit night and lay with her*.” He ﵊ said, “*Ask forgiveness of your Lord, and do not do it again until you expiate*.”

{Q} What slave suffices in the expiation of *ẓihār*? {A} A Muslim and an unbeliever alike, because it is unrestricted in the verse. With al-Shāfiʿī only a believer suffices, because of the saying of God Most High in the expiation for killing, “*he shall atone by freeing a believing slave*” (Q 4:92). A slave woman who has borne her master a child does not suffice, nor the slave promised freedom at his master’s death, nor the slave under contract who has paid something; if he has paid nothing, it is allowed. With al-Shāfiʿī it is not allowed.

{Q} And if he frees part of a slave, or fasts part of the fast, and then touches? {A} He must start again, whether he touched by day or by night, forgetting or on purpose, according to Abū Ḥanīfa. With Abū Yūsuf and Muḥammad, freeing part of a slave is freeing all of him, so it suffices him; and if the touching spoils the fast he starts over, and otherwise he builds on what he has done.

{Q} How much is the poor man given in the feeding? {A} Half a *ṣāʿ* of wheat, or a *ṣāʿ* of anything else, according to Abū Ḥanīfa; and according to al-Shāfiʿī, a *mudd* of the food of his land on which it lives.

{Q} Why is touching not mentioned with the expiation by feeding as it is mentioned with the other two? {A} There is a difference over that. According to Abū Ḥanīfa there is no difference among the three expiations in its being required that they come before touching; it was left unmentioned at the feeding only to show that if it happens in the midst of the feeding he does not start again, as he starts the fast again if it happens in its midst. According to others, it was not mentioned to show that {p|489}expiating before it and after it is the same.

{Q} To what does the pronoun in “*before resuming their sexual relations*” refer? {A} To what the speech points to, the man who declares *ẓihār* and the woman against whom he declares it.

{L|That|dhālika} the making plain and the teaching of the rulings and drawing attention to them, “*You shall believe in GOD and His messenger*” in acting on His laws that He laid down, of *ẓihār* and others, and rejecting what you were on in your Jāhiliyya. “*These are GOD’s laws*,” which may not be overstepped. “*The disbelievers*,” who do not follow them and do not act on them, “*have incurred a painful retribution*.”

{L|Fight|yuḥāddūna} are hostile to and set themselves against. “*Are committed to defeat*” (*kubitū*): they are disgraced and destroyed, “*like their previous counterparts were committed to defeat*,” of the enemies of the messengers. It is said that their abasement on the day of the Trench is meant. “*We have sent down clear proofs*” that point to the truthfulness of the Messenger and the soundness of what he brought. “*And for the unbelievers*” in these signs “*is a humiliating punishment*” that takes away their might and their pride.

{L|The day will come when GOD will resurrect them|yawma yabʿathuhumu} put in the accusative through “*for them*,” or through “*humiliating*,” or through an implied “remember,” to make the day great. “*All*” (*jamīʿan*): all of them, none of them left unraised; or gathered in one state, as you say, “a tribe gathered together.” “*Then He will tell them what they did*,” to shame them and rebuke them and make their state known; at that they will wish to be hurried to the Fire, for the disgrace that comes on them before the eyes of the witnesses. “*GOD has recorded everything*” (*aḥṣāhu llāh*): He has taken it in by number; nothing of it has escaped Him. “*And they forgot it*,” because they took it lightly when they committed it and paid it no heed, being so used to disobedience; only weighty matters are kept in mind.

::: passage 58:7-7 | 4:489-490

{L|No three people can conspire|mā yakūnu} from the complete *kāna*. It is read with *yāʾ* and with *tāʾ*: the *yāʾ* on the view that the femininity of *najwā* is not real and that *min* comes between; or on the view that the meaning is “there is not anything of private talk.” *Al-najwā* is talking privately; so it must either be annexed to “*three*,” that is, of the private talk of three persons; or described by it, that is, of people of private talk who are three, with “people” dropped; or they were themselves made private talk, for emphasis, as in the saying of God Most High “*they conferred together*” (Q 12:80). Ibn Abī ʿAbla read *thalāthatan* and *khamsatan*, in the accusative as a circumstantial clause, with “they talk privately” implied, because *najwā* points to it; or {p|490}taking *najwā* as “those who talk privately,” with the accusative being from the pronoun hidden in it.

{Q} What called for singling out three and five? {A} There are two ways in it. One: some of the hypocrites formed circles to talk privately, to enrage the believers, in these two numbers, three and five. So it was said: no three of them nor five talk privately, as you see them talking privately so, “*nor less than that*” than their two numbers “*nor more*,” but God is with them, hearing what they say. It is related from Ibn ʿAbbās ﵁ that it was revealed about Rabīʿa and Ḥabīb, the sons of ʿAmr, and Ṣafwān ibn Umayya. They were talking one day, and one of them said, “*Do you think God knows what we say?*” Another said, “*He knows some and does not know some*.” The third said, “*If He knows some, He knows all of it*”; and he spoke truly, because one who knows some things without a cause knows them all, since His being knowing without a cause holds for Him with everything that can be known. The second: He meant to mention what custom has run on of the numbers of the people of private talk and those who go apart to consult. Those called on for that are not just anyone, but a chosen band of those with intellect and understanding, and a group of the people of judgment and experience; the first of their number is two and upward, to five, to six, to whatever the situation requires and the judgment of what is right decides. Do you not see how ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb ﵁ left the matter as a consultation among six and did not go beyond them to a seventh? So He, mighty and exalted is He, mentioned three and five, and said “*nor less than that*,” which pointed to two and four, and said “*nor more*,” which pointed to what follows this number and is near it.

In the codex of ʿAbd Allāh: “*but God is their fourth, nor four but God is their fifth, nor five but God is their sixth, nor fewer than that nor more but God is with them when they confer*.” *Wa-lā adnā min dhālika wa-lā akthara* is also read, in the accusative, with *lā* as the negation of the kind. *Wa-lā aktharu* may also be in the nominative, coordinated on the place of *lā* with *adnā*, as you say *lā ḥawla wa-lā quwwatun illā bi-llāh*, with *fatḥa* on *ḥawla* and the nominative in *quwwatun*. Both may also be in the nominative as subjects, as you say *lā ḥawlun wa-lā quwwatun illā bi-llāh*; or their nominative may be by coordination on the place of “*of private talk*,” as though it were said: there is not fewer nor more but He is with them. They may also be in the genitive, coordinated on *najwā*, as though it were said: there is not of fewer nor of more but He is with them. *Wa-lā akbara*, with *bāʾ*, is also read. The meaning of His being with them is that He knows what they talk of privately and what they are about is not hidden from Him; so it is as though He were watching them and present with them, though He is exalted above place and watching. *Thumma yunbiʾuhum*, light, is also read.

::: passage 58:8-8 | 4:490-491

{p|491}The Jews and the hypocrites used to talk privately among themselves and wink at one another with their eyes when they saw the believers, wanting to enrage them. The Messenger of God ﷺ forbade them, and they went back to the like of what they had done. Their private talk was of what is sin and aggression against the believers, and of enjoining one another to disobey the Messenger and go against him. *Yantajūna bi-l-ithmi wa-l-ʿidwāni*, with *kasra* on the *ʿayn*, and *maʿṣiyāti l-rasūli*, is also read.

{L|They greet you with a greeting other than that decreed by GOD|ḥayyawka bi-mā lam yuḥayyika bihi llāhu} He means that in greeting you they say, “Death to you, Muḥammad!” *Al-sām* is death. God Most High says: “*peace be upon His servants whom He chose*” (Q 27:59), and “*O Messenger*,” and “*O Prophet*.” “*GOD will not punish us for our utterances*” (*law-lā yuʿadhdhibunā llāhu bi-mā naqūl*, “why does God not punish us for what we say?”): they used to say, “What is the matter with him, if he is a prophet, that he does not pray against us so that God punishes us for what we say?” So God Most High said: “*Hell is enough for them*” as punishment.

{L|O you who believe|yā ayyuhā lladhīna āmanū} an address to the hypocrites, who believed with their tongues. It may also be to the believers: that is, when you talk privately, do not be like those in their private talk of evil, “*You shall confer to work righteousness and piety*.” From the Prophet ﷺ: “*When you are three, let not two talk privately apart from their companion, for that grieves him*”; it is also related, “apart from the third.” *Fa-lā tanājaw* is also read; and from Ibn Masʿūd: *idhā ntajaytum fa-lā tantajū*.

{L|Secret conspiracy is the devil’s idea|innamā l-najwā} the article points to private talk of sin and aggression, as shown by the saying of God Most High “*to hurt those who believed*.” The meaning is that Satan makes it fair to them, so it is as though it were from him, to enrage those who believe and grieve them. “*He cannot*,” Satan or the grief, “*hurt them against GOD’s will*.”

{Q} How can Satan or grief not harm them except by God’s leave? {A} In their private talk and winking they used to make the believers imagine that their raiders had been beaten and that their relatives had been killed. So He said: Satan or grief does not harm them by that which they make them imagine except by God’s leave, that is, by His will, which is that He decrees death for their relatives or defeat for the raiders. *Li-yuḥzina* and *li-yaḥzuna* are also read.

{L|Please make room|tafassaḥū fī l-majālisi} make space in it, and let each of you make room for others, from their saying *afsiḥ ʿannī*, that is, move away; and do not crowd together. *Tafāsaḥū* is also read. What is meant is the sitting of the Messenger of God; they used to crowd together in it, vying to be near him and eager to hear his speech. It is also said: it is the place from among the places of fighting, which are the posts of the raiders, as in the saying of God Most High “*positions for battle*” (Q 3:121). *Fī l-majālisi* is read. It is said: a man would come to the line and say, “Make room,” and they would refuse, being eager for martyrdom. *Fī l-majlasi*, with *fatḥa* on the *lām*, is also read, which is sitting: that is, make room in your sitting and do not press on one another. “*GOD will then make room for you*”: unrestricted, in everything in which people seek room, place, provision, the breast, the grave and other things.

{L|Get up and move|unshuzū} rise, to make room for those who come; or rise from the sitting of the Messenger of God when you are commanded to rise from it, and do not weary the Messenger of God by staying fixed in it; or rise to prayer, to striving and to works of good when you are called to rise, and do not hang back or fall short. “*GOD raises*” the believers for obeying His commands and the commands of His Messenger, and the learned among them in particular, “*by degrees*.” “*GOD is fully Cognizant of everything you do*” is read with *tāʾ* and with *yāʾ*. From ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd ﵁: when he recited it he would say, “*O people, understand this verse, and let it make you desire knowledge*.” From the Prophet ﷺ: “*Between the learned man and the worshiper are a hundred degrees; between each two degrees is the run of a lean racing horse for seventy years*.” From him ﵇: “*The excellence of the learned man over the worshiper is like the excellence of the moon on the night it is full over the other stars*.” And from him {p|493}﵇: “*On the Day of Resurrection three will intercede: the prophets, then the learned, then the martyrs*.” How great, then, is a rank that stands between prophethood and martyrdom, by the witness of the Messenger of God! From Ibn ʿAbbās: Solomon was given the choice between knowledge, wealth and kingship, and he chose knowledge, and was given wealth and kingship with it. He ﵇ said: “*God revealed to Abraham: ‘Abraham, I am knowing, and I love every knowing one.’*” From one of the wise: “*Would that I knew what he has gained who has missed knowledge, and what he has missed who has gained knowledge*.” From al-Aḥnaf: “*The learned were almost lords; and every might not made firm by knowledge comes in the end to some abasement*.” From al-Zubayrī: “*Knowledge is male, and only the males among men love it*.”

{L|Before you do so|bayna yaday najwākum} borrowed from one who has two hands. The meaning is “before your private talk,” as in the saying of ʿUmar: “*Of the best things the Arabs were given is poetry; a man puts it in front of his need, and with it seeks rain from the generous and brings down the mean*”; he means before his need. “*That*” putting forward “*is better for you*” in your religion, “*and purer*,” because alms are a purification. It is related that people talked privately with the Messenger of God ﷺ so much about what they wanted that they wearied and vexed him. It was wanted that they should hold back from that, so they were commanded that whoever wanted to talk privately with him should put forward alms before his private talk. ʿAlī ﵁ said: “*When it was revealed, the Messenger of God ﷺ called me and said, ‘What do you say to a dinar?’ {p|494}I said, ‘They cannot bear it.’ He said, ‘How much?’ I said, ‘A grain, or a barleycorn.’ He said, ‘You are a man of small wants.’*” When they saw that, it was hard on them, and they held back and stopped: the poor man for his want, and the rich man for his avarice. It is said that it lasted ten nights and then was abrogated; it is also said that it was only an hour of a day. From ʿAlī ﵁: “*In the Book of God is a verse that no one acted on before me and no one will act on after me. I had a dinar, and I changed it, and whenever I talked privately with him I gave a dirham in alms*.” Al-Kalbī said: he gave it in alms for ten words he asked the Messenger of God ﷺ. From Ibn ʿUmar: “*ʿAlī had three things; had I had one of them, it would have been dearer to me than red camels: his marrying Fāṭima, his being given the banner on the day of Khaybar, and the verse of private talk*.” Ibn ʿAbbās said: it is abrogated by the verse after it; it is also said, it is abrogated by the alms tax.

{L|If you failed to give to charity|a-ashfaqtum} did you fear putting forward alms, because of the spending in it that you dislike, and because Satan promises you poverty and commands you to indecency? “*So since you did not*” do what you were commanded and it was hard on you, “*GOD accepts your repentance*,” excused you and granted you leave not to do it, then do not fall short in prayer, the alms tax and the other acts of obedience. “*Of what you do*” is read with *tāʾ* and with *yāʾ*.

{p|495}The hypocrites used to take as friends the Jews, who are those with whom God is angry in the saying of God Most High “*condemned by GOD after incurring His wrath*” (Q 5:60); they gave them their loyalty and carried the believers’ secrets to them. “*They are not of you*,” Muslims, “*nor with them*,” nor of the Jews, as in the saying of God Most High “*They waver in between, neither belonging to this group, nor that group*” (Q 4:143). “*They deliberately swear lies!*”: that is, they say, “By God, we are Muslims,” and so they swear to a lie, which is claiming Islam, “*while they know*” that what they swear to is a pure lie.

{Q} What is the use of His saying “*while they know*”? {A} A lie is a report that does not agree with what it reports, whether the reporter knows it or does not. The meaning is that they are the ones who report, their report goes against what they report about, and they know it and do it on purpose, like one who swears the immersing oath.

It is said: ʿAbd Allāh ibn Nabtal the hypocrite used to sit with the Messenger of God ﷺ and then carry his talk to the Jews. While the Messenger of God was in one of his rooms, he said to his companions, “*A man will come in to you now whose heart is the heart of a tyrant and who looks with the eye of a devil*.” Ibn Nabtal came in, and he was blue-eyed. The Prophet ﷺ said to him, “*Why do you and your companions revile me?*” He swore by God that he had not done it. He ﵇ said, “*You did*.” He went and brought his companions, and they swore by God that they had not reviled him; then it was revealed.

{L|A severe retribution|ʿadhāban shadīdan} a kind of punishment grown grave. “*Miserable indeed is what they used to do*”: He means that they were set in evil works through the long time past, persisting in them. Or it is the report of what will be said to them in the Hereafter. *Īmānahum*, with *kasra*, is also read: that is, they took their oaths that they swore, or the faith they showed, “*as a shield*,” that is, a cover with which they hid from the believers and from being killed by them. “*So they turned*” people, while they were safe and sound, “*from the path of GOD*.” They used to hold back those they met from entering Islam and weaken the matter of the Muslims in their eyes. God promised them the humiliating, disgracing punishment only for their unbelief and their turning others away, as in the saying of God Most High “*Those who disbelieve and repel from the path of GOD, we augment their retribution by adding more retribution*” (Q 16:88).

{L|Against God|mina llāhi} against God’s punishment, “*against GOD*” (*shayʾan*, “at all”): a little availing. It is related that one of them said: “{p|496}*We will surely be helped on the Day of Resurrection by ourselves, our wealth and our children*.” “*They will swear*” to God that they are Muslims, in the Hereafter, “*as they swear to you*” in this world about that; “*and they think they have something*” of benefit. He means: what is to be wondered at is not their swearing to you, for you are human beings from whom hidden things are hidden, and they have some benefit in that, keeping their lives safe and drawing worldly gains, and they do it in an abode in which they are not forced to know what they are promised. What is to be wondered at is their swearing to God, the Knower of the unseen and the seen, with no benefit, and being forced to know what the messengers warned them of. What is meant is to describe them as sunk deep in their hypocrisy and hardened in it, and that it remains in them after their death and their raising and does not fade, as He said: “*If they go back, they will commit exactly the same crimes*” (Q 6:28). The learned have differed about their lying in the Hereafter, and the Qurʾān speaks of its being so in an open speaking, as you see in this verse and in the saying of God Most High “*By GOD our Lord, we never were idol worshipers.’ Note how they lied to themselves, and how the idols they had invented have abandoned them*” (Q 6:23–24). Like their thinking they have some benefit when they swear is their asking the believers to wait so that they may borrow from their light, thinking that outward faith is something that profits them. It is said that at that their mouths will be sealed. “*Indeed, they are the real liars*”: He means that they are the utmost, beyond which there is nothing to aim at, in telling lies, since their state in it is the same in this world and in the Hereafter.

{L|The devil has possessed them|istaḥwadha ʿalayhimu} has taken hold of them; from *ḥādha l-ḥimāru l-ʿānata*, when the ass gathered the herd and drove it, mastering it; from this is “he was *aḥwadhī*, a man without equal.” It is one of those that come in the original form, like *istaṣwaba* and *istanwaqa*. That is: “*Satan*” owns them, because they obey him in everything he wants of them, until he has made them his subjects and his party. “*So he made them forget*” to remember God at all, neither with their hearts nor with their tongues. Abū ʿUbayda said: the party of Satan is his troops.

::: passage 58:22-22 | 4:496-497

{p|497}{L|You will not find people|lā tajidu qawman} of the kind of imagery. He makes it imagined that it is a thing ruled out, impossible, that you should find a believing people taking the associators as friends. The aim of it is that this ought not to be, and that its due is to be ruled out and not found in any case: to make the prohibition of it forceful, to restrain from taking part in it, and to enjoin standing firm in keeping away from God’s enemies, keeping distant from them and guarding against mixing and living with them. He added emphasis and force to that by His saying “*even if they were their parents*,” by His saying “*For these, He decrees faith into their hearts*,” and by setting against His saying “*These are the party of the devil*” His saying “*These are the party of GOD*.” So you will find nothing that enters more deeply into sincerity than taking God’s friends as friends and God’s enemies as enemies; rather, it is sincerity itself.

{L|He decrees faith into their hearts|kataba fī qulūbihimu l-īmāna} He has set it firm in them by the success He granted them in it and the opening of their breasts to it. “*And supports them with inspiration from Him*” (*wa-ayyadahum bi-rūḥin minhu*): with a kindness from Him by which their hearts were given life. The pronoun may also be for faith: that is, with a spirit from faith, on the view that it is itself a spirit by which hearts live. From al-Thawrī: “*They used to think it was revealed about those who keep company with the ruler*.” From ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz ibn Abī Rawwād: al-Manṣūr met him in the circumambulation, and when he recognized him he fled from him and recited it. From the Prophet ﷺ: he used to say, “*O God, let no wicked or corrupt man have a favor from me, for I have found in what was revealed to me, ‘You will not find a people…’*”

It is related that it was revealed about Abū Bakr ﵁. That is, Abū Quḥāfa reviled the Messenger of God ﷺ, and he struck him a blow from which he fell. The Messenger of God said, “*Did you do that?*” He said, “*Yes*.” He said, “*Do not do it again*.” He said, “*By God, had the sword been near me I would have killed him*.” It is also said: about Abū ʿUbayda ibn al-Jarrāḥ, who killed his father ʿAbd Allāh al-Jarrāḥ on the day of Uḥud; about Abū Bakr, who called his son to single combat on the day of Badr and said to the Messenger of God, “*Let me charge in the first troop*”; he said, “*Let us still have the enjoyment of you, Abū Bakr. Do you not know that you are to me as my hearing and my sight?*”; about Muṣʿab ibn ʿUmayr, who killed his brother ʿUbayd ibn ʿUmayr on the day of Uḥud; about ʿUmar, who killed his maternal uncle al-ʿĀṣ ibn Hishām on the day of Badr; and about ʿAlī, Ḥamza and ʿUbayda ibn al-Ḥārith, who killed ʿUtba and Shayba, the sons of Rabīʿa, and al-Walīd ibn ʿUtba on the day of Badr.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-Mujādila is written among the party of God on the Day of Resurrection*.”
