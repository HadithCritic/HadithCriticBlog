surah: 114
title: Sūrat al-Nās (114)
revelation: Meccan; it is also said Medinan. Six verses. [Revealed after al-Falaq (113).]

::: passage 114:1-6 | 4:823-825

It is read *qul aʿūdhu* with the *hamza* dropped and its vowel transferred to the *lām*, like *fa-khudh arbaʿa* (“so take four,” Q 2:260).

{Q} Why is it said “*Lord of the people*,” annexed to them specifically? {A} Because the seeking of refuge occurs from the evil of the one who whispers in the breasts of the people. It is as if it were said: I seek refuge from the evil of the one who whispers to people, with their Lord who owns for them their affairs, who is their God and their object of worship. It is like some clients who cry for help, when a calamity befalls them, with their master, the one they serve and the one who manages their affair.

{Q} “*King of the people*” and “*God of the people*”: what are they in relation to “*Lord of the people*”? {A} They are explanatory appositions (*ʿaṭf bayān*), as in your saying “*the biography of Abū Ḥafṣ ʿUmar al-Fārūq*.” It was clarified by “*King of the people*,” then further clarified by “*God of the people*,” because “*lord of the people*” may be said of other than Him, as in “*They have set up their religious leaders and scholars as lords, instead of GOD*” (Q 9:31), and “*king of the people*” may be said as well. But “*God of the people*” is specific, with no sharing in it, so it was made the final point of clarification.

{Q} Why was it not sufficient to mention the annexed word “*the people*” once? {A} Because explanatory apposition is for clarification, so it is a place for the explicit noun rather than the pronoun.

{L|Whisperers|al-waswās} a noun in the sense of *waswasa*, like *zalzāl* in the sense of *zalzala*. As for the verbal noun, it is *wiswās*, with *kasra*, like *zilzāl*. What is meant by it is Satan, named by the verbal noun as if he were whispering itself, because it is his craft and occupation, which he is devoted to; or else “*one possessing whispering*” is meant. *Waswasa* is the hidden sound; hence the *waswās* of ornaments.

{p|824}{L|Sneaky|al-khannās} the one whose habit is to withdraw (*yakhnis*). It is derived from *khunūs*, which is drawing back, like *al-ʿawwāj* and *al-battāt*, because of what is related from Saʿīd ibn Jubayr: when a person mentions his Lord, Satan withdraws and turns away; when he is heedless, he whispers to him.

{L|Who whisper|alladhī yuwaswis} three vowelings are possible for its position: genitive as an adjective; and nominative and accusative as a term of censure. It is good for the reciter to pause at *al-khannās* and begin *alladhī yuwaswis* on one of these two latter aspects.

{L|Be they of the jinns, or the people|min al-jinnati wa-l-nās} an explanation of the one who whispers, in that devils are of two kinds, jinn and human, as in “*human and jinn devils*” (Q 6:112). From Abū Dharr ﵁: he said to a man, “*Have you sought refuge in God from the devil of men?*” It is also possible that *min* is connected to *yuwaswis* and expresses the beginning of the span: he whispers in their breasts from the side of the jinn and from the side of men.

It is also said that “*of the jinns, or the people*” is an explanation of “*the people*,” and that the name *nās* applies to the jinn. They argued from “*a group of jinns*” (*nafarun mina l-jinn*, Q 72:1) and *rijālun* (“men”) of the jinn (Q 72:6) in Sūrat al-Jinn. They did not establish it, for the jinn were called jinn because of their concealment (*ijtinān*), and *nās* were called *nās* because of their visibility, from *īnās*, which is seeing, just as they are called *bashar*. Were *nās* to apply to both kinds, and this proved sound and established, it would not suit the eloquence of the Qurʾān and its distance from artifice.

Better than this is that “*the people*” be understood as *al-nāsī* (“the forgetful”), like “*the day will come when the caller will announce*” (*yawma yadʿu l-dāʿ*, Q 54:6) [with the final *yāʾ* dropped], as “*with the rest of the people who file*” (*min ḥaythu afāḍa l-nās*, “from where the people poured forth,” Q 2:199) is read [as *al-nāsī*]. It is then explained by “*the jinns, or the people*,” since the two weighty races are the two kinds described by forgetting the right of God the Mighty and Majestic.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*There have been revealed to me two sūras the like of which has not been revealed, and you will never recite two sūras more beloved or pleasing to God than they*,” meaning the two Muʿawwidhatayn. The two are also called *al-muqashqishatān*.

::: heading Al-Zamakhsharī’s closing prayer

{p|825}God’s servant, who stands in need of Him, says: And I seek refuge in these two [sūras], and in all the perfect and complete words of God, and I take shelter in the shade of His all-embracing, universal mercy, from everything that wounds religion and notches certainty, or turns to regret in the end, or impairs the faith that is mixed with flesh and blood. I ask Him with bowed neck and lowered gaze, my cheek laid down before His majesty, the greatest and the grandest, seeking His intercession through His light, which is grey hair in Islam, and taking as my means the repentance that purges sins, and what I have devoted myself to: my emigration to Him and my dwelling near His House, my keeping watch at Mecca and my endurance there, though my strength fails and my steps falter. Then I ask Him by the right of His straight path and His glorious, noble Qurʾān, and by the toil of the right hand and the sweat of the brow I met with in making *al-Kashshāf*, which uncovers its truths, frees [the reader] from its narrow places, shows its obscurities, and stands firm where its ground is slippery; which distills its subtle points and the fine features of its composition, and searches out its choice phrases and the jewels of its knowledge; which is packed with insights of every kind found nowhere else, and takes in the unfathomable novelties of its words and meanings, with a concision that cuts away the superfluous and avoids the forced and the tedious. Even if it held nothing but the setting out of everything according to its rule, that would be enough to make it the lost thing that exacting scholars seek, and the pearl that the divers of the seas long to find. And [I ask Him] by the honor and glory He gave me, singling me out for His favor and making me alone its author: that it was raised at my hands in the place where His glad tidings and warnings came down and His verses and sūras were revealed, in the Secure Land, in the midst of the Sanctuary and before the Sacred House, so that the interpretation took place where the revelation was found. [I ask Him] to grant me a good end, to guard me from the falls of evil, to pass over my lapses on the Day of Calling and not to shame me by them before the witnesses, and to settle me in the Abode of Permanence by His grace, through His ample bounty and abundant giving. He is the Generous, the Noble, the Kind, the Merciful.

::: heading Colophon

One copy has the following: In the author’s original, in his own hand ﵀: This copy is the first original, transcribed from the draft. It is the mother copy of *al-Kashshāf*, made in the Sanctuary, blessed, touched for its blessing, worthy that the blessings of heaven be brought down by it and rain be sought by it in a year of drought. The author’s hand finished it facing the Kaʿba, in the wing of his Sulaymāniyya house at the Ajyād gate, known as the Madrasa of al-ʿAllāma, in the forenoon of Monday, the twenty-third of Rabīʿ al-Ākhir in the year five hundred and twenty-eight, praising God for His dazzling generosity and calling down blessings on His servant and messenger, and on his family and all his Companions.
