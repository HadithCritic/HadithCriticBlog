title: Sūrat al-Qāriʿa (101)
revelation: Meccan. Eleven verses. [Revealed after Quraysh (106).]

::: passage 101:1-11 | 4:789-791

The adverb [“*the Day*”] is accusative through an implied verb that *al-qāriʿa* points to, that is: it will strike “*That is the day when the people come out like swarms of butterflies*” (*ka-l-farāshi l-mabthūth*, “like scattered moths”). He likened them to moths in their great number, their spreading, their weakness and abasement, and in their flying toward the caller from every side, as moths fly toward a fire. Jarīr said: “*As far as I know, al-Farazdaq and his people are like moths that crowd the fire of the man warming himself*.”

{p|790}Among their proverbs: “*weaker than a moth*,” “*more abased*,” and “*more ignorant*.” It is called *farāsh* because of its spreading (*tafarrush*) and scattering. He likened the mountains to *ʿihn*, which is wool dyed in many colors, because mountains are of many colors, and to the carded kind of it, because their parts come apart. Ibn Masʿūd read *ka-l-ṣūf* (“like wool”).

{L|Weights|al-mawāzīn} the plural of *mawzūn*, which is the deed that has weight and standing with God; or the plural of *mīzān*. Their being heavy is their outweighing. Of this is the counsel of Abū Bakr to ʿUmar ﵄ in his testament to him: “*Those whose scales are heavy on the Day of Resurrection are heavy only because they followed the truth and found it heavy in this world, and a scale in which nothing but good deeds is placed deserves to be heavy. Those whose scales are light are light only because they followed falsehood and found it light in this world, and a scale in which nothing but bad deeds is placed deserves to be light*.”

{L|His destiny is lowly|fa-ummuhu hāwiya} from their saying, when they pray for a man’s ruin, *hawat ummuhu* (“his mother has fallen”), because when he falls (*hawā*), that is, falls and perishes, his mother has fallen in bereavement and grief. He said: “*His mother has fallen! What does the dawn send out in the morning, and what does the night bring back when it returns?*” It is as if it were said: as for him whose scales are light, he has perished. It is also said that *hāwiya* is one of the names of the Fire, as if it were the deep Fire, because the people of the Fire fall a long way down into it, as it is related: “*He falls in it for seventy autumns*.” That is, his refuge is the Fire. The refuge was called a mother by comparison, because the mother is the child’s refuge and shelter. From Qatāda: *fa-ummuhu hāwiya* means the crown (*umm*) of his head falls to the bottom of Hell, because he is thrown into it head first.

{L|What it is|mā hiyah} *hiya* is the pronoun of the calamity {p|791}indicated by His saying *fa-ummuhu hāwiya*, on the first interpretation; or the pronoun of *hāwiya*, the *hāʾ* being the pausal *hāʾ* (*hāʾ al-sakt*), which the reciter drops when he reads on without pausing. It is also said that the reciter should not read on without pausing, lest reading on drop it, since it is written in the codex. Keeping it while reading on has also been allowed.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-Qāriʿa, God will make his scale heavy by it on the Day of Resurrection*.”
