{L|You shall warn|an andhir} its origin is “with that you warn” (*bi-an andhir*); the preposition was dropped and the verb joined directly. It is the *an* that puts the verb in the subjunctive, and the meaning is: We sent him with Our saying to him, “Warn”; that is, We sent him with the command to warn. It may also be explanatory, because sending has in it the meaning of saying. Ibn Masʿūd read *andhir* without *an*, meaning a saying. “*That you shall worship GOD*” (*an uʿbudū llāha*) is like *an andhir* in both faces.

{Q} How did He say “*and He will defer you*,” while telling that the term cannot be deferred? Is this anything but a contradiction? {A} God decreed, for example, that the people of Noah, if they believed, would live a thousand years, and if they stayed in their unbelief He would destroy them at the head of nine hundred. So it was said to them: believe, and He will defer you to a named term, that is, to a time God has named and set as a limit you reach and do not pass, which is the longer time, the completion of the thousand. Then He told that when that term, the limit, comes, it is not deferred as this time is deferred, and you have no device; so hurry in the times of respite and deferment.

::: passage 71:5-20 | 4:615-618

{p|616}{L|Night and day|laylan wa-nahāran} persistently, without slackening, taking up all the times with it. “*But my invitation only increased*”: he made the calling the doer of the increase in flight; and the meaning is that they increased in flight at it, because it was the cause of the increase. Like it is “*it actually added unholiness to their unholiness*” (Q 9:125), “*it did strengthen the faith*” (Q 9:124). “*To be forgiven by You*”: that they might repent of their unbelief and You forgive them. He mentioned the effect, which is their pure share, so that their turning away from it would be uglier. They stopped up their hearing from listening to the call; “*covered themselves with their clothes*” and covered themselves with them, as though they sought that their garments should cover them, or that they should cover them, so as not to see him, out of hatred of looking at the face of one who gave them sincere counsel in God’s religion. It is also said: so that he would not know them; and that is supported by the saying of God Most High “*they hide their innermost thoughts, as if to keep Him from knowing them. In fact, as they cover themselves with their clothes*” (Q 11:5). *Al-iṣrār* is from *aṣarra l-ḥimāru ʿalā l-ʿāna*, when the wild ass pricked up his ears and came at the herd, biting it and driving it; it was borrowed for coming at sins and bending over them. “*And turned arrogant*”: and pride kept them from following Noah and obeying him. Mentioning the verbal noun is emphasis, and points to how far they went in their arrogance and insolence.

{Q} He mentioned that he called them by night and day, then called them openly, then called them in secret and in public; so they must be three different callings for the joining to be sound. {A} He ﵊ did as one does who commands what is decent and forbids what is wrong: beginning with the lightest and rising to the harder and harder. So he opened with sincere counsel in secret; and when they did not accept, he followed with openness; and when that had no effect, he made a third by joining secret and public. The meaning of “*then*” is to point to the distance between the states, because openness is harsher than secrecy, and joining the two matters is harsher than either alone. “*Openly*” {p|617}is in the accusative by “*I called them*,” as the verbal noun is put in the accusative, because openness is one of the two kinds of calling; so it was put in the accusative by it as *al-qurfuṣāʾ* is put in the accusative by “he sat,” for being one of the kinds of sitting. Or because by “*I called them*” he meant “I was open with them.” It may also be a description of the verbal noun of “called,” in the sense of a calling made openly, that is, done in the open; or a verbal noun in the place of a circumstantial clause, that is, being open.

He commanded them to ask forgiveness, which is repenting of unbelief and sins, and put before them the promise of what falls deeper in their souls and is dearer to them, of present benefits and immediate gains, to make them desire faith and its blessings, and obedience and its fruits, of the good of both abodes; as He said: “*Additionally, you get something you truly love: support from GOD*” (Q 61:13), “*Had the people of those communities believed and turned righteous, we would have showered them with blessings*” (Q 7:96), “*If only they would uphold the Torah and the Gospel, and what is sent down to them herein from their Lord, they would be showered with blessings from above them*” (Q 5:66), “*If they remain on the right path, we will bless them with abundant water*” (Q 72:16). It is said: when they denied him after the long repeating of the call, God held back the rain from them and made their women’s wombs barren for forty years; and it is related: seventy. So he promised them that if they believed, God Most High would provide them plenty and remove from them what they were in. From ʿUmar ﵁: he went out to pray for rain, and did no more than ask forgiveness. It was said to him, “*We did not see you pray for rain!*” He said, “*I prayed for rain by the stars of the sky by which the rain is brought down*.” He likened asking forgiveness to the true rain-stars that do not fail. From al-Ḥasan: a man complained to him of drought, and he said, “*Ask God’s forgiveness*”; another complained of poverty, another of having few children, and another of the small yield of his land; and he commanded them all to ask forgiveness. Al-Rabīʿ ibn Ṣubayḥ said to him, “*Men came to you complaining of different things and asking for different kinds, and you commanded them all to ask forgiveness!*” And he recited this verse to him.

{L|He will then shower you|al-samāʾ} is the canopy, because the rain comes down from it to the clouds. It may also be that the clouds or the rain are meant, from his saying, “*When the sky came down on a people’s land*.” *Al-midrār* is much in flowing; and *mifʿāl* is among what the masculine and feminine are equal in, as in their saying, “a man or a woman *miʿṭār*,” perfumed, and “*mitfāl*,” unperfumed. “*Orchards*”: orchards. “*Why should you not strive to reverence GOD?*” (*mā lakum lā tarjūna li-llāhi waqāran*, “why do you not hope for dignity from God?”): you do not look for His honoring, that is, His making great. And the meaning is: what is the matter with you, that you are not in a state in which you look for God’s making you great in the abode of reward? “*From God*” makes clear who does the honoring; {p|618}and if it came after, it would be a complement of “*dignity*.” His saying “*He is the One who created you in stages*” is in the place of a circumstantial clause, as though He said: what is the matter with you, that you do not believe in God, when the state is this, and it is a state that requires faith in Him, because He created you in stages, that is, by turns: He created you first dust, then created you drops of sperm, then created you clots, then created you lumps of flesh, then created you bones and flesh, then brought you into being as another creation. Or: do you not fear God’s forbearance and His leaving off hastening the punishment, and so believe? It is also said: what is the matter with you, that you do not fear God’s greatness? From Ibn ʿAbbās: you do not fear God’s outcome; because the outcome is the state of things settling and reward and punishment becoming fixed; from *waqara*, when it became fixed and settled.

He alerted them to look at themselves first, because they are the nearest thing to be looked at, of theirs; then to look at the world and what wonders are shaped in it, witnessing to the Maker whose power and knowledge dazzle, of the heavens and the earth, the sun and the moon. “*Therein*”: in the heavens, while it is in the nearest heaven; because there is a connection between the heavens, in that they are in layers, so it may be said, “In them is such-and-such,” even if it is not in all of them, as it is said, “In the city is such-and-such,” while it is in one of its quarters. From Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn ʿUmar ﵄: the sun and the moon have their faces toward the heaven and their backs toward the earth. “*Placed the sun to be a lamp*”: the people of this world see by its light as the people of the house see by the light of the lamp what they need to see; and the moon is not like that; it is only a light that does not reach the strength of the sun’s radiance. Like it is the saying of God Most High “*He is the One who rendered the sun radiant, and the moon a light*” (Q 10:5); and radiance is stronger than light.

Making to grow was borrowed for bringing into being, as it is said, “God sowed you for good”; and this borrowing points more to coming into being in time, because if they are plants they are without doubt brought into being in time, as plants come into being. From it the Ḥashwiyya were called *al-nābita* and *al-nawābit*, the sprouts, for their doctrine coming into being in Islam without their having any early standing in it. And from it is their saying, “So-and-so sprouted,” of one of the renegades. And the meaning is: He made you grow, and you grew a growing. Or it is put in the accusative by “*GOD germinated you*,” for its containing the meaning of “you grew.” “*Then He returns you into it*,” buried, then “*He will surely bring you out*” on the Day of Resurrection; and He stressed it with the verbal noun, as though He said: He brings you out truly and without doubt. He made it “*a carpet*,” spread out, on which you turn about as a man turns about on his carpet. “*Roads*” (*fijājan*): broad, open.

::: passage 71:21-24 | 4:619-620

{L|And followed|wa-ttabaʿū} their chiefs set at their head, owners of wealth and children, and they kept to what these had laid down for them of holding fast to the worship of idols. He made their wealth and children, which increased them only in standing and benefit in this world, an increase of “*loss*” in the Hereafter; and He made that run the course of a description bound to them and a mark by which they are known, to make it true and fix it, and to make void what is besides it. *Wa-wulduhu* is also read, with *ḍamma* and with *kasra* on the *wāw*. “*They schemed*” is joined to “*did not increase him*,” and the pronoun was made plural while it goes back to “*whoever*,” because that is in the sense of a plural. The schemers are the chiefs; and their scheme is their devising in religion, their plotting against Noah, their inciting people to harm him, and their keeping them from leaning to him and listening to him; and their saying to them, “Do not leave your gods for the worship of Noah’s Lord.” “*terrible schemes*” (*makran kubbāran*): read light and heavy. *Kubār* is greater than *kabīr*, and *kubbār* is greater than *kubār*; and like it are *ṭuwāl* and *ṭuwwāl*.

{L|Do not abandon Wadd|wa-lā tadharunna waddan} as though these named ones were the greatest of their idols and the mightiest in their eyes, so they singled them out after their saying “*Do not abandon your gods*.” These idols passed from the people of Noah to the Arabs: Wadd belonged to Kalb, Suwāʿ to Hamdān, Yaghūth to Madhḥij, Yaʿūq to Murād, and Nasr to Ḥimyar; and for that reason the Arabs named themselves ʿAbd Wadd and ʿAbd Yaghūth. It is also said: they are names of righteous men. It is also said: of the children of Adam, who died; and Iblīs said to those after them, “If you made images of them, you could look at them”; and they did. When those died, he said to those after them, “They used to worship them”; and they worshiped them. It is also said: Wadd was in the form of a man, Suwāʿ in the form of a woman, Yaghūth in the form of a lion, Yaʿūq in the form of a horse, and Nasr in the form of a vulture. *Wuddan* is also read, with *ḍamma* on the *wāw*. Al-Aʿmash read *wa-lā yaghūthan wa-yaʿūqan*, declined; and this is a difficult reading, because whether they are Arabic or foreign, the two causes of indeclinability are in them: either definiteness and the verbal pattern, or definiteness and foreignness. Perhaps he aimed at pairing and so declined them, for their coming with their sisters declined, *waddan*, *suwāʿan* and *nasran*; as *wa-ḍuḥāhā* is read with inclination, for its coming among the inclined words, for pairing.

{L|They misled many|wa-qad aḍallū} the pronoun is for the chiefs. And its meaning is: they have led “*many*” astray before these who were charged to hold fast to the worship of idols; these are not the first they led astray. Or: they have led many astray by their leading astray, that is, these who lead astray are many. It may also be for the idols, as in the saying of God Most High “*they have misled so many people*” (Q 14:36).

{Q} To what is His saying {p|620}“*Therefore, let the wicked plunge deeper into loss*” joined? {A} To His saying “*My Lord, they disobeyed me*,” on the relating of the speech of Noah ﵇ after “*said*” and after the *wāw* standing in its place. And its meaning is: he said, “My Lord, they have disobeyed me,” and he said, “Do not increase the wrongdoers except in straying”; that is, he said these two sayings, and they are in the place of the accusative, because they are the two objects of “*said*”; as in your saying, “Zayd said, ‘The call to prayer has been made,’ and, ‘Pray in the mosque’”: you relate his two sayings, one joined to the other.

{Q} How could he want straying for them and pray God to increase it? {A} What is meant by straying is that they be forsaken and denied the favors, for their being set on unbelief and the despair of their believing having come about; and that is good and fair, and may be prayed for; rather, praying for its opposite is not good. It may also be that by straying he means being lost and destroyed, for the saying of God Most High “*But do not give the disbelievers anything but annihilation*” (Q 71:28).

::: passage 71:25-27 | 4:620-621

Putting first “*because of their sins*” is to make clear that their drowning in the flood, and then their being brought into the Fire, was only because of their sins; and He stressed this meaning by the extra *mā*. In the reading of Ibn Masʿūd: *min khaṭīʾātihim mā ughriqū*, with the complement put after. And it is enough as a deterrent for one who commits sins: for the unbelief of Noah’s people was one of their sins, even if it was the greatest of them. All their other sins were held against them as their unbelief was held against them, and He did not separate it from them in deserving punishment; so that the Muslim who sins does not lean on his Islam, and knows that he has with it what makes him deserve punishment, even if he is free of the greatest sin. *Khaṭīʾātihim* is read with the *hamza*, *khaṭiyyātihim* with it turned to *yāʾ* and assimilated, *khaṭāyāhum*, and *khaṭīʾatihim* in the singular, meaning the kind. It may also be that unbelief is meant.

{L|Assigned to the hellfire|fa-udkhilū nāran} He made their entering the Fire in the Hereafter as though it followed right on their drowning, for its nearness, and because it is bound to be, so it is as though it had been. Or the punishment of the grave is meant: whoever dies in water or in fire, or is eaten by beasts and birds, is struck by what strikes the buried of punishment. From al-Ḍaḥḥāk: they were drowned from one side and burned from another. The indefinite “*a Fire*” is either to make it great, or because God prepared for them, according to their sins, a kind of fire. “*They found no helpers to protect them from GOD*”: a hint at their taking gods besides God, and that these are not able {p|621}to help them; and a mockery of them, as though He said: they found for themselves no gods besides God to help them and keep God’s punishment from them; as in the saying of God Most High “*Do they have gods who can protect them from us?*” (Q 21:43). “*Dweller*” is one of the nouns used in general negation. One says, “There is no *dayyār* in the house,” and *dayyūr*, like *qayyām* and *qayyūm*. It is *fayʿāl* from *al-dawr*, or from *al-dār*; its origin is *daywār*, and what was done with the origin of *sayyid* and *mayyit* was done with it. Had it been *faʿʿāl*, it would have been *dawwār*.

{Q} How did he know that their children would disbelieve, and how did he describe them by unbelief at birth? {A} He stayed among them a thousand years less fifty, so he tasted them and tried them and knew their natures and their states. A man of them would go off with his son to him and say, “*Beware of this one, for he is a liar; my father warned me of him*”; and the old would die and the young grow up on that. And God, Mighty and Majestic, had told him that “*No more of your people are going to believe, beyond those who already believe*” (Q 11:36). The meaning of “*give birth to nothing but wicked disbelievers*” is: they will bear only one who will be wicked and disbelieve. So He described them by what they will come to, as in his saying ﵇, “*Whoever kills a slain man, his spoils are his*.”

::: passage 71:28-28 | 4:621-622

{L|And my parents|wa-li-wālidayya} his father was Lamak ibn Mutawashlakh, and his mother Shamkhā bint Anūsh; they were believers. It is also said: they are Adam and Eve. Al-Ḥusayn ibn ʿAlī read *wa-li-waladayya*, meaning Shem and Ham. “*My home*”: my dwelling. It is also said: my place of prayer. It is also said: my ship. He singled out first those connected with him, because they are first and most deserving of his prayer; then he prayed for all the believing men and believing women. “*Annihilation*”: destruction.

{Q} What was done with their small children when they were drowned? {A} They drowned with them, not by way of punishment, but as they die by the kinds of causes of death; and how many of them die by drowning and burning! And it was as though that were an increase in the punishment of the fathers and mothers, when they saw their children drowning. {p|622}From it is his saying ﵇: “*They perish in one perishing and go out by different ways*.” From al-Ḥasan: he was asked about that, and he said, “*God knew their innocence, so He destroyed them without punishment*.” It is also said: God made their women’s wombs barren and dried up their fathers’ loins forty or seventy years before the flood, so there was no child with them when they were drowned.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat Nūḥ will be of the believers whom the prayer of Noah ﵇ reaches*.”
