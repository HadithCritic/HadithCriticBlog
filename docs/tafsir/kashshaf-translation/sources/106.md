surah: 106
title: Sūrat Quraysh (106)
revelation: Meccan. Four verses. [Revealed after al-Tīn (95).]

::: passage 106:1-4 | 4:800-803

{L|This should be cherished by Quraish|li-īlāfi quraysh} connected to His saying “*They shall worship*”: He commanded them to worship Him on account of their familiarity with the two journeys.

{Q} Then why does the *fāʾ* come in? {A} Because of the conditional sense in the speech, for the meaning is: if nothing else, then let them worship Him for their familiarity; {p|801}in the sense that God’s favors to them cannot be counted, so if they will not worship Him for all His other favors, let them worship Him for this one, which is a plain favor.

It is also said that the meaning is: marvel at the familiarity of Quraysh. It is also said that it is connected with what comes before, that is: “*He made them like chewed up hay*” for the familiarity of Quraysh. This is like enjambment (*taḍmīn*) in poetry, which is when the meaning of a line depends on the one before it in a way that is sound only with it. In the codex of Ubayy the two are a single sūra with no break between them. From ʿUmar: he recited them both in the second *rakʿa* of the sunset prayer, having recited “*By the fig*” in the first.

The meaning is that He destroyed the Abyssinians who had set out against them so that people would hear of it and hold them in still greater awe and give them still more respect, until security was assured for them on their two journeys and no one would dare to attack them. Quraysh had two journeys: in winter they traveled to Yemen and in summer to Syria, bringing back provisions and trading. They were safe on their journeys because they were the people of God’s Sanctuary and the keepers of His House, so no one molested them, while other people were carried off and raided.

*Al-īlāf* is from your saying *ālaftu l-makān ūlifuhu īlāfan* when you have become familiar with the place, so that I am *mūlif*. He said: “*of the familiar ones (al-mūlifāt), easy-paced, not those that graze on arāk*.” It is also read *li-ilāfi quraysh*, that is, for the companionship of Quraysh. It is also said that one says *alaftuhu ilfan wa-ilāfan*. Abū Jaʿfar read *li-ilfi quraysh*, and the poet who said this used both: “*You claimed that your brothers are Quraysh: they have ilf, and you have no ilāf*.” {p|802}ʿIkrima read *li-yaʾlaf quraysh ilfahum riḥlata l-shitāʾi wa-l-ṣayf* (“let Quraysh be familiar, in their familiarity, with the journey of winter and summer”).

Quraysh are the descendants of al-Naḍr ibn Kināna. They were named with the diminutive of *qirsh*, a huge creature in the sea that toys with ships and can be overcome only with fire. From Muʿāwiya: he asked Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄, “*Why was Quraysh so called?*” He said, “*After a creature in the sea that eats and is not eaten, and overcomes and is not overcome*.” And he recited: “*Quraysh is the one that lives in the sea; from it Quraysh was named Quraysh*.” The diminutive is for magnifying. It is also said to be from *al-qarsh*, which is earning, because they were great earners through their trade and their travels through the land.

He gave the familiarity without qualification, then put in its place the familiarity restricted to the two journeys, to magnify the matter of the familiarity and to remind them of how great a favor lay in it. “*The caravans*” (*riḥlata*, “the journey”) is accusative as the object of “*they cherish*” (*īlāfihim*, “their familiarity”), just as *yatīman* (“an orphan”) is accusative through *iṭʿāmun* (“feeding”) [in Q 90:14–15]. What He meant is the journeys of winter and summer, but He put it in the singular because there was no risk of confusion, as in his saying: “*Eat in part of your belly…*” {p|803}It is also read *ruḥla*, with *ḍamma*, which is the direction one travels toward.

The indefinite nouns “*hunger*” and “*fear*” express how severe they were. He means: through the two journeys He fed them against a severe hunger they had been in before, and made them secure against a great fear, the fear of the People of the Elephant, or the fear of being carried off in their own land and on their travels. It is also said that hardship had struck them until they ate carrion and burnt bones, and that He made them secure against the fear of leprosy, so that it would not strike them in their land. It is also said that all of this came through the prayer of Abraham ﵊. Among the novelties of the commentaries: “*and provided them with security after fear*,” that is, against the caliphate going to anyone but them. *Min khawf* is also read with the *nūn* concealed.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat li-īlāfi quraysh, God will give him ten good deeds for every person who has circled the Kaʿba and stayed there in devotion*.”
