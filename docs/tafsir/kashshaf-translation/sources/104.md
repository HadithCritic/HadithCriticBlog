surah: 104
title: Sūrat al-Humaza (104)
revelation: Meccan. Nine verses. [Revealed after al-Qiyāma (75).]

::: passage 104:1-9 | 4:794-796

*Al-hamz* is breaking, like *al-hazm*. *Al-lamz* is stabbing; one says *lamazahu* and *lahazahu* when one stabs another. What is meant is breaking into {p|795}people’s honor and disparaging them, backbiting them and attacking them. The pattern *fuʿala* shows that this is a habit he is addicted to. Like these two are *luʿana* (“one who curses much”) and *ḍuḥaka* (“one who laughs much”). He said: “*And if I am absent, you are the slanderer, the backbiter (al-hāmiz al-lumaza)*.” It is also read *wayl li-l-humazati l-lumaza*. It is also read *wayl li-kulli humzatin lumza*, with *sukūn* on the *mīm*: this is the laughingstock who comes out with outlandish things and jokes, and so is laughed at and reviled.

It is said that it was revealed about al-Akhnas ibn Sharīq, whose habit was backbiting and slander; it is also said, about Umayya ibn Khalaf; and it is also said, about al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra and his backbiting of the Messenger of God ﷺ and his disparaging of him. The occasion may be particular while the threat is general, so that it covers everyone who commits that vile act, and so that it works as a pointed allusion to the one it came down about, for that deters him more and wounds him more.

{L|Who|alladhī} a substitute for *kull* (“every”), or accusative as a term of censure. *Jammaʿa* is also read, with doubling, which matches *ʿaddadahu*. It is said that “*and counts it*” (*wa-ʿaddadahu*) means he made it a store (*ʿudda*) against the misfortunes of time. *Wa-ʿadadahu* is also read, that is, he gathered wealth and kept track of its number and reckoned it; or he gathered his wealth and his people who would help him, from your saying *fulān dhū ʿadad wa-ʿudad* (“so-and-so has numbers and provisions”), when he has many supporters and what they need. It is also said that *wa-ʿaddadahu* means *wa-ʿaddahu*, with the doubled letter undone, as in *ḍaninū*.

{L|Will make him immortal|akhladahu} *akhladahu* and *khalladahu* mean the same. That is, his wealth lengthened his hopes and filled him with far-off wishes, until, from his great heedlessness and long hope, he came to think that wealth had left him to live forever in this world without dying. Or he works at raising buildings secured with rock and brick, planting trees and cultivating land: the work of one who thinks his wealth has kept him alive. Or it is an allusion to righteous deeds: it is they that make their doer immortal in bliss, while wealth has never made anyone immortal in it. It is related that al-Akhnas had four thousand dinars; it is also said, ten thousand.

{From|al-Ḥasan} he visited a wealthy man [who was ill], {p|796}who said, “*What do you say about thousands with which I never bought myself free of a base man nor did a kindness to a noble one?*” He said, “*But why [did you keep them]?*” He said, “*Against the harshness of the times, the coldness of the ruler, the blows of fate, and the fear of poverty*.” He said, “*Then you leave it to one who will not thank you, and you go before One who will not excuse you*.”

{L|Never|kallā} a rebuke to him for what he thought. *La-yunbadhān* is also read, that is, he and his wealth; *la-yunbadhunna*, with *ḍamma* on the *dhāl*, that is, he and his supporters; and *la-yanbidhannahu*.

{L|Into the Devastator|fī l-ḥuṭama} into the Fire whose nature is to crush (*taḥṭim*) everything thrown into it. A man who eats a great deal is called *al-ḥuṭama*. *Al-ḥāṭima* is also read. That is, it enters their bellies until it reaches their breasts and rises over their hearts (*afʾida*), which are the inmost parts of the hearts. Nothing in the human body is more delicate than the heart (*fuʾād*), or feels more pain at the least harm that touches it; so how will it be when the fire of Hell rises over it and takes hold of it? He may also single out the hearts because they are the seats of unbelief, false beliefs and foul intentions. The meaning of the fire’s rising over them is that it rises above them, overpowers them and envelops them; or that, figuratively, it searches out the places where the cause of it lies.

{L|Confined|muʾṣada} shut over them. He said: “*My she-camel yearns for the mountains of Mecca, and before them the gates of Ṣanʿāʾ are shut (muʾṣada)*.” It is also read *fī ʿumud*, with two *ḍammas*; *ʿumd*, with *sukūn* on the *mīm*; and *ʿamad*, with two *fatḥas*. The meaning is that it confirms their despair of getting out and their certainty of being imprisoned forever: the gates are shut over them, and the columns are stretched across the gates, one fastening upon another. The meaning may also be: it is closed over them while they are bound in long columns, like the stocks in which thieves are held.

O God, protect us from the Fire, O best of those whose protection is sought.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-Humaza, God will give him ten good deeds for every person who mocked Muḥammad and his Companions*.”
