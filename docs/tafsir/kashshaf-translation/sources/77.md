::: passage 77:1-6 | 4:677-678

He, glory be to Him, swore by bands of angels whom He sent with His commands, and they stormed in their going as the winds storm, making themselves light in obeying His command; and by bands of them who spread their wings in the air when they came down with revelation; or spread the laws through the earth; or brought to life the souls dead by unbelief and ignorance with what they revealed, and so divided between the true and the false, and cast a reminder to the prophets, “*Good news*” (*ʿudhran*, “as excuse”) for those in the right “*as well as warnings*” (*aw nudhran*) for those in the wrong. Or He swore by winds of punishment that He sent, and they stormed; and by winds of mercy that spread the clouds through the air, and divided them, as in His saying “*He then piles the clouds up*” (*wa-yajʿaluhu kisafan*, Q 30:48). Or by clouds that brought dead land to life, and so divided between whoever thanks God Most High and whoever is ungrateful; as in His saying “*we will bless them with abundant water. We will surely test them all*” (Q 72:16–17). And they cast a reminder: either an excuse for those who excuse themselves to God by their repentance and asking forgiveness when they see God’s blessing in the rain and give thanks for it; or a warning to those who neglect thanking God and attribute that to the rain-stars. And they were made casters of the reminder because they are a cause of its coming about, when the blessing in them is thanked for or denied.

{Q} What is the meaning of *ʿurfan*? {A} Following one on another, like the hair of the mane (*ʿurf*). One says, “They came as one mane,” and “They are against him like the hyena’s mane,” when they band together against him. It may also be in the sense of *al-ʿurf* that is the opposite of the reprehensible; and then it is in the accusative as an object of purpose, that is: sent for kindness and what is decent; and the first is a circumstantial clause. *ʿUruf* is also read, heavy, like *nukur* for *nukr*.

{Q} You have explained *al-mursalāt* (“those sent”) as the angels of punishment; so how is their sending a kindness? {A} If it is not a kindness to the unbelievers, it is a kindness to the prophets and the believers, for whom God took vengeance on them.

{Q} What are *ʿudhr* and *nudhr*, and by what are they in the accusative? {A} They are verbal nouns from *aʿdhara*, when he wiped out the wrong, and from *andhara*, when he frightened, on the pattern {p|678}*fuʿl*, like *al-kufr* and *al-shukr*. It may also be the plural of *ʿadhīr* in the sense of excuse, and the plural of *nadhīr* in the sense of warning; or in the sense of one who excuses and one who warns. As for their being in the accusative: it is as a substitute for “*messages*” (*dhikran*, “a reminder”), on the first two faces; or as an object of purpose. As for the third face, it is as a circumstantial clause, in the sense of excusing or warning. They are read light and heavy.

::: passage 77:7-15 | 4:678-678

What you are promised, of the coming of the Day of Resurrection, is coming, descending, there is no doubt of it; and it is the answer to the oath. From one of them: the meaning is, by the Lord of those sent. “*Put out*”: wiped out and effaced. It is also said: their light is taken and their substance effaced; agreeing with His saying “*scattered*” (Q 82:2) and “*crashed into each other*” (*inkadarat*, Q 81:2). It may also be that their light is effaced, then they are scattered with their light effaced. “*Opened up*”: opened, and it became gates. He said: “*the one who opens* (al-fāriji) *the prince’s shut gate*.” “*blown up*”: like grain when it is winnowed with the winnowing basket. Like it is “*The mountains will be wiped out*” (Q 56:5), “*the mountains will turn into a weightless pile*” (Q 73:14). It is also said: taken swiftly from their places, from *intasaftu l-shayʾa*, when I snatched it. *Ṭummisat*, *furrijat* and *nussifat* are read doubled.

*Uqqitat* and *wuqqitat* are read, with doubling and lightening in both; and the origin is the *wāw*. The meaning of the appointing of the messengers’ time is the making clear of their time in which they come forward to bear witness against their communities. *Al-taʾjīl* is from *al-ajal*, the term, as *al-tawqīt* is from *al-waqt*. “*That is the appointed day*” (*li-ayyi yawmin ujjilat*, “for what day are they deferred?”) is making the day great and wondering at its terror. “*The Day of Decision*” makes clear the day of deferment, and it is the day on which the creatures are judged between. The right face is that the meaning of “*appointed*” is: they have reached their appointed time that they were waiting for, which is the Day of Resurrection; and “*deferred*” is: put off.

{Q} How did an indefinite fall as subject in His saying “*Woe on that day to the rejectors*”? {A} It is in its origin a verbal noun in the accusative, standing in the place of its verb; but it was turned to the nominative to point to the meaning of the destruction being fixed and lasting for the one prayed against. Like it is “*Peace be upon you*” (Q 6:54). *Waylan*, in the accusative, is allowed, but it was not read. One says, “*waylan lahu waylan kaylan*.”

::: passage 77:16-19 | 4:678-679

Qatāda read *nahliki*, with *fatḥa* on the *nūn*, from *halakahu* in the sense of *ahlakahu*. Al-ʿAjjāj said: {p|679}“*and many a waste destroying* (hālik) *whoever turns aside into it*.” “*Then we made others follow them*” (*thumma nutbiʿuhumu*, “then We make them follow”) is in the nominative, as a fresh start; and it is a threat to the people of Mecca. He means: then We do to their likes among the later ones the like of what We did to the former, and take them on their way, because they denied as they denied. The reading of Ibn Masʿūd strengthens it, *thumma sa-nutbiʿuhum*. It is also read jussive, joined to “*destroy*”; and its meaning is that He destroyed the former ones, of the people of Noah, ʿĀd and Thamūd, then made the later ones follow them, of the people of Shuʿayb, Lot and Moses. “*Thus*”: like that hideous deed “*We do*” to everyone who has committed crime; as a warning and a caution against the outcome of crime and its evil effect.

::: passage 77:25-28 | 4:679-680

*Al-kifāt* is from *kafata l-shayʾa*, when he drew it in and gathered it; and it is the name of what draws in, as in their saying *al-ḍimām* and *al-jimāʿ* for what draws together and gathers; one says, “This chapter is the gathering (*jimāʿ*) of the chapters.” And by it *aḥyāʾan wa-amwātan* (“living and dead”) are put in the accusative, as though it were said: drawing in the living and the dead. Or by an implied verb that it points to, which is “it draws in.” And the meaning is: it draws in the living on its back and the dead in its belly. Some of the followers of al-Shāfiʿī ﵀ have argued for cutting the hand of the grave robber from this: that God Most High made the earth a container for the dead, so its belly is a place of safekeeping for them; so the grave robber is a thief from a place of safekeeping.

{Q} Why was it said *aḥyāʾan wa-amwātan* (“living and dead”), indefinite, when it is the container of all the living and the dead? {A} {p|680}It is of the indefiniteness of making great, as though it were said: it draws in living who are not counted and dead who are not reckoned; besides that the living of mankind and their dead are not all the living and the dead. It may also be that the meaning is: it draws you in, living and dead; so they are in the accusative as circumstantial clauses from the pronoun, because it is known that it is the container of mankind.

{Q} Then the indefiniteness in “*high mountains*” (*rawāsiya shāmikhātin*) and “*fresh water*” (*māʾan furātan*)? {A} It may give the sense of a part, because in the heaven there are mountains (God Most High said: “*and He sends down from the heaven, from mountains in it, of hail*,” Q 24:43), and in it is sweet water too; rather, it is its mine and its pouring place. And it may be for making great.

That is, it is said to them: go off to what you denied, of the punishment; and the second “*go off*” is repetition. *Inṭalaqū* is read in the past form, telling, after the command, of their acting on what it required; because they are forced to it and cannot hold back from it. “*To a shade*”: He means the smoke of Hell, as in His saying “*Even their shade is hot*” (*wa-ẓillin min yaḥmūm*, “and a shade of black smoke,” Q 56:43). “*Of three different densities*” (*dhī thalāthi shuʿab*, “of three branches”): that branches, for its greatness, into three branches; and so you see great smoke parting into locks. It is also said: a tongue comes out of the Fire and surrounds the unbelievers like a pavilion, and from its smoke three branches split off and shade them until their reckoning is done, while the believers are in the shade of the Throne. “*It provides neither coolness*” (*lā ẓalīlin*, “no shade”) is a mockery of them, and a hint that their shade is not the shade of the believers. “*Nor protection from the heat*” is in the place of the genitive, that is: and not availing them anything against the heat of the flame.

{L|It throws sparks|bi-shararin} *bi-shirārin* is also read. “*As big as mansions*”: that is, each spark like a castle of the castles in its greatness. It is also said: it is the thick of trees, the singular *qaṣra*, like *jamra* and *jamr*. *Ka-l-qaṣari* is read, with two *fatḥa*s, and it is the necks of camels, or the trunks of palms, like *shajara* and *shajar*. Ibn Masʿūd read *ka-l-qusur*, in the sense of castles, like *rahn* and *ruhun*. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr read *ka-l-qiṣar*, as the plural of *qaṣra*, like *ḥāja* and *ḥiwaj*. *Jimālātun* is the plural of *jimāl*, or of *jimāla*, the plural of *jamal*. They were likened to castles, then to camels, to make the likening clear. Do you not see them likening camels to fortresses and towers? *Jumālātun* is read with *ḍamma*, and they are the thick ropes of bridges; it is also said: the thick ropes of ships of the sea; the singular *jumāla*. {p|681}*Jimālatun* is read, with *kasra*, in the sense of camels; and *jumālatun*, with *ḍamma*, which is the thick rope. It is also said: “*yellow*,” for meaning the kind. It is also said: “*yellow*” is black shading into yellow. In the poetry of ʿImrān ibn Ḥiṭṭān the Khārijite:

> It called them at the top of its voice and threw at them / the like of yellow camels, a stripper of the scalp.

And Abū l-ʿAlāʾ said:

> Red, its locks blazing in the dark, / it throws every spark like a leather tent.

So he likened it to the *ṭirāf*, which is the leather tent, in greatness and redness. And it is as though by his malice he aimed to go beyond the likening of the Qurʾān; and in his boasting over what his fancy of going beyond had prompted him to, he brought at the head of his verse his saying “red,” to prepare for it, call it out and alert the hearers to where it was. And he was blind (God join for him the blindness of both abodes!) to the saying of the Mighty and Exalted “*As yellow as the color of camels*”; for it is in the position of his saying “like a red tent.” Besides, in the likening to the castle, which is the fortress, there is a likening from two sides: from the side of greatness, and from the side of height in the air. And in the likening to the *jumālāt*, which are the thick ropes, there is a likening from three sides: from the side of greatness, length and yellowness. So God put far off his strangeness in his *ṭirāf*, and what he puffed his cheeks out with in thinking it a fine find.

*Yawma* is read in the accusative, and al-Aʿmash put it in the accusative; that is, this that has been told you is happening on that day. The Day of Resurrection is long, with stations and times: they speak at one time and do not speak at another; for that reason both matters have come in the Qurʾān. Or He made their speaking as no speaking, because it does not profit and is not heard. “*To apologize*” (*fa-yaʿtadhirūn*, “so that they excuse themselves”) is joined {p|682}to “*Nor are they given permission*,” strung in the thread of the negation. And the meaning is: and they will have no permission and no excusing following on it, without the excusing being made caused by the permission. Had it been put in the accusative, it would have been caused by it without doubt.

{L|We have summoned you and the previous generations|jamaʿnākum wa-l-awwalīna} is speech that makes plain His saying “*This is the Day of Decision*”; because if it is the day of decision between the happy and the wretched, and between the prophets and their communities, there must be a gathering of the former and the later, so that that decision falls among them. “*If you have any schemes, go ahead and scheme*”: a rebuke of them for their plotting against God’s religion and its people, and a setting down against them of helplessness and submission. “*Eat and drink*” is in the place of a circumstantial clause from the pronoun of “*The righteous*” in the adverbial, which is “*shade*,” that is: they are settled in shades, that being said to them.

::: passage 77:46-50 | 4:682-683

“*Eat and enjoy temporarily*” is a circumstantial clause from “*the rejectors*,” that is: woe is fixed for them in the state of its being said to them, “Eat and enjoy yourselves.”

{Q} How is it sound that that should be said to them in the Hereafter? {A} That is said to them in the Hereafter to give notice that in this world they deserved to have it said to them, and were fit for it; reminding them of their ugly state and of what they brought on themselves by preferring the little enjoyment to the lasting bliss and dominion. And of its manner is his saying:

> My brothers, may you never be far! / Yet, by God, far they have gone.

He means: you deserved in your lives that that prayer should be made for you. And He gave its reason by their being criminals, to point to this: that every criminal has nothing but eating and enjoying for a few days, then staying in destruction forever. It may also be that “*Eat and enjoy temporarily*” is speech begun afresh, an address to the deniers in this world. “*Bow down*”: be humble before God and lowly before Him, by accepting {p|683}His revelation and following His religion; and throw off this pride and haughtiness. They are not humble and do not accept that, and they persist in their pride. It is also said: nothing was harder on the Arabs than bowing and prostrating. It is also said: it was revealed about Thaqīf when the Messenger of God ﷺ commanded them to pray, and they said, “*We will not bend over, for it is a disgrace for us*.” And the Messenger of God ﷺ said, “*There is no good in a religion in which there is no bowing and no prostrating*.” “*other than this*” (*baʿdahu*, “after it”): after the Qurʾān. He means that the Qurʾān, among the books sent down, is a sign that makes see and a dazzling miracle; so when they did not believe in it, in what book after it “*do they uphold*”? *Tuʾminūna* is read, with *tāʾ*.
