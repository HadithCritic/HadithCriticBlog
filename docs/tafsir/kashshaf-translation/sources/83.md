surah: 83
title: Sūrat al-Muṭaffifīn (83)
revelation: Meccan. Thirty-six verses. [Revealed after al-ʿAnkabūt (29); it is the last sūra revealed in Mecca.]

::: passage 83:1-6 | 4:718-720

*Al-taṭfīf* is short measure and short weight, because what is kept back is something slight (*ṭafīf*) and paltry. It is related that the Messenger of God ﷺ came to Medina when its people were among the worst of all people in their measuring. It was revealed, and they measured well. It is also said that when he came there was a man there known as Abū Juhayna who had two *ṣāʿ* measures: he measured out with one and measured in with the other. It is also said that the people of Medina were traders who gave short measure, and their sales were by casting (*munābadha*), by touching (*mulāmasa*) and by hazard (*mukhāṭara*). It was revealed, and the Messenger of God ﷺ went out and recited it to them, and said, “*Five for five*.” He was asked, “*Messenger of God, what is five for five?*” He said, “*No people break a covenant without God setting their enemy over them; they do not judge by other than what God has sent down without poverty spreading among them; indecency does not appear among them without death spreading among them; they do not give short measure without being denied crops and seized by years of drought; and they do not withhold the zakāt without the rain being held back from them*.”

From ʿAlī ﵁: he passed a man weighing saffron who was tipping the scale, and said to him, “*Set the weight right with justice, and then tip it after that as much as you like*.” It is as if he told him to make it even first, so that he would grow used to it and keep what is owed apart from what is extra. From Ibn ʿAbbās: “*You non-Arabs have been put in charge of two things by which those before you perished: the measure and the scale*.” He named the non-Arabs because they use measure and weight together, while in the two Sanctuaries the two were kept apart: the people of Mecca weighed {p|719}and the people of Medina measured.

From Ibn ʿUmar: he would pass a seller and say to him, “*Fear God and give full measure, for those who give short measure will be stood up on the Day of Resurrection before the majesty of the Merciful, until the sweat reaches their mouths like a bridle*.” From ʿIkrima: “*I testify that every measurer and weigher is in the Fire*.” He was told, “*Your son is a measurer*,” or “*a weigher*,” and he said, “*I testify that he is in the Fire*.” From Ubayy ﵁: “*Do not seek what you need from one whose living lies at the heads of measures and the tongues of scales*.”

{L|When receiving from the people|idhā ktālū ʿalā l-nās} since their taking measure from people was a taking that harmed them and bore down on them, He put *ʿalā* (“against”) in place of *min* (“from”) to show this. *ʿAlā* may also attach to “*demand full measure*” (*yastawfūn*, “they take full”), with the object put before the verb to restrict it, that is: they take full measure from people in particular, while for themselves they take full measure. Al-Farrāʾ said that *min* and *ʿalā* take turns here, because it is a debt owed by him: when one says *iktaltu ʿalayka*, it is as if one said “I took what you owed,” and when one says *iktaltu minka*, it is like saying “I took my full due from you.”

The pronoun in “*giving them the measures or weights*” (*kālūhum aw wazanūhum*) is an accusative pronoun for the people. There are two ways of taking it. One is that it means *kālū lahum aw wazanū lahum*, with the preposition dropped and the verb made to reach the object directly, as he said: “*I gathered you truffles and white truffles, and I warned you off the poor kind (banāt awbar)*,” and “*It is the greedy one who hunts for you, not the generous*,” meaning “I gathered for you” and “hunts for you.” The other is that the governing noun is left out and the noun it governs put in its place, the governing noun being what is measured or weighed. It cannot be a nominative pronoun for those who give short measure, because the speech would then fall into a faulty construction. The meaning is: when they take from people they take in full, and when they give to them they give short. If you make the pronoun refer to those who give short measure, it turns into your saying: when they take from people they take in full, and when they themselves in particular take charge of the measuring or weighing they give short. That is incoherent, {p|720}because what is being talked about is the act, not the one who carries it out.

To rely, in rejecting this, on the script of the codex, where the *alif* written after the plural *wāw* is not there, is feeble, because in much of it the script of the codex does not keep to the rule agreed on in the art of writing. Besides, I have seen this *alif* left out in books written by the hands of careful scholars, since it is present neither in the sound nor in the sense: the *wāw* alone gives the sense of the plural. The *alif* is written only to tell the plural *wāw* from other *wāw*s, as when you say *hum lam yadʿū* (“they did not call”) and *huwa yadʿū* (“he calls”); and those who leave it out say that the sense is enough to tell them apart. From ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar and Ḥamza: they used to take it this way, that is, they made both pronouns refer to those who give short measure, and paused briefly at the two *wāw*s to make clear what they meant.

{Q} Why was it not said *aw ittazanū* (“or take weight”), as it was said *aw wazanūhum* (“or weigh for them”)? {A} It is as if those who give short measure took what is measured or weighed only with measures and not with scales, because measuring let them take in full and steal, since they shook the measure and played tricks in filling it; and when they gave, they measured or weighed, because they could give short in both ways.

{L|They cheat|yukhsirūn} they reduce. One says *khasara l-mīzān* and *akhsarahu*.

{L|Do they not know|a-lā yaẓunnu} a reproach and a great expression of wonder at their state in daring to give short measure, as if it never crossed their minds, and they never so much as guessed, “*that they will be resurrected*” and called to account for the weight of an atom or a mustard seed. From Qatāda: “*Give full measure, son of Adam, as you would like to be given full measure, and be fair as you would like others to be fair to you*.” From al-Fuḍayl: “*Short weight is a blackened face on the Day of Resurrection*.” From ʿAbd al-Malik ibn Marwān: a Bedouin said to him, “*You have heard what God said about those who give short measure*.” He meant: the one who gives short measure has the great threat you have heard of turned on him, so what do you think of yourself, when you take the wealth of the Muslims with no measure or weight at all?

In this reproach and wonder, in the word “*think*,” in calling the Day great, in people standing on it humbly before God, and in His describing Himself as Lord of the worlds, there is an eloquent statement of how great a sin it is and how grave the wrong in giving short measure, and in anything like it of injustice and failing to uphold fairness and to deal evenly and justly in every taking and giving, indeed in every word and deed. It is also said that *yaẓunnu* (“think”) has the sense of being certain; but the way to take it is as stated.

“*That is the day when all people will stand*” is accusative through “*will be resurrected*.” It is also read in the genitive, as a substitute for “*a tremendous day*.” From Ibn ʿUmar: he recited this sūra, and when he reached His saying “*the day when all people will stand before the Lord of the universe*,” he wept aloud and could not go on reciting.

{p|721}{L|Indeed|kallā} He rebuked them for their giving short measure and their heedlessness of the raising and the reckoning, and told them that it is something to be repented of and regretted. Then He followed it with the threat to the wicked in general. The record of the wicked is what is written of their deeds.

{Q} God has told that the record of the wicked is in Sijjīn and explained Sijjīn as “*a written record*,” as if it were said: their record is in a written record. What does this mean? {A} Sijjīn is a book that gathers everything, the register of evil, in which God has set down the deeds of the devils and of the unbelievers and the wicked among jinn and humans. It is “*a written record*,” set down in clear writing; or marked, so that whoever sees it knows there is no good in it. So the meaning is that what is written of the deeds of the wicked is entered in that register. It is called Sijjīn, the pattern *fiʿʿīl* from *sijn*, which is imprisonment and confinement, because it is the cause of imprisonment and confinement in Hell; or because it is thrown down, as is related, beneath the seventh earth in a dark and desolate place, the dwelling of Iblīs and his offspring, in contempt and scorn for it, and so that the outcast devils may witness it, as the angels brought near witness the register of good.

{Q} Is Sijjīn an adjective or a noun? {A} It is a proper name carried over from an adjective, like Ḥātim. It is fully declined, because it has only one of the causes, which is being definite.

::: passage 83:10-17 | 4:721-722

{L|They do not believe|alladhīna yukadhdhibūna} this is one of the descriptions given to condemn, not to explain, as when you say, “So-and-so, the vile sinner, did that.”

{L|Indeed|kallā} a rebuke to the sinful transgressor for what he said. “*Their hearts have become shielded*” (*rāna ʿalā qulūbihim*): it has covered them as rust covers, and taken them over. That is, he persists in grave sins and puts off repentance until his heart is sealed, so that it neither accepts good nor leans toward it. From al-Ḥasan: sin after sin, until the heart turns black. One says *rāna ʿalayhi l-dhanb* and *ghāna ʿalayhi*, *raynan* and *ghaynan*; *al-ghayn* is cloud. One says *rāna fīhi l-nawm*, “sleep sank deep in him,” and *rānat bihi l-khamr*, “the wine carried him off.” It is read with the *lām* assimilated into the *rāʾ* and with it pronounced, and assimilation is better; and the *alif* is read with *imāla* and with full vowel.

{L|Indeed|kallā} a rebuke for the earnings that have rusted over their hearts. {p|722}Their being kept back from Him is an image of the contempt and scorn shown them, because only the notables honored by kings are admitted to them, and only the lowly and scorned are kept out. He said: “*When they come to the door of a proud man, they are received with awe; and people are either received with awe or kept out*.” From Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda and Ibn Abī Mulayka: kept back from His mercy. From Ibn Kaysān: from His favor.

::: passage 83:18-21 | 4:722-723

{L|Indeed|kallā} a rebuke for denying. The record of the righteous is what is written of their deeds. *ʿIlliyyūn* is the proper name of the register of good, in which is set down all that the angels and the righteous of men and jinn have done. It is carried over from the plural of *ʿilliyy*, the pattern *fiʿʿīl* from *ʿuluww* (“height”), as *sijjīn* is from *sijn*. It is called so either because it is the cause of rising to the highest ranks in Paradise, or because it is raised up in the seventh heaven, where the Cherubim dwell, to honor and magnify it. It is related: “*The angels go up with a servant’s deed and think it little. When they bring it as far as God wills of His dominion, He reveals to them: ‘You are the guardians over My servant, and I am the watcher over what is in his heart. He made his deed sincere, so put it in ʿIlliyyūn, for I have forgiven him.’ And they go up with a servant’s deed and think well of it. When they bring it as far as God wills, He reveals to them: ‘You are the guardians over {p|723}My servant, and I am the watcher over what is in his heart. He did not make his deed sincere for Me, so put it in Sijjīn.’*”

{L|On luxurious furnishings|ʿalā l-arāʾik} the beds in the bridal canopies. “*They watch*”: at whatever sights of Paradise they wish to turn their eyes to, at the favor and honor God has given them, and at their enemies being punished in the Fire; and the canopies do not keep their eyes from seeing.

{L|The joy of bliss|naḍrata l-naʿīm} the brightness of living in comfort, its freshness and gloss, as you see on the faces of the rich and those who live in ease. *Tuʿrafu* is also read, in the passive, with *naḍratu l-naʿīm* in the nominative.

{L|Their drinks|raḥīq} the clear drink with nothing mixed in it. “*Spiced*” (*makhtūm*, “sealed”): its vessels, cups and jugs, are sealed with musk in place of clay. It is also said that “*Its spice is like musk*” (*khitāmuhu misk*, “its seal is musk”) means that its last taste is the scent of musk when it is drunk. It is also said that it is mixed with camphor and its mixture sealed with musk. *Khātamuhu* is also read, with *fatḥa* on the *tāʾ* and with *kasra*, that is, what it is sealed and finished with. “*This is what the competitors should compete for*”: let those who long for things long for this.

{L|Special flavors|tasnīm} the proper name of a particular spring. It is named with *tasnīm*, the verbal noun of *sannamahu*, “he raised it high”: either because it is the highest drink in Paradise, or because it comes to them from above, as it is related that it runs through the air, raised high, and pours into their vessels. “*A spring*” is accusative in praise; al-Zajjāj said it is accusative as a circumstantial clause. It is also said that it is for those brought near, who drink it pure, and that it is mixed for the rest of the people of Paradise.

::: passage 83:29-33 | 4:723-724

{L|The wicked|alladhīna ajramū} they are the idolaters of Mecca, Abū Jahl, al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra, al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil and their followers. They used to laugh {p|724}at ʿAmmār, Ṣuhayb, Khabbāb, Bilāl and others of the poor believers, and mock them. It is also said that ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib ﵁ came with a group of Muslims, and the hypocrites jeered at them, laughed and winked at one another; then they went back to their companions and said, “Today we saw the bald one,” and laughed at him. It was revealed before ʿAlī reached the Messenger of God ﷺ.

{L|They used to poke fun|yataghāmazūn} they nudge one another and signal with their eyes. “*They used to joke*” (*fakihīn*, “merry”): relishing talk of them and mockery of them; that is, they charge the Muslims with going astray.

{L|They have no such thing|wa-mā ursilū} over the Muslims “*as (invisible) guards*,” put in charge of them to keep watch on their states, oversee their deeds, and bear witness to whether they are rightly guided or astray. This is mockery of them. Or it is part of what the unbelievers say: when they saw the Muslims they said, “These people are astray,” and “they were not sent as guardians over us,” objecting to the Muslims’ turning them away from idolatry, calling them to Islam, and working hard at it.

{L|On luxurious furnishings they watch|ʿalā l-arāʾiki yanẓurūn} a circumstantial clause to “*are laughing*,” that is: they laugh at them while looking at them and at the abasement and lowliness they are in after might and pride, and the kinds of punishment after bliss and ease, while they themselves are safe on the couches. It is also said that a door into Paradise will be opened for the unbelievers and they will be told, “Come out to it,” and when they reach it, it will be shut in their faces. This will be done to them again and again, and the believers will laugh at them.

{L|Are requited|thuwwiba} *thawwabahu* and *athābahu* have one meaning, when one repays someone. Aws said: “*I will repay you, or one who repays will repay you for me; and it is enough for you to be praised and lauded*.” It is also read with the *lām* assimilated into the *thāʾ*.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-Muṭaffifīn, God will give him drink from the sealed pure wine on the Day of Resurrection*.”
