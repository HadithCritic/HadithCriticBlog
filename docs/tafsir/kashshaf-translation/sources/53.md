title: Sūrat al-Najm (53)
revelation: Meccan, except verse 32, which is Medinan. Sixty-two verses; it is also said sixty-one. [Revealed after al-Ikhlāṣ (112).]

::: passage 53:1-18 | 4:416-421

*Al-najm* (“the Star”) is the Pleiades; it is a name that has come to belong to it. He said: “*When the Star rises at nightfall, the herdsman looks for a cloak*.” {p|417}Or it is the kind, the stars. He said: “*So she spent the night counting the stars in a brimming [pool]*,” meaning the stars. “*Fell away*” (*idhā hawā*): when it sets, or when it is scattered on the Day of Resurrection. Or the star that is thrown at [the devils], when it falls, that is, when it shoots down. Or the star (*najm*) is one of the installments (*nujūm*) of the Qurʾān, which came down in installments over twenty years; “when it falls,” when it comes down. Or it is the plants (*najm*), when they fall, that is, when they drop to the ground.

From ʿUrwa ibn al-Zubayr: ʿUtba ibn Abī Lahab, who was married to a daughter of the Messenger of God ﷺ, wanted to set out for Syria, and he said, “*I will go to Muḥammad and hurt him*.” He came to him and said, “*Muḥammad, he disbelieves in ‘As the stars fell away’ and in ‘He drew nearer by moving down’*.” Then he spat in the face of the Messenger of God ﷺ, sent his daughter back to him and divorced her. The Messenger of God ﷺ said, “*O God, set on him a dog of Your dogs*.” Abū Ṭālib was present, and he was stricken with grief at it and said, “*How little you needed that prayer, my nephew!*” ʿUtba went back to his father and told him. Then they set out for Syria and stopped at a halting place, and a monk looked down on them from the monastery and said to them, “*This is a land full of beasts of prey*.” Abū Lahab said to his companions, “*Help us, people of {p|418}Quraysh, this night, for I fear for my son the prayer of Muḥammad*.” So they gathered their camels, made them kneel around them, and surrounded ʿUtba. The lion came sniffing at their faces until it struck ʿUtba and killed him. Ḥassān said: “*Whoever goes back this year to his people, the beast’s prey is not one who goes back*.”

{L|Your friend (Muhammad) was not astray|mā ḍalla ṣāḥibukum} He means Muḥammad ﷺ, and the address is to Quraysh. It is the answer of the oath. Straying is the opposite of guidance, and error the opposite of right conduct: that is, he is guided and rightly led, and not as you claim when you charge him with straying and error; and the Qurʾān he has brought you is not speech that issues from his own desire and opinion, but only revelation from God revealed to him. Those who do not allow independent reasoning to the prophets argue from this verse; the answer to them is that if God Most High permitted them to reason independently, the reasoning and what it rests on would all be revelation, not speech from desire.

{L|The Most Powerful|shadīdu l-quwā} an angel whose powers are mighty. The annexation is not a real one, because it is the annexation of a resembling adjective to its agent. He is Gabriel ﵇. Of his power is that he pulled up the towns of the people of Lot from the black water, carried them on his wing, raised them to the sky, and then turned them over; and he gave one cry at Thamūd, and morning found them fallen prone. His coming down to the prophets and his going up were quicker than the turning of a glance. He saw Iblīs speaking to Jesus ﵇ on {p|419}one of the mountain passes of the Holy Land, and he struck him with his wing a single stroke and threw him onto the farthest mountain in India.

{L|Possessor of all authority|dhū mirratin} possessed of soundness in his mind and judgment, and steadfastness in his religion. “*From His highest height*” (*fa-stawā*): he stood firm in his own true form, not the form in which he used to appear whenever he came down with the revelation; he used to come down in the form of Diḥya. That was because the Messenger of God ﷺ wished to see him in the form in which he was created, so he stood upright for him on the highest horizon, the horizon of the sun, and filled the horizon. It is said that none of the prophets saw him in his true form except Muḥammad ﷺ, twice: once on earth and once in heaven. “*He drew nearer*” to the Messenger of God ﷺ, “*by moving down*” (*fa-tadallā*): he hung over him in the air. From this is *tadallat al-thamara* (“the fruit hung down”) and *dallā rijlayhi mina l-sarīr* (“he let his legs hang down from the couch”); *al-dawālī* is hanging fruit. He said: “*He hung down over it between a rope and a cord*.” And it is said: he is like the kingfisher; if he sees good he comes down, and if he does not see it he turns away.

{L|As close as possible|qāba qawsayni} the measure of two Arab bows. *Al-qāb*, *al-qīb*, *al-qād*, *al-qīd* and *al-qīs* are the measure. Zayd ibn ʿAlī read *qāda*; *qīda* and *qadra* are also read. Measuring has come with the bow, the spear, the whip, the forearm, the fathom, the step, the span, the hand’s breadth and the finger. From this is “*There is no prayer until the sun rises the measure of two spears*,” and in the ḥadīth: “*The length of one of your bows in Paradise, and the place of his whip, is better than the world and what is in it*”; *al-qidd* is the whip. And it is said: between them are a few steps. He said: {p|420}“*And she has made me a finger’s length from Ḥuzayma*.”

{Q} How is His saying “*and he was two bows’ length*” to be supplied? {A} It is supplied as: and the measure of the distance of his nearness was like two bows’ length. These annexed words were dropped, as Abū ʿAlī said of his saying “*And she has made me a finger’s length from Ḥuzayma*,” that is, at the measure of the distance of a finger. “*As close as possible*” (*aw adnā*, “or nearer”): that is, by your reckoning, as in the saying of God Most High “*or more*” (Q 37:147).

{L|To His servant|ilā ʿabdihi} to the servant of God, though no mention of His name, mighty and majestic is He, has gone before, because there is no confusion, as in His saying “*on earth*” (Q 35:45). “*What was to be revealed*”: making grand the revelation that was revealed to him. It is said that He revealed to him: “*Paradise is forbidden to the prophets until you enter it, and to the communities until your community enters it*.”

{L|Never made up|mā kadhaba} the heart of Muḥammad ﷺ did not lie about what he saw with his eyes, the form of Gabriel ﵇: that is, his heart did not say, when he saw him, “I do not know you”; had it said that, it would have lied, because it knew him. He means that he saw him with his eye and knew him with his heart, and did not doubt that what he saw was true. *Mā kadhdhaba* is also read: that is, it believed it and did not doubt that it was Gabriel ﵇ in his own form.

{L|Are you doubting|a-fa-tumārūnahu} from *al-mirāʾ*, which is quarreling and disputing. It is derived from *marā l-nāqata* (“he stroked the she-camel’s udder”), as though each of the two who dispute strokes out what the other has. *A-fa-tamrūnahu* is also read: will you beat him in disputing, from *māraytuhu fa-maraytuhu* (“I disputed with him and beat him”); and because of the sense of prevailing in it, it is made transitive with *ʿalā*, as you say, “I prevailed over him in such-and-such.” It is also said that *a-fa-tamrūnahu* means “will you deny him”; and they recited: “*If you have satirized a brother of truth and nobility, you have denied a brother who never denied you*.” {p|421}They said one says *maraytuhu ḥaqqahu* when you deny him his right; and making it transitive with *ʿalā* is sound only on the doctrine of implication.

{L|Another descent|nazlatan ukhrā} another time of coming down. *Al-nazla* is put in the accusative as the adverb *marra* (“time”) is, because the pattern *faʿla* is the name of a single instance of the act, so it takes its rule. That is: Gabriel ﵇ came down to him another time in his own form, and he saw him in it; and that was on the night of the Ascension. It is said of the Lote Tree of the Boundary: it is a lote tree in the seventh heaven, at the right of the Throne; its fruit is like the jars of Hajar and its leaves like the ears of elephants; the rivers God mentions in His book spring from its root; a rider travels in its shade seventy years and does not cross it. *Al-muntahā* is in the sense of the place where things end, or the ending, as if it were at the end and last of Paradise. It is also said: no one has passed beyond it, and the knowledge of the angels and others ends at it, and no one knows what is beyond it. It is also said: the spirits of the martyrs end at it.

{L|The eternal Paradise|jannatu l-maʾwā} the Garden to which the God-fearing come; this is from al-Ḥasan. It is also said: the spirits of the martyrs take refuge in it. ʿAlī, Ibn al-Zubayr and a group read *jannahu l-maʾwā*, that is, the refuge covered him with its shades and he entered into it. From ʿĀʾisha: she rejected it and said, “*Whoever reads it so, may God cover him*.”

{L|Was overwhelmed|mā yaghshā} making great and many what covers it. By this phrasing it is known that what covers it, of the creatures that point to God’s greatness and majesty, are things that description cannot reach to the core of and portrayal cannot encompass. It has been said: a great host of angels covers it, worshiping God at it. From the Messenger of God ﷺ: “*I saw on every leaf of its leaves an angel standing, glorifying God*.” From him ﵇: “*A flock of green birds covers it*.” From Ibn Masʿūd and others: “*Moths of gold cover it*.”

{L|The eyes did not waver|mā zāgha} the sight of the Messenger of God ﷺ, “*nor go blind*”: that is, he took in what he saw with a certain and sound taking-in, without his sight swerving from it or going beyond it. Or: it did not turn aside from seeing the wonders it was commanded to see and given the power to see; “*nor go blind*”: it did not go beyond what it was commanded to see. “*He saw*”: by God, he surely saw “*great signs of his Lord*” the signs that are the greatest and mightiest of them; He means when his Lord raised him to heaven and he was shown the wonders of the kingdom.

::: passage 53:19-23 | 4:422-423

{L|Allaat and Al-'Uzzah|al-lāta wa-l-ʿuzzā} and Manāt were idols they had, and they are feminine. Al-Lāt belonged to Thaqīf at al-Ṭāʾif; it is also said that it was at Nakhla, and Quraysh worshiped it. It is *faʿla* from *lawā*, because they used to turn to it (*yalwūna*) and stay at it in worship; or they used to turn about it (*yaltawūna*), that is, go around it. *Al-Lātt* is also read, with doubling; they claimed that it was named after a man at whose place butter was mixed (*yulattu*) with oil and fed to the pilgrims. From Mujāhid: there was a man at al-Ṭāʾif who mixed barley meal, and they used to stay at his grave, and they made it an idol. Al-ʿUzzā belonged to Ghaṭafān; it was an acacia tree, and in origin it is the feminine of *al-aʿazz* (“the mightiest”). The Messenger of God ﷺ sent Khālid ibn al-Walīd to it and he cut it down. A she-devil came out of it with her hair loose, crying woe, her hand on her head, and he kept striking her with the sword until he killed her, saying: “*ʿUzzā, I deny you, I do not glorify you; I have seen that God has abased you*.” {p|423}He went back and told the Messenger of God ﷺ, and he ﵇ said, “*That was al-ʿUzzā, and she will never be worshiped again*.”

Manāt was a rock that belonged to Hudhayl and Khuzāʿa; from Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄, to Thaqīf. *Manāʾa* is also read. It seems it was called Manāt because the blood of sacrifices was shed (*tumnā*) at it; and *Manāʾa* is *mafʿala* from *al-nawʾ* (“the rain star”), as though they used to ask the rain stars for rain at it, seeking blessing from it. *Al-ukhrā* (“the other”) is censure: it is the one put back, low in worth, as in the saying of God Most High “*the latest one will say of the previous one*” (Q 7:38), that is, their low ones to their heads and nobles. It may also be that the first rank and precedence among them belonged to al-Lāt and al-ʿUzzā.

They used to say that the angels and these idols were the daughters of God, and they worshiped them and claimed that they were their intercessors with God Most High, while they themselves buried their daughters alive. So it was said to them: “*Do you have sons, while He has these as daughters?*” It may also be meant that al-Lāt, al-ʿUzzā and Manāt are females, and you have made them partners to God, while it is your way to despise females and to disdain that they should be born to you and traced to you; so how do you make these females rivals to God and call them gods?

{L|What a disgraceful distribution!|qismatun ḍīzā} an unjust one, from *ḍāzahu yaḍīzuhu* when he wronged him. The origin is *ḍūzā*, and it was treated as *bīḍ* was treated, so that the *yāʾ* might stay sound. *Ḍiʾzā* is also read, from *ḍaʾazahu*, with *hamza*; and *ḍayzā*, with *fatḥa* on the *ḍād*.

{L|They|hiya} the pronoun is for the idols: that is, they are nothing “*but names*” with nothing named beneath them in reality, because you claim divinity for what is furthest from it and most at odds with it. Like it is the saying of God Most High “*You do not worship beside Him except innovations that you have made up*” (Q 12:40). Or it is the pronoun for the names, which are their words “al-Lāt,” “al-ʿUzzā” and “Manāt,” by which they mean the gods. He means: these names are only names you have named out of your desire and your appetite; you have no proof from God for the soundness of naming them that you can hold to. The meaning of *sammaytumūhā* is “you named with them”; one says *sammaytuhu zaydan* and *sammaytuhu bi-zaydin* (“I named him Zayd”). “*They follow*”: also read with *tāʾ*. “*They follow conjecture*”: only the fancy that what they are on is true, and that their gods are their intercessors, and what their souls desire; and they leave the guidance that has come to them and the proof that their religion is false.

::: passage 53:24-25 | 4:424-424

{L|What is it that the human being desires?|am li-l-insāni mā tamannā} it is the *am* that breaks off, and the sense of the *hamza* in it is for disowning: that is, man shall not have what he wishes. What is meant is their hope in the intercession of the gods, which is a wish upon God of the utmost remoteness. It is also said: it is their saying “*Even if I am returned to my Lord, I will find at Him better things*” (Q 41:50). It is also said: it is the saying of al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra, “*I will be given wealth and children*” (Q 19:77). It is also said: it is the wish of some of them to be the prophet ﷺ. “*To GOD belongs both the Hereafter, and this world*”: that is, He is their owner, so He gives of them to whom He will and withholds from whom He will, and no one may dictate to Him in anything of them.

::: passage 53:26-26 | 4:424-424

He means that the matter of intercession is narrow. That is, the angels, for all their nearness and closeness, their great number and the heavens being crowded with their hosts, if they all interceded for someone their intercession would never avail him anything or profit him, except when they intercede after God gives them leave to intercede for one whom He wills to be interceded for, whom He approves and sees as worthy to be interceded for. So how should the idols intercede with Him for their worshipers?

::: passage 53:27-30 | 4:424-425

{L|Have given the angels feminine names|la-yusammūna l-malāʾikata} that is, each one of them, “*with the naming of the female*,” because when they said the angels are the daughters of God, they named each one of them “daughter,” and that is the naming of the female. “*They had no knowledge about this*”: that is, of that, or of what they say. In the reading of Ubayy: *bihā*, that is, of the angels, or of the naming. “*Conjecture is no substitute for the truth*”: He means that the truth, which is the reality of a thing and what it is, is grasped only by knowledge and certainty, not by conjecture and fancy. “*You shall disregard*” from calling one whom you have seen turning away from the remembrance of God and the Hereafter and wanting only this world, and do not wear yourself out over his becoming a Muslim. Then He said: “*Your Lord is fully aware*”: {p|425}that is, only God knows who will answer and who will not, and you do not know; so go easy on yourself and do not weary it, for you do not guide whom you love, and you have only to deliver. His saying “*This is the extent of their knowledge*” is a parenthesis. Or: turn away from him and do not meet him; your Lord knows best the one who strays and the one who is guided, and He will repay both with the repayment they deserve.

::: passage 53:31-32 | 4:425-426

*Li-yajziya* and *wa-yajziya* are read with *yāʾ*, and with *nūn* in both. Its meaning is that God, mighty and majestic is He, created the world and fashioned this kingdom only for this aim: to repay the doer of good among those under obligation and the doer of evil among them. It may also be attached to His saying “*He knows best who strays from His way, and He knows best who is guided*,” because the outcome of knowing the one who strays and the one who is guided is repaying them. “*For their works*”: with the punishment of the evil they did. “*For their righteousness*” (*bi-l-ḥusnā*): with the best reward, which is Paradise; or because of the evil they did and because of the best works.

{L|Gross sins|kabāʾira l-ithmi} that is, the grave ones of sin, because sin is a kind that takes in grave ones and small ones. The grave sins are the sins whose punishment does not fall away except by repentance. It is also said: those whose punishment is great in proportion to the reward of the one who commits them. “*And transgressions*” (*wa-l-fawāḥish*): those of the grave sins that are foul, as though He said: and the indecencies among them in particular. *Kabīra l-ithmi* is also read, that is, the grave kind of it; it is also said that it is associating others with God. *Al-lamam* is what is slight and small. From it is *al-lamam*, a touch of madness, a trace of it; and *alamma bi-l-makān*, when his stay in the place is short; and *alamma bi-l-ṭaʿām*, when he eats little of it. From it is: “*The meeting of pure friends is brief*.” {p|426}What is meant is the small sins.

His saying “*except for minor offenses*” must be either an exception that stands apart, or a description, as in the saying of God Most High “*If there were in them (the heavens and the earth) other gods beside GOD*” (Q 21:22); as though it were said: grave sins other than the slight, and gods other than God. From Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī: the slight is the glance, the wink and the kiss. From al-Suddī: the passing thought of a sin. From al-Kalbī: every sin for which God has mentioned no fixed penalty and no punishment. From ʿAṭāʾ: the habit of the soul from time to time. “*Your Lord’s forgiveness is immense*”: since He expiates the small sins by the avoiding of the grave ones, and the grave ones by repentance.

{L|Therefore, do not exalt yourselves|fa-lā tuzakkū anfusakum} do not ascribe to them purity of works, increase in good and the doing of acts of obedience, or purity and cleanness from acts of disobedience; do not praise them, but hold them down, for God knew the pure and the God-fearing among you first and last, before He brought you out of the loins of Adam and before you came out of your mothers’ wombs. It is said that some people used to do good works and then say, “Our prayer, our fasting, our pilgrimage,” and it was revealed. This is when it is by way of self-admiration or showing off. But whoever believes that the righteous work he has done is from God, by His granting success and His support, and does not mean by it to praise himself, is not among those who claim purity for themselves, because joy at obedience is obedience, and speaking of it is thanks.

::: passage 53:33-54 | 4:426-429

{p|427}{L|And then very little|wa-akdā} he cut off his giving and held back. Its origin is the digger’s *ikdāʾ*, when he meets a *kudya*, a hardness like rock, and stops digging; like it is *ajbala l-ḥāfiru* (“the digger struck a mountain”); then it was borrowed, and it was said *ajbala l-shāʿiru* when the poet was struck dumb. It is related that ʿUthmān ﵁ used to give his wealth in good causes. ʿAbd Allāh ibn Saʿd ibn Abī Sarḥ, his foster brother, said to him, “*Soon nothing will be left to you*.” ʿUthmān said, “*I have sins and faults, and by what I do I seek the pleasure of God Most High and hope for His pardon*.” ʿAbd Allāh said, “*Give me your she-camel with her saddle and I will bear all your sins for you*.” He gave it to him, took witnesses against him, and held back from giving. Then it was revealed. The meaning of “*turned away*” is that he left his post on the day of Uḥud. Then ʿUthmān went back to what was better and finer than that. “*Could he see it?*”: so he knows that what his brother told him about bearing his burdens is true.

{L|Who fulfilled|wa-ffā} read light and doubled. The doubling is for emphasis in fulfilling; or it is in the sense of “he gave in full and completed,” as in the saying of God Most High “*and he fulfilled them*” (Q 2:124). It is left unrestricted so as to take in every fulfilling and every giving in full. Of that is his delivering the message, his bearing the loads of prophethood alone, his patience in the sacrifice of his son and in the fire of Nimrod, his tending to his guests and serving them himself, and that he used to go out every day and walk a parasang looking for a guest; if he found one he honored him, and otherwise he made the intention to fast. From al-Ḥasan: God commanded him nothing but he fulfilled it. From al-Hudhayl ibn Shuraḥbīl: between Noah and Abraham a man was seized for the crime of another; he was killed for his father, his son, his paternal uncle and his maternal uncle, the husband for his wife and the slave for his master; and the first to oppose them was Abraham. From ʿAṭāʾ ibn al-Sāʾib: he pledged not to ask any created being; and when he was thrown into the fire, Gabriel and Michael said to him, “*Have you any need?*” He said, “*Of you two, no*.” From the Prophet ﷺ: “*He fulfilled his work each day with four* rakʿa*s at the start of the day*,” which is the forenoon prayer. It is related: “*Shall I not tell you why God named His friend ‘who fulfilled’? He used to say when he rose in the morning and at evening: ‘you shall glorify GOD when you retire at night…’ to ‘…in the middle of your day’*” (Q 30:17–18). It is also said: he fulfilled the portions of Islam, which are thirty: ten in al-Tawba, “*They are the repenters…*” (Q 9:112); ten in al-Aḥzāb, “*The submitting men, the submitting women…*” (Q 33:35); and ten in al-Muʾminūn, “*Successful indeed are the believers…*” (Q 23:1). *Fī ṣuḥfi*, with the light form, is also read.

{L|No soul bears|allā taziru} *an* is lightened from the heavy form. The meaning is that it is so that {p|428}no bearer bears; the pronoun is the pronoun of the matter. The place of *an* and what follows it is the genitive, as a substitute for “*the scripture of Moses*”; or the nominative, as in “it is that no bearer bears,” as if someone said, “And what is in the scrolls of Moses and Abraham?” and it was said, “That no bearer bears.” “*for his own works*”: only his own striving.

{Q} Is it not sound in the reports that alms on behalf of the dead and pilgrimage on his behalf [profit him], and that the reward is multiplied by them? {A} There are two answers to it. One: since another’s striving profits him only when it is built on his own striving, which is that he be a righteous believer, and likewise the multiplying, another’s striving is as though it were his own, because it follows it and stands by its standing. The second: another’s striving does not profit him when that one does it for himself; but when he intends it for him, then by the ruling of the law he is like one who stands in for him, an agent taking his place.

{L|Then they will be paid fully|thumma yujzāhu} then the servant will be repaid his striving. One says *jazāhu llāhu ʿamalahu* and *jazāhu ʿalā ʿamalihi*, dropping the preposition and joining the verb directly. The pronoun may also be for the repayment, which He then explains with His saying “*the fullest repayment*,” or makes it a substitute for it, as in the saying of God Most High “*And the transgressors confer secretly*” (Q 21:3).

{L|To your Lord is the final destiny|wa-anna ilā rabbika l-muntahā} read with *fatḥa*, with the sense that all this is in the scrolls; and with *kasra*, as a fresh start; and likewise what follows. *Al-muntahā* is a verbal noun in the sense of *al-intihāʾ*: that is, creatures come to an end with Him and return to Him, as in the saying of God Most High “*To GOD is the ultimate destiny*” (Q 3:28). “*Makes you laugh or cry*”: He created the powers of laughing and weeping. “*From a tiny drop of semen*” (*idhā tumnā*, “when it is emitted”): when it gushes into the womb; one says *manā* and *amnā*. From al-Akhfash: it is created, from *manā l-mānī*, that is, the One who decrees has decreed. *Al-nashʾa* and *al-nashāʾa*, with the long vowel, are read. He said “*on Him*” because it is binding on Him in wisdom, so that He may repay good and evil. “*Makes you rich or poor*” (*aqnā*, “gave possessions”): He gave *qinya*, which is the wealth you have gathered and have resolved not to let out of your hand.

{L|The Lord of the galaxies|al-shiʿrā} the *mirzam* of Orion, the star that rises behind it; it is called the Dog of the Giant. There are two *Shiʿrā*s, al-Ghumayṣāʾ and al-ʿAbūr, and He meant al-ʿAbūr. Khuzāʿa used to worship it; Abū Kabsha, a man of their nobles, set that up for them, {p|429}and Quraysh used to call the Messenger of God ﷺ “Abū Kabsha,” likening him to him for his going against them in their religion. He means that He is the Lord of this thing they worship. The first ʿĀd is the people of Hūd, and the other ʿĀd is Iram. It is also said: “*the first*” is the ancient ones, because they were the first of the communities to be destroyed after the people of Noah; or the foremost in this world, the nobles. *ʿĀdani llūlā* and *ʿĀda llūlā* are also read, assimilating the nunation into the *lām*, dropping the *hamza* of *ūlā*, and moving its *ḍamma* to the *lām* of the article. *Wa-thamūda* is also read.

{L|They were evil transgressors|aẓlama wa-aṭghā} because they used to hurt him and beat him until he could not stir, and drove people from him so that they warned their children against listening to him, and his calling had no effect on them for nearly a thousand years. “*The evil communities*” (*wa-l-muʾtafika*): the towns that were overturned with their people, that is, turned over; they are the people of Lot. One says *afakahu fa-ʾtafaka* (“he overturned it and it was overturned”). *Wa-l-muʾtafikāt* is also read. “*Were the lowliest*” (*ahwā*): He raised it to the sky on the wing of Gabriel, then brought it down to the earth, that is, made it fall. “*They utterly vanished*” (*mā ghashshā*): making terrible and great the punishment that was poured on it and the stone laid in layers that was rained on it.

{L|Which of your Lord’s marvels can you deny?|fa-bi-ayyi ālāʾi rabbika tatamārā} do you doubt. The address is to the Messenger of God ﷺ, or to man in general. He has counted up favors and vengeances, and called them all favors because of the restraints and admonitions in His vengeances for those who take warning. “*This*,” the Qurʾān, “*This is a warning like the older ones*”: that is, a warning of the kind of the first warnings with which those before you were warned. Or: this messenger is a warner of the first warners; He said *al-ūlā* (feminine) taking it as a group.

{L|The inevitable is imminent|azifati l-āzifatu} the one described with nearness in the saying of God Most High “*The Hour has come closer*” (Q 54:1) has come near. “*It has*” no soul “*can relieve it*” (*kāshifa*): that is, that makes plain when it will come, as in the saying of God Most High “*Only He reveals its time*” (Q 7:187). Or: it has no soul that discloses it, that is, able to lift it away when it comes, except God, though He does not lift it. Or: it has no soul now that puts it off by delaying it. It is also said that *kāshifa* is a verbal noun in the sense of disclosing, like *al-ʿāfiya*.
