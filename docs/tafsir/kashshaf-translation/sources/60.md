::: passage 60:1-2 | 4:510-513

{p|511}It is related that a freedwoman of Abū ʿAmr ibn Ṣayfī ibn Hāshim, called Sāra, came to the Messenger of God ﷺ in Medina while he was making ready for the conquest. He said to her, “*Have you come as a Muslim?*” She said, “*No*.” He said, “*Have you come as an emigrant?*” She said, “*No*.” He said, “*Then what brought you?*” She said, “*You were the family, the patrons and the clan, and the patrons are gone*,” meaning they were killed on the day of Badr, “*and I am in great want*.” He urged the Banū ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib on her behalf, and they clothed her, gave her a mount and provisioned her. Ḥāṭib ibn Abī Baltaʿa came to her, gave her ten dinars, clothed her in a cloak, and had her carry a letter to the people of Mecca. Its copy was: “*From Ḥāṭib ibn Abī Baltaʿa to the people of Mecca: know that the Messenger of God ﷺ means to come against you, so take your precautions*.” Sāra went out, and Gabriel came down with the news. The Messenger of God ﷺ sent ʿAlī, ʿAmmār, ʿUmar, Ṭalḥa, al-Zubayr, al-Miqdād and Abū Marthad, who were horsemen, and said, “*Go until you come to the meadow of Khākh. In it is a woman in a litter who has a letter from Ḥāṭib to the people of Mecca. Take it from her and let her go; and if she refuses, strike off her head*.” They overtook her, and she denied it and swore. They were about to turn back when ʿAlī ﵁ said, “*By God, we have not been lied to, and the Messenger of God has not lied*.” He drew his sword and said, “*Bring out the letter or lay down your head*.” She brought it out from the braids of her hair. It is related that on the day of the conquest the Messenger of God ﷺ gave safety to all the people except four, and she was one of them. The Messenger of God summoned Ḥāṭib and said, “{p|512}*What led you to it?*” He said, “*Messenger of God, I have not disbelieved since I became Muslim, nor deceived you since I gave you good counsel, nor loved them since I parted from them. But I was a man attached to Quraysh*,” it is also related, “*a stranger among them*,” “*and not of their own stock. Everyone with you of the emigrants has kin in Mecca who protect their families and wealth, except me. So I feared for my family, and I wanted to have a hand of favor with them. I knew that God would bring His might down on them, and that my letter would avail them nothing*.” He believed him and accepted his excuse. ʿUmar said, “*Let me, Messenger of God, strike off the head of this hypocrite*.” He said, “*And what makes you know, ʿUmar? Perhaps God has looked upon the people of Badr and said to them, ‘Do what you will, for I have forgiven you.’*” ʿUmar’s eyes overflowed, and he said, “*God and His Messenger know best*”; and it was revealed.

He made “*take*” pass to its two objects, which are “*My enemy*” and “*friends*.” *ʿAduww* is of the pattern *faʿūl*, from *ʿadā*, as *ʿafuww* is from *ʿafā*; and because it is on the pattern of the verbal noun it is applied to the plural as it is applied to the singular.

{Q} With what is “*offering*” connected? {A} It may be connected with “*do not take*” as a circumstantial clause from its pronoun, and with “*friends*” as a description of it. It may also be a new sentence.

{Q} When you make it a description of “*friends*” while it runs on someone other than the one it belongs to, where is the pronoun set out in the open, as in your saying “offering them, you, affection”? {A} They made that a condition only in nouns, not in verbs. Had it been said, “friends offering them affection,” as a description, there would have been no escaping the open pronoun. Offering is an expression for conveying affection and laying it out to them. One says, “He threw him the scraps of his breast,” and “He laid open to him his husks.” The *bāʾ* in “*affection*” is either extra, strengthening the passing of the verb, as it is in “*do not throw yourselves with your own hands into destruction*” (Q 2:195); or it is firm, on the view that the object of “*offering*” is dropped, meaning: you offer them news of the Messenger of God because of the affection between you and them. Likewise His saying “*how can you secretly love them?*” (*tusirrūna ilayhim bi-l-mawadda*): that is, you lay out your affection to them secretly; or you confide to them the secrets of the Messenger of God because of the affection.

{Q} “*even though they have disbelieved*” is a circumstantial clause from what? {A} Either from “*do not take*” or from “*offering*,” that is: do not take them as friends, or show them affection, while this is their state. “*They drive out*” is a new sentence, like an explanation of their unbelief and their insolence; or it is a circumstantial clause from “*disbelieved*.” “*Because you believe*” gives the reason for “*they drive out*,” that is: they drive you out for your faith. “*If you have gone out*” is connected with “*do not take*.” He means: do not take My enemies as friends if you are My friends. The grammarians say of the like of it: it is a condition whose answer is dropped because what comes before points to it. “*You confide*” is a new sentence, and its meaning is: what profit is there for you in your confiding, when you have learned that hiding and showing are alike in My knowledge, with no difference between them, and that I inform My Messenger of what you confide? “*Those among you who do this*”: whoever does this confiding has missed the way of truth and right. Al-Jaḥdarī read *li-mā jāʾakum*, that is, they disbelieved because of what came to you; meaning that what ought {p|513}to have been the cause of their faith they made the cause of their unbelief.

{L|Whenever they encounter you|in yathqafūkum} if they overcome you and gain power over you, “*they treat you as enemies*,” pure in enmity, and will not be friends to you as you are to them, “*and hurt you with their hands and tongues*,” with fighting and reviling; and they wished that you would turn back from your religion. So then, affection for the like of them and giving them good counsel is a great error on your part and a deceiving of yourselves. Like it is the saying of God Most High “*who never cease to wish you harm*” (Q 3:118).

{Q} How did He bring the answer of the condition as an imperfect like it, and then say “*and they wished*” in the wording of the perfect? {A} The perfect, though in the chapter of conditions it runs as the imperfect does in the science of case endings, has a fine point here. It is as though it were said: and before anything they wished for your unbelief and your apostasy. He means that they want to bring on you the harms of this world and of religion together: killing of lives, tearing of honor, and turning you back unbelievers. Turning you back unbelievers is the foremost and first of the harms with them, because they know that religion is dearer to you than your lives, since you are ready to give your lives for it; and the enemy’s chief concern is to aim at what is dearest to his opponent.

{L|Your relatives and your money can never help you|lan tanfaʿakum arḥāmukum} that is, your kin, “*nor your children*,” for whose sake you take the unbelievers as friends and draw near to them to protect them. Then He said: “*On the Day of Resurrection, He will judge among you*,” from your kin and your children: “*the day when one flees from his brother*” (Q 80:34). So what is the matter with you that you cast off God’s due out of regard for the due of one who will flee from you tomorrow? He showed their judgment wrong in befriending the unbelievers, first by what comes back to the state of those they befriended, then second by what comes back to the state of those who called for that friendship, to show them that from whatever side you look at what they ventured on, you find it false. *Yufṣalu* and *yufaṣṣalu* are read, built for the passive; *yafṣilu* and *yufaṣṣilu*, built for the active, its doer being God, mighty and majestic is He; and *nafṣilu* and *nufaṣṣilu*, with *nūn*.

{p|514}*Uswa* and *iswa* are read. It is the name of what is followed as a model. That is, there was in them a fine way, approved, to be followed as a model and walked after: their saying to the unbelievers of their people what they said, when they laid bare their enmity to them, stripped the stick bare to them, showed hatred and loathing, and declared that the cause of their enmity and hatred was nothing but their unbelief in God, and that while this cause stood the enmity would stand; so that if they removed it and believed in God alone, the enmity would turn to friendship, the hatred to love, and the loathing to fondness. So they spoke plainly of pure sincerity. The meaning of “*we disbelieve in you*” and in what you worship apart from God is: we take no account of you or of your gods, and you are nothing to us.

{Q} From what is His saying “*However, a mistake was committed by Abraham when he said*” excepted? {A} From His saying “*a good example*,” because by the good example He meant their saying, which they were bound to take as a model and take as a practice to follow.

{Q} If His saying “*I will pray for your forgiveness*” is excepted from the saying that is a good example, what of His saying “*but I possess no power to protect you from GOD*,” which does not deserve to be excepted? Do you not see His saying “*Say, ‘Who could oppose GOD …?’*” (Q 5:17) {A} He meant to except the whole of his saying to his father, the aim being the promise to ask forgiveness for him; and what comes after is built on it and follows it, as though he said: I will ask forgiveness for you, and nothing is in my power but asking forgiveness.

{Q} With what is His saying “*Our Lord, we trust in You*” joined? {A} With what comes before the exception, and it is part of the good example. The meaning may also be: say, “Our Lord,” a command from God Most High to the believers to say it, and a teaching from Him to them, completing what He charged them with of cutting the ties between them and the unbelievers and following Abraham and his people in disowning them; and drawing attention to turning to God, seeking refuge with Him from the trial of the people of unbelief, and asking forgiveness for what they had let slip. *Buraʾāʾu*, like *shurakāʾu*, is also read; *birāʾun*, like *ẓirāfun*; *burāʾun*, with *ḍamma* put in place of *kasra*, like *rukhālun* and *rubābun*; and *barāʾun*, describing by the verbal noun. *Al-barāʾ* and *al-barāʾa* are like *al-ẓamāʾ* and *al-ẓamāʾa*.

Then He repeated the urging to follow Abraham and his people, to set it firm and stress it on them. For that reason He brought it opening with an oath, because that is the utmost in emphasis. He made “*for those who seek GOD and the Last Day*” a substitute for “*for you*,” and followed it with His saying “*As for those who turn away, GOD is in no need (of them), Most Praiseworthy*.” So He left no kind of emphasis that He did not bring.

::: passage 60:7-7 | 4:515-515

When these verses were revealed, the believers were hard in their enmity to their fathers, their sons and all their kin among the associators and in cutting them off. When God, mighty and majestic is He, saw from them earnestness and patience in that hard way, and long wishing for a cause that would make it lawful for them to be friends and keep ties, He had mercy on them and promised them to make easy what they wished. When He made the conquest of Mecca easy, God granted them their wish: their people became Muslim, and there came about between them the love and clear friendship that came about. It is also said: the Messenger of God ﷺ married Umm Ḥabība, and at that the temper of Abū Sufyān softened and his bit slackened in enmity. Umm Ḥabība had become Muslim and emigrated with her husband ʿAbd Allāh ibn Jaḥsh to Abyssinia. He became a Christian and wanted her to become a Christian; she refused and held patiently to her religion, and her husband died. The Messenger of God ﷺ sent to the Negus, who asked her in marriage for him and paid her bride-gift for him, four hundred dinars. That reached her father, and he said, “*That stallion is not one whose nose is struck back*.” “*Perhaps*” is a promise from God, after the custom of kings, who say of some needs, “perhaps” or “it may be,” and the one in need is left with no doubt that it will be done. Or by it He meant to give the believers hope. “*GOD is Omnipotent*” to turn hearts about, change states and make easy the causes of affection; “*GOD is Forgiver, Most Merciful*” to whoever of the associators becomes Muslim.

{p|516}{L|You may befriend them|an tabarrūhum} a substitute for “*those who do not fight you*”; and likewise “*befriending*” for “*those who fight you*.” The meaning is: He does not forbid you goodness to these, but forbids you only friendship with those. This too was a mercy to them for their hardness and earnestness in enmity, coming before His mercy in making easy their people’s becoming Muslim, since He gave them leave to keep ties with those of them who did not openly fight the believers and drive them from their homes. It is said that by them He meant Khuzāʿa, who had made peace with the Messenger of God ﷺ on the terms that they would not fight him nor help against him. From Mujāhid: they are those who believed in Mecca and did not emigrate. It is also said: they are the women and children. It is also said: the mother of Asmāʾ bint Abī Bakr, Qutayla bint ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā, came to her while she was an associator, with gifts, and she did not accept them and did not give her leave to come in. It was revealed, and the Messenger of God ﵌ commanded her to let her in, accept from her, honor her and treat her well. From Qatāda: the verse of fighting abrogated it. “*And be equitable towards them*”: give them their due with justice and do not wrong them. It is enough for you that God charged the believers to use justice with those who associate others with Him and to keep from wronging them, which speaks for the state of a Muslim who dares to wrong his Muslim brother.

::: passage 60:10-11 | 4:516-519

{p|517}{L|When believing women (abandon the enemy and) ask for asylum with you|idhā jāʾakumu l-muʾminātu} He named them believers because they confessed with their tongues and uttered the word of witness, and nothing appeared from them that ran against it; or because they were close to having their faith made firm by the testing. “*Test them*”: try them by oath and by looking at the signs, so that the truthfulness of their faith becomes most likely in your minds. The Messenger of God ﷺ used to say to the woman being tested: “*By God, besides whom there is no god, you did not go out from hatred of a husband; by God, you did not go out wanting one land over another; by God, you did not go out seeking this world; by God, you went out only for love of God and His Messenger*.” “*God knows best their faith*,” better than you, because you do not gain in it a knowledge with which your souls are at rest, even if you have made them swear and weighed their states; with God is the reality of knowing it. “*Once you establish that they are believers*,” with the knowledge that your capacity reaches, which is the most likely opinion through oath and the appearing of signs, “*you shall not return them to the disbelievers*”: do not return them to their associator husbands, because there is nothing lawful between a believing woman and an associator. “*Give back the dowries that the disbelievers have paid*”: give their husbands the like of what they paid them in bride-gifts. That is because the peace of al-Ḥudaybiya was on the terms that whoever {p|518}came to you from the people of Mecca would be returned to them, and whoever of you came to Mecca would not be returned to you; they wrote a document of that and sealed it. Then Subayʿa bint al-Ḥārith al-Aslamiyya came as a Muslim while the Prophet ﷺ was at al-Ḥudaybiya. Her husband Musāfir al-Makhzūmī came up, it is also said Ṣayfī ibn al-Rāhib, and said, “*Muḥammad, give my wife back to me, for you made it a condition for us that you would return to us whoever of us came to you, and this is the clay of the document, not yet dry*.” It was revealed to make plain that the condition was only about men, not women. From al-Ḍaḥḥāk: there was a covenant between the Messenger of God ﷺ and the associators: “*No woman of ours who is not of your religion shall come to you but you return her to us; and if she enters your religion and has a husband, that she be returned to her husband who spent on her*”; and the Prophet ﷺ had the like of that in the terms. From Qatāda: then Barāʾa abrogated this ruling and this covenant. The Messenger of God ﷺ had her swear, and she swore; he gave her husband what he had spent, and ʿUmar married her.

{Q} How did He call opinion knowledge in His saying “*if you know them*”? {A} To make known that the most likely opinion, and what independent reasoning and analogy lead to, runs as knowledge does, and that one who holds it does not come under His saying “*You shall not accept any information, unless you verify it for yourself*” (Q 17:36).

{Q} Then what is the use of His saying “*God knows best their faith*,” when that is known with no doubt in it? {A} Its use is to make plain that you have no way to what the soul rests in and the breast is cooled by, of taking in the reality of their faith, for that is among what the Knower of the unseen keeps to Himself; that the knowledge the testing leads to is enough in that; and that your obligation does not go beyond it.

Then He denied that there was any blame on them in marrying these emigrant women when they gave them their wages, that is, their bride-gifts, because the bride-gift is the wage of the private part. And it must be one of these: either what was paid to them so that they would pay it to their husbands is meant, and so paying it first is made a condition of its being lawful to marry them; or it is meant that, if that was paid to them as a loan and they were then married on that basis, there is no harm in it; or He makes plain to them that what was given to their husbands does not stand in place of the bride-gift, and that a bride-gift must be given. By it Abū Ḥanīfa argued that when one of the spouses comes out of the abode of war as a Muslim, or under protection, and the other remains a person of the abode of war, separation takes place; and he does not hold that the emigrant woman owes a waiting period, and allows her marriage, unless she is pregnant.

{L|Do not keep disbelieving wives|wa-lā tumsikū bi-ʿiṣami l-kawāfiri} *al-ʿiṣma* is what one holds to, of a contract or a bond. He means: keep away from them, and let there be between you and them no tie and no bond of marriage. Ibn ʿAbbās said: whoever had an unbelieving wife in Mecca, let him not count her among his wives, because the difference of the two abodes has cut her tie with him. From al-Nakhaʿī: she is the Muslim woman who joins the abode of war and disbelieves. From Mujāhid: He commanded them to divorce those who remained with the unbelievers and to part from them. “*You may ask them for the dowry you had paid*,” of the bride-gifts of your wives who joined the unbelievers, “*and they may ask for what they paid*,” of the bride-gifts of their wives who emigrated. *Wa-lā tumsikū* is read, light; *wa-lā tumassikū*, heavy; and *wa-lā tamassakū*, that is, do not hold fast. “*This is GOD’s rule*”: He means everything mentioned in this verse. “*He judges between you*” is a new sentence; or a circumstantial clause from “*GOD’s rule*,” on {p|519}the dropping of the pronoun, that is, God judges it; or He made the judgment a judge, for emphasis.

It is related that when this verse was revealed the believers did what they were commanded, paying the bride-gifts of the emigrant women to their associator husbands, and the associators refused to pay anything of the bride-gifts of the unbelieving women to their Muslim husbands. Then His saying was revealed: “*If any of your wives join the enemies’ camp*”: and if anything of your wives, any one of them, gets away from you and slips off to the unbelievers. In the reading of Ibn Masʿūd it is *aḥadun*.

{Q} Does putting “*anything*” in this place have a use? {A} Yes. Its use is that nothing of this kind, however small and slight, is to be left uncompensated, to make this ruling heavy and strict.

{L|You are forced to fight|fa-ʿāqabtum} from *al-ʿuqba*, which is the turn. He likened what was ruled for the Muslims and the unbelievers, of these paying the bride-gifts of those men’s wives at one time and those the bride-gifts of these men’s wives at another, to a matter in which they take turns, as turns are taken in riding and other things. Its meaning is: then your turn came to pay the bride-gift; so give whoever lost his wife to the unbelievers the like of her bride-gift from the bride-gift of the emigrant woman, and do not give it to her unbelieving husband. So it is from al-Zuhrī: he is given from the dowry of the one who joined them. *Fa-aʿqabtum* is also read; *fa-ʿaqqabtum*, heavy; and *fa-ʿaqabtum* and *fa-ʿaqibtum*, light, with *fatḥa* and *kasra* on the *qāf*. The meaning of *aʿqabtum* is: you entered on the turn. *ʿAqqabtum* is from *ʿaqqabahu*, when he followed behind him, because each of those taking turns follows behind the other. Likewise *ʿaqabtum*, light; one says *ʿaqabahu yaʿqubuhu*; and *ʿaqibtum* is like *tabiʿtum*. Al-Zajjāj said: “*you are forced to fight*” (*fa-ʿāqabtum*, “then you had your turn”): you struck them in fighting with a punishment until you took spoils; and the one whose wife had gone was given the bride-gift from the spoils. He explained the other readings as: then the outcome was yours, that is, victory was yours until you took spoils. It is said that all the women of the emigrant believers who joined the associators, turning back from Islam, were six: Umm al-Ḥakam bint Abī Sufyān, who was the wife of ʿIyāḍ ibn Shaddād al-Fihrī; Fāṭima bint Abī Umayya, the wife of ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, the sister of Umm Salama; Barwaʿ bint ʿUqba, the wife of Shammās ibn ʿUthmān; ʿAbda bint ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā ibn Naḍla, whose husband was ʿAmr ibn ʿAbd Wudd; Hind bint Abī Jahl, the wife of Hishām ibn al-ʿĀṣ; and Kulthūm bint Jarwal, the wife of ʿUmar. The Messenger of God ﷺ gave them the bride-gifts of their wives from the spoils.

{p|520}{L|Nor kill their children|wa-lā yaqtulna awlādahunna} *yuqattilna*, heavy, is also read. He means the burying alive of daughters. “*Nor fabricate any falsehood*” (*wa-lā yaʾtīna bi-buhtānin yaftarīnahu bayna aydīhinna wa-arjulihinna*, “nor bring a slander that they invent between their hands and their feet”): a woman would pick up a foundling and say to her husband, “He is my child by you.” He alluded by the slander invented between her hands and her feet to the child she falsely fastens on her husband, because her belly, in which she carries it, is between the hands, and her private part, by which she bears it, is between the feet. “*Nor disobey your righteous orders*”: in the good things you command them and the ugly things you forbid them. It is also said: everything that agrees with obedience to God is right.

{Q} Had He stopped at His saying “*nor disobey your righteous orders*,” it would be known that the Messenger of God ﷺ commands only what is right. {A} By that He drew attention to the fact that obeying a creature in disobedience to the Creator deserves the utmost guarding against and keeping away from.

It is related that when the Messenger of God ﷺ had finished the pledge of the men on the day of the conquest of Mecca, he began on the pledge of the women while he was on al-Ṣafā, and ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb ﵁ was below him, taking their pledge by his command and passing it on to them from him. Hind bint ʿUtba, the wife of Abū Sufyān, was veiled and disguised, fearing that the Messenger of God ﷺ would recognize her. He ﵊ said, “*I take your pledge that you will not associate anything with God*.” Hind raised her head and said, “*By God, we have worshiped idols, and you are taking from us a thing we have not seen you take from the men; you take the pledge of the men for Islam and striving*.” He ﵊ said, “*And that they will not steal*.” She said, “*Abū Sufyān is a stingy man, and I have taken some small things of his wealth, and I do not know whether they are lawful to me or not*.” Abū Sufyān said, “*Whatever you took of anything in what is past and what remains is lawful to you*.” The Messenger of God ﷺ laughed and recognized her, and said to her, “*And are you Hind bint ʿUtba?*” She said, “*Yes. So pardon what is past, Prophet of God, and may God pardon you*.” He said, “*And that they will not commit adultery*.” She said, “*Does a freeborn woman commit adultery?*”; in another report, “*Not one of them ever committed adultery*.” He ﵊ said, “*And that they will not kill their children*.” She said, “*We raised them small and you killed them grown, so you and they know best*.” Her son Ḥanẓala ibn Abī Sufyān had been killed on the day of Badr. ʿUmar laughed until he fell on his back, and the Messenger of God ﷺ smiled. He said, “*And that they will not bring a slander*.” She said, “*By God, slander is an ugly thing, and you command us only to right guidance and noble manners*.” He said, “*And that they will not disobey you in what is right*.” She said, “*By God, we have not sat in this sitting of ours with any thought in our souls of disobeying you in anything*.” It is said of the manner of {p|521}the pledge: he called for a bowl of water and dipped his hand in it, then they dipped their hands. It is also said: he clasped their hands, and on his hand was a Qaṭarī cloth. It is also said: ʿUmar clasped their hands for him.

::: passage 60:13-13 | 4:521-521

It is related that some of the poor Muslims used to keep ties with the Jews so as to get some of their fruits. So it was said to them: “*do not befriend people*” with whom He is angry, “*who are hopelessly stuck in disbelief*” of having any share in the Hereafter because of their stubbornness against the Messenger of God ﷺ, while they know he is the messenger described in the Torah, “*as the unbelievers have despaired*” of their dead being raised and returning alive. It is also said: “*who are already in the graves*” explains “*the disbelievers*,” that is, as the unbelievers who have been buried have despaired of the good of the Hereafter, because they have seen clearly the ugliness of their state and the evil of their end.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-Mumtaḥana, the believing men and women will be intercessors for him on the Day of Resurrection*.”
