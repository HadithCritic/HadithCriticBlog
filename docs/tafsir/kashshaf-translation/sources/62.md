{L|The King, the Most Sacred|al-maliki l-quddūsi} the attributes of God, mighty and exalted is He, are also read in the nominative, as praise, as though it were said: He is the King, the Holy. Had they been read in the accusative it would have been a sound way, as in the Arabs’ saying, “Praise be to God, the One worthy of praise.” *Al-ummī* is related to the community (*umma*) of the Arabs, because among the nations they did not write or read. It is also said: writing began at al-Ṭāʾif; they took it from the people of al-Ḥīra, and the people of al-Ḥīra from the people of al-Anbār. The meaning of “*He is the One who sent to the gentiles a messenger from among them*” is: He sent an unlettered man among an unlettered people, as came in the ḥadīth of Isaiah: “*I will send a blind man among the blind, and an unlettered man among the unlettered*.” It is also said: “*from among them*” is like the saying of God Most High “*from among yourselves*” (Q 9:128): they know his lineage and his states. *Fī l-ummīna* is also read, with the two *yāʾ*s of relation dropped.

{p|530}{L|To recite to them His revelations|yatlū ʿalayhim āyātihi} reading them to them, though he was unlettered like them, no reading had been known from him and he was not known to have learned; and the reading of an unlettered man without learning is a clear sign. “*Purify them*”: cleansing them of associating and the foul things of the Jāhiliyya; “*and teach them the scripture and wisdom*”: the Qurʾān and the Sunna. *In* in “*though they had been*” is the lightened form of the heavy one, and the *lām* points to it: that is, they were in an error than which you see no greater error. “*And others*” is in the genitive, coordinated on “*the unlettered*.” He means that He sent him among the unlettered who were in his time, and among others of the unlettered who have not yet joined them and will join them; they are those after the Companions ﵃. It is said that when it was revealed, he was asked, “*Who are they, Messenger of God?*” He put his hand on Salmān and said, “*Were faith at the Pleiades, men of these would reach it*.” It is also said: they are those who come after them until the Day of Resurrection. It may also be in the accusative, coordinated on the object in “*teaching them*,” that is: He teaches them and teaches others; because when teaching follows on in order to the end of time, all of it rests on its beginning, so it is as though he were the one who took charge of all that came of it. “*He is the Almighty, Most Wise*,” in giving an unlettered man power for that great matter, supporting him in it, and choosing him from among all humankind. “*That*”: the favor He gave Muḥammad, which is that he is the prophet of the people of his age and the prophet of the people of the ages to come, “*Such is GOD’s grace that He bestows upon whomever He wills*” to give it, and His wisdom requires it.

::: passage 62:5-5 | 4:530-530

He likened the Jews, in their being the bearers of the Torah, its readers and the keepers of what is in it, while they do not act on it nor benefit from its signs (that is, in it is the description of the Messenger of God ﷺ and the good news of him, and they did not believe in him), to the ass carrying volumes, that is, large books of the books of learning. He walks with them and knows nothing of them but what passes over his sides and back of toil and fatigue. Everyone who knows and does not act on his knowledge, this is his likeness; and how evil a likeness! “*Evil*” as a likeness “*Miserable indeed is the example of people who rejected GOD’s revelations*”: they are the Jews, who denied God’s signs that point to the soundness of the prophethood of Muḥammad ﷺ. The meaning of “*were given the Torah*” is: they were made responsible for knowing it and acting on it; “*then failed to uphold it*”: then did not act on it, so it was as though they had not carried it. *Ḥamalū l-tawrāta* is also read, that is, they carried it, then did not carry it in reality, for lack of acting on it. *Yaḥmilu l-asfāra* is also read.

{Q} What is the place of “*carrying*”? {A} The accusative, as a circumstantial clause; or the genitive, as a description, because “the ass” is like “the base man” in his saying:

> I pass by the base man who reviles me.

::: passage 62:6-8 | 4:531-531

{L|O you who are Jewish|alladhīna hādū} *hāda yahūdu*: when one becomes a Jew. “*GOD’s chosen*” (*awliyāʾu li-llāh*, “friends of God”): they used to say, “We are the sons of God and His beloved” (Q 5:18). That is: if your saying is true and you are confident, “*then wish*” of God that He make you die and move you quickly to the abode of His honor that He has prepared for His friends. Then He said: “*They will never long for it*,” because of the unbelief they sent ahead. The Messenger of God ﷺ had said to them: “*By Him in whose hand is my soul, none of you says it but he chokes on his spit*.” Had they not been certain of the truthfulness of the Messenger of God ﷺ they would have wished; but they knew that if they wished they would die that very hour and the threat would overtake them. So not one of them could bring himself to wish, and it is one of the miracles. *Fa-tamannawi l-mawta*, with *kasra* on the *wāw*, is also read, likened to *lawi staṭaʿnā*. There is no difference between *lā* and *lan* in that each of them negates the future, except that in *lan* there is an emphasis and stress that is not in *lā*. So once He brought the wording of emphasis, “*and they will never* (lan) *wish for it*” (Q 2:95), and once without it, “*and they will never* (lā) *wish for it*.” Then it was said to them: “*The death that you are trying to evade*,” and that you do not dare to wish for, for fear of being taken for the ill consequence of your unbelief, you will not escape; “*it will meet you*” without fail; “*then you will be returned*” to God, and He will repay you with the punishment you deserve. Zayd ibn ʿAlī ﵁ read *innahu mulāqīkum*. In the reading of Ibn Masʿūd: *tafirrūna minhu mulāqīkum*, and it is plain. As for the one with *fāʾ*, it is because *alladhī* contains the meaning of a condition. In the reading of Zayd, “*The death that you are trying to evade*” was made a speech on its own, that is: death is the thing you flee from; then a new sentence was begun: it will meet you.

::: passage 62:9-10 | 4:531-536

{p|532}*Yawm al-jumuʿa* is the day of the gathered crowd, as they say *ḍuḥka* for the one laughed at. *Yawm al-jumaʿa*, with *fatḥa* on the *mīm*, is the day of the time that gathers, as they say *ḍuḥaka*, *luʿana* and *luʿaba*. *Yawm al-jumuʿa* is the heavy form of *al-jumʿa*, as one says *ʿusur* for *ʿusr*. All of them have been read.

{Q} What is *min* in His saying “*on Friday*” (*min yawmi l-jumuʿa*, “on the day of Friday”)? {A} It explains *idhā* and makes it plain. The call is the call to prayer. They said: what is meant by it is the call when the imām sits on the pulpit. The Messenger of God ﷺ had a single caller to prayer: when he sat on the pulpit he called at the door of the mosque, and when he came down he called the start of the prayer. Then Abū Bakr and ʿUmar ﵄ were on that, until, when ʿUthmān came, the people had grown many and the dwellings far apart, he added another caller, and commanded the first call on his house, which was called al-Zawrāʾ. When he sat on the pulpit the second caller called, and when he came down he called the start of the prayer; and no one found fault with him for that. It is said that the first who named it *jumuʿa* was Kaʿb ibn Luʾayy; it used to be called *al-ʿArūba*. It is also said: the Anṣār said, “*The Jews have a day on which they gather every seven days, and the Christians the like of that; come, let us make a day for ourselves on which we gather, remember God and pray*.” They said, “*Saturday is for the Jews and Sunday for the Christians, so make it the day of al-ʿArūba*.” They gathered to Saʿd ibn Zurāra, and he prayed two *rakʿa*s with them that day and reminded them; and they named it the day of *jumuʿa* for their gathering on it. Then God sent down the verse of Friday. It was the first Friday in Islam. As for the first Friday the Messenger of God ﷺ gathered: when he came to Medina as an emigrant he stopped at Qubāʾ with the Banū ʿAmr ibn ʿAwf, stayed there Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday, and founded their mosque. Then he went out on Friday making for Medina, and the Friday prayer overtook him among the Banū Sālim ibn ʿAwf, in the bed of a valley of theirs; he preached and prayed the Friday prayer. From one of them: God brought to nothing the saying of the Jews in three things. They boasted that they were the friends of God and His beloved, and He gave them the lie in His saying “*then wish for death, if you are truthful*”; that they were the People of the Book and the Arabs had no book, and He likened them to the ass carrying volumes; and in the Sabbath, that the Muslims had nothing like it, and God laid down Friday for them.

From the Prophet ﷺ: “*The best day on which the sun rises is Friday. On it Adam was created, on it he was brought into Paradise, on it he was sent down to the earth, on it the Hour will come; and with God it is the day of more*.” From him ﵇: “*Gabriel came to me with a white mirror in his palm and said, ‘This is Friday, which your Lord presents to you, to be a festival for you and for your community after you. It is the lord of days with us, and in the Hereafter we call it {p|533}the day of more.’*” From him ﷺ: “*God Most High has, on every Friday, six hundred thousand freed from the Fire*.” From Kaʿb: God gave excellence among lands to Mecca, among months to Ramaḍān, and among days to Friday. He ﵊ said: “*Whoever dies on Friday, God writes for him the reward of a martyr, and he is guarded from the trial of the grave*.” In the ḥadīth: “*When it is Friday, the angels sit at the doors of the mosques with sheets of silver and pens of gold in their hands, writing down the first and then the next, by their ranks*.” In the days of the early generations the roads at the time before dawn and after the dawn prayer were choked with those going early to Friday, walking with lamps. {p|534}It is said: the first innovation brought into Islam was leaving off going early to Friday. From Ibn Masʿūd: he went early and saw that three men had come before him. He was grieved and began to reproach himself, saying, “*I see you the fourth of four, and the fourth of four is not fortunate*.”

According to Abū Ḥanīfa ﵁, Friday is held only in a city with a congregational mosque, because of his saying ﵇: “*There is no Friday, no rites of the days of tashrīq, no breaking of the fast and no sacrifice, except in a city with a congregational mosque*.” The city with a congregational mosque is one in which the prescribed punishments are carried out and judgments put into effect. Among its conditions is the imām or whoever stands in his place, because of his saying ﵇: “*So whoever leaves it while he has an imām, just or unjust …*” to the end of the ḥadīth; and his saying ﷺ: “*Four belong to the rulers: the spoils, the alms, the prescribed punishments and the Friday prayers*.” If a man leads without the leave of the imām or of one he has put in charge, a judge or a chief of police, it is not allowed. If asking leave is not possible, and they agree on one man and he prays with them, it is allowed. It is constituted by three besides the imām; according to al-Shāfiʿī, by forty. There is no Friday duty on travelers, slaves, women, the sick and the chronically disabled; nor on the blind, according to Abū Ḥanīfa; nor on the old man who can walk only with a guide.

ʿUmar, Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd and others read *fa-mḍū*, “go.” From ʿUmar ﵁: he heard a man reading *fa-sʿaw*, and said, “*Who taught you to read this?*” He said, “*Ubayy ibn Kaʿb*.” He said, “*He still reads what is abrogated. Were it ‘hasten,’ I would hasten until my cloak fell*.” It is also said: what is meant by *saʿy* is going with intent, not {p|535}running. *Saʿy* is busying oneself in any work; from this is the saying of God Most High “*When he grew enough to work with him*” (Q 37:102), and “*Every human being is responsible for his own works*” (Q 53:39). From al-Ḥasan: *saʿy* is not on the feet, but on intentions and hearts. Muḥammad ibn al-Ḥasan ﵀ mentioned in his *Muwaṭṭaʾ* that ʿUmar heard the call to the start of prayer while he was at al-Baqīʿ, and hastened his walking. Muḥammad said: there is no harm in this, so long as he does not exhaust himself.

{L|To the commemoration of GOD|ilā dhikri llāhi} to the sermon and the prayer. Because God named the sermon a remembrance of Him, Abū Ḥanīfa ﵀ said: if the preacher keeps to a measure that is called a remembrance of God, such as his saying, “Praise be to God, glory be to God,” it is allowed. From ʿUthmān: he went up on the pulpit and said, “*Praise be to God*,” and then his words failed him. He said, “*Abū Bakr and ʿUmar used to prepare a speech for this station. You are in more need of an imām who acts than of an imām who talks; and sermons will come to you*.” Then he came down. That was in the presence of the Companions, and no one objected to him. According to his two companions and al-Shāfiʿī, there must be speech that is called a sermon.

{Q} How is the remembrance of God explained as the sermon, when there is in it remembrance of other than God? {A} What is of remembrance of the Messenger of God ﷺ, praise of him and of his rightly guided successors and the God-fearing believers, admonition and reminding, comes under the ruling of the remembrance of God. As for what is beyond that, of mentioning the wrongdoers and their titles, praising them and praying for them, when they deserve the opposite of that, it is of the remembrance of Satan, and it is stages away from the remembrance of God. When one who is listening silently to the sermon says to his companion, “Hush,” he has spoken idly; so is not the preacher who goes to excess in that speaking idly? We seek refuge with God from the strangeness of Islam and the misery of the days.

He meant the command to leave the worldly business that distracts from the remembrance of God, and singled out selling from among it only because {p|536}Friday is a day on which people come down from their villages and their deserts and pour into the city from every side. The time of their coming down, gathering and filling the markets is when the day has risen high, the forenoon has climbed and noon has drawn near; and then trade is hot, and selling and buying grow many. Since that time was where distraction by selling from the remembrance of God and from going to the mosque was to be expected, it was said to them: hurry to the trade of the Hereafter, leave the trade of this world, and hasten to the remembrance of God, than which nothing is more profitable and more gainful; “*and leave selling*,” whose profit is small and whose gain is middling.

{Q} If selling at this time is commanded to be left and is forbidden, is it then void? {A} Most of the learned hold that this does not make the sale void. They said: because selling was not forbidden for itself, but for what is in it of distraction from what is obligatory; so it is like prayer on usurped land and in a usurped garment, and ablution with usurped water. From some people: it is void.

Then He set free for them, after the prayer is finished, what He had barred to them, of dispersing and seeking gain, with the charge to remember Him much, and that nothing of trade or anything else should distract them from it, and that their concerns in all their states and times should be bound to it, not slipping away from it, because their success lies in it and their winning hangs on it. From Ibn ʿAbbās: they were not commanded to seek anything of this world; it is only visiting the sick, attending funerals and visiting a brother in God. From al-Ḥasan and Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyab: seeking knowledge. It is also said: voluntary prayer. From one of the early generations: he would busy himself after Friday with some matter of this world, looking to this verse.

::: passage 62:11-11 | 4:536-537

It is related that the people of Medina were struck by hunger and great dearness. Diḥya ibn Khalīfa came with a trading caravan of Syrian oil while the Prophet ﷺ was preaching on Friday. They rose to go to it, fearing to be beaten to it, and only a few remained with him: it is said eight, eleven, twelve, or forty. He ﵇ said, “*By Him in whose hand is Muḥammad’s soul, had they all gone out, God would have kindled the valley on them in fire*.” When {p|537}a caravan came they used to meet it with drum and clapping, and that is what is meant by amusement. From Qatāda: they did that three times, at every arrival of a caravan.

{Q} If it happens that the people disperse from the imām in the Friday prayer, what does he do? {A} If he remains alone, or with fewer than three, then according to Abū Ḥanīfa he starts the noon prayer afresh if they scattered from him before the bowing. According to his two companions, if he said *Allāhu akbar* while they were with him, he goes on with it. According to Zufar, if they scattered before the testimony it is void.

{Q} How did He say “*to it*” when He had mentioned two things? {A} Its implied form is: when they saw trade they broke away to it, or amusement, they broke away to it; and He dropped one of them because the one mentioned points to it. Likewise the reading of whoever read *infaḍḍū ilayhi*, and the reading of whoever read *lahwan aw tijāratan infaḍḍū ilayhā*. *Ilayhimā* is also read.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-Jumuʿa is given as reward ten good deeds by the number of those who come to Friday and by the number of those who do not come to it, in the cities of the Muslims*.”
