{L|By the forenoon|wa-l-ḍuḥā} what is meant by *al-ḍuḥā* is the time of *ḍuḥā*, which is the early part of the day, until the sun is up and casts its rays. It is said that the time of *ḍuḥā* was singled out for the oath only because it is the hour in which Moses ﵇ was spoken to and in which the sorcerers were thrown down in prostration, because of His saying “*Let us all meet in the forenoon*” (Q 20:59). It is also said that by *al-ḍuḥā* the day is meant; this is made clear by His saying “*our retribution will not come to them in the daytime*” (*ḍuḥan*, Q 7:98), set against “*in the night*” (Q 7:97).

{L|As it falls|idhā sajā} when it is quiet and its darkness settles. It is said that *layla sājiya* is a night still of wind. It is also said that its meaning is the stillness of people and voices in it. *Sajā l-baḥru*: its waves grew still; and *ṭarfun sājin* is a still, languid glance.

{L|Never abandoned you|mā waddaʿaka} the answer to the oath. Its meaning: He has not cut you off as one who takes leave cuts off. It is also read without doubling (*wadaʿaka*), meaning: He has not left you. He said: {p|766}“*And there the clan of ʿAmr and ʿĀmir left us prey to the tips of the brown, straightened spears*.” *Al-tawdīʿ* is an intensive of *al-wadʿ*, because one who takes leave of you on parting has gone to the utmost in leaving you. It is related that the revelation was held back from the Messenger of God ﷺ for some days, and the idolaters said, “*Muḥammad’s Lord has taken leave of him and hates him*.” It is also said that Umm Jamīl, the wife of Abū Lahab, said to him, “*Muḥammad, I see nothing but that your devil has left you*,” and it was revealed. The pronoun is dropped from *qalā* (“hates”) as it is dropped from *al-dhākirāt* in His saying “*the men who commemorate GOD frequently, and the commemorating women*” (*wa-l-dhākirīna llāha kathīran wa-l-dhākirāt*, Q 33:35), meaning “and the women who remember Him”; and like it are “*gave you a home*” (*fa-āwā*, “sheltered”), “*guided*” and “*made you rich*” (*fa-aghnā*, “enriched”). It is a shortening of the wording, since what is left out is plain.

::: passage 93:4-5 | 4:766-767

{Q} How does His saying “*The Hereafter is far better for you than this first (life)*” connect with what comes before? {A} Since the denial of leave-taking and hatred implied that God keeps joined to you through His revelation to you, and that you are God’s beloved, and you see no honor greater than that nor any favor more splendid, He told him that his state in the Hereafter will be greater and more splendid than that: going before all God’s prophets and messengers, his community’s witnessing over all other communities, the raising of the believers in rank and the lifting of their degrees through his intercession, and other lofty honors.

{L|And your Lord will give you enough; you will be pleased|wa-la-sawfa yuʿṭīka rabbuka fa-tarḍā} a promise that covers what He gave him in this world: triumph and victory over his enemies on the day of Badr and the day of the conquest of {p|767}Mecca, people entering the religion in throngs, the defeat of Qurayẓa and al-Naḍīr and their expulsion, the spreading of his armies and raiding parties through the lands of the Arabs, the cities opened to his rightly guided successors in every region of the earth, the kingdoms of tyrants brought down by their hands and the treasures of the Chosroes they gave to plunder, the terror and dread of Islam cast into the hearts of the people of east and west, the spread of the call and the mastery of the Muslims; and what He has laid up for him of a reward whose full measure only God knows. Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄ said: “*He has in Paradise a thousand palaces of white pearl, whose soil is musk*.”

{Q} What is this *lām* that enters on *sawfa*? {A} It is the *lām* of the subject, which confirms the content of the sentence, and the subject is left out. The implied sense is *wa-la-anta sawfa yuʿṭīka* (“and you, He will give you”), as we said on *lā uqsimu* that the meaning is *la-ana uqsimu* (Q 75:1). That is because it must be either the *lām* of oath or the *lām* of the subject. The *lām* of oath does not enter on the imperfect except together with the *nūn* of emphasis, so it remains that it is the *lām* of the subject; and the *lām* of the subject enters only on a sentence made of subject and predicate. So a subject and predicate must be supplied, its original form being *wa-la-anta sawfa yuʿṭīka*.

{Q} What is the meaning of joining the particle of emphasis and the particle of delay? {A} Its meaning is that the giving will come without fail, even if it is delayed, because of the benefit that lies in the delay.

::: passage 93:6-8 | 4:767-768

He counted over to him His favors and gifts, and that He had never left him without them from the start of his upbringing and the beginning of his growth, in preparation for what He intended for him; so that he might measure what he awaits of God’s bounty by what had gone before, and so expect only the best and more good and honor, and his breast would not be straitened nor his patience grow less.

{L|Did He not find you|a-lam yajidka} from *wujūd* in the sense of knowing, and the two accusatives are the two objects of *wajada*. The meaning: were you not an orphan? That is because his father died while he was in the womb, six months along, and his mother died when he was eight years old. His uncle Abū Ṭālib took charge of him, and God made him kind to him, and he raised him well. Among the odd interpretations is that it is from their saying *durra yatīma* (“an unmatched pearl”), and that the meaning is: did He not find you one alone in Quraysh, without {p|768}peer, and shelter you? *Fa-awā* is also read, and it has two senses: either from *awāhu* in the sense of *āwāhu* (a herdsman was heard saying, “*Where shall I shelter these mangy camels?*”), or from *awā lahu*, when he pitied him.

{L|Astray|ḍāllan} its meaning is straying from knowledge of the laws and of what can be learned only by hearing, as in His saying “*You had no idea about the scripture*” (Q 42:52). It is also said: he strayed as a child in one of the ravines of Mecca, and Abū Jahl brought him back to ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib. It is also said: Ḥalīma lost him at the gate of Mecca when she had weaned him and brought him to return him to ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib. It is also said: he strayed on the road to Syria when Abū Ṭālib took him out with him. “*And guided you*” you: made you know the Qurʾān and the laws; or ended your straying and brought you back to your grandfather and your uncle. As for the one who said that he followed the way of his people for forty years: if he means that he was like them in lacking the knowledge that comes by hearing, then yes; but if he means that he was of their religion and unbelief, God forbid. The prophets must be protected, before prophethood and after it, from major sins and from shameful minor ones; how much more, then, from unbelief and ignorance of the Maker: “*We never set up any idols beside GOD*” (Q 12:38). It is flaw enough in a prophet, in the eyes of the unbelievers, that unbelief should have gone before for him.

{L|Poor|ʿāʾilan} poor. *ʿayyilan* is also read, as *sayyiḥātin* is also read, and *ʿadīman* (“destitute”). “*And made you rich*” you: with the wealth of Khadīja, or with the spoils He gave you. He ﵇ said: “*My provision has been put under the shade of my spear*.” It is also said: He made you content and enriched your heart.

::: passage 93:9-11 | 4:768-769

{L|You shall not forsake|fa-lā taqhar} do not overpower him in his property and his right because he is weak. In the reading of Ibn Masʿūd: *fa-lā takhar*, which is frowning in his face; one says “So-and-so has *kahrūra*,” a frowning face. From this is the ḥadīth: “*My father and mother be his ransom, he did not frown at me*.”

{L|Nor shall you reprimand|fa-lā tanhar} *al-nahr* and *al-nahm* are rebuffing. From the Prophet ﷺ: “*When you have turned the beggar away three times and he does not go back, {p|769}then there is no harm in your driving him off*.” It is also said: it is not the beggar asking for gifts, but the seeker of knowledge: when he comes, do not rebuff him.

{L|You shall proclaim|fa-ḥaddith} speaking of God’s favor is thanking Him for it and making it known. He means the favor He mentioned, of sheltering, guiding and enriching, and what is beyond that. From Mujāhid: [the favor is] the Qurʾān; “*proclaim*”: recite it, and deliver what you were sent with. From ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ghālib: in the morning he would say, “*God gave me good last night: I recited such-and-such and prayed such-and-such*.” When he was told, “*Abū Firās, does one like you say the like of this?*” he said, “*God Most High says ‘and as for the favor of your Lord, proclaim,’ and you say, ‘Do not speak of God’s favor.’*” The like of this is allowed only if one intends by it a grace to others, that others may follow him, and he is safe from temptation for himself; and keeping it hidden is better. Were there nothing in it but resembling the people of show and reputation, that would be enough. In the reading of ʿAlī ﵁: *fa-khabbir*.

The meaning: you were an orphan, astray and in need, and God sheltered you, guided you and enriched you. So whatever may be, and whatever you imagine, do not forget God’s favor to you in these three. Follow God’s example: be kind to the orphan and shelter him, for you have tasted orphanhood and its lowliness and seen how God did with you; be merciful to the beggar, look after him with your kindness and do not drive him from your door, as your Lord had mercy on you and enriched you after poverty; and speak of all of God’s favor, under which comes His guiding [you] out of straying and His teaching you the laws and the Qurʾān, following God’s example in that He guided him out of straying.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat wa-l-ḍuḥā, God will put him among those for whom Muḥammad is pleased to intercede, and God will write for him ten good deeds for every orphan and beggar*.”
