The splitting of the moon is one of the signs of the Messenger of God ﷺ and one of his shining miracles. From Anas ibn Mālik ﵁: the unbelievers asked the Messenger of God ﷺ for a sign, and the moon split twice. {p|431}The like is from Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Masʿūd ﵄. Ibn ʿAbbās said: “*It split into two halves; one half went and one half stayed*.” Ibn Masʿūd said: “*I saw Ḥirāʾ between the two halves of the moon*.” From some people: its meaning is that it will split on the Day of Resurrection; but His saying “*and if they see a sign they turn away and say, ‘Sorcery, continuing’*” refutes it, and it is refutation enough. In the reading of Ḥudhayfa: *wa-qad inshaqqa l-qamaru*: that is, the Hour has drawn near, and of the signs of its drawing near it has already come about that the moon has split; as you say, “The emir has drawn near, and the one bringing news of his coming has already arrived.” From Ḥudhayfa: he preached at al-Madāʾin and then said, “*Indeed the Hour has drawn near, and the moon split in the time of your prophet*.”

{L|Old magic|mustamirrun} lasting and running on. Of everything whose course has been set and whose state lasts, it is said: “It has continued.” When they saw the miracles following one another and the signs coming one after another, they said, “This is sorcery, continuing.” It is also said: *mustamirr* is strong and firm, from their saying *istamarra marīruhu* (“his twisted rope held fast”). It is also said: it is from *istamarra l-shayʾu*, when its bitterness grew strong: that is, loathsome to us, bitter on our palates, we cannot swallow it, as the bitterest bitter thing is not swallowed. It is also said: *mustamirr* is passing, going, it will vanish and not stay; feeding their own hopes and comforting themselves. *Wa-in yuraw* is also read.

{L|Followed their opinions|wa-ttabaʿū ahwāʾahum} and what Satan made fair to them of pushing back the truth after it had appeared. “*And adhered to their old traditions*” (*wa-kullu amrin mustaqirrun*, “and every matter is settled”): that is, every matter must come to an end where it settles; and the matter of Muḥammad will come to an end at which it will be made plain whether it is true or false, and its outcome will appear to them. Or: and every matter of their matter and his is settled, that is, it will hold firm and settle in a state of being forsaken or helped in this world, and of wretchedness or happiness in the Hereafter. It is also read with *fatḥa* on the *qāf*; He means every matter has a settling, that is, has a settling, or a place of settling, or a time of settling. From Abū Jaʿfar: *mustaqirrin*, with *kasra* on the *qāf* and in the genitive, coordinated on “*the Hour*”: that is, the Hour has drawn near, and every settled matter has drawn near, that settles and whose state is made plain.

::: passage 54:4-7 | 4:431-433

{p|432}{L|Sufficient warnings|mina l-anbāʾi} of the Qurʾān, in which are set the reports of the bygone generations or the reports of the Hereafter, and what is described of the punishment of the unbelievers. “*To alert them*” (*muzdajar*): a restraining, or a place of restraint. The meaning: it is in itself a place of restraint and where it is to be looked for, as in the saying of God Most High “*The messenger of GOD has set up a good example*” (Q 33:21), that is, he is an example. *Muzzajar* is also read, turning the *tāʾ* of the *iftiʿāl* pattern into *zāy* and assimilating the *zāy* into it. “*Great wisdom*” is a substitute for *mā*; or it is as in “it is wisdom.” It is also read in the accusative, as a circumstantial clause to *mā*.

{Q} If *mā* is a relative, you may put *ḥikma* in the accusative as a circumstantial clause; so what do you do if it is indefinite and described, which is the plain sense? {A} The description makes it specific, so a circumstantial clause from it is fine.

{L|But all the warnings have been in vain|fa-mā tughni l-nudhuru} a negation, or a disowning; and *mā* is then accusative, that is, what avail do warnings avail? “*So turn away from them*,” since you know that warning does not avail with them. “*The day will come when the caller will announce*” is put in the accusative through “*they come out*,” or through an implied “remember.” It is also read without the *yāʾ*, the *kasra* being enough in its place. The caller is Isrāfīl or Gabriel, as in the saying of God Most High “*the day when the caller calls*” (Q 50:41). “*A terrible disaster*” (*ilā shayʾin nukur*): a thing denied and horrible, which souls deny because they have known nothing like it; it is the terror of the Day of Resurrection. *Nukr*, with the light form, is also read, and *nukira*, in the sense of “it was denied.”

{L|With their eyes humiliated|khushshaʿan abṣāruhum} a circumstantial clause to those who come out; it is a verb-like form belonging to the eyes, and it is masculine, as you say *yakhshaʿu abṣāruhum*. *Khāshiʿatan* is also read, as in *takhshaʿu abṣāruhum*; and *khushshaʿan*, as in *yakhshaʿna abṣāruhum*, which is the dialect of those who say *akalūnī l-barāghīthu* (“the fleas ate me”), and they are Ṭayyiʾ. It may also be that their pronoun is in *khushshaʿan*, and “*their eyes*” comes as a substitute for it. *Khushshaʿun abṣāruhum* is also read, as subject and predicate, and the place of the sentence is the accusative as a circumstantial clause, as in his saying: “*I found him with generosity and nobility present in him*.” The humbling of the eyes is a figure for abasement and dejection, because the abasement of the abased and the might of the mighty show in their eyes. *Yakhrujūna mina l-ajdāthi* is also read: from the graves. “*Like scattered locusts*”: locusts are a byword for multitude and surging. Of a great army whose parts surge into one another it is said, “They came like locusts,” and “like the young locusts”: spread in every place because of their number. “*As they respond to the caller*” (*muhṭiʿīna ilā l-dāʿ*): hurrying, stretching their necks toward it. It is also said: looking toward it, not taking their eyes off it. He said: {p|433}“*Namir ibn Saʿd has made me a slave, though I have seen Namir ibn Saʿd obedient to me and hastening*.”

::: passage 54:9-17 | 4:433-436

{L|Before them|qablahum} before the people of Mecca. “*They disbelieved our servant*”: He means Noah.

{Q} What is the meaning of His saying “*and they called a liar*” after His saying “*called liars*”? {A} Its meaning is: they called [the messengers] liars, and so they called Our servant a liar; that is, they called him a liar, one giving of the lie on the heels of another; whenever one generation of them that gave the lie passed away, another generation that gave the lie followed it. Or: the people of Noah called the messengers liars, and so they called Our servant a liar; that is, since they gave the lie to the messengers and denied prophethood outright, they gave the lie to Noah, because he was one of the messengers.

{L|Crazy!|majnūnun} he is a madman. “*He was persecuted*” (*wa-zdujir*): they rebuked him with reviling, beating and the threat of stoning, in their saying “*you will be stoned*” (Q 26:116). It is also said that it is part of what they said: that is, they said he is a madman, and the jinn have driven him and struck him and taken his mind and made his heart fly. *Annī* is read, in the sense “he called on Him with ‘I am overcome’”; and *innī*, with the intent of {p|434}saying: he called and said, “I am overcome; my people have overcome me, they will not hear from me, and despair of their answering me has taken firm hold.” “*grant me victory*” (*fa-ntaṣir*): so take vengeance on them with a punishment You send upon them. He called with that only after the matter had overwhelmed him and the flood had reached the heights; for it is related that one of his community would meet him and choke him until he fell down senseless, and he would come to, saying, “*O God, forgive my people, for they do not know*.”

{L|We then opened|fa-fataḥnā} read light and doubled; likewise *wa-fajjarnā*. “*Pouring*”: pouring down in abundance and in succession, not cut off for forty days. “*We caused springs to gush out of the earth*”: and We made the whole earth as though it were springs gushing. That goes further than your saying, “We made the springs of the earth gush,” and its counterpart in the composition is “*my hair is aflame with gray*” (Q 19:4). “*The waters met*”: He means the waters of the sky and the earth. *Al-māʾāni* (“the two waters”) is also read, that is, the two kinds of water, the heavenly and the earthly; like your saying, “I have two dates,” meaning two kinds of date, *barnī* and *maʿqilī*. He said: “*We have two herds of camels, in which is what you know*.” Al-Ḥasan read *al-māwāni*, turning the *hamza* into *wāw*, as they say *ʿilbāwāni*. “*To effect a predetermined decision*”: in a state God decreed as He willed. It is also said: in a state that came measured and even, namely that the amount sent down from the sky was the same as the amount brought out from the earth, measure for measure. It is also said: for a matter already decreed in the Tablet that it would be, which is the destruction of the people of Noah by the Flood.

{L|On a watercraft made of logs and ropes|ʿalā dhāti alwāḥin wa-dusurin} He meant the ship. It is one of the descriptions that stand in place of the things described, take their place and do their work, so that no line is drawn between them. Like it is: “*… but my shirt is a close-woven thing of iron*.” {p|435}He meant: but my shirt is a coat of mail. Likewise: “*Even in the eyes of the leapers with shanks*”: he meant, even in the eyes of the locusts. Do you not see that were you to put together the ship and this description, or the coat of mail and the locusts with these two descriptions, it would not be sound? This is part of the eloquence and beauty of speech. *Al-dusur* is the plural of *disār*, which is the nail; it is *fiʿāl* from *dasarahu* when he pushed it, because its way in is pushed with it.

{L|A reward|jazāʾan} an object of purpose for what came before, the opening of the gates of the sky and what follows it: that is, We did that as repayment “*for one who was rejected*,” and he is Noah ﵇. He made him one denied (*makfūr*) because a prophet is a favor and a mercy from God; God Most High said: “*We have sent you out of mercy from us towards the whole world*” (Q 21:107). So Noah ﵇ was a favor denied. Of this sense is what is told: a man said to al-Rashīd, “*Praise be to God for you*.” He said, “*What does this saying mean?*” He said, “*You are a favor for which I have praised God*.” It may also be on the supposing of a dropped preposition with the verb joined directly. Qatāda read *kafara*, that is, a repayment for the unbelievers. Al-Ḥasan read *jizāʾan*, with *kasra*, that is, a requiting.

The pronoun in “*We left it*” is for the ship, or for the deed: that is, We made it a sign to take warning from. From Qatāda: God kept it in the land of al-Jazīra; it is also said, on al-Jūdī, for a long age, so that the first of this community looked on it. *Al-muddakir* is the one who takes warning. *Mudhtakir* is also read, in the original form; and *mudhdhakir*, turning the *tāʾ* into *dhāl* and assimilating the *dhāl* into it; and this is like *mudhjar*. *Al-nudhur* is the plural of *nadhīr*, which is warning.

{L|We made the Quran easy to learn|wa-la-qad yassarnā l-qurʾāna li-l-dhikri} that is, We have made it easy to remember and take admonition, by filling it with healing admonitions and turning in it of promise and threat. “*So is there anyone*” who will take admonition? It is also said: We have made it easy to learn by heart and have helped whoever wants to learn it by heart; so is there anyone seeking to learn it by heart, so that he may be helped in it? The meaning may also be: We have made it ready for remembrance, from *yassara nāqatahu li-l-safar* when he saddled her, and *yassara farasahu li-l-ghazw* when he saddled and bridled him. He said: “*And I rose to him with the bridle, making him ready; there what I used to do repays me*.” {p|436}It is related that the books of the people of the religions, such as the Torah and the Gospel, are recited by their people only from sight, and they do not know them by heart as the Qurʾān is known.

{L|After the warnings|wa-nudhuri} and My warning them of the punishment before it came down; or a warning that came in their punishment for those after them. “*On a day of continuous misery*” (*fī yawmi naḥsin*): on a day of evil omen. *Fī yawmin naḥisin* is also read, as in His saying “*for a few miserable days*” (Q 41:16). “*Continuous*” (*mustamirr*): it continued on them and lasted until it destroyed them; or it continued on all of them, great and small, until not a soul of them was left. It was on a Wednesday at the end of the month that does not come round [to the next]. By *mustamirr* He may also mean what is very bitter and loathsome.

{L|It tossed the people around|tanziʿu l-nāsa} uprooting them from their places. They used to stand in rows holding one another’s hands, go into the ravines, and dig pits and creep into them; and it would snatch them away, throw them down on their faces and break their necks. “*As if they were decayed palm tree trunks*” (*aʿjāzu nakhlin munqaʿir*): He means that they were falling to the ground dead, being long, huge bodies, as though they were trunks of palms, that is, their bases without branches; *munqaʿir* is uprooted from its planting places. It is also said: they were likened to palm trunks because the wind was cutting off their heads, so that they remained bodies without heads. He made the description of “*palms*” masculine, following the wording; had He taken it by the sense He would have made it feminine, as He said: “*decayed palm trunks*” (Q 69:7).

::: passage 54:23-32 | 4:437-438

{L|Shall we follow one of us; a human being?|a-basharan minnā wāḥidan} put in the accusative by an implied verb that “*Shall we follow*” explains. *A-basharun minnā wāḥidun* is also read, as a subject, with “*Shall we follow*” as its predicate; the first is more fitting because of the question. He used to say, “If you do not follow me you are in straying from the truth, and in *suʿur*,” that is, in fires, plural of *saʿīr*. So they turned it back on him and said, “If we follow you, we will then be as you say.” It is also said: straying is error and distance from what is right, and *suʿur* is madness; one says *nāqa masʿūra* (“a crazed she-camel”). He said: “*As though she had madness, when the trotting and the tiring loose pace shake the white camels*.”

{Q} How did they disown following a single human being from among them? {A} They said “*a human being*” in disowning that they should follow one like them in kind, and they demanded that he be of a kind higher than the kind of humans, namely the angels. They said “*one of us*” because if he were from them the likeness would be stronger. They said “*single*” in disowning that the community should follow one man; or they meant one of the common people, not the noblest and best of them. Their saying points to it: “*Did the message come down to him, instead of us?*”: that is, has the revelation been sent down to him from among us, when among us is one more deserving than he of being chosen for prophethood?

{L|Flagrant liar|ashirun} arrogant and proud; his insolence, his roguery and his seeking to be great over us have carried him to claim that. “*They will find out tomorrow*”: when the punishment comes down on them, or on the Day of Resurrection, “*who the flagrant liar is*”: Ṣāliḥ, or the one who gave him the lie. *Sa-taʿlamūna*, with *tāʾ*, is also read, as the report of what Ṣāliḥ said to them in answering them; or it is the speech of {p|438}God Most High by way of turning. *Al-ashur* is also read, with *ḍamma* on the *shīn*, as they say *ḥadith* and *ḥadhur*, *ḥadhir* and *ḥadhur*, and the like. *Al-asharru* is also read, which goes furthest in wickedness. *Al-akhyar* and *al-asharr* are the origin of their saying “he is better (*khayr*) than him and worse (*sharr*) than him”; it is an origin that has been dropped, though Ibn al-Anbārī has told of the Arabs saying “he is *akhyar* and *asharr*,” and “how good he is, how bad he is.”

{L|We are sending the camel|mursilū l-nāqati} raising her and bringing her out of the rock, as they asked. “*As a test for them*”: a test of them and a proving. “*Watch them*”: so wait for them and see what they will do; “*and be patient*” with their hurt, and do not hasten until My command comes to you. “*Divided among them*”: shared between them, she having a day’s drink and they a day’s drink. He said “*between them*” only to give precedence to the rational. “*Allowed to drink on her designated day*” (*muḥtaḍar*, “attended”): attended by them, or by the she-camel. It is also said: they attend the water on their turn, and the milk on her turn. “*Their friend*”: Qudār ibn Sālif, the ruddy one of Thamūd. “*And he obliged*” (*fa-taʿāṭā*): he dared to take on the great deed, caring nothing for it, and did the hamstringing of the she-camel. It is also said: he took hold of the she-camel and hamstrung her; or he took hold of the sword.

{L|One blow|ṣayḥatan wāḥidatan} the cry of Gabriel. *Al-hashīm* is dry trees, crumbled and broken. *Al-muḥtaẓir* is the one who makes the pen; and what is used for the pen dries out over a long time, and the beasts trample it, and it breaks and crumbles. Al-Ḥasan read it with *fatḥa* on the *ẓāʾ*, and it is the place of penning, that is, the pen.

{L|We showered them with rocks|ḥāṣiban} a wind that pelts them with stones, that is, throws them at them. “*At dawn*” (*bi-saḥarin*): in a part of the night, which is its last sixth. It is also said that there are two dawns: the higher dawn before the break of day, and the other at its breaking. And he recited: “*She passed at the higher of the two dawns, trotting*.” {p|439}It is declined because it is indefinite. One says *laqītuhu saḥara*, when you met him in the dawn of his day.

{L|We blessed him|niʿmatan} as a favoring; an object of purpose. “*Whoever gives thanks*” for God’s grace by his faith and his obedience. “*He warned them*”: Lot ﵇; “*about our requital*”: of Our seizing with the punishment. “*But they ridiculed*” (*fa-tamāraw*): they gave the lie “*the warnings*,” doubting. “*We blinded them*” (*fa-ṭamasnā aʿyunahum*): We wiped them and made them like the rest of the face, with no slit to be seen in them. It is related that when they worked at the door of Lot ﵇ to get in, the angels said, “Let them come in; ‘*we are the messengers of your Lord; they will not reach you*’” (Q 11:81). Gabriel ﵇ struck them a stroke with his wing and left them groping about, not finding the way to the door, until Lot brought them out. “*Suffer*” (*fa-dhūqū*, “so taste”): so I said to them, “Taste,” on the tongues of the angels.

{L|Early|bukratan} the first of the day and its beginning, as in His saying “*at sunrise*” and “*Early the next morning*.” Zayd ibn ʿAlī ﵄ read *bukrata*, not declined. You say *ataytuhu bukratan* and *ghudwatan*, with nunation, when you mean it indefinite, and without it when you make it definite and mean the early morning of your day and its forenoon. “*A devastating retribution*” (*ʿadhābun mustaqirr*): firm, settled on them until it brings them to the punishment of the Hereafter.

{Q} What is the use of repeating His saying “*Suffer My retribution; you have been warned*,” and “*We made the Quran easy to learn. Does any of you wish to learn?*” {A} Its use is that at hearing each report of the reports of the ancients they should renew remembrance and admonition, and take up afresh alertness and waking when they hear the urging to that and the prompting to it; and that the stick should be knocked for them time and again and the old water-skin rattled for them over and over, so that forgetfulness should not overcome them nor heedlessness take hold of them. Such is the rule of repetition, as in His saying “*So which of the favors of your Lord do you two deny?*” at each favor He counted in Sūrat al-Raḥmān, and His saying “*Woe on that day to those who give the lie*” at each sign He brought in Sūrat al-Mursalāt. Likewise the repetition of the reports and stories themselves, so that those lessons may be present to the hearts, pictured to the minds, remembered and not forgotten at every time.

{L|Were warned|al-nudhuru} Moses and Aaron and other prophets, because the two set before them what the messengers had warned of; or it is the plural of *nadhīr*, which is warning. “*All Our signs*”: the nine signs. “*The seizing of one mighty*,” who is not overcome, “*all-powerful*,” whom nothing frustrates.

{p|440}{L|Are your disbelievers|a-kuffārukum} people of Mecca, “*better than those*,” the unbelievers counted up: the people of Noah, of Hūd, of Ṣāliḥ, of Lot, and the folk of Pharaoh? That is, are they better in strength, means and standing in this world, or less in unbelief and obstinacy? He means that your unbelievers are like those, or rather worse than them. “*Or*” has an acquittal been sent down to you, people of Mecca, in the earlier books, that whoever of you disbelieves and gives the lie to the messengers is safe from God’s punishment, so that you feel safe through that acquittal?

{L|We will be the winners|naḥnu jamīʿun} a company whose affair is united; “*the winners*” (*muntaṣir*): holding out, not to be attacked or wronged. From Abū Jahl: on the day of Badr he struck his horse and went forward in the line and said, “*We will take vengeance today on Muḥammad and his companions*.” Then “*The host will be routed*” was revealed. From ʿIkrima: when this verse was revealed, ʿUmar said, “*What host will be routed?*” Then when he saw the Messenger of God ﷺ leaping in his coat of mail and saying, “*The host will be routed*,” he knew its interpretation.

{L|They will turn around and flee|wa-yuwallūna l-duburu} that is, their backs, as he said: “*Eat in some of your belly and be content*.” *Al-adbāra* is also read. “*Far worse*” (*adhā*): harder and more horrible. *Al-dāhiya* is the hateful thing for which no remedy can be found. “*And more painful*” than rout, killing and captivity. *Sa-nahzimu l-jamʿa* (“We will rout the host”) is also read.

::: passage 54:47-50 | 4:440-441

{L|Are astray, and will end up in Hell|fī ḍalālin wa-suʿurin} in ruin and fires; or in straying from the truth in this world, and fires in the Hereafter. “*The agony of retribution*” (*massa saqar*, “the touch of Saqar”): like your saying, “He felt the touch of fever and tasted the taste of the beating,” because when the Fire strikes them with its heat and scorches them with its pain, it is as though it touches them with that, as a living thing is touched and met with what hurts and pains. “*Taste*” is on the intent of saying. *Saqar* is a proper name for Hell, from *saqarathu l-nāru* and *ṣaqarathu*, when it scorched him. Dhū l-Rumma said: {p|441}“*When the sun melts, he guards against its scorching blasts with the branches of a sand-tract grown thick in spring, thick-leaved*.” It is not declined because it is definite and feminine.

{L|Everything|kulla shayʾin} put in the accusative through an implied verb that the plain one explains. *Kullu shayʾin* is also read, in the nominative. *Al-qadar* and *al-qadr* are measuring, and both are read: that is, We created everything measured, firm and ordered according to what wisdom required; or measured and written in the Tablet, known before it came to be, its state and its time already known to Us.

{L|Our commands are done|wa-mā amrunā illā wāḥidatun} but a single word, swift in bringing into being, “*within the blink of an eye*.” He meant His saying “Be.” He means that when He wills to bring a thing into being, its being is not delayed.

{L|And rivers|wa-naharin} and rivers; He was content with the name of the kind. It is also said: it is breadth and light, from *al-nahār* (“daytime”). It is also read with *sukūn* on the *hāʾ*; and *nuhur*, the plural of *nahar*, like *asad* and *usud*. “*In a position of honor*” (*fī maqʿadi ṣidqin*): in a place approved. *Fī maqāʿidi ṣidqin* is also read. “*At an Omnipotent King*”: brought near to a King whose affair in kingship and power is left unstated, so that there is nothing that is not under His kingship and His power. So what rank is more honored than that rank, and gathers more of all that is enviable and of happiness entire?
