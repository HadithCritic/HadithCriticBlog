title: Sūrat al-Ṭāriq (86)
revelation: Meccan. Seventeen verses. [Revealed after al-Balad (90).]

::: passage 86:1-3 | 4:734-734

{L|The bright star|al-najmu l-thāqib} the bright one, as if it pierces the darkness with its light and passes through it, just as a star is called *durrīʾ* because it *yadraʾuhu*, that is, drives it back. It is called *al-ṭāriq* because it appears by night, as one who comes by night is called *ṭāriq*; or because it strikes (*yaṭruqu*) the jinn, that is, hits them. What is meant is stars as a kind, or the kind of meteors that are thrown at them.

{Q} His saying “*Do you know what Al-Taareq is? The bright star*” (*wa-mā adrāka mā l-ṭāriq al-najmu l-thāqib*, “and what will make you know what the night-comer is? The piercing star”) looks like nothing more than rendering one word by another. Show me what benefit lies under it. {A} God, mighty is He as speaker, wished to swear by the piercing star to honor it, for the wonders of power and the fine wisdom known in it, and to draw attention to this. So He brought a description that it shares with others, “*Al-Taareq*” (*al-ṭāriq*, “the night-comer”), then said “*Do you know what Al-Taareq is?*”, then explained it with His saying “*The bright star*.” All this is to show how great its standing is, as He said: “*I swear by the positions of the stars. This is an oath, if you only knew, that is awesome*” (Q 56:75–76).

It is related that Abū Ṭālib was with the Messenger of God ﷺ when a star shot down and the place was filled with light. Abū Ṭālib took fright and said, “*What is this?*” He ﵇ said, “*This is a star that has been thrown, and it is one of God’s signs*.” Abū Ṭālib was amazed, and it was revealed.

::: passage 86:4-4 | 4:734-734

{Q} What is the answer to the oath? {A} “*Absolutely, everyone is well guarded*,” because for those who read *lammā* with doubling, in the sense of *illā* (“except”), *in* has to be negative; and for those who read it without doubling, with *mā* extra, it is *in* lightened from *inna*. Whichever it is, it is one of the forms with which an oath is answered.

{L|Well guarded|ḥāfiẓ} one who oversees it and watches over it, who is God the Mighty and Majestic: “*GOD is watchful over all things*” (Q 33:52), “*GOD controls all things*” (Q 4:85). It is also said: an angel who keeps its deeds and counts up the good and evil it earns. It is related from the Prophet ﷺ: “*A hundred and sixty angels are put in charge of the believer, driving harm away from him as flies are driven away from a bowl of honey. Were the servant left to himself for the blink of an eye, the devils would snatch him away*.”

::: passage 86:5-7 | 4:735-735

{Q} How does His saying “*Let the human reflect*” connect with what comes before? {A} It connects with it in this way: when He had said that every soul has a guardian over it, He followed it by urging man to look at the start of his existence and his first making, so that he may know that the One who made him is able to bring him back and repay him, and so work for the Day of return and repayment and dictate to his guardian only what will please him in the end. “*On his creation*” (*mimma khuliqa*, “from what he was created”) is a question, and its answer is “*He was created from ejected liquid*.”

{L|Ejected|dāfiq} *al-dafq* is pouring with force. The sense of *dāfiq* is relation to *al-dafq*, the verbal noun of *dafaqa*, like *lābin* and *tāmir* (“one with milk,” “one with dates”); or it is a figurative attribution, the gushing really belonging to its owner. He did not say “two waters,” because they mix in the womb and become one when his creation begins.

{L|From between the spine and the viscera|min bayni l-ṣulbi wa-l-tarāʾib} from between the man’s backbone and the woman’s breastbones, which are the bones of the chest where the necklace lies. *Al-ṣalab* is also read, with two *fatḥa*s, and *al-ṣulub*, with two *ḍamma*s. There are four forms of it: *ṣulb*, *ṣalab*, *ṣulub* and *ṣālib*. Al-ʿAjjāj said: “*… with a waist (ṣulub) like a well-plaited rein*.” It is also said that the bone and sinew come from the man, and the flesh and blood from the woman.

::: passage 86:8-10 | 4:735-736

{L|He|innahu} the pronoun is for the Creator, since “*he was created*” points to Him. Its meaning: the One who created man in the first place from a drop is “*able to resurrect him*” (*ʿalā rajʿihi*, “able to bring him back”) in particular, plainly able, never faltering or failing at it; like the poet’s saying “*I am poor indeed*.”

{L|The Day|yawma} accusative through *rajʿihi*. Those who make the pronoun in *rajʿihi* refer to the water {p|736}and explain it as its return to the place it came from, the backbone and the breastbones, or the urethra, or to its first state, put the adverb in the accusative through an implied verb.

{L|All secrets|al-sarāʾir} what is kept secret in the hearts, of beliefs, intentions and the like, and the deeds that were hidden. Their being tried is their being made known and examined, and the good among them told apart from the bad. From al-Ḥasan: he heard a man recite, “*In the hidden depths of my heart and breast there will remain for her a secret love, on the day when secrets are tried*,” and he said, “*How heedless he is of what is in ‘By the sky and the night-comer’!*”

{L|He will have|fa-mā lahu} so man will have “*no power*,” no strength in himself with which to defend himself, “*nor a helper*,” no one to defend him.

::: passage 86:11-14 | 4:736-737

{L|That returns (the water)|dhāti l-rajʿ} rain is called *rajʿ* (“return”), just as it is called *awb*. He said: “*A watcher on a lofty peak, to whose summit nothing comes but the clouds, the awb and the rain*.” These are names from the verbal nouns of *rajaʿa* and *āba* (“to come back”), because the Arabs claimed that the clouds carry water from the seas of the earth and then send it back to the earth. Or they meant it as a good omen and called it *rajʿ* and *awb* so that it would come back again and again. It is also said: because God brings it back time after time. Al-Khansāʾ said: “*like the rajʿ in the dark cloud traveling by night*.” *Al-ṣadʿ* is the plants that the earth splits open with.

{L|This is|innahu} the pronoun is for the Qurʾān. “*A serious narration*” (*la-qawlun faṣl*, “decisive”) (*faṣl*): it divides {p|737}truth from falsehood, as it was also called *al-furqān*. “*Not to be taken lightly*”: He means that it is all earnest, with no slackness in it. Since God has described it so, it is its due to be held in awe in the breasts and honored in the hearts, and for whoever recites it or hears it to rise above stooping to jest or amusing himself with joking, and to bear in mind that the Almighty of the heavens is addressing him, commanding and forbidding him, promising and threatening him. So even if fear does not stir him and dread does not grow strong in him, the least he can do is be in earnest and not jest. God condemned this in the idolaters, in His saying “*Are you laughing, instead of crying? Are you insisting on your ways?*” (*wa-antum sāmidūn*, “while you amuse yourselves,” Q 53:60–61) and “*and distort it*” (*wa-lghaw fīhi*, “and talk idly about it,” Q 41:26).

::: passage 86:15-17 | 4:737-737

{L|They plot and scheme|innahum yakīdūna} He means the people of Mecca: they are working schemes to undo God’s command and put out the light of truth, and I meet them with My plot, leading them on step by step and waiting for the appointed time I have set for taking revenge on them.

{L|Just respite the disbelievers|fa-mahhili l-kāfirīn} He means: do not pray for their destruction or ask to hasten it. “*A short respite*” (*amhilhum ruwaydā*, “give them respite gently”): that is, a little respite. He repeated it and used two different words to soothe him more and give him more patience.

{From|the Messenger of God ﷺ} “*Whoever recites Sūrat al-Ṭāriq, God will give him ten good deeds for every star in the sky*.”
