# **The Faulty Logic and Fallacies**

Before we get into all of the errors in their thinking and the true reason for this narration's existence, our apologists have given us their arbitrary four criteria in discerning whether or not a prophecy is legitimate. They are as follows:

1.  The prophecies must be unintuitive and cannot be expected to happen/easy to predict.
2.  There needs to be risk to the prophecy, which means it needs to be falsifiable in the sense that it can have the chance of  going wrong and be false.
3.  The prophecy has to be specific and not vague. There can not be various interpretations, meaning there would be no means of allowing reinterpretation of the prophecy to fit any and every situation.
4.  A true prophet of God is not allowed to get any prophecy incorrectly. Someone receiving divine revelation should not be receiving false prophecies and then attribute them to God. This exposes them as a false prophet.

When it comes to the prophecy of bedouins building tall buildings, let's see if it reaches all four of these criteria. **Criteria 1:** The claim is that the prophecy is unintuitive because it predicts that impoverished Bedouins, who were barely surviving, would compete in building tall buildings. This prophecy is not as intuitive as it seems. Throughout history, societies have undergone dramatic transformations due to external factors like resource discovery (e.g., oil), technological advancements, or geopolitical changes. The Bedouins’ transformation in the UAE was driven by the discovery of oil, a random but explainable event. Predicting societal change, especially in a region with significant potential for resource wealth, is not as unlikely as the argument suggests. Additionally, the rise of civilizations from humble beginnings is a recurring theme in history (e.g., the rise of the Mongols or the transformation of medieval Europe). The prophecy’s fulfillment relies on hindsight bias—it only seems remarkable after the fact. The fact that we have barefooted bedouins i.e. Nabateans, who were capable of building structures like that of Petra, Mada’in Saleh, and others, show how any individual could claim that the prophet was aware of such structures do to being a merchant and traveling, and thus made a similar prophecy about his own people after attaining a lot of wealth when he conquered Mecca and expanded his state to include all of Arabia. So in reality, the prophecy was not very intuitive and fails the first criteria.

**Criteria 2:** The next claim is that the prophecy carries risk because it predicts something improbable—impoverished Bedouins building tall buildings. The prophecy is not truly falsifiable because it lacks specificity about timing, location, or mechanism. It doesn’t say _when_ the Bedouins would build tall buildings, _which_ Bedouins would do so, or _how_ this transformation would occur. Without these details, the prophecy can be applied to any group of Bedouins who experience wealth or modernization at any point in history. For example, if the Bedouins in the UAE had remained poor, the prophecy could have been reinterpreted to apply to another group or time period. This lack of specificity makes the prophecy unfalsifiable and therefore not risky. Not to mention, the hadith that these apologists cite are not all in the same. Some of these variants carry very different prophecies despite referencing the same thing:

The Prophet said, "One of the signs of the Hour is that **the shepherds of sheep will compete in constructing tall buildings** and that the barefoot, naked, destitute people will become leaders. And that a slave woman will give birth to her mistress and her owner." (Ahmad 9128 - Sahih)

The Prophet said, “…When the servant girl gives birth to her master, and you see **the barefooted, naked, destitute shepherds competing in the construction of tall buildings,** that is among its signs…” (Ibn Hibban 159 - Sahih)

The Prophet said, “…When the barefoot and naked become leaders of the people, that is one of its portents. **When shepherds compete in constructing buildings, that is one of its portents.**” (Ibn Majah 4044)

The variants do not universally agree on the characteristics of _who_ will fulfill this prophecy. In some narrations, it's the shepherds of sheep, in others it characterizes them as barefooted, naked and destitute shepards. What makes one more valid over the other if they all agree on the highest grading for each of these narrations? On top of this, during the lifetime of the prophet, they had: two - three storied buildings, large mosques were considered tall buildings, a palace was considered a tall building, and the Umayyad period (known for its distinctive and elaborate architecture: see Qubbat Al Sakhra) created many tall buildings prior to the compilation of the hadith corpus.

**Criteria 3:** The next claim is that the prophecy is specific because it mentions "barefoot, naked Bedouins" competing in building tall buildings. In reality, The prophecy is actually quite vague. It doesn’t specify: Which Bedouins: It doesn’t identify a particular tribe, region, or group. What constitutes "tall buildings": This is subjective and open to interpretation. Tall buildings have existed for centuries (e.g., pyramids, cathedrals), so the prophecy could be applied to almost any society that constructs large structures. Timing: It doesn’t provide a timeframe for when this would happen. Mechanism: It doesn’t explain how the Bedouins would achieve this transformation (e.g., through oil wealth, technological advancement, or conquest). The vagueness of the prophecy allows it to be reinterpreted and applied to various situations, undermining its specificity. One of the other requirements under this criteria is that it can’t be easily interpretable to fit another circumstance. Ibn Hajr Al-Asqalani states in Fath al-Bari-

تقدم في كتاب الإيمان من وجه آخر عن أبي هريرة في سؤال جبريل عن الإيمان قوله في أشراط الساعة ويتطاول الناس في البنيان ، وهي من العلامات التي وقعت عن قرب في زمن النبوة

According to Ibn Hajr, this narration was believed from Abu Hurairah himself (the one relating the prophecy to begin with), that the bedouin prophecy was actually something to have occurred during the time or close to the time of the prophet. **Criteria 4:** The claim is that the prophecy about the Bedouins is evidence of Muhammad’s divine insight because it came true. The fault about this logic is that it's just plainly not true. The previous three endeavors into _the apologists own criteria_ ends up exposing this narration as something easily fathomable. By framing the construction of buildings as a sign of excess or a distraction from spiritual pursuits, the hadith could have served as a rhetorical tool to promote ethical behavior rather than a genuine prophecy. The argument highlights a critical flaw in the claim that the prophecy has been fulfilled by the construction of skyscrapers in the Gulf. In conclusion, the argument demonstrates that the prophecy is either a historical observation, a fabricated moral lesson, or a misapplied prediction that fails to align with the realities of the modern world.

## Tall Buildings Were Known in Pre-Islamic Times

## _**Al-Khazneh** - The Nabataeans (4th Century BCE)_

![](https://hadithcriticblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/photo-1554357475-accb8a88a330-819x1024.avif)

The picture above is known as **'Al-Khazneh'** located in Petra, Jordan. This site was inhabited by a group known as _Nabataeans_ that resided in the area around the 4th century BCE. The Nabataeans, an Arab nomadic group, leveraged the strategic location of Petra near the incense trade routes, turning it into a prominent regional trading center. Skilled in surviving the harsh desert, they effectively used the area’s mountainous landscape to ward off invasions. The city of Petra thrived in the 1st century AD, a period marked by the erection of the Al-Khazneh structure, thought to be the mausoleum of King Aretas IV of the Nabataeans, and a population boom to an estimated 20,000 people. To survive in the dry desert conditions, the Nabataeans built a sophisticated system for collecting and storing rainwater, comprising cisterns, channels, and dams. Most of the well-known structures carved into rock, which are mostly tombs, were constructed during this time and the subsequent period. In contrast, fewer of the city’s standalone buildings have been preserved.

This is an example of Bedouin Arabs building tall structures that defy the popular conception of what Bedouin Arabs are capable of. ## **The Eastern Roman Empire** (Byzantium)

## _The Temple of Jupiter_ (Optimus Maximus) - 509 BCE

![](https://hadithcriticblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Temple_de_Jupiter_Stator.jpeg)

The Temple of Jupiter Optimus Maximus, also known as the Temple of Jupiter Capitolinus, was a significant structure in Ancient Rome, situated on the Capitoline Hill. It was traditionally dedicated in 509 BC and was the oldest large temple in Rome. The first building was made of wood and was destroyed by fire in 83 BC. The reconstruction of the temple involved craftsmen from Greece, and the new building was essentially Greek in style, but retained many elements of Etruscan form. The second version of the temple was completed in 69 BC.

Over the centuries, the temple was reconstructed twice more due to fires. The subsequent temples followed the contemporary Roman architectural style, but were of exceptional size. The first version is the largest Etruscan-style temple recorded, and much larger than other Roman temples for centuries after. However, its size remains heavily disputed by specialists.

The reconstructions usually show very wide eaves, and a wide colonnade stretching down the sides. A crude image on a coin of 78 BC shows only four columns, and a very busy roofline. With two further fires, the third temple only lasted five years, to 80 AD, but **the fourth survived until the fall of the empire**.

## _The Great Temple of Yeha_ (Axumite Kingdom) - 700 BCE

![](https://hadithcriticblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/yeha-temple-of-the-moon-entrance-1024x873.jpg)

The Yeha Temple, also known as the Great Temple of Yeha, is believed to be the oldest standing structure in Ethiopia12. It was built around 700 BC during the reign of the D’mt, a pre-Aksumite kingdom. The builders of the Yeha Temple were part of the Da’amat people, who ruled the area during the temple’s construction. The temple is located in the Tigray region of northern Ethiopia and is a significant archaeological site. A remarkable aspect of the Yeha Temple is that it was constructed without the use of mortar. The temple was repurposed in the 6th century CE as a Christian church and St Abuna Aftse monastery, which could explain its preservation. Other places of interest at Yeha include a burial ground and ruined buildings containing, amongst other things, some interesting square columns.

## The Persian Empire

## _The Pasargadae_ - 6th Century CE

![](https://hadithcriticblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/4285798.jpg)

Pasargadae was an ancient city in Iran, founded by Cyrus the Great, the founder of the Achaemenid Empire, in the 6th century BCE. It served as the first capital of the Achaemenid Empire and held significant historical and cultural importance. The city's most famous structure is the Tomb of Cyrus, which dates back to approximately 530 BCE. The tomb is a simple yet imposing structure built on a stepped platform, housing the remains of Cyrus the Great. Pasargadae was not only a political and administrative center but also a symbol of Cyrus's vision of tolerance and multiculturalism, as evidenced by its diverse architectural influences and the incorporation of various cultural elements. The city's strategic location at the crossroads of trade routes facilitated its growth and prosperity during the Achaemenid period. Pasargadae's significance endured even after the capital was moved to Persepolis, and it remains an important archaeological site, attracting visitors interested in exploring the ancient history of Iran.
