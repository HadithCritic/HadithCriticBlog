In modern Islamic discourse, few topics are as actively suppressed as the question of classical racial hierarchy. Today, Muslims universally (and correctly) preach that Islam is a colorblind religion, pointing to the egalitarian message of the Quran.

But when we open the theological manuals of the classical Sunni era, we find an entirely different religion. Some of the most revered authorities in Sunni history presented a formalized doctrine of Arab supremacy, framing it not as a cultural preference, but as an essential pillar of orthodox Islamic belief (*ʿAqīdah*). 

Perhaps the most unabashed defender of this racial hierarchy was **Taqī al-Dīn Ibn Taymiyah** (1263–1328 CE). His writings on Arab superiority raise devastating questions about the theological integrity of classical Sunnism when examined alongside the clear teachings of the Quran.

## The Doctrine of Arab Supremacy

In his foundational work, *Iqtiḍāʾ al-ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm mukhālafat aṣḥāb al-jaḥīm* (The Requirement of the Straight Path for Opposing the People of Hellfire), Ibn Taymiyah dedicates an entire chapter to establishing the divinely ordained superiority of the Arab race. 

What makes his position particularly damning is that he does not present this as his personal opinion. He attributes this racist theology to the absolute consensus of mainstream Sunnism.

<ClaimBox>
**Ibn Taymiyah on the Sunni Consensus (Iqtiḍāʾ, Vol. 1, p. 419):**<br/>
"What the people of Sunnah and the community (Ahl al-Sunnah wa'l-Jamāʿah) hold is the belief that the Arab race is superior to the non-Arab races—be they Hebrews, Syriacs, Romans, Persians, or others."
</ClaimBox>

Ibn Taymiyah constructs a rigid, divine caste system: Arabs are inherently superior to all non-Arabs; the Quraysh tribe is superior to other Arabs; the Banu Hashim are superior to other Quraysh. 

Lest modern apologists try to excuse this by claiming he only meant Arabs were superior *because* the Prophet was an Arab, Ibn Taymiyah explicitly shuts that defense down. He argues their superiority is genetic and intrinsic:

> *"And the superiority of the Arabs, then of Quraysh, then of Banu Hashim, is not merely because the Prophet was from among them... but rather, they are inherently the best in and of themselves."* (Vol. 1, p. 420)

He then goes so far as to excommunicate those who advocate for racial equality (the *Shuʿūbiyyah* movement), labeling them as heretics and innovators:

> *"We do not adopt the view of the Shuʿūbiyyah and the base-born mawālī (non-Arab converts) who do not love the Arabs and do not acknowledge their virtue—for their view is an innovation (bidʿah) and contrary to the truth."* (Vol. 1, p. 421)

## The Quranic Annihilation of Racism

When we turn to the Quran, we find a text that actively dismantles the very racial hierarchy Ibn Taymiyah spent pages trying to build. The Quran establishes human equality as a biological and spiritual fact. 

The most direct and famous contradiction to Ibn Taymiyah's orthodox racism is found in Surah Al-Hujurat:

<QuranVerse verse="49:13" />

This verse destroys the classical Sunni framework of ethnic supremacy on three fronts:
1. **Common Origin:** All humans share the exact same biological start ("a male and a female").
2. **Horizontal, Not Vertical Diversity:** Tribes and nations exist "that you may recognize one another," not so that one may subjugate or claim inherent superiority over the other.
3. **The Ultimate Criterion:** The *only* measure of superiority in God's sight is righteousness (*Taqwā*), an internal state of piety, not an external bloodline.

Furthermore, the Quran presents linguistic and racial diversity not as a hierarchy of divine preference, but as an aesthetic sign of God's creative power:

## A Systemic Theological Infection

It is crucial to understand that Ibn Taymiyah was not a lone extremist operating in a vacuum. This doctrine of Arab supremacy infected centuries of Islamic scholarship. It was a cultural bias born out of the Umayyad and Abbasid empires, retroactively justified by fabricating hadiths and writing theological treatises.

Respected scholars produced an entire genre of literature dedicated to this racial hierarchy:
- **Ibn Qutaybah** wrote *The Virtue of the Arabs and a Note on Their Sciences*.
- **Al-ʿIrāqī** wrote *The Path to Nearness in the Virtue of the Arabs*.
- **Al-Haythamī** authored treatises on Arab excellence.
- **Marʿī al-Karmī** wrote *The Cast Gold on the Virtue of the Arabs and the Nobility of Knowledge over Lineage*.

All of these works represent a long, dark tradition where classical scholars treated Arab supremacy as an established religious fact. 

## The Irony of Harran

<ContextNote>
There is a profound irony in Ibn Taymiyah's rabid defense of Arab supremacy: **He was not an Arab.**
</ContextNote>

Taqī al-Dīn Ibn Taymiyah was born in 1263 CE in the city of Harran (located in modern-day southeastern Turkey), a region deeply influenced by Turkic, Kurdish, and Syriac populations. His family fled southwest to Damascus to escape the advancing Mongol armies. 

That a scholar of Turkish/Levantine origins, living under the protection of the Mamluks (a dynasty of freed Turkic and Circassian slaves), would author a theological defense of Arab genetic superiority shows how deeply this imperial propaganda had penetrated the Sunni psyche. It was no longer just Arab tribalism; it had become codified orthodoxy.
