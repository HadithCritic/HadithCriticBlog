Later appeals to **the consensus of the Companions (*ijmāʿ al-ṣaḥāba*)** can easily create the impression that the first Muslim generation functioned as a single, harmonious evidentiary bloc: one Companion reports something from the Prophet, the others accept it, and a unified Sunnah passes cleanly into the later hadith collections.

The hadith corpus itself preserves a much messier picture.

Companions disputed one another's recollections, accused one another of mistake or forgetfulness, demanded corroborating witnesses before accepting reports, and sometimes continued to practice in ways that conflicted with narrations attributed to other Companions. None of this proves that the Companions disagreed about *everything*, nor does it disprove every later claim of consensus. It does, however, make any appeal to a blanket, undifferentiated "the Companions all agreed" historically untenable.

<ClaimBox title="Central Thesis">
The hadith corpus does not support treating the Companions as a single, invariably agreeing witness. The surviving reports portray disputed recollections, explicit corrections, requests for corroboration, contradictory chronologies, and occasional retractions. Claims of consensus must therefore be established **issue by issue**, not assumed from Companion status alone.
</ClaimBox>

## ʿĀʾisha vs. Abū Hurayra: "He Heard It Wrong"

One of the clearest examples is a report preserved by al-Ḥākim in which ʿĀʾisha is told that Abū Hurayra was circulating several statements as sayings of the Prophet, including:

**Reports attributed by Abū Hurayra to the Prophet:**
1. "The child of adultery is the worst of the three."
2. "The deceased is punished due to the crying of the living."

ʿĀʾisha's response is explicit: **"May Allah have mercy on Abū Hurayra; he heard wrongly and thus erred."** She then supplies different contexts for the statements and invokes the Qurʾān against the meanings being attributed to the Prophet. On the "child of adultery" statement she invokes the principle, *"No bearer of burdens shall bear the burden of another"*; on the punishment of the deceased she rejects the idea that a person bears another's act.

<HadithBlock
  label={"ʿĀʾisha challenges the reported wording"}
  arabic={"رَحِمَ اللَّهُ أَبَا هُرَيْرَةَ أَسَاءَ سَمْعًا فَأَسَاءَ إِصَابَةً"}
  translation={"May Allah have mercy on Abū Hurayra; he heard wrongly and thus erred."}
  source={"al-Ḥākim, al-Mustadrak 2855 — disputed/weak chain"}
  url={"https://hadithunlocked.com/hakim/21/3"}
/>

The report is preserved as [al-Ḥākim, *al-Mustadrak* 2855](https://hadithunlocked.com/hakim/21/3), with a parallel in [al-Bayhaqī, *al-Sunan al-Kubrā* 20048](https://hadithunlocked.com/bayhaqi/63/30). HadithUnlocked's grading metadata records al-Dhahabī's assessment of the al-Ḥākim route as **weak**, so this particular report should not be used by itself. But the broader phenomenon—ʿĀʾisha explicitly correcting Companion reports—is independently attested in much stronger material.

<ContextNote title="Source-Critical Caution">
This al-Ḥākim route is not equivalent in evidentiary weight to the parallel disputes recorded in al-Bukhārī and Muslim. Its transmission includes a *balāgh* attribution in the route and has been assessed as weak. It is included as a transmitted example, not as independently established proof of the exact historical exchange.
</ContextNote>

### A Stronger Parallel: ʿĀʾisha Corrects Ibn ʿUmar on the Weeping of the Dead

Ibn ʿUmar transmitted the maxim that **"the deceased is punished because of his family's weeping over him."** When this was reported to ʿĀʾisha, she replied that Ibn ʿUmar had **erred** or **forgotten**, and explained that the Prophet had instead passed a grave whose occupant was being punished while the family happened to be weeping. She then recited:

> *"No bearer of burdens shall bear the burden of another."*

This correction is preserved through multiple routes, including [Abū Dāwūd 3129](https://hadithunlocked.com/abudawud/21/29), [al-Nasāʾī 1855](https://hadithunlocked.com/nasai/21/15), and [Aḥmad 4959](https://hadithunlocked.com/ahmad/5.2).

<HadithBlock
  label={"A stronger parallel: she says Ibn ʿUmar failed to retain it"}
  arabic={"رَحِمَ اللَّهُ أَبَا عَبْدِ الرَّحْمَنِ سَمِعَ شَيْئًا فَلَمْ يَحْفَظْهُ"}
  translation={"May Allah have mercy upon Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān; he heard something but did not retain it well."}
  source={"Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 931"}
  url={"https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim/11/9"}
/>

<QuranVerse verse="6:164" />

That is not a later critic accusing a Companion of error. It is a Companion explicitly saying that another Companion misremembered what the Prophet said, and using the Qurʾān to reject the transmitted formulation.

## ʿĀʾisha vs. the "Bad Omen" Report

The same pattern appears in the famous dispute over the claim that bad omens reside in **a woman, a house, and a mount**.

Two men told ʿĀʾisha that Abū Hurayra was narrating this as a saying of the Prophet. According to the fuller Aḥmad version, she became extremely angry and swore:

<HadithBlock
  label={"ʿĀʾisha disputes the bad-omen attribution"}
  arabic={"وَالَّذِي أَنْزَلَ الْقُرْآنَ عَلَى أَبِي الْقَاسِمِ ، مَا هَكَذَا كَانَ يَقُولُ ! وَلَكِنَّ نَبِيَّ اللهِ ﷺ كَانَ يَقُولُ : كَانَ أَهْلُ الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ يَقُولُونَ : الطِّيَرَةُ فِي الْمَرْأَةِ وَالدَّارِ وَالدَّابَّةِ"}
  translation={"By the One who sent down the Qurʾān upon Abū al-Qāsim, he did not say that! Rather, the Prophet used to say: The people of Jāhiliyya used to say that bad omens are in a woman, a dwelling, and a riding beast."}
  source={"Musnad Aḥmad 26088"}
  url={"https://hadithunlocked.com/ahmad/15.01"}
/>

She instead said that the Prophet had been reporting what **the people of Jāhiliyya** used to say and then recited Qurʾān 57:22. The matching Aḥmad report is **not** Musnad Aḥmad 25557, as previously cited; the relevant versions are [Aḥmad 26088](https://hadithunlocked.com/ahmad/15.01) and [Aḥmad 25168](https://hadithunlocked.com/ahmad/15.01). A closely related version says that Abū Hurayra entered after the beginning of the statement and heard only its ending: [*al-Maṭālib al-ʿĀliya* 1882](https://hadithunlocked.com/matalib/15/8).

<QuranVerse verse="57:22" />

At the same time, the opposing formulation—"ill omen is in the woman, house, and horse"—is independently attributed to Ibn ʿUmar in major collections such as [al-Bukhārī 5093](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/67/18) and [Muslim 2225a](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim/39/34).

The corpus therefore preserves the disagreement rather than a single uncontested Companion memory.

## Did Muḥammad See God?

The disagreement becomes even more striking on the question of whether Muḥammad saw God.

When Masrūq asked ʿĀʾisha, *"Did Muḥammad see his Lord?"* she responded:

<HadithBlock
  label={"ʿĀʾisha on seeing God"}
  arabic={"لَقَدْ قَفَّ شَعَرِي مِمَّا قُلْتَ ... مَنْ حَدَّثَكَ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا ﷺ رَأَى رَبَّهُ فَقَدْ كَذَبَ"}
  translation={"What you have said makes my hair stand on end! Whoever tells you that Muḥammad saw his Lord has lied."}
  source={"Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 4855 (abridged)"}
  url={"https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/65/53/1"}
/>

She immediately cited Qurʾān 6:103 and 42:51. This is recorded in [Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 4855](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/65/53/1); a parallel appears at [al-Bukhārī 7380](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/97/4).

<QuranVerse verse="6:103, 42:51" />

Yet Ibn ʿAbbās is transmitted saying the opposite: **"Muḥammad saw his Lord."** See [al-Nasāʾī al-Kubrā 11473](https://hadithunlocked.com/nasai-kubra/54/53/3). Another report has ʿIkrima object to Ibn ʿAbbās by citing the very verse ʿĀʾisha used—*"Vision perceives Him not"*—only for Ibn ʿAbbās to defend his position: [al-Ṭabarānī 11619](https://hadithunlocked.com/tabarani/19/3/81).

<ContextNote title="Theological Disagreement and Historical Inference">
This is exactly the kind of first-generation disagreement that later theological systems had to interpret and harmonize. The point is not that every report in every strand is equally historical; the point is that the transmitted corpus itself does not present a single unanimous Companion memory.
</ContextNote>

## Abū Hurayra Retracts a Fasting Ruling After ʿĀʾisha and Umm Salama Contradict Him

A particularly useful example survives around fasting.

Abū Hurayra was reported to have ruled that a person who reaches dawn in a state of major ritual impurity (*junub*) **cannot fast that day**. Marwān had the matter checked with ʿĀʾisha and Umm Salama. Both women replied that the Prophet himself would reach dawn *junub* from intercourse and then continue his fast.

When their testimony was brought back to Abū Hurayra, several versions have him concede the point. In one he says, **"They know better."** Other routes explicitly say that he **withdrew** or **abandoned** his earlier ruling.

See [al-Nasāʾī al-Kubrā 2945](https://hadithunlocked.com/nasai-kubra/5/125), [al-Nasāʾī al-Kubrā 2974](https://hadithunlocked.com/nasai-kubra/5/131), [Ibn Ḥibbān 3486](https://hadithunlocked.com/ibnhibban/12/6/1), and the concise retraction notice in [Ibn Abī Shayba 9673](https://hadithunlocked.com/ibnabishaybah/7/79).

<HadithBlock
  label={"Abū Hurayra concedes their greater knowledge"}
  arabic={"قَالَ هُمَا أَعْلَمُ إِنَّمَا أَنْبَأَنِيهِ الْفَضْلُ بْنُ عَبَّاسٍ"}
  translation={"He said: They know better. It was only al-Faḍl b. ʿAbbās who informed me of it."}
  source={"al-Nasāʾī, al-Sunan al-Kubrā 2945"}
  url={"https://hadithunlocked.com/nasai-kubra/5/125"}
/>

This is direct evidence of a Companion-level report or fatwa being checked against other eyewitness testimony and then retracted.

## Ibn ʿUmar Says "Rajab"; ʿĀʾisha Corrects Him in His Presence

Ibn ʿUmar was asked whether the Prophet performed an ʿumra in Rajab. He answered **yes**.

ʿĀʾisha, listening from her room, corrected him: the Prophet never performed an ʿumra in Rajab, and Ibn ʿUmar had in fact accompanied him on his ʿumras. In the Muslim and Nasāʾī versions, Ibn ʿUmar hears her correction and does not respond.

The episode is preserved in [al-Bukhārī 1775](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/26/3), [al-Bukhārī 4253](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/64/44), [Muslim 1254](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim/15/35), [Muslim 1255a](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim/15/35), and [al-Nasāʾī al-Kubrā 4208](https://hadithunlocked.com/nasai-kubra/8/284).

<HadithBlock
  label={"ʿĀʾisha corrects the Rajab attribution"}
  arabic={"يَرْحَمُ اللَّهُ أَبَا عَبْدِ الرَّحْمَنِ مَا اعْتَمَرَ عُمْرَةً إِلاَّ وَهُوَ شَاهِدُهُ وَمَا اعْتَمَرَ فِي رَجَبٍ قَطُّ"}
  translation={"May Allah have mercy on Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān! The Prophet never performed an ʿumra except that he was present with him, and he never performed an ʿumra in Rajab."}
  source={"Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 1775"}
  url={"https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/26/3"}
/>

Here the disagreement is not about an abstract theological interpretation. It concerns the calendar month in which a concrete journey supposedly occurred—and one eyewitness directly corrects another.

## ʿUmar Demands a Witness Before Accepting Abū Mūsā's Hadith

The early corpus also preserves skepticism toward **solitary Companion testimony itself**.

Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī sought permission to enter upon ʿUmar three times and then left, explaining that the Prophet had instructed Muslims to depart after three unanswered requests for permission. ʿUmar did not simply accept the attribution. He demanded that Abū Mūsā produce another witness.

Abū Mūsā then went to the Anṣār in alarm until Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī agreed to testify that he had heard the same rule.

This is found in [al-Bukhārī 6245](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/79/13), [Muslim 2153a](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim/38/7), and several parallel versions such as [Ibn Ḥibbān 5806](https://hadithunlocked.com/ibnhibban/43/20).

<HadithBlock
  label={"ʿUmar demands corroborating testimony"}
  arabic={"فَقَالَ وَاللَّهِ لَتُقِيمَنَّ عَلَيْهِ بِبَيِّنَةٍ"}
  translation={"ʿUmar said: By Allah, you must establish it with evidence."}
  source={"Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6245"}
  url={"https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/79/13"}
/>

Whatever one concludes about ʿUmar's motive, the episode is impossible to square with the idea that a Companion's bare formula *"the Messenger of Allah said..."* was automatically treated as sufficient proof by every other Companion.

## ʿUmar Rejects Fāṭima bint Qays's Report as Potentially Misremembered

Fāṭima bint Qays reported that after her final divorce the Prophet assigned her **neither housing nor maintenance**.

The counter-report attributes to ʿUmar a striking response:

<HadithBlock
  label={"ʿUmar questions the accuracy of Fāṭima’s memory"}
  arabic={"لاَ نَدَعُ كِتَابَ اللَّهِ وَسُنَّةَ نَبِيِّنَا ﷺ لِقَوْلِ امْرَأَةٍ لاَ نَدْرِي أَحَفِظَتْ أَمْ نَسِيَتْ"}
  translation={"We do not leave the Book of Allah and the Sunnah of our Prophet for the statement of a woman when we do not know whether she remembered or forgot."}
  source={"Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī 1180 (ʿUmar’s statement transmitted through al-Shaʿbī)"}
  url={"https://hadithunlocked.com/tirmidhi/13/5"}
/>

The wording is preserved in [Abū Dāwūd 2291](https://hadithunlocked.com/abudawud/13/40), [al-Tirmidhī 1180](https://hadithunlocked.com/tirmidhi/13/5), and related material in [Ibn Abī Shayba 18982–18983](https://hadithunlocked.com/ibnabishaybah/13/139).

Whether one sides legally with Fāṭima or with the position attributed to ʿUmar is beside the point here. The report explicitly represents a Companion rejecting another Companion's claimed Prophetic precedent on the grounds that **memory can fail**.

## ʿUmar's Restrictions on Hadith Transmission

The corpus also preserves reports portraying ʿUmar as actively restricting the volume of Prophetic narration.

In the report of Qaraẓa b. Kaʿb, ʿUmar warns a delegation heading toward Iraq that they are going to people deeply occupied with the Qurʾān and tells them to **reduce narration from the Messenger of Allah**. See [al-Ḥākim 347](https://hadithunlocked.com/hakim/2/7), [al-Ṭabarānī al-Awsaṭ 2120](https://hadithunlocked.com/tabarani-awsat/1/100), and a related version in [al-Dārimī 287](https://hadithunlocked.com/darimi/0/28).

Another report preserved by al-Ṭabarānī states that ʿUmar summoned Ibn Masʿūd, Abū Masʿūd al-Anṣārī, and Abū al-Dardāʾ and demanded:

> *"What is this hadith that you are narrating so much from the Messenger of Allah?"*

It then says he kept them in Medina until his death. This is [al-Ṭabarānī al-Awsaṭ 3453](https://hadithunlocked.com/tabarani-awsat/6/29). HadithUnlocked lists **no grading** for this report, so it should be presented as a transmitted narrative rather than as an uncontested historical fact.

Likewise, the threat attributed to ʿUmar against Abū Hurayra—

> *"You will stop narrating from the Messenger of Allah, or I will send you back to the land of Daws."*

—is preserved in al-Suyūṭī's collection at [*Jamʿ al-Jawāmiʿ* 2-3464b](https://hadithunlocked.com/suyuti/31.04), again with no grade supplied in HadithUnlocked.

The strongest point is therefore not that every later story of an "Umaric hadith ban" must be accepted literally. It is that early and medieval transmitters themselves preserved a memory of **restriction, verification, and anxiety about excessive Prophetic attribution**.

<ContextNote title="Evidence Is Not All of One Weight" collapsible={true}>
The reports of ʿUmar limiting narration are not a single uniform, independently demonstrated policy. Each strand needs its own isnād analysis, and late collections cannot be treated as interchangeable with contemporaneous testimony. The historically secure conclusion for this article is the *existence of divergent transmitted accounts* about early attitudes toward hadith.
</ContextNote>

## ʿUmar vs. ʿAmmār: "Do You Not Remember?"

The tayammum reports preserve an unusually sharp conflict of memory.

A man asked ʿUmar what he should do if he became *junub* and could not find water. ʿUmar answered that he should **not pray**. ʿAmmār then reminded him of a journey they had supposedly experienced together: both had become *junub* without water; ʿUmar did not pray, while ʿAmmār rolled in the dust and later reported the episode to the Prophet, who taught him how tayammum should be performed.

The basic exchange is preserved in [al-Bukhārī 338](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/7/4).

<HadithBlock
  label={"ʿAmmār invokes a shared remembered event"}
  arabic={"فَقَالَ عَمَّارٌ أَمَا تَذْكُرُ يَا أَمِيرَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ إِذْ أَنَا وَأَنْتَ فِي سَرِيَّةٍ فَأَجْنَبْنَا فَلَمْ نَجِدْ مَاءً"}
  translation={"ʿAmmār said: Do you remember, Commander of the Faithful, when you and I were on a military expedition and became junub but could not find water?"}
  source={"Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 368c (excerpt)"}
  url={"https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim/3/28"}
/>

Muslim preserves the disagreement even more starkly. ʿAmmār asks ʿUmar whether he remembers the shared incident. ʿUmar responds, **"ʿAmmār, fear Allah."** ʿAmmār then says that if ʿUmar wishes, he will stop narrating the report. See [Muslim 368c](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim/3/28) and [Muslim 368d](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim/3/28). A similar version appears in [Abū Dāwūd 322](https://hadithunlocked.com/abudawud/1/124).

The disagreement did not end there. When Abū Mūsā later cited ʿAmmār's report to Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn Masʿūd answered:

> *"Did you not see that ʿUmar was not satisfied with ʿAmmār's statement?"*

That exchange is preserved in [al-Bukhārī 345](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/7/7).

This is difficult to reduce to a simple difference of legal interpretation. The transmitted narrative has one Companion appealing to a **shared Prophetic-era event**, another failing to accept or remember it, and a third Companion subsequently invoking that rejection against the report.

## ʿUmar Says They Left Some of Ubayy's Recitation

The corpus also preserves a remarkable statement attributed to ʿUmar about Ubayy b. Kaʿb.

ʿUmar calls Ubayy **"the best reciter among us"**, but immediately adds that **"we leave some of what Ubayy says/recites."** The reason supplied is that Ubayy insisted:

> *"I will not leave anything that I heard from the Messenger of Allah."*

ʿUmar then invokes Qurʾān 2:106 concerning abrogation or causing a revelation to be forgotten.

See [al-Bukhārī 4481](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/65/2/7), [al-Bukhārī 5005](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/66/8), and [al-Nasāʾī al-Kubrā 10928](https://hadithunlocked.com/nasai-kubra/54/2).

<HadithBlock
  label={"ʿUmar’s explanation concerning Ubayy’s recitation"}
  arabic={"أَقْرَؤُنَا أُبَىٌّ وَأَقْضَانَا عَلِيٌّ وَإِنَّا لَنَدَعُ مِنْ قَوْلِ أُبَىٍّ وَذَاكَ أَنَّ أُبَيًّا يَقُولُ لاَ أَدَعُ شَيْئًا سَمِعْتُهُ مِنْ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ"}
  translation={"Our best reciter is Ubayy, and our best judge is ʿAlī. Yet we leave some of what Ubayy says, because he says: I will leave nothing that I heard from the Messenger of Allah."}
  source={"Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 4481"}
  url={"https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/65/2/7"}
/>

<QuranVerse verse="2:106" />

<ContextNote title="What the Ubayy Report Does and Does Not Claim">
The report's own explanation is abrogation, so it should not be carelessly turned into a claim that ʿUmar thought Ubayy was fabricating Qurʾān. Its historical importance here is narrower: Ubayy appealed to direct Prophetic auditory memory, while other Companions nevertheless left aside some of what he continued to recite.
</ContextNote>

Whatever theological explanation one adopts, the report itself is incompatible with a picture in which every Companion preserved and accepted exactly the same body of material without dispute.

## Ibn Masʿūd and Abū al-Dardāʾ Refuse the Reading Used by Others

An even more concrete Qurʾānic example concerns Q 92:3.

The canonical text reads:

<QuranVerse verse="92:3" />

But reports in both al-Bukhārī and Muslim say that Ibn Masʿūd recited instead:

<HadithBlock
  label={"Reported alternative recitation of Q 92:3"}
  arabic={"وَالذَّكَرِ وَالأُنْثَى"}
  translation={"By the male and the female."}
  source={"Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 4944 — reported recitation of Ibn Masʿūd / Abū al-Dardāʾ"}
  url={"https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/65/92/2"}
/>

Abū al-Dardāʾ explicitly says that he too heard the Prophet recite it this way and complains that **"these people"** wanted him to recite *wa-mā khalaqa* instead. In one version he swears that he will not follow them.

See [al-Bukhārī 4944](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/65/92/2), [Muslim 824a](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim/6/50), and [al-Tirmidhī 2939](https://hadithunlocked.com/tirmidhi/46/7).

This is not merely a later qirāʾāt technicality. The narrative depicts prominent early authorities appealing to **their own memory of the Prophet's recitation** against the reading preferred by another community.

## Ibn ʿAbbās vs. Abū Hurayra on the Pregnant Widow

The waiting period of a pregnant widow produced another direct Companion disagreement.

Ibn ʿAbbās held that she must observe **the longer of two periods**: either childbirth or four months and ten days. Abū Hurayra sided with the contrary view that childbirth itself ended the waiting period.

The dispute was referred to Umm Salama, who cited the case of Subayʿa al-Aslamiyya: she gave birth shortly after her husband's death, and the Prophet permitted her to remarry.

Muslim explicitly describes Ibn ʿAbbās and the opposing side as **contending with each other**: [Muslim 1485a](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim/18/8). Al-Nasāʾī states directly that **"Abū Hurayra and Ibn ʿAbbās differed"** on the question: [al-Nasāʾī 3509](https://hadithunlocked.com/nasai/27/56). An early parallel is preserved in [Ibn Abī Shayba 17374](https://hadithunlocked.com/ibnabishaybah/12/148).

<HadithBlock
  label={"The pregnant widow: two rulings and a third-party check"}
  arabic={"فَقَالَ ابْنُ عَبَّاسٍ عِدَّتُهَا آخِرُ الأَجَلَيْنِ وَقَالَ أَبُو سَلَمَةَ قَدْ حَلَّتْ فَجَعَلاَ يَتَنَازَعَانِ ذَلِكَ ... فَقَالَ أَبُو هُرَيْرَةَ أَنَا مَعَ ابْنِ أَخِي"}
  translation={"Ibn ʿAbbās said: Her waiting period is the longer of the two. Abū Salama said: Her waiting period has ended. They disputed this, and Abū Hurayra said: I agree with my nephew."}
  source={"Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 1485a (excerpt)"}
  url={"https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim/18/8"}
/>

Again, one side had to appeal to another eyewitness tradition to settle a dispute among major Companions.

## Ibn ʿAbbās vs. Abū Hurayra: Must One Perform Wuḍūʾ After Food Touched by Fire?

Abū Hurayra transmitted that the Prophet ordered wuḍūʾ after eating food **touched by fire**. One canonical version says:

> *"Perform wuḍūʾ from what fire has touched, even if it is a piece of cheese."*

See [al-Tirmidhī 79](https://hadithunlocked.com/tirmidhi/1/58).

Ibn ʿAbbās challenged this. In one report he asked whether the rule would also require wuḍūʾ because of hot water; Abū Hurayra objected to drawing analogies against a Prophetic report:


<HadithBlock
  label={"Ibn ʿAbbās challenges Abū Hurayra on food cooked by fire"}
  arabic={"فَقَالَ ابْنُ عَبَّاسٍ أَتَوَضَّأُ مِنَ الْحَمِيمِ فَقَالَ لَهُ يَا ابْنَ أَخِي إِذَا سَمِعْتَ عَنْ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ حَدِيثًا فَلاَ تَضْرِبْ لَهُ الأَمْثَالَ"}
  translation={"Ibn ʿAbbās said: Should I perform wuḍūʾ because of hot water? Abū Hurayra said: Nephew, when you hear a hadith from the Messenger of Allah, do not make analogies against it."}
  source={"Sunan Ibn Mājah 485"}
  url={"https://hadithunlocked.com/ibnmajah/1/65"}
/>

This is preserved in [Ibn Mājah 485](https://hadithunlocked.com/ibnmajah/1/65). ʿAbd al-Razzāq preserves another direct encounter in which Abū Hurayra performs wuḍūʾ after eating dried curd and Ibn ʿAbbās replies that he personally saw the Prophet eat roasted meat and then pray **without renewing wuḍūʾ**: [ʿAbd al-Razzāq 645](https://hadithunlocked.com/abdalrazzaq/1/70). A parallel appears in [Aḥmad 3464](https://hadithunlocked.com/ahmad/4.5).

This is almost a textbook example of competing Prophetic memories: Abū Hurayra cites a verbal command; Ibn ʿAbbās counters with claimed eyewitness practice.

## Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn ʿUmar vs. Abū Saʿīd on Ribā

The ribā material provides another example where Companion opinions collided with a competing Prophetic report.

A narrator says that he asked **Ibn ʿUmar and Ibn ʿAbbās** about exchanging gold for gold with an excess and that they did not see harm in it. He then asked Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī, who declared the excess to be ribā and cited the Prophet.

The narrator says he initially **refused to accept Abū Saʿīd's answer because of what Ibn ʿUmar and Ibn ʿAbbās had said**. See [Muslim 1594d](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim/22/18).

Later material explicitly remembers Ibn ʿAbbās retracting his earlier view after receiving Abū Saʿīd's report. Ibn Mājah preserves Ibn ʿAbbās saying:

<HadithBlock
  label={"Ibn ʿAbbās is reported to retract his earlier view"}
  arabic={"قَالَ نَعَمْ إِنَّمَا كَانَ ذَلِكَ رَأْيًا مِنِّي وَهَذَا أَبُو سَعِيدٍ يُحَدِّثُ عَنْ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ أَنَّهُ نَهَى عَنِ الصَّرْفِ"}
  translation={"He said: Yes. That was merely my own opinion, while Abū Saʿīd narrates that the Messenger of Allah forbade such exchanges."}
  source={"Sunan Ibn Mājah 2258"}
  url={"https://hadithunlocked.com/ibnmajah/12/49"}
/>

See [Ibn Mājah 2258](https://hadithunlocked.com/ibnmajah/12/49). Al-Ṭabarānī likewise preserves reports in which Ibn ʿAbbās repents of the earlier fatwa after Abū Saʿīd confronts him: [al-Ṭabarānī 454](https://hadithunlocked.com/tabarani/2/3) and [al-Ṭabarānī 456](https://hadithunlocked.com/tabarani/2/3).

The significance is not that retraction is a defect. Quite the opposite: it shows an active process of **disagreement, evidence-testing, and correction** rather than automatic unanimity.

## ʿĀʾisha Rejects the Claim That Women "Cut Off" Prayer

A final strong example concerns reports that a passing woman, donkey, or dog interrupts a man's prayer.

When this doctrine was mentioned before ʿĀʾisha, she objected:

<HadithBlock
  label={"ʿĀʾisha objects to the prayer-interruption rule"}
  arabic={"فَقَالَتْ شَبَّهْتُمُونَا بِالْحُمُرِ وَالْكِلاَبِ وَاللَّهِ لَقَدْ رَأَيْتُ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ يُصَلِّي وَإِنِّي عَلَى السَّرِيرِ بَيْنَهُ وَبَيْنَ الْقِبْلَةِ مُضْطَجِعَةً"}
  translation={"She said: You have compared us to donkeys and dogs! By Allah, I saw the Prophet praying while I lay on a bed between him and the qibla."}
  source={"Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 514"}
  url={"https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/8/105"}
/>

She then appealed to her own eyewitness experience: she had lain between the Prophet and the qibla while he prayed.

See [al-Bukhārī 514](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/8/105), [Muslim 512d](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim/4/51), and [Muslim 512e](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim/4/51).

Yet the contrary rule is attributed directly to the Prophet through Abū Hurayra—*"The prayer is cut off by a woman, a dog, and a donkey"*—at [Ibn Mājah 950](https://hadithunlocked.com/ibnmajah/5/38) and [Aḥmad 7983](https://hadithunlocked.com/ahmad/5.5).

The surviving corpus therefore preserves both the Prophetic attribution and ʿĀʾisha's experiential rebuttal to the doctrine.


## What These Reports Actually Demonstrate

The cumulative point is narrower—and stronger—than simply saying "the Companions disagreed."

The corpus depicts first-generation Muslims doing all of the following:

- **Correcting one another's memory** (ʿĀʾisha vs. Ibn ʿUmar; ʿĀʾisha vs. reports attributed to Abū Hurayra).
- **Appealing to the Qurʾān against a transmitted formulation** (the punishment of the deceased).
- **Retracting a ruling after contrary eyewitness testimony** (Abū Hurayra on fasting while *junub*).
- **Disputing concrete chronology** (Ibn ʿUmar's Rajab ʿumra; the competing mutʿah prohibition dates).
- **Demanding corroboration for a Prophetic attribution** (ʿUmar and Abū Mūsā).
- **Explicitly allowing for Companion forgetfulness** (the Fāṭima bint Qays dispute).
- **Restricting or discouraging excessive narration** in reports attributed to ʿUmar.
- **Rejecting or failing to remember a shared Prophetic-era event** (ʿUmar vs. ʿAmmār on tayammum).
- **Leaving aside material another Companion insisted he heard directly from the Prophet** (ʿUmar and Ubayy).
- **Preserving competing Qurʾānic recitations as Prophetic memory** (Ibn Masʿūd/Abū al-Dardāʾ and Q 92:3).
- **Reversing legal positions after a rival Companion produced contrary Prophetic evidence** (Ibn ʿAbbās on ribā).

That picture is incompatible with the simplistic apologetic image of a first generation whose individual Prophetic reports were automatically accepted by their peers and transmitted without serious contest.

## The Verdict: "The Companions" Were Not a Single Voice

<VerdictBox title="Analytical Verdict">
The historical record preserved inside the hadith corpus is not one of seamless uniformity. It is one of **competing memories, correction, verification, disagreement, and later harmonization**.

A later scholar may still argue for consensus on a *particular* issue, but that consensus has to be demonstrated issue by issue. It cannot simply be assumed from the prestige of the Companion generation itself.
</VerdictBox>

## The Qurʾān Alone Is the Criterion

These reports show why human transmission cannot be treated as self-authenticating merely because a report is attributed to a Companion. The earliest transmitted material itself remembers Companions correcting, challenging, and checking one another. The Qurʾān remains the fixed criterion against which later reports must be judged.
