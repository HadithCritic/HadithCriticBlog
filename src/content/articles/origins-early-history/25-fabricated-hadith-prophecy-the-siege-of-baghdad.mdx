There are many hadith apologists who reach for a handful of reports to demonstrate the corpus’s “miraculous” nature. The goal is twofold: to use these prophetic-sounding hadiths as evidence for the reliability of the corpus, and to validate the religion by claiming the Prophet knew things he could not have known. The report examined here is a favorite of that genre. It is said to predict the **siege of Baghdad** by the Mongols in 656/1258 — a great Muslim city on the Tigris, its bridge, its destruction at the hands of a broad-faced, narrow-eyed steppe people.

Before any of the historical detail, the Qurʾān removes the foundation of the whole exercise. The Prophet is made to disclaim exactly this kind of foreknowledge.

<QuranVerse verse="7:188" />

But even setting the Qurʾān aside and meeting the claim entirely on its own historical ground, the prophecy collapses. The report is not about Baghdad and not about the Mongols. It names **Baṣra**. It begins life as a **non-prophetic saying** of a Companion known for retailing biblical and apocalyptic lore. And it was given its final, “prophetic” shape by a single Baṣran transmitter — **Saʿīd b. Jumhān** — who modeled it on a terror he actually lived through: the **Türgesh Turkish wars** that were devouring Muslim armies in the East during his own lifetime.

## The hadith in question

<HadithBlock label="The Marfūʿ (Prophetic) Version" source="Sunan Abī Dāwūd 4306; cf. Ibn Ḥibbān 6748; Musnad Aḥmad 20451, 20413, 20414">
  <div class="hadith-block__arabic" dir="rtl" lang="ar">
    يَنْزِلُ نَاسٌ مِنْ أُمَّتِي بِغَائِطٍ يُسَمُّونَهُ الْبَصْرَةَ عِنْدَ نَهْرٍ يُقَالُ لَهُ دِجْلَةُ يَكُونُ عَلَيْهِ جِسْرٌ، يَكْثُرُ أَهْلُهَا وَتَكُونُ مِنْ أَمْصَارِ الْمُهَاجِرِينَ … فَإِذَا كَانَ فِي آخِرِ الزَّمَانِ جَاءَ بَنُو قَنْطُورَاءَ عِرَاضُ الْوُجُوهِ صِغَارُ الْأَعْيُنِ حَتَّى يَنْزِلُوا عَلَى شَطِّ النَّهْرِ، فَيَتَفَرَّقُ أَهْلُهَا ثَلَاثَ فِرَقٍ: فِرْقَةٌ يَأْخُذُونَ أَذْنَابَ الْبَقَرِ وَالْبَرِّيَّةَ وَهَلَكُوا، وَفِرْقَةٌ يَأْخُذُونَ لِأَنْفُسِهِمْ وَكَفَرُوا، وَفِرْقَةٌ يَجْعَلُونَ ذَرَارِيَّهُمْ خَلْفَ ظُهُورِهِمْ وَيُقَاتِلُونَهُمْ وَهُمُ الشُّهَدَاءُ.
  </div>
  <div class="hadith-block__translation">
    “Some people of my nation will settle in a low-lying land they call al-Baṣra, by a river called the Tigris, over which there is a bridge. Its people will be numerous, and it will be one of the garrison-cities of the Muhājirūn … When the end of time approaches, the Banū Qanṭūrāʾ — broad-faced, small-eyed — will come and descend on the riverbank. Its people will split into three groups: a group that takes hold of the tails of cattle and the open country, and they perish; a group that takes safety for themselves, and they disbelieve; and a group that puts their children behind their backs and fights them, and they are the martyrs.”
  </div>
</HadithBlock>

The Aḥmad recensions add the closing line that the apologetic depends on — that **God grants victory to the surviving remnant** — and one of them spells out the identification directly.[^variants]

  <div class="hadith-block__arabic" dir="rtl" lang="ar">
    … وَأَمَّا فِرْقَةٌ فَيَجْعَلُونَ عِيَالَهُمْ خَلْفَ ظُهُورِهِمْ وَيُقَاتِلُونَ، فَقَتْلَاهُمْ شُهَدَاءُ، وَيَفْتَحُ اللَّهُ عَلَى بَقِيَّتِهَا.
    <br/>
    قَالَ الْعَوَّامُ: بَنُو قَنْطُورَاءَ هُمُ التُّرْكُ.
  </div>
  <div class="hadith-block__translation">
    “… and as for one group, they put their dependents behind their backs and fight; their slain are martyrs, and God grants victory to its remnant.” al-ʿAwwām [b. Ḥawshab, one of Saʿīd b. Jumhān’s own transmitters] said: “The Banū Qanṭūrāʾ are the Turks.”
  </div>
</HadithBlock>

<ClaimBox title="The apologetic argument">
The hadith allegedly predicts (1) the rise of a great Muslim capital on the Tigris with a bridge, read as Baghdad; (2) its destruction by a broad-faced, small-eyed steppe people, read as the Mongols; (3) the splitting of its inhabitants into three fates — flight, surrender, martyrdom — read as the events of 656/1258. The conclusion is that the detail is too precise to be coincidence, and therefore proves prophecy.
</ClaimBox>

Every link in that chain of reasoning fails, and they fail in a specific order: the wording is not originally prophetic, the common link is identifiable, the imagery is borrowed from a contemporary war, the city is the wrong city, and the “fulfillment” contradicts the very details it is supposed to match.

## The earliest layer is not a prophecy at all

The oldest recoverable forms of this material are **not** attributed to the Prophet. They are a saying of the Companion **ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ**, who was famous for transmitting from the *Ahl al-Kitāb* and for eschatological lore. In ʿAbd al-Razzāq’s *Muṣannaf* the saying is *mawqūf* — it stops at Ibn ʿAmr, with no Prophet in sight.[^mawquf]

<HadithBlock label="The Mawqūf Source" source="ʿAbd al-Razzāq, al-Muṣannaf 20799 (Maʿmar ← Ayyūb ← Ibn Sīrīn ← ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Abī Bakra)">
  <div class="hadith-block__arabic" dir="rtl" lang="ar">
    قَالَ عَبْدُ اللَّهِ بْنُ عَمْرِو بْنِ الْعَاصِ: يُوشِكُ بَنُو قَنْطُورَاءَ أَنْ يُخْرِجُوكُمْ مِنْ أَرْضِ الْعِرَاقِ. قُلْتُ: ثُمَّ نَرْجِعُ؟ قَالَ: أَوَ تُحِبُّونَ ذَلِكَ؟ … ثُمَّ تَرْجِعُونَ فَيَكُونُ لَكُمْ فِيهَا عَيْشٌ رَخِيٌّ.
  </div>
  <div class="hadith-block__translation">
    ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ said: “The Banū Qanṭūrāʾ are about to drive you out of the land of Iraq.” I said, “Then shall we return?” He said, “Would you like that? … Then you will return, and you will have a pleasant life there.”
  </div>
</HadithBlock>

A second early form, preserved by Nuʿaym b. Ḥammād through Ibn Burayda, gives the same Ibn ʿAmr saying with the concrete Baṣran geography and a three-way split — but, again, **no Prophet**:

<HadithBlock label="The Three-Groups Motif, Still Non-Prophetic" source="Nuʿaym b. Ḥammād, Kitāb al-Fitan 1906 (← Ibn Burayda ← Sulaymān b. Rabīʿa); cf. the parallel in ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿAmr material">
  <div class="hadith-block__arabic" dir="rtl" lang="ar">
    يُوشِكُ بَنُو قَنْطُورَاءَ أَنْ يُخْرِجُوا أَهْلَ خُرَاسَانَ وَأَهْلَ سِجِسْتَانَ … حَتَّى يَرْبِطُوا خُيُولَهُمْ بِنَخْلِ الْأُبُلَّةِ، ثُمَّ يُرْسِلُونَ إِلَى أَهْلِ الْبَصْرَةِ: اخْرُجُوا مِنْ أَرْضِنَا أَوْ نَأْتِيكُمْ، ثُمَّ يَفْتَرِقُونَ ثَلَاثَ فِرَقٍ: فِرْقَةٌ تَلْحَقُ بِالْعَرَبِ، وَفِرْقَةٌ تَأْخُذُ نَحْوَ الشَّامِ، وَفِرْقَةٌ تُقَاتِلُهُمْ.
  </div>
  <div class="hadith-block__translation">
    “The Banū Qanṭūrāʾ are about to drive out the people of Khurāsān and Sijistān … until they tie their horses to the date-palms of al-Ubulla. Then they will send to the people of Baṣra: ‘Leave our land or we come to you.’ Then they will split into three groups: a group that joins the Arabs, a group that heads toward Syria, and a group that fights them.”
  </div>
</HadithBlock>

And the meaning of “Banū Qanṭūrāʾ” is fixed for us by the early transmitters themselves, who tell us plainly that it is a scriptural label for the **Turks** — not a coded reference to any future Mongol khan.

<HadithBlock label="Banū Qanṭūrāʾ = the Turks" source="Ibn Abī Shayba, al-Muṣannaf 15/112 (Rabīʿa b. Jushn ← ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿAmr)">
  <div class="hadith-block__arabic" dir="rtl" lang="ar">
    تَهَيَّؤُوا يَا أَهْلَ الْبَصْرَةِ … وَاللَّهِ لَيُخْرِجَنَّكُمْ بَنُو قَنْطُورَاءَ مِنْ هَا هُنَا … قُلْنَا: وَمَنْ بَنُو قَنْطُورَاءَ؟ قَالَ: فِي الْكِتَابِ يُسَمَّوْنَ كَذَا، وَفِي النَّعْتِ هُمُ التُّرْكُ.
  </div>
  <div class="hadith-block__translation">
    “Prepare yourselves, O people of Baṣra! … By God, the Banū Qanṭūrāʾ will drive you out of here …” We said, “And who are the Banū Qanṭūrāʾ?” He said, “In the scripture they are named thus, but in description they are the Turks.”
  </div>
</HadithBlock>

So the raw materials are clear and pre-prophetic: a Companion saying, derived from scriptural-apocalyptic lore, that **Turks** would one day drive the people of Iraq out of **Baṣra**. There is no Prophet, no Baghdad, and no Mongols. The job of converting this into a “prophecy of Muhammad” was done by one man.

## The common link: Saʿīd b. Jumhān

Every prophetic version of this report runs through a single Baṣran transmitter, **Saʿīd b. Jumhān al-Aslamī (d. 136/753)**. He takes the floating Baṣran saying, attaches the dramatic three-groups expansion, and supplies an isnād that climbs to the Prophet through a father-and-son pair of Baṣrans (Abū Bakra and his son) — conveniently, the household of one of Baṣra’s most famous Companions, in the very city the report is about.

**First, Saʿīd b. Jumhān is a soft transmitter who is criticized precisely for solitary, peculiar reports.** He is broadly “acceptable,” but the critics flag exactly the trait that matters here: he narrates things no one else does.

“He has solitary narrations,” “he is not used as proof,” “not corroborated,” “strange things” — this is the rijāl profile of a transmitter who introduces new material. That is precisely what we see: the older sources have a Companion saying; Saʿīd is where the prophetic attribution and the elaborate three-groups ending first appear.[^rijal]

**Second, the link just above Saʿīd is unstable — a sign of a constructed chain.** Saʿīd cannot keep the name of his own informant straight. He variously calls him **Muslim** b. Abī Bakra (Abū Dāwūd, Ibn Ḥibbān), **ʿAbd Allāh** b. Abī Bakra (Aḥmad 20451), **Ibn Abī Bakra** unspecified (Aḥmad 20413–20414), while the genuinely sound Baṣran chain for related material — Ibn Sīrīn’s — runs through **ʿAbd al-Raḥmān** b. Abī Bakra. A transmitter who shuffles the identity of the man who supposedly handed him a prophecy is not preserving a fixed text; he is assembling one.

<ContextNote title="What the common-link picture means">
The diagram is not “many independent witnesses to a prediction.” It is one Baṣran narrator, criticized for solitary reports, who sits at the only point where the material turns prophetic — and whose immediate “source” keeps changing names. Below him the chains multiply into the collectors; above him there is a single fragile, unstable stem. That is the signature of a raised (marfūʿ) report, not an inherited one.
</ContextNote>

## Pinpointing each element of the hadith to history

If this is not a prediction of 1258, what is it a picture of? The answer is that **every element of the report maps onto Saʿīd b. Jumhān’s own world** — late-Umayyad and early-ʿAbbāsid Baṣra, terrified of the Turks who were then annihilating Muslim armies in the East.

  </tr>
  <tr>

Bukhārī/Muslim: ṣighār al-aʿyun, wujūhuhum ka-l-majānn). Saʿīd’s own student al-ʿAwwām states it: “the Banū Qanṭūrāʾ are the Turks.”</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>

  </tr>
  <tr>

  </tr>

The clinching parallel is in the chronicles. al-Ṭabarī’s account of the Türgesh siege of the Muslim garrison at **Qaṣr al-Bāhilī** in the Sughd describes a relief column under al-Musayyab b. Bishr that **sheds its waverers in three successive stages** — 1,300 turn back, then 1,000, then another 1,000 — until only the steadfast remnant remains to fight and break through; and it describes the besieged resolving to **put their women and children to the front and die together**.

<HadithBlock label="The Historical Model: Turks Besiege a Muslim Garrison; the Army Fragments" source="al-Ṭabarī, Tārīkh al-rusul wa-l-mulūk, the Khurāsān governorship of Saʿīd 'Khudhayna' (Qaṣr al-Bāhilī); cf. the catastrophe of Yawm al-Shiʿb, 113 AH, under al-Junayd">
  <div class="hadith-block__arabic" dir="rtl" lang="ar">
    فَانْصَرَفَ عَنْهُ أَلْفٌ وَثَلَاثُ مِائَةٍ … ثُمَّ سَارَ فَرْسَخًا فَاعْتَزَلَ أَلْفٌ، ثُمَّ سَارَ فَرْسَخًا آخَرَ فَاعْتَزَلَ أَلْفٌ … قَالَ أَهْلُ الْقَصْرِ: قَدْ أَجْمَعْنَا عَلَى تَسْلِيمِ نِسَائِنَا وَتَقْدِيمِهِمْ لِلْمَوْتِ أَمَامَنَا حَتَّى نَمُوتَ جَمِيعًا.
  </div>
  <div class="hadith-block__translation">
    “So 1,300 turned back from him … then he marched a league and 1,000 withdrew, then he marched another league and 1,000 withdrew … The people of the fortress said: ‘We have resolved to put our women forward, to death before us, so that we may all die together.’”
  </div>
</HadithBlock>

A relief army splitting into thirds under Turkish attack; defenders shielding their families in a last stand. The hadith’s “three groups” and “they put their children behind their backs and fight” are not a vision of the future — they are the **literary residue of the Türgesh wars Saʿīd b. Jumhān lived through**, projected onto his home city and lifted onto the lips of the Prophet. The single worst of those defeats, **Yawm al-Shiʿb** (“the Day of the Defile,” 113/731) under al-Junayd b. ʿAbd al-Raḥmān, in which the Turks nearly annihilated a Khurāsānī army largely raised from Baṣra and Kūfa, is exactly the kind of trauma that makes “the Turks will overrun us” feel like prophecy waiting to happen.[^turgesh]

## It was never Baghdad — it was Baṣra

The report names **al-Baṣra**, repeatedly and explicitly, and locates it correctly: a garrison-city of the Muhājirūn on the Tigris with a bridge. Baghdad is a different city in a different place, and it did not yet exist when the saying was circulating — it was founded by al-Manṣūr in 145/762, **after Saʿīd b. Jumhān was already dead (d. 136)**. To read “Baṣra” as “Baghdad” is not interpretation; it is substitution. The apologetic move of folding Baghdad into “Baṣra’s hinterland” has no textual basis: the text gives a named city, on a named river, with a named feature (the bridge), described as one of the original Muslim *amṣār* — and that is Baṣra.

## An elastic label, reapplied again and again

The reason “Banū Qanṭūrāʾ” can be made to “predict” the Mongols is that it was never a fixed referent. It was a movable label for whatever Turkic or steppe threat was current, and Muslim writers openly slid it from one enemy to the next:

A term that is reassigned to the Türgesh, then the palace Turks, then the Zanj, then the Mongols is not a precise prediction. It is a flexible cipher, and the 1258 reading is just its latest tenant — applied centuries after the fact.

## The 1258 reading collapses on its own details

Set aside even all of the above, and grant the apologist his city-swap. The “fulfillment” still fails, because the hadith’s specific picture is the **opposite** of what the chronicles say happened in 656/1258. The report describes a slow, single-front, negotiable confrontation in which the defenders have the leisure to sort themselves into three deliberate fates and the fighting remnant is **delivered by God**. The sack of Baghdad was a rapid, total, multi-front catastrophe in which the caliphate was annihilated and there was no victorious remnant at all.

  <th slot="header">What the hadith says</th>
  <th slot="header">What actually happened in 656/1258</th>
  <tr>

  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td>“A group that takes safety for themselves” — i.e., some survive by seeking terms.</td>

  </tr>

    <td>There was no victorious remnant. The caliph was killed, his sons were killed, a third son and his daughters were captured, the office of the caliphate fell vacant, and the ʿAbbāsid line in Baghdad ended outright.</td>
  </tr>

That last contradiction is fatal. The whole rhetorical payload of the hadith is the promise that the believers’ remnant is **rescued and granted victory**. The reality of 1258 is the precise inverse — a terminal defeat that extinguished the caliphate. Ibn Kathīr, recording the event, states it without consolation:

<HadithBlock label="The Reality of 656/1258" source="Ibn Kathīr, al-Bidāya wa-l-Nihāya, events of 656 AH (death of al-Mustaʿsim)">
  <div class="hadith-block__arabic" dir="rtl" lang="ar">
    قَتَلَتْهُ التَّتَارُ مَظْلُومًا مُضْطَهَدًا … وَقَدْ قُتِلَ بَعْدَهُ وَلَدَاهُ، وَأُسِرَ الثَّالِثُ مَعَ بَنَاتٍ ثَلَاثٍ مِنْ صُلْبِهِ، وَشَغَرَ مَنْصِبُ الْخِلَافَةِ بَعْدَهُ، وَلَمْ يَبْقَ فِي بَنِي الْعَبَّاسِ مَنْ سَدَّ مَسَدَّهُ.
  </div>
  <div class="hadith-block__translation">
    “The Tatars killed him, wronged and oppressed … After him his two sons were killed, the third was captured along with three of his daughters, the office of the caliphate fell vacant, and there remained no one among the ʿAbbāsids to fill his place.”
  </div>
</HadithBlock>

A caliph executed, his sons slaughtered, the dynasty erased — this is not “God grants victory to the remnant.” The hadith and the history do not merely fail to match; they point in opposite directions.[^baghdad1258]

## Verdict

<VerdictBox title="Historical Verdict">
The Banū Qanṭūrāʾ report is not a prophecy of the Mongol sack of Baghdad. Its earliest layer is a non-prophetic saying of ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿAmr that the Turks would drive the people of Iraq out of Baṣra. The single Baṣran transmitter Saʿīd b. Jumhān — criticized in the rijāl literature for solitary, peculiar reports, and unable to keep his own informant’s name stable — added the three-groups motif and raised the saying to the Prophet. Every concrete feature of the report belongs to his own world: Baṣra on the Tigris, the Turks (“Banū Qanṭūrāʾ”) of the Türgesh wars, and the fragmentation of armies he could observe in the chronicles of Transoxiana. The “Baghdad” reading swaps the named city for a different one founded after Saʿīd’s death; the “Mongol” reading rides an elastic label that was reassigned from the Türgesh to the palace Turks to the Zanj before it ever reached the Mongols; and the 1258 “fulfillment” contradicts the hadith’s own details — a single-front descent versus an all-sides storm, an orderly three-way split versus a rapid massacre, and above all a promised victorious remnant versus the total extinction of the caliphate.
</VerdictBox>
