## Canonical doubts from within the tradition

The doubt in these attributions isn't some fringe theory; it comes from transmitters of hadith and major traditional scholars.

<div class="hc-fig hc-fig--c2">
  <article class="hc-fig__item">
    <p class="hc-fig__kicker">Al-Albānī</p>
    <h3>“He did not leave behind any books.”</h3>
    <p>
      In tape 52 of <em>Al-Huda wa Al-Nur</em>, Al-Albānī states: "Considering his early period (d. 150 AH), he did not leave behind any books, but he left behind students. The book attributed to him [Fiqh Al-Akbar] represents the views of those <em>affiliated</em> with Abu Hanifa. Its attribution to Imam Abu Hanifa is not correct."
    </p>
  </article>
  <article class="hc-fig__item">
    <p class="hc-fig__kicker">Anwar Shāh Kashmīrī</p>
    <h3>“They cannot be definitively attributed.”</h3>
    <p>
      Kashmīrī notes that the discussion of increase and decrease in faith was later added. He reviewed several copies of <em>Fiqh al-Akbar</em>, <em>Al-‘Alim wal-Muta‘allim</em>, and <em>Ar-Risalah</em>—all differing in wording and content. He concluded: "The correct view is that they are not his own writings."
    </p>
  </article>
</div>

## Book 1: Fiqh al-Akbar II — The Theological Anachronisms

There is a severe theological tension within *Fiqh al-Akbar* that creates a crisis for those who adhere to Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal's strict traditionalism. The central issue revolves around **God’s Actions** and **God’s Speech**.

<div class="hc-fig hc-fig--cols">
  <article class="hc-fig__item">
    <span class="hc-fig__kicker">01</span>
    <h3>God's Actions</h3>
    <p><strong>Ahmad:</strong> God's actions happen in time.<br/><strong>FA II:</strong> God's actions (like creation) are timeless and pre-eternal.</p>
  </article>
  <article class="hc-fig__item">
    <span class="hc-fig__kicker">02</span>
    <h3>God's Speech</h3>
    <p><strong>Ahmad:</strong> Takfir on anyone who says Quranic letters are created.<br/><strong>FA II:</strong> "Letters are created, but the speech of God is not created."</p>
  </article>
  <article class="hc-fig__item">
    <span class="hc-fig__kicker">03</span>
    <h3>The Dilemma</h3>
    <p>If FA II is authentic, a strict Hanbali must logically declare takfir on Abu Hanifa's stated creed.</p>
  </article>
  <article class="hc-fig__item">
    <span class="hc-fig__kicker">04</span>
    <h3>The Reality</h3>
    <p>The text relies on advanced 4th-century Kalam, revealing it as a later anachronistic retrojection.</p>
  </article>
</div>

<ClaimBox title="The Takfir Collision">
  Imam Ahmad explicitly declared takfir on the belief that actions like creation are eternal, and he equated the belief that "letters are created" to the Jahmi position. If *Fiqh al-Akbar II* authentically represents Abu Hanifa's views, then Abu Hanifa's theological stance falls precisely into the category that Imam Ahmad condemned as disbelief.
</ClaimBox>

## The Transmission Problem & ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz al-Bukhārī

The theological sophistication of *Fiqh al-Akbar II* (FA II)—affirming the Quran as uncreated while explicitly rejecting anthropomorphic implications through sounds and letters—reflects the refined discourse of the 3rd/4th centuries (Ashʿarī/Maturidi development). It was far too advanced for Abū Ḥanīfa's time in the 2nd century.

The *Musnad* of Abu Hanifa was compiled by **Abu Muhammad Abdullah ibn Muhammad ibn Yaqub al-Harithi**. While he expended great effort, the work is saturated with fabrications.

## The Narrator Dilemma and the "Unknown" Gap

This creates a systemic vulnerability. With hundreds of subsequent unknown transmitters, it became incredibly easy to fabricate chains back to Abu Hanifa. 

<ContextNote title="Methodological Contradiction">
  Ibn Khaldun asserted that Abu Hanifa transmitted **only 17 hadith reports**. Furthermore, Ibn Abi Shayba dedicated a section refuting Abu Hanifa's doctrine on 125 questions specifically because his rationalist positions contradicted hadith. The sudden emergence of extensive hadith collections (Musnads) attributed to him centuries later is a transparent attempt to "traditionalize" his legacy.
</ContextNote>

<VerdictBox title="Conclusion">
  The statistical patterns in transmission chains, historical contradictions, 500-year gaps in text reception, and severe theological anachronisms collectively demonstrate that the works and Musnads attributed to Abu Hanifa are largely later constructions. They do not authentically represent his original teachings, but rather the later evolution and retro-fitting of the Hanafi school.
</VerdictBox>
