*How a Companion tested reports by their content, and why a reliable narrator was never the end of the question.*

## A test that the chain cannot run

Muslim hadith scholarship built its most famous tool around one question: who carried this report? If the Companion was honest and the chain was sound, the report earned trust. That tool is powerful, but it answers only one question. It tells us that a reliable person transmitted these words. It does not tell us that the words accurately represent Muḥammad or God's revelation.

ʿĀʾisha's recorded objections show a second test. In at least ten reported disputes, she answered a competing narration or interpretation by quoting the Qur’an. She did not accuse the narrator of lying, and she did not cite a broken chain. She named a verse.

This is the historical question we should begin with. Rather than asking whether each confrontation can be proven word for word, we need to ask what the surviving texts show her doing, and what standard she applied. **In the strongest cases, ʿĀʾisha judged the content of a report against the Qur’an and left the narrator's standing untouched. The Qur’an functioned as the criterion, and the transmitter's reliability did not override it.**[^1]

## The cleanest case: mourning and the burden of another's sin

The clearest example sits inside one narration, so both sides are preserved together. At the funeral of one of ʿUthmān's daughters in Mecca, Ibn ʿUmar urged ʿAmr b. ʿUthmān to stop the weeping, reporting that the Prophet said the dead are punished by their relatives' crying. Ibn ʿAbbās answered that ʿUmar used to say something similar, then reported how ʿĀʾisha responded when he told her of it after ʿUmar's death:

<HadithBlock source="Ṣaḥīḥ, al-Bukhārī, nos. 1286–1288 (continuous report; excerpt)" url="https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1286">
<div class="hc-report__en">

“When ʿUmar died I told all this to Aisha and she said, ‘May Allah be merciful to ʿUmar. By Allah, Messenger of Allah ﷺ did not say that a believer is punished by the weeping of his relatives. But he said, Allah increases the punishment of a non-believer because of the weeping of his relatives.’ Aisha further added, ‘The Qur'an is sufficient for you (to clear up this point) as Allah has stated: No burdened soul will bear another's burden.’ (35.18).”[^2]

</div>
<div class="hc-report__ar" lang="ar" dir="rtl">

**<Arabic>فَقَالَتْ رَحِمَ اللَّهُ عُمَرَ وَاللَّهِ مَا حَدَّثَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَيُعَذِّبُ الْمُؤْمِنَ بِبُكَاءِ أَهْلِهِ عَلَيْهِ وَلَكِنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَالَ إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَيَزِيدُ الْكَافِرَ عَذَابًا بِبُكَاءِ أَهْلِهِ عَلَيْهِ وَقَالَتْ حَسْبُكُمُ الْقُرْآنُ &#123;وَلاَ تَزِرُ وَازِرَةٌ وِزْرَ أُخْرَى&#125;</Arabic>.**

</div>
</HadithBlock>

Notice the order of her moves. She begins with mercy for ʿUmar, so she protects his character. She then denies the *wording* he and his son transmitted. She supplies a narrower statement she says the Prophet actually made. Finally she grounds the whole correction in 35:18. The verse works as a test that the report's broad form fails and the narrower form passes.

The ending is just as informative. Ibn ʿAbbās replied with a verse-like line, “It is Allah who makes one laugh and weep,” and Ibn ʿUmar said nothing more.[^3] A Companion of Ibn ʿUmar's rank was not accused of anything. He simply had no answer to the verse.

## Hearing and knowing: the dead at Badr

The second case shows a different kind of correction. Bukhārī 3980 reports that the Prophet stood at the well of Badr and addressed the slain Meccans. One transmission has him say that they now *hear* what he says:

<HadithBlock source="Ṣaḥīḥ, al-Bukhārī, no. 3980" url="https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/64/8">
<div class="hc-report__en">

“The Prophet stood at the well of Badr, where he said, ‘Have you found what your Lord promised to be true?’ And he then said, ‘They now hear what I say.’ This was mentioned before ʿĀʾisha. She responded: ‘Rather, the Prophet said, “They now know that what I used to tell them was the truth.”’ Then she recited the verse, ‘You cannot make the dead hear,’ continuing through its end.”[^4]

</div>
<div class="hc-report__ar" lang="ar" dir="rtl">

**<Arabic>وَقَفَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ عَلَى قَلِيبِ بَدْرٍ فَقَالَ &#123;هَلْ وَجَدْتُمْ مَا وَعَدَ رَبُّكُمْ حَقًّا ثُمَّ قَالَ إِنَّهُمُ الآنَ يَسْمَعُونَ مَا أَقُولُ&#125; فَذُكِرَ لِعَائِشَةَ فَقَالَتْ إِنَّمَا قَالَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ إِنَّهُمُ الآنَ لَيَعْلَمُونَ أَنَّ الَّذِي كُنْتُ أَقُولُ لَهُمْ هُوَ الْحَقُّ ثُمَّ قَرَأَتْ &#123;إِنَّكَ لاَ تُسْمِعُ الْمَوْتَى&#125; حَتَّى قَرَأَتِ الآيَةَ</Arabic>.**

</div>
</HadithBlock>

She did not deny the event. She agreed the Prophet stood at the well and spoke. What she disputed was one verb. The report said *yasmaʿūna* (“they hear”), and she held that he said *laya ʿlamūna* (“they know”). The verse she cited, 27:80, denies that the Prophet can make the dead hear. A single word carried the entire disagreement, and the Qur’an decided it.

This is a different failure from the funeral case. In the funeral case a narrow statement had been widened. At Badr a wording had shifted. In both, the narrator could have been sincere throughout.

## Context lost: omens in a woman, a mount and a house

The third failure is a missing frame. According to Aḥmad 26088, two men told ʿĀʾisha that Abū Hurayra narrated a saying that ill omen lies in a woman, a riding animal and a house. The report says she was intensely angry:

<HadithBlock source="al-Musnad, Aḥmad b. Ḥanbal, no. 26088" url="https://hadithunlocked.com/ahmad/15.01">
<div class="hc-report__en">

“Two men entered upon ʿĀʾisha and said: ‘Abū Hurayra narrates that the Prophet said ill omens are in a woman, riding beast, and house.’ She became intensely angry and said: ‘By the One who sent the Qur’an down to Abū al-Qāsim, he did not say it like this! Rather, he used to say: “The people of the pre-Islamic period claimed that omens were in a woman, riding animal, and house.”’ Then she recited: ‘No calamity befalls the earth or yourselves except that it is in a record...’ [Q 57:22].”[^5]

</div>
<div class="hc-report__ar" lang="ar" dir="rtl">

**<Arabic>أَنَّ رَجُلَيْنِ دَخَلَا عَلَى عَائِشَةَ ، فَقَالَا : إِنَّ أَبَا هُرَيْرَةَ يُحَدِّثُ أَنَّ نَبِيَّ اللهِ ﷺ كَانَ يَقُولُ : إِنَّمَا الطِّيَرَةُ فِي الْمَرْأَةِ وَالدَّابَّةِ وَالدَّارِ . قَالَ : فَطَارَتْ شُقَّةٌ مِنْهَا فِي السَّمَاءِ ، وَشُقَّةٌ فِي الْأَرْضِ فَقَالَتْ : وَالَّذِي أَنْزَلَ الْقُرْآنَ عَلَى أَبِي الْقَاسِمِ ، مَا هَكَذَا كَانَ يَقُولُ ! وَلَكِنَّ نَبِيَّ اللهِ ﷺ كَانَ يَقُولُ : كَانَ أَهْلُ الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ يَقُولُونَ : الطِّيَرَةُ فِي الْمَرْأَةِ وَالدَّارِ وَالدَّابَّةِ ، ثُمَّ قَرَأَتْ عَائِشَةُ مَا أَصَابَ مِنْ مُصِيبَةٍ فِي الأَرْضِ وَلا فِي أَنْفُسِكُمْ إِلا فِي كِتَابٍ إِلَى آخِرِ الْآيَةِ</Arabic>.**

</div>
</HadithBlock>

The point is the logic of her correction. On her account, the Prophet reported what the people of the pre-Islamic period said, and Abū Hurayra transmitted the claim without the framing clause. Verse 57:22 states that every calamity is recorded before it occurs, which leaves no room for a woman or a house to cause one. She did not need to call Abū Hurayra a liar. She needed to show that the report, as stripped of its frame, contradicts the verse.

A caution applies here. Muslim 2225a preserves an affirmative formula (“If there is bad luck, it is in the house, the wife, and the horse”), but it runs through a different line and is not necessarily the report she heard.[^6] The two sides should be compared by chain before they are treated as one dispute.

## Three objections, one report: Bukhārī 4855

Three of the ten topics come from a single narration. A questioner asks ʿĀʾisha whether Muḥammad saw his Lord. Her reply, in Bukhārī 4855, gives three claims and a verse for each:

<HadithBlock source="Ṣaḥīḥ, al-Bukhārī, no. 4855" url="https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/65/53/1">
<div class="hc-report__en">

“I said to ʿAisha, ‘O Mother! Did Prophet Muhammad see his Lord?’ Aisha said, ‘What you have said makes my hair stand on end! Know that if somebody tells you one of the following three things, he is a liar: whoever tells you that Muhammad saw his Lord is a liar.’ Then Aisha recited: ‘No vision can grasp Him, but His grasp is over all vision. He is the Most Courteous, Well-Acquainted with all things’ (6:103) and ‘It is not fitting for a human being that Allah should speak to him except by inspiration or from behind a veil’ (42:51). ʿAisha further said, ‘Whoever tells you that the Prophet knows what is going to happen tomorrow is a liar.’ She then recited, ‘No soul can know what it will earn tomorrow’ (31:34). She added: ‘Whoever tells you that he concealed (some of Allah's orders) is a liar.’ Then she recited: ‘O Apostle! Proclaim the Message which has been sent down to you from your Lord’ (5:67). ʿAisha added: ‘But the Prophet ﷺ saw Gabriel in his true form twice.’”[^7]

</div>
<div class="hc-report__ar" lang="ar" dir="rtl">

**<Arabic>قُلْتُ لِعَائِشَةَ ؓ يَا أُمَّتَاهْ هَلْ رَأَى مُحَمَّدٌ ﷺ رَبَّهُ فَقَالَتْ لَقَدْ قَفَّ شَعَرِي مِمَّا قُلْتَ أَيْنَ أَنْتَ مِنْ ثَلاَثٍ مَنْ حَدَّثَكَهُنَّ فَقَدْ كَذَبَ مَنْ حَدَّثَكَ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا ﷺ رَأَى رَبَّهُ فَقَدْ كَذَبَ ثُمَّ قَرَأَتْ &#123;لاَ تُدْرِكُهُ الأَبْصَارُ وَهُوَ يُدْرِكُ الأَبْصَارَ وَهُوَ اللَّطِيفُ الْخَبِيرُ&#125; &#123;وَمَا كَانَ لِبَشَرٍ أَنْ يُكَلِّمَهُ اللَّهُ إِلاَّ وَحْيًا أَوْ مِنْ وَرَاءِ حِجَابٍ&#125; وَمَنْ حَدَّثَكَ أَنَّهُ يَعْلَمُ مَا فِي غَدٍ فَقَدْ كَذَبَ ثُمَّ قَرَأَتْ &#123;وَمَا تَدْرِي نَفْسٌ مَاذَا تَكْسِبُ غَدًا&#125; وَمَنْ حَدَّثَكَ أَنَّهُ كَتَمَ فَقَدْ كَذَبَ ثُمَّ قَرَأَتْ &#123;يَا أَيُّهَا الرَّسُولُ بَلِّغْ مَا أُنْزِلَ إِلَيْكَ مِنْ رَبِّكَ&#125; الآيَةَ وَلَكِنَّهُ رَأَى جِبْرِيلَ عَلَيْهِ السَّلاَمُ فِي صُورَتِهِ مَرَّتَيْنِ</Arabic>.**

</div>
</HadithBlock>

Two points matter for dating and weighing this evidence. First, the three objections are one encounter. The register indexes them separately by claim, but they should not be counted as three independent incidents. Second, here she does use the word “liar” (*kadhaba*), but she aims it at *whoever transmits the claim* in the form she rejects, and she grounds each denial in a verse. The structure is still claim, verse, correction.

The comparison needs one qualification. Muslim 176a ascribes to Ibn ʿAbbās a vision “with his heart,” which is not obviously a claim of sight by the eyes.[^8] The cleanest contradiction is with the flat claim that Muḥammad saw his Lord. Treating Ibn ʿAbbās's formulation as a point-for-point opponent would overstate the disagreement.

## Separate transmissions: the child born of adultery

The case of the child born of adultery shows why the type of evidence matters. Abū Dāwūd 3963 records the saying, “The child of adultery is the worst of the three,” together with a statement attributed to Abū Hurayra that he would rather give a whip for God's cause than free such a child. Separately, Ibn Abī Shayba 6150 records ʿĀʾisha's answer when asked about such a child:

<HadithBlock source="al-Muṣannaf, Ibn Abī Shayba, no. 6150" url="https://hadithunlocked.com/ibnabishaybah/6/26">
<div class="hc-report__en">

“When ʿĀʾisha was asked about a child born of adultery, she answered: ‘None of the wrongdoing of his parents falls upon him: “No bearer of burdens bears another's burden.”’”[^9]

</div>
<div class="hc-report__ar" lang="ar" dir="rtl">

**<Arabic>كَانَتْ إِذَا سُئِلَتْ عَنْ وَلَدِ الزِّنَا ؟ قَالَتْ : لَيْسَ عَلَيْهِ مِنْ خَطِيئَةِ أَبَوَيْهِ شَيْءٌ ، ﴿وَلا تَزِرُ وَازِرَةٌ وِزْرَ أُخْرَى﴾</Arabic>.**

</div>
</HadithBlock>

ʿAbd al-Razzāq 13930 preserves a shorter parallel.[^10] These are two independent statements, and they are the stronger evidence. A longer composite in al-Ḥākim 2855 stages a face-to-face correction of Abū Hurayra, with ʿĀʾisha supplying occasions for each of three sayings and citing 6:164-type language and 2:286. Its transmission is disputed, including the figure of Salama b. al-Faḍl, and its “it reached ʿĀʾisha” wording leaves the encounter itself uncertain.[^11] I therefore do not rest any claim on the composite. The independently transmitted statement carries the argument without it.

## The same standard applied to readings of the Qur’an

If the Qur’an is the criterion, then ʿĀʾisha should apply it even to people interpreting the Qur’an. She did. These cases differ in kind: the dispute is over how a passage is read, not over a prophetic hadith.

**Ṣafā and Marwa.** ʿUrwa b. al-Zubayr, a Successor, reasoned from 2:158 that omitting the walk between the two hills would not harm a pilgrim. She answered from the verse's grammar and its occasion:

<HadithBlock source="Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, no. 1277a (excerpt)" url="https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim/15/43">
<div class="hc-report__en">

“Allah has not completed the Hajj or Umrah of any person who did not go between al-Safa and al-Marwah. If it were as you say, it would have been: ‘There is no blame upon him if he does not go between them.’ Do you know concerning what that was? It was only that the Ansar, in the time of Jahiliyyah, used to raise the talbiyah for two idols on the shore of the sea called Isaf and Nailah ... When Islam came, they disliked going between them because of what they used to do in Jahiliyyah.”[^12]

</div>
<div class="hc-report__ar" lang="ar" dir="rtl">

**<Arabic>مَا أَتَمَّ اللَّهُ حَجَّ امْرِئٍ وَلاَ عُمْرَتَهُ لَمْ يَطُفْ بَيْنَ الصَّفَا وَالْمَرْوَةِ، وَلَوْ كَانَ كَمَا تَقُولُ لَكَانَ: فَلاَ جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِ أَنْ لاَ يَطَّوَّفَ بِهِمَا، وَهَلْ تَدْرِي فِيمَا كَانَ ذَاكَ؟ إِنَّمَا كَانَ ذَاكَ أَنَّ الأَنْصَارَ كَانُوا يُهِلُّونَ فِي الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ لِصَنَمَيْنِ عَلَى شَطِّ الْبَحْرِ يُقَالُ لَهُمَا إِسَافٌ وَنَائِلَةٌ … فَلَمَّا جَاءَ الإِسْلاَمُ كَرِهُوا أَنْ يَطُوفُوا بَيْنَهُمَا لِلَّذِي كَانُوا يَصْنَعُونَ فِي الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ</Arabic>**

</div>
</HadithBlock>

She tests his inference against the verse's own wording (what it would say if it meant what he claimed), then restores the occasion that explains the “no blame” phrasing.

**Temporary marriage.** Asked about *mutʿa*, she answered with a sentence that states the principle outright:

<HadithBlock source="al-Sunan al-Kubrā, al-Bayhaqī, no. 14286" url="https://hadithunlocked.com/bayhaqi/40/12/2">
<div class="hc-report__en">

“ʿĀʾisha was asked about temporary marriage with women. She replied, ‘Between me and them is the Book of God, Mighty and Exalted.’ She recited: ‘And those who guard their private parts, except with their spouses or those whom their right hands possess, then they are not blameworthy. Whoever seeks beyond that—those are the transgressors.’”[^13]

</div>
<div class="hc-report__ar" lang="ar" dir="rtl">

**<Arabic>سُئِلَتْ عَائِشَةُ ؓ عَنْ مُتْعَةِ النِّسَاءِ فَقَالَتْ بَيْنِي وَبَيْنَهُمْ كِتَابُ اللهِ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ وَقَرَأَتْ هَذِهِ الْآيَةَ وَالَّذِينَ هُمْ لِفُرُوجِهِمْ حَافِظُونَ إِلَّا عَلَى أَزْوَاجِهِمْ أَوْ مَا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَانُهُمْ فَإِنَّهُمْ غَيْرُ مَلُومِينَ فَمَنِ ابْتَغَى وَرَاءَ ذَلِكَ فَأُولَئِكَ هُمُ الْعَادُونَ</Arabic>.**

</div>
</HadithBlock>

“Between me and them is the Book of God” is the closest the corpus comes to a stated method. The record names no opposing Companion in this report, so it should not be assigned to one.

**Q 12:110.** The disagreement here is over a single vocalization. Ibn ʿAbbās read *kudhibū* (without doubling the consonant). ʿĀʾisha rejected the implication that God's promise to his messengers could fail:

<HadithBlock source="Ṣaḥīḥ, al-Bukhārī, nos. 4524–4525 (excerpt)" url="https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4524">
<div class="hc-report__en">

“He said: ‘ʿĀʾisha said: “God forbid! By God, God never promised His messenger anything without his knowing that it would occur before his death. But hardship continued to afflict the messengers until they feared their followers might accuse them of lying.”’ She used to recite the verse with kudhdhibū, doubling the consonant.”[^14]

</div>
<div class="hc-report__ar" lang="ar" dir="rtl">

**<Arabic>فَقَالَ قَالَتْ عَائِشَةُ مَعَاذَ اللَّهِ وَاللَّهِ مَا وَعَدَ اللَّهُ رَسُولَهُ مِنْ شَىْءٍ قَطُّ إِلاَّ عَلِمَ أَنَّهُ كَائِنٌ قَبْلَ أَنْ يَمُوتَ وَلَكِنْ لَمْ يَزَلِ الْبَلاَءُ بِالرُّسُلِ حَتَّى خَافُوا أَنْ يَكُونَ مَنْ مَعَهُمْ يُكَذِّبُونَهُمْ فَكَانَتْ تَقْرَؤُهَا &#123;وَظَنُّوا أَنَّهُمْ قَدْ كُذِّبُوا&#125; مُثَقَّلَةً</Arabic>.**

</div>
</HadithBlock>

This case matters because she cites no *different* verse. She argues from what God's promise to his messengers can mean. That fits the pattern. Her authority was the text and its internal logic, and not her own standing or an appeal to another report.

Three failure modes appear repeatedly, and none requires bad faith from the narrator. A saying can be **widened** past its original scope (the funeral report). A word can **shift** during transmission (Badr). A frame can be **dropped** (omens). Each of these can happen to a sincere, accurate-minded transmitter, which is why the chain cannot detect them.

Notice also what she does not do. In no case above does she attack the narrator's honesty. Where she uses the word “liar” (Bukhārī 4855), she applies it to those transmitting a specific claim, and the claim itself is what the verse contradicts. This is the difference between **matn criticism by the Qur’an** and **isnād criticism by character**.

## Objections worth taking seriously

Three objections deserve a direct answer.

**The register is not a transcript.** These reports are preserved in later collections, and the compilation itself warns that it is a source register and not an endorsement of every confrontation's historicity. The strongest cases (Bukhārī 1286–1288, 3980, 4855) are well attested. Others carry the qualifications marked above. The argument here rests on the strongest ones.

**Classical scholars harmonized.** Many argued that the funeral saying applies to a family that bequeathed wailing as custom, or that the dead at Badr heard in a special sense. These are serious readings, and they show that scholars did not simply side with ʿĀʾisha. A reader can accept her method without accepting that she was right in every instance.

**She was doing hadith criticism, not demoting hadith.** She often supplied what she said the Prophet actually said, so one can argue she replaced one report with another. That is partly right. But her stated ground was the Qur’an each time, and the trigger for her objection was the conflict with a verse. A competing report alone would not have produced the phrase “the Qur’an is sufficient for you.”

<VerdictBox title="Conclusion">

The surviving texts support a modest but real claim. In the best-attested disputes, ʿĀʾisha tested the **content** of a report against the Qur’an and treated the narrator's reliability as a separate question. She restricted a widened saying (Bukhārī 1286–1288), restored a changed word (Bukhārī 3980), restored a dropped context (Aḥmad 26088), and rejected claims that clash with verses (Bukhārī 4855). She also applied the same text-first standard to readings of the Qur’an itself (Muslim 1277a, al-Bayhaqī 14286, Bukhārī 4524–4525).

What this shows is **not** that Companions were careless, and not that hadith should be discarded. It shows that trustworthy transmission and accurate content are different questions, and that one of the people closest to Muḥammad's household asked the content question first. A chain of reliable narrators tells us who spoke. It does not settle whether the words fit revelation.

The disputed composite in al-Ḥākim 2855 and the unnamed opponents in the *mutʿa* case limit how far the evidence reaches. They do not limit the pattern in the cleanest cases, where ʿĀʾisha, a verse, and a report's content are all that is in view.

</VerdictBox>
