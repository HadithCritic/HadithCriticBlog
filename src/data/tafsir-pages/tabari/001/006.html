On “Guide us”

Abū Jaʿfar said: al-Ṭabarī’s own explanation The meaning of His words Guide us to the straight path in this place, in our view, is: grant us success in remaining steadfast in it. As it has been reported (8) of this from Ibn ʿAbbās.

Transmitted report: Abū Kurayb related to us: ʿUthmān b. Saʿīd told us: Bishr b. ʿUmāra told us: Abū Rawq told us, from al-Ḍaḥḥāk, from ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿAbbās, who said: Gabriel said to Muḥammad: Say, O Muḥammad: Guide us to the straight path . He says (1) : Inspire us with the guiding way (2) . And His inspiring him with that is His granting him success, as we have said in its interpretation. Its meaning is like the meaning of His words and You alone we ask for help , in that it is the servant's petition to his Lord for success in remaining steadfast in acting in obedience to Him, and in hitting upon the truth and the right in what He commanded him and forbade him, in what lies ahead of his lifetime, not what has already passed of his deeds and been concluded in what is past of his life, just as His words and You alone we ask for help are a petition from him to his Lord for help in performing what He has charged him with of obedience in the remainder of his lifetime. So the meaning of the speech is: O God, You alone we worship, alone, with no partner for You, devoting worship to You exclusively, to the exclusion of any other gods and idols; so help us in Your worship, and grant us success in what You granted success to those You have favoured among Your prophets and the people of obedience to You, of the paths (3) and the way.

If someone says: And where did you find hidāya in the speech of the Arabs with the meaning of tawfīq (granting success)? [It is said to him] (4) : That is in their speech more frequent and more manifest than that the number of attestations that have come from them for it could be counted; of this is the verse of the poet (5) : Do not deny me — may God guide you — my request, and let me not be like one whom travel has destroyed meaning (1) : may God grant you success in fulfilling my need. And of this is the verse of another (2) : [Do not hasten me] (3) — may the King guide you — for every occasion has its fitting word. So it is known that he only meant: may God grant you success in hitting upon the truth in my affair. And of this are the words of God, Mighty and Majestic: and God does not guide the wrongdoing people [al-Baqara 2:258; Āl ʿImrān 3:86; al-Tawba 9:19, 109; al-Ṣaff 61:7; al-Jumuʿa 62:5], in more than one verse of His revelation.

And it is known by that that He did not mean that He does not make clear to the wrongdoers what is obligatory on them of His obligations. And how could that be its meaning, when He has covered with explanation all those under obligation among His creatures? Rather, He, exalted be His mention, meant that He does not grant them success and does not open their breasts to the truth and to faith. And some of them have claimed that the interpretation of His words Guide us is: increase us in guidance. This statement cannot be free of one of two things: either its sayer supposed that the Prophet, ﷺ, was commanded [to petition his Lord for] (4) an increase in explanation, or (5) an increase in help and success. If he supposed that he was commanded to petition (6) for an increase in explanation, that has no ground, because God, majestic is His praise, does not charge a servant with any of His obligations except after making it clear to him and establishing the proof against him by it; and if the meaning of that were the meaning of petitioning for explanation, he would have been commanded to call upon his Lord to make clear to him what He had made obligatory on him, and that in supplication is defective, because He does not impose an obligation except made clear to the one on whom He imposes it; or else he was commanded to call upon his Lord to impose on him the obligations that He had not imposed.

And in the invalidity of this line of the servant's petition to his Lord there is what makes plain that the meaning of Guide us to the straight path is other than: make clear to us Your obligations and Your limits. Or else he supposed that he was commanded to petition his Lord for an increase in help and success; if that is so, then his petition for that increase cannot be free of being a petition for an increase of help with regard to what has already passed of his deeds, or with regard to what will occur; and in the removal of the servant's need for help with regard to what has been concluded of his deeds there is what shows that the meaning of the petition for that increase is only his petition for an increase for what will occur of his deeds. And since that is so, the matter comes back to what we described and said on this, that it is the servant's petition to his Lord for success in performing what he is charged with of [his Lord's obligations] (2) in what lies ahead of his lifetime.

And in the soundness of that is the refutation of the statement (3) of the people of qadar [the Qadariyya], who claim that everyone commanded with a command or charged with an obligation has been given of help for it that which removes, as to that obligation, his need of his Lord; for if the matter were as they say, the meaning of God's words, majestic is His praise: You alone we worship and You alone we ask for help (5) Guide us to the straight path would be void. And in the soundness of its meaning as we have explained lies the refutation of their statement. And some of them have claimed that the meaning of His words Guide us to the straight path is: make us travel the path of Paradise in the Return; that is: send us forward to it and proceed with us to it. As He, majestic is His praise, said: then guide them to the path of Hell [al-Ṣāffāt 37:23], that is: make them enter the Fire, as a bride is led (tuhdā) to her husband, meaning that she is made to enter to him, and as a gift is sent (tuhdā) to a man, and as the foot guides (tahdī) the leg, the like of the verse of Ṭarafa b. al-ʿAbd (1) : The torrents played with it after me, and its drizzle (2) ran in splendour / A man has an intellect by which he lives, wherever his foot guides his leg — that is: it brings him to the watering places.

And in the words of God, majestic is His praise, You alone we worship and You alone we ask for help there is that which informs of the error of this interpretation, besides the witness of the authority of the exegetes against its error; for all the exegetes among the Companions and the Successors are agreed that the meaning of the path in this place is other than the meaning that the sayer of this statement interpreted, and that His words and You alone we ask for help are the servant's petition to his Lord for help in his worship; so likewise His words Guide us are only his petition (3) for steadfastness in guidance in what remains of his lifetime. And the Arabs say: hadaytu fulānan al-ṭarīq, hadaytuhu li-l-ṭarīq, and hadaytuhu ilā al-ṭarīq: when you have directed him to it (4) and set him straight for it. And with all of that the Qurʾān has (5) come. God, majestic is His praise, said: and they said: Praise be to God who guided us to this [al-Aʿrāf 7:43].

And He said in another place: He chose him and guided him to a straight path [al-Naḥl 16:121].

Poetic evidence / as rendered in the source And He said: Guide us to the straight path . And all of that is widespread in their speech, found in their discourse; of this is the verse of the poet (1) : I seek God's forgiveness for a sin I cannot count, the Lord of the servants, to whom are the face and the deed.

He means: I seek God's forgiveness for a sin (li-dhanbin). As He, majestic is His praise, said: and ask forgiveness for your sin [Ghāfir 40:55]. And of this is the verse of al-Nābigha of Banū Dhubyān (2) : So he hunts for us the wild ass (3) , swaggering in his running (4) , before weariness, and the ashʿab (5) , the barker. He means: fa-yaṣīdu lanā (so he hunts for us). And this is abundant in their poetry and speech, and what we have mentioned of it suffices. And God is the one who grants success.

On “the straight path ”

Abū Jaʿfar said: al-Ṭabarī’s own explanation The authority (6) of the people of interpretation, all together, is agreed that the straight path is the clear way in which there is no crookedness; and likewise that (7) is so in the language of all the Arabs. Of this is the verse of Jarīr b. ʿAṭiyya al-Khaṭafā (8) : The Commander of the Faithful is on a path, straight, when the watering places go crooked He means: on the way of truth. And of this is the verse of the Hudhalī Abū Dhuʾayb (1) : We came upon their land in the morning with horses until we left it thinner than the ṣirāṭ. And of this is the verse of the rajaz poet (2) : And he turned away from the course of the straight (qāṣid) path (3) . And the attestations of this are more than can be counted, and what we have mentioned suffices in place of what we have left. Then the Arabs use ṣirāṭ metaphorically and employ it for every statement and action described as straight or crooked, describing the straight by its straightness and the crooked by its crookedness. And what is more fitting as the interpretation of this verse, in my view, I mean (4) Guide us to the straight path , is that what is meant by it is: grant us success in remaining steadfast in what You have approved and granted success in to those You have favoured among Your servants, of statement and action; and that is the straight path. For whoever is granted success in what was granted to those whom God has favoured among the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs [and the righteous] (5) , has been granted success in Islam, in believing the messengers, in holding fast to the Book, in acting by what God commanded (6) him, in refraining from what He forbade him, and in following the way (7) of the Prophet, ﷺ, and the way of Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿUthmān and ʿAlī, may So be patient. Lo, the promise of God is true, and ask forgiveness for your sin, and glorify the praise of your Lord in the evening and in the morning (55) [Ghāfir 40:55] God be pleased with them all, and every righteous servant of God; and all of that is of the straight path.

The interpreters of the Qurʾān have differed over what is meant by the straight path; the meanings of all of them are comprehended by the interpretation that we have reported (1) of it. And of what they said on this is what is reported from ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib, may God be pleased with him, from the Prophet, ﷺ, that he mentioned the Qurʾān and said: “It is the straight path.”

al-Ṭabarī's explanation

Abū Jaʿfar said: al-Ṭabarī’s own explanation God, majestic is His praise, described it as straight only because it is right, with no error in it. And one of the people of dullness has claimed that God called it straight because of its keeping its people straight to Paradise; and that is an interpretation contrary to the interpretation of all the people of exegesis, and the consensus of all of them against it, all of them (2) , is sufficient proof of its error.

02

What is the straight path?

The authority (6) of the people of interpretation, all together, is agreed that the straight path is the clear way in which there is no crookedness; and likewise that (7) is so in the language of all the Arabs. Of this is the verse of Jarīr b. ʿAṭiyya al-Khaṭafā (8): The Commander of the Faithful is on a path, straight, when the watering places go crooked He means: on the way of truth. And of this is the verse of the Hudhalī Abū Dhuʾayb (1): We came upon their land in the morning with horses until we left it thinner than the ṣirāṭ. And of this is the verse of the rajaz poet (2): And he turned away from the course of the straight (qāṣid) path (3). And the attestations of this are more than can be counted, and what we have mentioned suffices in place of what we have left. Then the Arabs use ṣirāṭ metaphorically and employ it for every statement and action described as straight or crooked, describing the straight by its straightness and the crooked by its crookedness.

And what is more fitting as the interpretation of this verse, in my view, I mean (4) Guide us to the straight path, is that what is meant by it is: grant us success in remaining steadfast in what You have approved and granted success in to those You have favoured among Your servants, of statement and action; and that is the straight path. For whoever is granted success in what was granted to those whom God has favoured among the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs [and the righteous] (5), has been granted success in Islam, in believing the messengers, in holding fast to the Book, in acting by what God commanded (6) him, in refraining from what He forbade him, and in following the way (7) of the Prophet, ﷺ, and the way of Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿUthmān and ʿAlī, may So be patient. Lo, the promise of God is true, and ask forgiveness for your sin, and glorify the praise of your Lord in the evening and in the morning (55) [Ghāfir 40:55] God be pleased with them all, and every righteous servant of God; and all of that is of the straight path.

The interpreters of the Qurʾān have differed over what is meant by the straight path; the meanings of all of them are comprehended by the interpretation that we have reported (1) of it. And of what they said on this is what is reported from ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib, may God be pleased with him, from the Prophet, ﷺ, that he mentioned the Qurʾān and said: “It is the straight path.”

04

Why the path is called straight

God, majestic is His praise, described it as straight only because it is right, with no error in it. And one of the people of dullness has claimed that God called it straight because of its keeping its people straight to Paradise; and that is an interpretation contrary to the interpretation of all the people of exegesis, and the consensus of all of them against it, all of them (2), is sufficient proof of its error.

The supplied source identifies the Dār Hajar edition and its editors but does not name the English translator.

Translation passages are presented by verse; paragraph, report and quotation groupings are editorial. The English wording is preserved.
