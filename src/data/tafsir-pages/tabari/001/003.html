01

The repeated divine names

The explanation of the interpretation of His words Most Gracious, Most Merciful has already been given in the interpretation of In the name of God, Most Gracious, Most Merciful, which makes repeating it in this place unnecessary (5). We also did not need to explain the reason for the repetition (6) of that in this place, since we do not regard In the name of God, Most Gracious, Most Merciful as a verse of the Opening of the Book, such that a questioner could press on us the question: What is the reason for repeating this in this place, when God, majestic is His praise, has already described Himself with it in His words In the name of God, Most Gracious, Most Merciful, with one of the two verses near the other and adjacent to its companion? Rather, this is for us an argument against the error of the claim of one who claimed that In the name of God, Most Gracious, Most Merciful is a verse of the Opening of the Book; for if it were so, it would be the repetition of a verse with one meaning and one wording twice, with no separator (1) between them. and His words Most Gracious, Most Merciful in Praise be to God, Lord of the Worlds.

02

The question of word order

If someone (3) says: But Praise be to God, Lord of the Worlds is a separator between (2) them. It is said: A group of the interpreters denied this, and said: This belongs to the postponed that has the sense of being advanced, and the text is rather: Praise be to God, Most Gracious, Most Merciful, Lord of the Worlds, Master of the Day of Judgement. They cited as evidence for the soundness of what they claimed His words: (Malik of the Day of Judgement). They said: His words (Malik of the Day of Judgement) are God's teaching His servant to describe Him as King (malik) in the reading of one who reads (Malik), and as owner (mālik, possessing milk) in the reading of one who reads Mālik. They said: What is more fitting to be placed next to His description as sovereignty (mulk) or ownership (milk) is a description of the same kind, and that is His words: Lord of the Worlds, which is a report of His ownership of all classes of creation; and what is next to His description with greatness and divinity should be what is its like in meaning, of praise of Him, and that is His words Most Gracious, Most Merciful.

They claimed that this is for them evidence that His words Most Gracious, Most Merciful have the sense of coming before Lord of the Worlds, although apparently placed after. And they (1) said: Instances of this, of advancing that has the sense of postponing and postponing that has the sense of advancing, are more widespread in the speech of the Arabs and more numerous in their utterance than can be counted. Of this is the verse of Jarīr b. ʿAṭiyya (2):

It means: the phantom visited briefly (limāman), and how far is it from you? And as He, majestic is His praise, said in His mighty Book: Praise be to God, who sent down to His servant the Book and made not in it any crookedness [al-Kahf 18:1], with the sense: Praise be to God who sent down to His servant the Book straight and made not in it any crookedness; and the like.

In that is evidence attesting to the soundness of the statement of those who denied that In the name of God, Most Gracious, Most Merciful is a verse of the Opening of the Book.
