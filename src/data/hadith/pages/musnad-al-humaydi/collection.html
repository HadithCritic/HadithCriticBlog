Musnad al-Ḥumaydī

Compiled byʿAbd Allāh ibn al-Zubayr al-Ḥumaydīd. 219 AH

The Musnad of al-Ḥumaydī (d. 219 AH), the Meccan teacher of al-Bukhārī, arranged as he arranged it: the ḥadīths of each Companion under that Companion’s name, beginning with Abū Bakr. Every narration is given in Arabic and English with its chain set apart from its words.

Edition. Edited by Ḥasan Salīm Asad al-Dārānī; Dār al-Saqqā, Damascus; first edition, 1996. Two volumes.

Structure of the work. A musnad: each of the compiler's headings names a Companion and is followed by that Companion's ḥadīths. The 187 headings and the narrations under them are kept in the order of the printed edition.

Numbering. Narrations keep the printed numbers 1 to 1337. The supplied text is a set of page records: a record that holds several numbered narrations is divided at the printed numbers, and a narration that runs into a second record is shown as one narration with its continuation. Numbers 341, 502, 636, 847, 1240 are printed with no text after them in the source and are not shown.

English translation. The English is the supplied translation, assembled from several batches that name no translator and spell names in more than one way. It has not been checked word by word against the Arabic; readings the translation marks for review carry a note.

Arabic text. The Arabic is shown as supplied. Printed numbers appear in the label instead of the text; page markers are carried over.

Isnad and matn. The Arabic is divided where its chain ends and the English at the same link of the chain, by a fixed rule that does not alter the wording. A passage the rule cannot divide is shown whole and says so.

Transmitters. Each transmission path lists the names exactly as the isnad words them, read from the text between its transmission terms. English names are shown only where they line up one for one with the Arabic. No transmitter is identified beyond the wording of the source.

Catalog header. Each volume opens with a record that repeats the Shamela catalog header. It is not reproduced as an entry.

Dating. Century is based on the compiler/associated authority's death year, not a verified date of composition. The date shown is the death year of the compiler, 219 AH.
