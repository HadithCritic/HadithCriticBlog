On “the path of those upon whom You have…”

al-Ṭabarī's explanation

And His words: the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour . This is an exposition of the straight path — which path is it? — since every road of the roads of truth is [a straight path] (3) . So it was said to Muḥammad, ﷺ: Say, O Muḥammad: Guide us, O our Lord, to the straight path, the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour with Your obedience, Your worship, of Your angels, Your prophets, the truthful, the martyrs and the righteous. And that is the like of what our Lord, majestic is His praise, said in His revelation: And if they had done what they are admonished to do, it would have been better for them and more strengthening (66) And then We would have given them from Ourselves a great reward (67) And We would have guided them to a straight path (68) And whoever obeys God and the Messenger, those are with those upon whom God has bestowed favour, of the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs, and the righteous [al-Nisāʾ 4:66–69].

Abū Jaʿfar said: al-Ṭabarī’s own explanation So what Muḥammad, ﷺ, and his community were commanded to ask (1) their Lord, of guidance to the straight way, is guidance to the way whose attribute God, majestic is His praise, described; and that way is the way of those (2) whom God described with what He described them with in His revelation, and He promised whoever follows it and stays straight in it, obedient to God and to His Messenger, ﷺ, that He will bring him (3) to where they arrive; and God does not break the promise. And the report has been transmitted in the like of what we said on this from Ibn ʿAbbās and others.

Language and usage

Abū Jaʿfar said: al-Ṭabarī’s own explanation In this verse is clear proof that obedience to God, majestic is His praise, is not attained by those who obey except by God's bestowing it as a favour upon them and His granting them success in it. Do they not hear Him say: the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour ? So He ascribed (6) what came from them of guidance, obedience and worship to its being a favour from Him upon them.

? If someone says: And where is the completion of this report? You know that one's saying to another: I have bestowed favour upon you, requires a report of that with which he has bestowed favour on him; so where is that report in His words the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour ? And what is that favour that He bestowed upon them? ↳ It is said to him: We have already set out earlier in this book of ours the Arabs' contentment, in their speech, with some in place of some, when the apparent part indicates the hidden part and suffices for it; and His words (1) the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour are of that, because God's, majestic is His praise, commanding His servants to ask Him for help and their seeking from Him guidance to the straight path, when it was preceded by His words the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour , which is an exposition of the straight path and a substitute for it — it was known that the favour that God bestowed on those whom He commanded us (2) to ask Him to guide us to their way is the upright course (3) and the straight path, the explanation of whose interpretation we have given above; so what was apparent of that — with the nearness of the two words' adjacency — sufficed in place of repeating it, as al-Nābigha of Banū Dhubyān said (4) : As if you were of the camels of Banū Uqayš, behind whose legs a water-skin is rattled (5) (6) . He means: as if you were, of the camels of Banū Uqayš, a camel behind whose legs a water-skin is rattled. So he was content with what appeared of the mention of the camels, which indicates what was omitted, instead of showing what he omitted. And as al-Farazdaq b. Ghālib said (7) : You see their ropes (8) worn about them, when the iron rusts on the warriors (9) He means: wearing them, they (mutaqalliduhā hum). So he dropped “hum” since what was apparent of his words arbāqahum indicated it.

And the attestations of this from the poetry and speech of the Arabs are more than can be counted; so likewise that is in His words the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour .

On “not of those who incur wrath”

Abū Jaʿfar said: al-Ṭabarī’s own explanation The reciters are unanimous in reading ghayr with a kasra on the rāʾ. The genitive comes to it by two routes: one is that ghayr be an attribute of alladhīna and a qualifier of them, so that you put it in the genitive, since alladhīna is in the genitive and it is a qualifier and attribute of them. And it is permissible for ghayr to be a qualifier of alladhīna although alladhīna is definite and ghayr indefinite, because alladhīna with its relative clause is not the specific definite, like the nouns that are marks among people, such as Zayd and ʿAmr and the like, but is like the unknown indefinites, such as “the man” and “the camel” and the like. So since alladhīna is thus in its description, and ghayr was annexed to an unspecified noun, the like of alladhīna in being a definite that is not specific, as alladhīna is a definite that is not specific, it was permissible on that account that ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim be a qualifier of alladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim [as it is said: I sit only with the learned, ghayr the ignorant.

Meaning: I sit only with one who knows, not with one who is ignorant. And if alladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim were a specific definite, it would be not] (2) [permissible for ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim to be a qualifier of it, and that is because it is an error in the speech of the Arabs, when a specific definite is described by an indefinite, to make its qualifier, the indefinite, take the case-ending of the definite it qualifies, except with the intention of repeating what governed the qualified noun. It is an error in their speech to say: I passed by ʿAbd Allāh [ghayri al-ʿālim, putting ghayr in the genitive, except with the intention of repeating the bāʾ that governed ʿAbd Allāh. So the sense of that, were it said so, would be: I passed by ʿAbd Allāh] (1) , I passed by ghayri al-ʿālim. This is one of the two aspects of the genitive in ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim ] (2) . The other of the two aspects of the genitive in it is that alladhīna has the sense of the specific definite; and if it is directed to that, ghayr is in the genitive with the intention of repeating the path that put alladhīna in the genitive, so that it is as if you said: the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour, the path of ghayri al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim. And these two interpretations of ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim , although they differ by the difference of their grammarians, are close in meaning, because whoever God has favoured and guided to His true religion has been safe from the wrath of his Lord and delivered from error in his religion. So it is the same — since one who hears His words Guide us to the straight path (6) the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour cannot possibly doubt, on hearing that from its reciter, that those upon whom God has bestowed guidance to the path are not objects of their Lord's anger, along with the favour whose bounty to them in their religion has been great, nor that they are astray when their Lord has guided them to the truth (3) , since it is impossible in their natures that God's, majestic is His praise, being pleased with a person and being angry with him be joined in one state, or that guidance and error be joined in him at one time — whether the people were described — along with God's describing them with what He described them with of His granting them success and guiding them and bestowing His favour on them in their religion — as not being those with whom He is angry nor astray, or were not so described; because the apparent attribute by which they were described has already informed of them that they are so, even if He did not state their description by it explicitly. This is if we direct ghayr to being in the genitive with the intention of repeating the path that put alladhīna in the genitive, and do not make ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā al-ḍāllīn part of the description of the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour , but rather make them others, even though both groups are undoubtedly favoured in their religions. But if we direct ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā al-ḍāllīn to being among the qualifiers of alladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim , then the hearer has no need for (1) inference, since the explicit sense of it has made proof unnecessary. And it is also permissible to put ghayr in the accusative (2) in ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim , although I dislike the reading of it so, for its irregularity (shudhūdh) from the reading of the reciters, and because whatever departs, of readings, from what the community has brought by manifest, widespread transmission is an opinion contrary to the truth, and one deviating from the way of God and the way of His Messenger, ﷺ, and the way of the Muslims, even if it has — [were the reading of it permissible] (3) — a way out in soundness. And the interpretation of its aspect of soundness if you put it in the accusative is that it be directed to being a qualifier of the hāʾ and mīm in ʿalayhim , which refers back to alladhīna , because although it is in the genitive by ʿalā, it is in the position of the accusative by His word anʿamta . So the interpretation of the speech (1) — if you put ghayr that is with al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim in the accusative — would be: the path of those whom You guided as a favour from You upon them, ghayra maghḍūbin ʿalayhim — that is: not as ones upon whom wrath has been brought — and not astray. The accusative in that would then be like the accusative of ghayr in your saying: I passed by ʿAbd Allāh ghayra al-karīm wa-lā al-rashīd; you cut off ghayra al-karīm from ʿAbd Allāh, since ʿAbd Allāh is a specific definite and ghayr al-karīm an unknown indefinite. And one of the Basran grammarians used to claim that the reading of whoever put ghayr in the accusative in ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim is on the aspect of an exception: ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim excepted from the meanings of the description of alladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim , as if he thought that the meaning of those who read it in the accusative is: Guide us to the straight path, the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour, except those upon whom wrath has been brought, who are those You have not favoured in their religions and have not guided to the truth, so do not make us among them. As al-Nābigha of Banū Dhubyān said (2) : I stood in it at evening (3) , asking it; it was at a loss (4) for an answer, and there was no one in the abode (5) except for tethering-places (awārī) (1) that I can scarcely (2) make out, and the trench-ditch (3) like a trough in the wronged (4) , hard (5) ground. And it is known that the awārī are not counted among anyone in anything. So likewise, in his view, he excepted ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim from alladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim , although they are not among their kinds in religion in anything. As for the Kufan grammarians, they rejected this interpretation and considered it mistaken (6) , and claimed that if it were as the claimant said [what he claimed] (7) of the people of Basra, it would be an error to say wa-lā al-ḍāllīn , because lā is negation and denial, and one does not coordinate with a negation except on a negation. They said: We have not found in anything of the speech of the Arabs an exception on which a negation is coordinated; we have only found them coordinating an exception on an exception, and a negation on a negation. So they say, in exception: The people stood except your brother and except your father. And in negation: Your brother did not stand, nor your father. But as for: The people stood except your father and not your brother — we have not found it in the speech of the Arabs. They said: So since that is nonexistent in the speech of the Arabs, and the Qurʾān was revealed in the most eloquent tongue of the Arabs, we know — since His words wa-lā al-ḍāllīn are coordinated on His words ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā al-ḍāllīn — that ghayr has the sense of negation, not the sense of exception, and that the interpretation of whoever directs it to exception is an error. These, then, are the aspects of the interpretation of ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim by the difference of the aspects of its grammatical analysis. We have gone into what we went into here, of setting out the aspects of its grammatical analysis — although our aim (1) in this book is the disclosure of the interpretation of the verses of the Qurʾān — because of what the differences in the aspects of its analysis entail of differences in the aspects of its interpretation, so need compelled us to disclose the aspects of its analysis, so that the aspects of its interpretation may be disclosed to the seeker of its interpretation according to the differences of those who differ over its interpretation and reading. And the correct view on its interpretation and reading, in our opinion, is the first view, which is the reading of ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim with a kasra on the rāʾ of ghayr , on the interpretation that it is an attribute of alladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim and a qualifier of them — for what we have set out already — if you wish; and if you wish, on the interpretation of repeating ṣirāṭa ; all of that is correct and good.

al-Ṭabarī's explanation

Abū Jaʿfar said: al-Ṭabarī’s own explanation There has been disagreement about the description of wrath on the part of God, exalted be His mention.

Some of them said: God's wrath against any of His creatures with whom He is angry is the bringing down of His punishment on the one with whom He is angry, whether in his worldly life or in his hereafter, as He described Himself, exalted be His mention, in His Book, saying: So when they angered Us, We took vengeance on them and drowned them all [al-Zukhruf 43:55]. And as He said: Say: Shall I inform you of something worse than that as to recompense with God? Those whom God has cursed and with whom He is angry, and of whom He made apes and swine [al-Māʾida 5:60]. And some of them said: God's wrath against any of His servants with whom He is angry is His censure of them and of their deeds, and His reviling of them in speech. And some of them said: Wrath on His part is an understood meaning, like what is known of the meanings of wrath, except that — although it is so in respect of affirmation — its meaning on His part differs from the meaning of the wrath of human beings, which disturbs them, stirs them, burdens them and harms them; because God, majestic is His praise, is not subject in His essence to afflictions, but it is an attribute of His, as knowledge is an attribute of His, and power is an attribute of His, as is understood in respect of affirmation, even though the meanings of those differ from the meanings of the knowledge of servants, which are cognitions of hearts, and their faculties, which exist with the existence of acts and cease with their ceasing (2) .

On “nor of those who go astray ”

Abū Jaʿfar said: al-Ṭabarī’s own explanation One of the people of Basra (3) used to claim that lā with al-ḍāllīn was inserted to complete the speech, and that its meaning is its nullification (4) , and he cites in support of his statement [the verse of] (5) al-ʿAjjāj (6) : In a well of no ḥūr he went by night, and did not perceive and he interprets it as: in a well of ḥūr he went by night; that is: in a well of ruin; and that “lā” has the sense of nullification and a filler particle (ṣila) (1) .

Poetic evidence / as rendered in the source He also argues for that with the verse of Abū al-Najm (2) : So I do not blame the fair ones for not mocking, when they saw the gray hair, the ugly one (3) — and he means: I do not blame the fair ones for mocking. And with the verse of al-Aḥwaṣ (4) : And they blame me (5) for pleasure that I not love it … while pleasure has a persistent caller, not heedless.

He means: they blame me for pleasure that I love it. And with His, exalted, words: What prevented you that you did not prostrate [al-Aʿrāf 7:12]. He means: that you prostrate. And it is related of the sayer of this statement that he used to interpret ghayr that is with al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim as having the sense of “siwā” (other than). So the meaning of the speech in his view would be: Guide us to the straight path, the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour, who are other than those who incur wrath and (6) those who go astray. And one of the Kufan grammarians (7) used to reject this of his statement, and claimed that if ghayr that is with al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim had the sense of “siwā”, it would be an error to coordinate on it with “lā”, since “lā” is coordinated with only on a negation that has preceded it, just as it would be an error for a speaker to say: I have siwā your brother and not your father.

Because “siwā” is not among the particles of negation and denial.

Poetic evidence / as rendered in the source And he says: Since that is an error in the speech of the Arabs, and the Qurʾān is in the most eloquent (1) of the languages of the Arabs, it was known that what the sayer claimed — that ghayr with al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim means: other than those who incur wrath — is an error, since he then reverted the speech upon it with “lā”, and claimed that ghayr there has only the sense of denial, and (2) it was sound in the speech of the Arabs and widespread and manifest in their discourse to direct “ghayr” to the sense of negation, and it is used among them: Your brother is ghayr a doer of good and not a doer of beauty.

By that is meant: Your brother is neither a doer of good nor a doer of beauty. And he rejects that “lā” should come with the sense of deletion in speech at the beginning of it, when no negation has preceded it. And he says: If its coming with the sense of deletion at the beginning were permissible, before an indication pointing to that in the form of a preceding negation, the statement of one who said would be sound: I wanted not to honour your brother — meaning: I wanted to honour your brother. And he used to say: In the testimony of the people of knowledge of the tongue of the Arabs to the error of the sayer of that there is clear indication that “lā” does not come at the beginning with the sense of deletion when no negation has preceded it. And he used to interpret the “lā” in the verse of al-ʿAjjāj, which (3) we mentioned that the Basran cited in support of his statement (4) , as a genuine negation, and that the meaning of the verse is: He went by night in a well that does not yield him any good, and no trace of work is evident to him in it, and he does not perceive that, nor know (5) of it.

From their saying: The grinder ground and did not yield anything (mā aḥārat shayʾan). That is: no trace of work was evident to it. And he says of the other verses, I mean like the verse of Abū al-Najm: So I do not blame the fair ones for not mocking it was permissible for “lā” to have the sense of deletion only because negation had preceded it at the beginning of the speech, so the latter speech was connected with the first, as the poet said (1) : The Messenger of God would not have been pleased with their deed … nor the two good ones, Abū Bakr and (lā) ʿUmar. That was permissible because negation had preceded at the start of the speech.

Abū Jaʿfar said: al-Ṭabarī’s own explanation And this other view is more likely to be correct than the first, since it is not found in the speech of the Arabs to begin speech, without a preceding negation, with “lā” whose meaning is deletion, nor is coordination with it on “siwā” permissible, nor on a particle of exception. And “ghayr” in the speech of the Arabs has only three meanings: one is exception, another negation, and the third siwā. So when [it is established that it is an error for “lā” to be] (2) in the sense of nullification at the beginning, and it is invalid for it to be coordinated on ghayr that is with al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim were it in the sense of “illā”, which is exception, and it is also not permissible for it to be coordinated on it were it in the sense of “siwā”, with “lā” present as coordinated by the wāw that coordinates it on what is before it — it is sound and established that there is no aspect of ghayr with al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim to which it may soundly be directed except the sense of negation and denial, and that there is no aspect to His words wa-lā al-ḍāllīn except coordination on ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim . So the interpretation of the speech then — since what we have said is sound by what we have cited as evidence — is: Guide us to the straight path, the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour, not (3) those who incur wrath and not those who go astray.

Divine favour and the language of the verse

In this verse is clear proof that obedience to God, majestic is His praise, is not attained by those who obey except by God's bestowing it as a favour upon them and His granting them success in it. Do they not hear Him say: the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour? So He ascribed (6) what came from them of guidance, obedience and worship to its being a favour from Him upon them.

Question

And where is the completion of this report? You know that one's saying to another: I have bestowed favour upon you, requires a report of that with which he has bestowed favour on him; so where is that report in His words the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour? And what is that favour that He bestowed upon them?

Response

We have already set out earlier in this book of ours the Arabs' contentment, in their speech, with some in place of some, when the apparent part indicates the hidden part and suffices for it; and His words (1) the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour are of that, because God's, majestic is His praise, commanding His servants to ask Him for help and their seeking from Him guidance to the straight path, when it was preceded by His words the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour, which is an exposition of the straight path and a substitute for it — it was known that the favour that God bestowed on those whom He commanded us (2) to ask Him to guide us to their way is the upright course (3) and the straight path, the explanation of whose interpretation we have given above; so what was apparent of that — with the nearness of the two words' adjacency — sufficed in place of repeating it, as al-Nābigha of Banū Dhubyān said (4): As if you were of the camels of Banū Uqayš, behind whose legs a water-skin is rattled (5) (6). He means: as if you were, of the camels of Banū Uqayš, a camel behind whose legs a water-skin is rattled. So he was content with what appeared of the mention of the camels, which indicates what was omitted, instead of showing what he omitted. And as al-Farazdaq b. Ghālib said (7): You see their ropes (8) worn about them, when the iron rusts on the warriors (9) He means: wearing them, they (mutaqalliduhā hum). So he dropped “hum” since what was apparent of his words arbāqahum indicated it.

And the attestations of this from the poetry and speech of the Arabs are more than can be counted; so likewise that is in His words the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour.

04

The meaning of “those who incur wrath”

The reciters are unanimous in reading ghayr with a kasra on the rāʾ. The genitive comes to it by two routes: one is that ghayr be an attribute of alladhīna and a qualifier of them, so that you put it in the genitive, since alladhīna is in the genitive and it is a qualifier and attribute of them. And it is permissible for ghayr to be a qualifier of alladhīna although alladhīna is definite and ghayr indefinite, because alladhīna with its relative clause is not the specific definite, like the nouns that are marks among people, such as Zayd and ʿAmr and the like, but is like the unknown indefinites, such as “the man” and “the camel” and the like. So since alladhīna is thus in its description, and ghayr was annexed to an unspecified noun, the like of alladhīna in being a definite that is not specific, as alladhīna is a definite that is not specific, it was permissible on that account that ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim be a qualifier of alladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim [as it is said: I sit only with the learned, ghayr the ignorant.

Meaning: I sit only with one who knows, not with one who is ignorant. And if alladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim were a specific definite, it would be not] (2) [permissible for ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim to be a qualifier of it, and that is because it is an error in the speech of the Arabs, when a specific definite is described by an indefinite, to make its qualifier, the indefinite, take the case-ending of the definite it qualifies, except with the intention of repeating what governed the qualified noun. It is an error in their speech to say: I passed by ʿAbd Allāh [ghayri al-ʿālim, putting ghayr in the genitive, except with the intention of repeating the bāʾ that governed ʿAbd Allāh. So the sense of that, were it said so, would be: I passed by ʿAbd Allāh] (1), I passed by ghayri al-ʿālim. This is one of the two aspects of the genitive in ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim] (2). The other of the two aspects of the genitive in it is that alladhīna has the sense of the specific definite; and if it is directed to that, ghayr is in the genitive with the intention of repeating the path that put alladhīna in the genitive, so that it is as if you said: the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour, the path of ghayri al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim.

And these two interpretations of ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim, although they differ by the difference of their grammarians, are close in meaning, because whoever God has favoured and guided to His true religion has been safe from the wrath of his Lord and delivered from error in his religion. So it is the same — since one who hears His words Guide us to the straight path (6) the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour cannot possibly doubt, on hearing that from its reciter, that those upon whom God has bestowed guidance to the path are not objects of their Lord's anger, along with the favour whose bounty to them in their religion has been great, nor that they are astray when their Lord has guided them to the truth (3), since it is impossible in their natures that God's, majestic is His praise, being pleased with a person and being angry with him be joined in one state, or that guidance and error be joined in him at one time — whether the people were described — along with God's describing them with what He described them with of His granting them success and guiding them and bestowing His favour on them in their religion — as not being those with whom He is angry nor astray, or were not so described; because the apparent attribute by which they were described has already informed of them that they are so, even if He did not state their description by it explicitly.

This is if we direct ghayr to being in the genitive with the intention of repeating the path that put alladhīna in the genitive, and do not make ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā al-ḍāllīn part of the description of the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour, but rather make them others, even though both groups are undoubtedly favoured in their religions. But if we direct ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā al-ḍāllīn to being among the qualifiers of alladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim, then the hearer has no need for (1) inference, since the explicit sense of it has made proof unnecessary. And it is also permissible to put ghayr in the accusative (2) in ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim, although I dislike the reading of it so, for its irregularity (shudhūdh) from the reading of the reciters, and because whatever departs, of readings, from what the community has brought by manifest, widespread transmission is an opinion contrary to the truth, and one deviating from the way of God and the way of His Messenger, ﷺ, and the way of the Muslims, even if it has — [were the reading of it permissible] (3) — a way out in soundness.

And the interpretation of its aspect of soundness if you put it in the accusative is that it be directed to being a qualifier of the hāʾ and mīm in ʿalayhim, which refers back to alladhīna, because although it is in the genitive by ʿalā, it is in the position of the accusative by His word anʿamta. So the interpretation of the speech (1) — if you put ghayr that is with al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim in the accusative — would be: the path of those whom You guided as a favour from You upon them, ghayra maghḍūbin ʿalayhim — that is: not as ones upon whom wrath has been brought — and not astray. The accusative in that would then be like the accusative of ghayr in your saying: I passed by ʿAbd Allāh ghayra al-karīm wa-lā al-rashīd; you cut off ghayra al-karīm from ʿAbd Allāh, since ʿAbd Allāh is a specific definite and ghayr al-karīm an unknown indefinite. And one of the Basran grammarians used to claim that the reading of whoever put ghayr in the accusative in ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim is on the aspect of an exception: ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim excepted from the meanings of the description of alladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim, as if he thought that the meaning of those who read it in the accusative is: Guide us to the straight path, the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour, except those upon whom wrath has been brought, who are those You have not favoured in their religions and have not guided to the truth, so do not make us among them.

As al-Nābigha of Banū Dhubyān said (2): I stood in it at evening (3), asking it; it was at a loss (4) for an answer, and there was no one in the abode (5) except for tethering-places (awārī) (1) that I can scarcely (2) make out, and the trench-ditch (3) like a trough in the wronged (4), hard (5) ground. And it is known that the awārī are not counted among anyone in anything. So likewise, in his view, he excepted ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim from alladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim, although they are not among their kinds in religion in anything. As for the Kufan grammarians, they rejected this interpretation and considered it mistaken (6), and claimed that if it were as the claimant said [what he claimed] (7) of the people of Basra, it would be an error to say wa-lā al-ḍāllīn, because lā is negation and denial, and one does not coordinate with a negation except on a negation.

They said: We have not found in anything of the speech of the Arabs an exception on which a negation is coordinated; we have only found them coordinating an exception on an exception, and a negation on a negation. So they say, in exception: The people stood except your brother and except your father. And in negation: Your brother did not stand, nor your father. But as for: The people stood except your father and not your brother — we have not found it in the speech of the Arabs. They said: So since that is nonexistent in the speech of the Arabs, and the Qurʾān was revealed in the most eloquent tongue of the Arabs, we know — since His words wa-lā al-ḍāllīn are coordinated on His words ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā al-ḍāllīn — that ghayr has the sense of negation, not the sense of exception, and that the interpretation of whoever directs it to exception is an error. These, then, are the aspects of the interpretation of ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim by the difference of the aspects of its grammatical analysis.

We have gone into what we went into here, of setting out the aspects of its grammatical analysis — although our aim (1) in this book is the disclosure of the interpretation of the verses of the Qurʾān — because of what the differences in the aspects of its analysis entail of differences in the aspects of its interpretation, so need compelled us to disclose the aspects of its analysis, so that the aspects of its interpretation may be disclosed to the seeker of its interpretation according to the differences of those who differ over its interpretation and reading. And the correct view on its interpretation and reading, in our opinion, is the first view, which is the reading of ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim with a kasra on the rāʾ of ghayr, on the interpretation that it is an attribute of alladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim and a qualifier of them — for what we have set out already — if you wish; and if you wish, on the interpretation of repeating ṣirāṭa; all of that is correct and good.

How al-Ṭabarī understands divine wrath

There has been disagreement about the description of wrath on the part of God, exalted be His mention.

Some of them said: God's wrath against any of His creatures with whom He is angry is the bringing down of His punishment on the one with whom He is angry, whether in his worldly life or in his hereafter, as He described Himself, exalted be His mention, in His Book, saying: So when they angered Us, We took vengeance on them and drowned them all [al-Zukhruf 43:55]. And as He said: Say: Shall I inform you of something worse than that as to recompense with God? Those whom God has cursed and with whom He is angry, and of whom He made apes and swine [al-Māʾida 5:60]. And some of them said: God's wrath against any of His servants with whom He is angry is His censure of them and of their deeds, and His reviling of them in speech. And some of them said: Wrath on His part is an understood meaning, like what is known of the meanings of wrath, except that — although it is so in respect of affirmation — its meaning on His part differs from the meaning of the wrath of human beings, which disturbs them, stirs them, burdens them and harms them; because God, majestic is His praise, is not subject in His essence to afflictions, but it is an attribute of His, as knowledge is an attribute of His, and power is an attribute of His, as is understood in respect of affirmation, even though the meanings of those differ from the meanings of the knowledge of servants, which are cognitions of hearts, and their faculties, which exist with the existence of acts and cease with their ceasing (2).

07

The grammar of “nor those who go astray”

One of the people of Basra (3) used to claim that lā with al-ḍāllīn was inserted to complete the speech, and that its meaning is its nullification (4), and he cites in support of his statement [the verse of] (5) al-ʿAjjāj (6): In a well of no ḥūr he went by night, and did not perceive and he interprets it as: in a well of ḥūr he went by night; that is: in a well of ruin; and that “lā” has the sense of nullification and a filler particle (ṣila) (1).

He means: they blame me for pleasure that I love it. And with His, exalted, words: What prevented you that you did not prostrate [al-Aʿrāf 7:12]. He means: that you prostrate. And it is related of the sayer of this statement that he used to interpret ghayr that is with al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim as having the sense of “siwā” (other than). So the meaning of the speech in his view would be: Guide us to the straight path, the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour, who are other than those who incur wrath and (6) those who go astray. And one of the Kufan grammarians (7) used to reject this of his statement, and claimed that if ghayr that is with al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim had the sense of “siwā”, it would be an error to coordinate on it with “lā”, since “lā” is coordinated with only on a negation that has preceded it, just as it would be an error for a speaker to say: I have siwā your brother and not your father.

Because “siwā” is not among the particles of negation and denial.

By that is meant: Your brother is neither a doer of good nor a doer of beauty. And he rejects that “lā” should come with the sense of deletion in speech at the beginning of it, when no negation has preceded it. And he says: If its coming with the sense of deletion at the beginning were permissible, before an indication pointing to that in the form of a preceding negation, the statement of one who said would be sound: I wanted not to honour your brother — meaning: I wanted to honour your brother. And he used to say: In the testimony of the people of knowledge of the tongue of the Arabs to the error of the sayer of that there is clear indication that “lā” does not come at the beginning with the sense of deletion when no negation has preceded it. And he used to interpret the “lā” in the verse of al-ʿAjjāj, which (3) we mentioned that the Basran cited in support of his statement (4), as a genuine negation, and that the meaning of the verse is: He went by night in a well that does not yield him any good, and no trace of work is evident to him in it, and he does not perceive that, nor know (5) of it.

From their saying: The grinder ground and did not yield anything (mā aḥārat shayʾan). That is: no trace of work was evident to it. And he says of the other verses, I mean like the verse of Abū al-Najm: So I do not blame the fair ones for not mocking it was permissible for “lā” to have the sense of deletion only because negation had preceded it at the beginning of the speech, so the latter speech was connected with the first, as the poet said (1): The Messenger of God would not have been pleased with their deed … nor the two good ones, Abū Bakr and (lā) ʿUmar. That was permissible because negation had preceded at the start of the speech.

And this other view is more likely to be correct than the first, since it is not found in the speech of the Arabs to begin speech, without a preceding negation, with “lā” whose meaning is deletion, nor is coordination with it on “siwā” permissible, nor on a particle of exception. And “ghayr” in the speech of the Arabs has only three meanings: one is exception, another negation, and the third siwā. So when [it is established that it is an error for “lā” to be] (2) in the sense of nullification at the beginning, and it is invalid for it to be coordinated on ghayr that is with al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim were it in the sense of “illā”, which is exception, and it is also not permissible for it to be coordinated on it were it in the sense of “siwā”, with “lā” present as coordinated by the wāw that coordinates it on what is before it — it is sound and established that there is no aspect of ghayr with al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim to which it may soundly be directed except the sense of negation and denial, and that there is no aspect to His words wa-lā al-ḍāllīn except coordination on ghayr al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim.

So the interpretation of the speech then — since what we have said is sound by what we have cited as evidence — is: Guide us to the straight path, the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour, not (3) those who incur wrath and not those who go astray.

09

Guidance, error and the composition of al-Fātiḥa

[Everyone who deviates] (2) from the straight course and travels other than the upright way is “astray” (ḍāll) among the Arabs, because of his losing the direction of the road; for that reason God, exalted be His mention, named the Christians “those who go astray,” for their error regarding the truth, the course of the way, and their taking of religion in other than the straight path.

Question

Is not that too a description of the Jews?

Response

Yes.

Question

Then how did He single out the Christians with this description, and single out the Jews with what He described them with, that they are those who incur wrath?

Response

(3) Both groups are astray and have incurred wrath; but God, majestic is His praise, marked each group of them, in His describing to His servants, with what they know it by when He mentions it to them or informs them of it, and did not name either of the two groups except with what is a description of it in truth, even if it has additions to it of attributes of blame. [And] some of the people of dullness among the Qadariyya [have supposed] (4) that in God's, majestic is His praise, describing the Christians as astray in His words nor those who go astray and His ascribing the going astray to them rather than ascribing their being led astray to Himself, and His leaving off describing them as being led astray (al-muḍallalūn) (1), as He described the Jews as those who incur wrath, is evidence for the soundness of what his brethren among the ignorant Qadariyya said — out of his ignorance of the breadth of the speech of the Arabs and the varieties of its aspects. Had the matter been as the dullard whose case we have described supposed, it would be necessary that the case (3) of everything described by an attribute, or to which an act is ascribed, be such that it is impermissible for there to be in it a cause for another, and that everything in which there is [of that, for another,] (4) a cause, the truth about it is that it be ascribed to its causer; and were that necessary, it would be an error to say: The tree moved, when the winds moved it; and the earth shook, when the earthquake shook it, and the like of this kind of speech that would make a book long to enumerate.

And in the words of God, majestic is His praise: until when you are in the ships and they run with them [Yūnus 10:22], and in [His ascribing] (5) the running to the ships, although their running is by another's making them run, there is that which indicates the error of the interpretation of the one whose statement we have described regarding His words nor those who go astray, and his claim that in God's, majestic is His praise, attributing going astray to those of the Christians to whom He attributed it, there is a validation of what the deniers claimed, that (6) God has in the acts of His creatures a cause (7) on account of which their acts came to exist, notwithstanding God's, majestic is His praise, making plain in express terms in many verses of His revelation that He is the one who leads astray and guides; of this are His words, Mighty and Majestic: Have you seen him who takes his desire as his god, and God has led him astray knowingly, and sealed his hearing and his heart, and placed over his sight a veil. Who then will guide him after God? Will you not remember? [al-Jāthiya 45:23].

So He, majestic is His praise, announced that He is the one who leads astray and guides, and no other. But the Qurʾān was revealed in the tongue of the Arabs, as we have already explained at the beginning of the book, and it is the way of the Arabs sometimes to ascribe the act to the one from whom it came into being, even if its causer is other than the one from whom it came into being, and sometimes to its causer, even if the one from whom the act came into being is other; so how about the act that the servant acquires by acquisition (kasb), and that God, majestic is His praise, brings into existence in its essence [and its coming-to-be] (1)! Rather, that is more fitting to be ascribed to the one who acquires it as an acquisition of his, by the power he has over it and the choice he has for it, and to God, majestic is His praise, by the bringing into existence of its essence (2) and its origination, as governance.

A question that the people of heresy who attack the Qurʾān ask, if one of them asks us, saying: You have stated (3) at the beginning of this book of yours, in describing explanation (bayān), that its highest in rank and noblest in station is that which is most eloquent in making clear the need of the one making clear by it on his own behalf, plainest as to the intent of its sayer, and nearest to the understanding of its hearer; and you said besides that the most fitting of speech to be so is the speech of God, majestic is His praise, because of its excellence (4) over all other speech (5), by the elevation of its rank above the highest ranks of explanation; so what is the reason — if the matter is as you have described — for the lengthening of speech in the like of Sūrat Umm al-Qurʾān with seven verses, when two of them have already contained the meanings of all of them, namely His words: (Malik of the Day of Judgement * You alone we worship and You alone we ask for help).

For there is no doubt that whoever knows the King (malik) of the Day of Judgement has known Him by His most beautiful names and His loftiest attributes, and that whoever is obedient to God is undoubtedly a follower of the way of those upon whom God has bestowed favour in their religion, and one who turns away from the way of those with whom He is angry and who have gone astray; so what is there in the addition of the remaining five verses of wisdom that the two verses we mentioned did not contain? It is said to him: God, exalted be His mention, gathered for our Prophet Muḥammad, ﷺ, and for his community, by what He sent down to him of His Book, meanings that He did not gather in a book (1) He sent down to a prophet before him, nor for any of the nations before them; for every book that He, exalted be His mention, sent down on one of His prophets before him, He sent down with only some of the meanings that the entirety of His Book that He sent down to our Prophet Muḥammad contains, such as the Torah, which is admonitions and detailed exposition, the Psalms, which is praise and glorification, and the Gospel, which is admonitions and reminding, none of them having a miracle that testifies in confirmation of the one to whom it was sent down; while the Book that was sent down on our Prophet Muḥammad, ﷺ, contains the meanings of all of that and adds to it many meanings of which all the other books are empty, and we have already mentioned them earlier in this book (2).

And among the noblest of those meanings by which our Book excels all books before it are its wondrous composition (naẓm), its strange arrangement (raṣf) (3), and its marvellous structure, such that orators were unable to compose the like of its smallest sūra, and the eloquent were too weary to arrange the like of part (4) of it, and poets were bewildered at its structure and the understandings of the intelligent were dulled before it, falling short of bringing the like of it, so they found for it nothing but submission and acknowledgement that it is from the One, the Subduer; along with what it contains in addition of meanings that are encouragement and warning, command and prohibition, narratives, disputation and parable, and the like of those meanings that have not been gathered (1) in any book sent down to earth from heaven. So whatever lengthiness it has of the sort found in Umm al-Qurʾān, it is for what I described before: that God, exalted be His mention, wished to combine, by its wondrous arrangement and strange composition, which departs from the metres of poems, the rhymed prose of soothsayers, the speeches of orators and the letters of the eloquent, the like of which all humankind are unable to arrange and the equal of which all servants are unable to compose — the indication of the prophethood of our Prophet Muḥammad, ﷺ.

And by what it contains of praise, glorification and laudation of Him, to alert servants to His greatness, authority, power and the vastness of His kingdom, so that they remember Him by His blessings, praise Him for His favours, thereby deserve more from Him and earn from Him abundant reward. And by what it contains of the description of those whom He has favoured with knowledge of Him and graced with success in obeying Him, to teach His servants that every blessing they have in their religion and worldly life is from Him, so that they direct their desire to Him and seek their needs from Him rather than from other gods and equals. And by what it contains of mention of the exemplary punishments He inflicted on those who disobeyed Him and the penalties He sent down on those who opposed His command, to make servants fearful of committing disobedience to Him and of exposing themselves to what they have no strength to bear of His anger, lest the way of those of the destroyed who committed it be followed with them in chastisement and retribution.

That, then, is the reason for lengthening the explanation in Sūrat Umm al-Qurʾān, and in what is its like of the other sūras of the Furqān, and that is the far-reaching wisdom and the perfect proof.

The supplied source identifies the Dār Hajar edition and its editors but does not name the English translator.
