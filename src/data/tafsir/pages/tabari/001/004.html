01

On “Mālik of the Day of Judgement”

al-Ṭabarī’s heading

The Discussion of the Interpretation of His words: Mālik of the Day of Judgement.

Abū Jaʿfar said

The reciters differ in the recitation of Mālik of the Day of Judgement. Some recite it (Malik of the Day of Judgement), some recite it Mālik of the Day of Judgement, and some recite it (Mālika yawmi l-dīn), with fatḥa on the kāf (4). We have given an exhaustive account of the transmission from those from whom a reading on this has been reported, in the book “al-Qirāʾāt”, and informed of the reading we choose on it and the reason that makes what we chose of the reading sound; so we disliked repeating it in this place, since what we intend in this book of ours is the explanation of the interpretations of the verses of the Qurʾān, not of the modes of their recitation. There is no disagreement among all who know the languages of the Arabs that malik (king) is derived from mulk (sovereignty), and that mālik (owner) is taken from milk (ownership). The interpretation of the reading of one who reads (Malik of the Day of Judgement) is that sovereignty belongs purely to God on the Day of Judgement, to the exclusion of all His creation who were before that in the world tyrant kings contending with Him for sovereignty and disputing His sole possession of pride, greatness, authority, and might; and so they became certain (1), by meeting God on the Day of Judgement, that they were the small and abased, and that to Him, apart (2) from them and from others, belong sovereignty, pride, might and splendour, as He, majestic is His mention and hallowed be His names, said in His revelation: On the day when they come forth, nothing of them hidden from God: To whom belongs sovereignty this day? To God, the One, the Subduer [Ghāfir 40:16].

So He, Exalted, announced that He alone possesses sovereignty on that day, to the exclusion of the kings of the world, who on the Day of Judgement pass from their sovereignty to abasement and smallness, and from their worldly life to loss in the return.

As for the interpretation of the reading of one who reads Mālik of the Day of Judgement, it is what Abū Kurayb related to us:

Transmitted explanation 01

ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿAbbās

Transmission wording
ʿUthmān b. Saʿīd told us, from Bishr b. ʿUmāra: Abū Rawq told us, from al-Ḍaḥḥāk, from Ibn ʿAbbās:
Mālik of the Day of Judgement. He says: No one on that day will have with Him authority, like their ownership in the world. Then He said: They will not speak, except for him whom the Most Gracious permits and who speaks what is right [al-Naba 78:38]. And He said: And the voices will be humbled before the Most Gracious [Ṭā Hā 20:108]. And He said: And they do not intercede except for those He approves of (1) [al-Anbiyāʾ 21:28].
Abū Jaʿfar said

Of the two interpretations, the more fitting for the verse, and of the two readings, the sounder in recitation in my view, is the first interpretation and (2) the reading of one who reads (Malik), in the sense of sovereignty; because in acknowledging His sole possession of sovereignty is a necessary affirmation of His sole possession of ownership, together with the excellence of the surplus of malik over mālik (3), since it is known that there is no king who is not an owner, while an owner may not be a king. Furthermore, God, majestic is His mention, has informed His servants in the verse before His words Mālik of the Day of Judgement that He is the owner of all the worlds, their master, the one who sets them right, [the one who looks after them] (4), and the Most Merciful to them in this world and the Hereafter, in His words Lord of the Worlds (2) Most Gracious, Most Merciful.

Since (5) He, majestic is His mention, had informed them of His ownership of them in this way by His words Lord of the Worlds, the most fitting of His attributes to follow that is the one that His words Lord of the Worlds (2) Most Gracious, Most Merciful did not contain, given the nearness of the two verses in continuity and adjacency, since His wisdom is the wisdom that no wisdom resembles. And in repeating His description as Mālik of the Day of Judgement there would be a repeating of what had already preceded of His description with it in His words Lord of the Worlds, with the two verses close together and the two attributes adjacent; and there would be in repeating this a repetition of different wordings with the same meanings, which gives a hearer of what is repeated no benefit that he needs. And that which His, majestic is His mention of His attributes, preceding His words Mālik of the Day of Judgement, lacks is the meaning in His words (Malik of the Day of Judgement), and that is His description as the King.

It is evident then that the sounder of the two readings and the more deserving of the two interpretations of the Book is the reading of one who reads (Malik of the Day of Judgement), in the sense of sovereignty belonging purely to Him on the Day of Judgement, rather than the reading of one who reads Mālik of the Day of Judgement in the sense (1) that He owns judgement between them and the decision of the verdict, alone in it among all His creation. If someone supposes that His words Praise be to God, Lord of the Worlds are a report of His ownership of them in this world and not the Hereafter, so that it was necessary (2) [to connect it] (3) with the report from Himself that He (4) owns them in the Hereafter as He owns them in this world, by His words Mālik of the Day of Judgement, he has erred (5) and supposed wrongly. For if it were permissible for someone to suppose that His words Lord of the Worlds are restricted in their meaning to the report of His lordship (6) over the world of this life and not the world of the Hereafter, with no indication that the meaning is so, in the apparent text of the revelation, or in a report transmitted from the Messenger, may God bless him and grant him peace, or in an argument found in reason, it would be permissible (7) for another to suppose that it is restricted to the world of the time in which Lord of the Worlds was sent down, to the exclusion of all the worlds that come into being after it in subsequent times; since it is sound, by what we presented earlier (8) in explanation, that the world of every time is other than the world of the time that follows it.

And if someone of dull understanding is heedless of knowing the soundness of this by what we presented, then in the words of God, majestic is His praise: And We gave the Children of Israel the Book, and judgement, and prophethood, and provided them with good things, and preferred them over the worlds [al-Jāthiya 45:16], there is a clear indication that the world of every time is other than the world of the time that was before it and the world of the time that comes after it, since God, majestic is His praise, preferred the community of our Prophet Muḥammad, may God bless him and grant him peace, over all the past communities, and told them of this in His words: You are the best community brought forth for mankind [Āl ʿImrān 3:110], the verse. It is thus known that the Children of Israel in the age of our Prophet, may God bless him and grant him peace, were not, while denying him, the best of the worlds; rather the best of the worlds in that age and after it until the Hour were the believers in him who followed his way, to the exclusion of the other communities that denied him and strayed from his way.

Since the falsity of the interpretation of an interpreter who would interpret His words Lord of the Worlds as meaning that God is the Lord of the worlds of the time of our Prophet Muḥammad, may God bless him and grant him peace, to the exclusion of the worlds of all other times, is evident, the falsity of the statement of one who claimed that its interpretation is: Lord of the world of this life, not the world of the Hereafter, is also evident; and likewise that Mālik of the Day of Judgement deserved to be joined to it, so that it be known that in the Hereafter He has the same ownership and lordship over them as He had in this world. The one who claims this is asked for the difference between him and an arbitrary person like him in the interpretation of His words Lord of the Worlds, who arbitrarily said (1): He meant only that He is the Lord of the worlds of the time of Muḥammad, to the exclusion of the worlds of other times, past before him and coming after him, like what the author (2) of this statement asserts that He [meant by it the worlds of] (1) this life to the exclusion of the worlds (2) of the Hereafter, in terms of a basis or an indication.

He will say nothing about one of the two without being held to the same about the other. As for one who claims that the interpretation of His words Mālik of the Day of Judgement is that He is the one who has power to establish the Day of Judgement, what we pressed on the author of the preceding statement applies equally to him, since the establishing of the resurrection is the restoration of the creatures who have perished to their forms in which they were before destruction, in the abode (3) in which He (4) prepared for them what He prepared; and they are the worlds of whom He, majestic is His mention, informed that He is their Lord in His words Lord of the Worlds. As for the interpretation of this in the reading of one who reads (Mālika yawmi l-dīn), he meant: O (5) Mālika of the Day of Judgement. He put it in the accusative with the intent of vocative and invocation, as He, majestic is His praise, said: Yūsuf, turn away from this [Yūsuf 12:29], with the sense: O Yūsuf, turn away from this.

And as the poet of Banū Asad said, which is a verse, it is said, of the Jāhiliyya (6): If you have accused me (7) of it falsely, Jazʾ, then may you meet its like quickly. He means: Yā Jazʾ (O Jazʾ). And as another said (8): You lie, by the House of God, you will not marry her, O sons of the one whose two braids (1) have turned grey, who ties the udder (2) and milks. He means: O (3) sons of the one whose two braids have turned grey. What led him into reading this with the accusative of the kāf of (Mālika), in the sense I described, was his confusion over how to direct His words You alone we worship and You alone we ask for help along its course, while Mālik of the Day of Judgement is in the genitive (4). He supposed that its meaning would not be sound after the genitive Mālik of the Day of Judgement, so he put it in the accusative, (Mālika yawmi l-dīn), so that You alone we worship would be an address to Him, as if he meant: O Mālik of the Day of Judgement, You alone we worship and You alone we ask for help.

Had he known the interpretation of the beginning of the surah, that Praise be to God, Lord of the Worlds is a command from God to His servant (5) to say it, as we mentioned before of the report from Ibn ʿAbbās that Gabriel said to the Prophet, may God bless him and grant him peace, from God: Say, O Muḥammad: Praise be to God, Lord of the Worlds (2) Most Gracious, Most Merciful (3) Mālik of the Day of Judgement, and say also, O Muḥammad: You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help (6), and had he understood from the Arabs that it is their habit, when they relate, or command the relating of, a report that follows saying, to address and then report [about an absent person] (7), and to report about an absent person and then return to address, because relating by saying has the sense of both the absent and the addressed, as they say to a man: I said to your brother: If you stood, I would stand; and: I said to your brother: If he stood, I would stand, it would have become easy (1) for him to find the way out of what he found difficult to direct, the genitive Mālik of the Day of Judgement.

And among the like of Mālik of the Day of Judgement in the genitive, followed by a return to the address with You alone we worship, as (2) we mentioned before, is the well-known verse of Abū Kabīr al-Hudhalī (3): Alas for my soul! the freshness (4) of Khālid, and the whiteness of your face, given to the dusty (5) earth! He returned to the address with his words “and the whiteness of your face” after the report about Khālid had been in the manner of a report about an absent person. And of this is the verse of Labīd b. Rabīʿa (6): The soul (7) spent the night complaining to me, about to weep (8), when I have carried you seven years after seventy. He returned to addressing himself, after the report about it had preceded in the manner of a report about an absent person. And of this are the words of God, who is the most truthful in speech and the firmest proof: Until, when you are in the ships and they sail with them with a good wind [Yūnus 10:22].

So He addressed and then returned to reporting about the absent, and did not say: and they sail with you. The witnesses from verse and Arab speech for this are more than can be counted, and what we have mentioned suffices for one granted understanding of it. So the reading (1) (Mālika yawmi l-dīn) is forbidden and impermissible, because of the consensus of the authoritative body (2) of the reciters and scholars of the community on rejecting recitation by it.

02

On “the Day of Judgement ”

al-Ṭabarī’s heading

The Discussion of the Interpretation of His words, majestic is His mention: the Day of Judgement (4).

Abū Jaʿfar said

Dīn in this place has the sense of reckoning and recompense for deeds, as Kaʿb b. Juʿayl said (3): If they shoot at us we shoot at them, and we repay them (dinnāhum) as they lend to us. And as another said (4): [Know and be certain that your sovereignty will pass away] (5), and know that as you requite (tadīnu), you will be requited (tudānu). He means: as you recompense, you will be recompensed. And of this are the words of God, majestic is His praise: No indeed, but you deny the Dīn, meaning the recompense, and indeed there are over you guardians [al-Infiṭār 82:9, 10], who record what you do of deeds. And His words, Exalted: Then why, if you are not to be requited (madīnīn) [al-Wāqiʿa 56:86], meaning not to be recompensed for your deeds nor reckoned with. Dīn has meanings in the speech of the Arabs other than the meaning of reckoning and recompense, which we shall mention in their places, God willing.

The reports from the early exegetes have come with what we said in the interpretation of His words the Day of Judgement, with the witnesses confirming the interpretation they gave of it (1).

About the edition

Abū Jaʿfar Muḥammad ibn Jarīr al-Ṭabarī (d. 310 AH), Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān. The English of this surah is from the supplied English translation, Tafsīr al-Ṭabarī = Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān (version 1422 AH / 2001 CE, first edition).

The supplied source identifies the Dār Hajar edition and its editors but does not name the English translator.

Translation passages are presented by verse; paragraph, report and quotation groupings are editorial. The English wording is preserved.

The editor's notes are those of ʿAbd Allāh al-Turkī’s edition (Dār Hajr, 2001), cited by volume, page and note number. Verse numbering follows the Kufan count; the Fātiḥa verse wording follows the supplied English translation of al-Ṭabarī.

How this page is set out: the commentary is read into blocks (the commentator's own explanation, transmitted reports with their chains, quoted verses, objections and answers, cited poetry) and grouped into sections. These divisions, their titles and the highlighted verse wording are editorial; the words are unchanged, except that a backtick standing for the letter ʿayn is set as ʿ.
