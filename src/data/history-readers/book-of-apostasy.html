Chapter 1

The upheaval among the people at the death of the Prophet

In the name of God, Most Gracious, Most Merciful, and in Him we seek help.

Abū l-Qāsim ʿAbd Allāh b. Ḥafṣ b. Mihrān al-Bardaʿī, may God honor him, related, saying: Abū Muḥammad Aḥmad b. Aʿtham al-Kūfī narrated to me, by reading to him, saying: Abū Jaʿfar ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz b. al-Mubārak narrated to me, saying: Nuʿaym b. Muzāḥim al-Minqarī narrated to me, saying:

Muḥammad b. ʿUmar b. Wāqid al-Wāqidī al-Aslamī narrated to me. Ibrāhīm b. ʿAbd Allāh b. al-ʿAlāʾ al-Qurashī al-Madanī also narrated to me, saying: Aḥmad b. al-Ḥusayn al-Kindī, Naṣr b. Khālid al-Naḥwī, and Abū Ḥamza al-Qurashī narrated to me, from Muḥammad b. Isḥāq b. Yasār al-Muṭṭalibī, saying: al-Zuhrī, Zayd b. Rūmān, and Ṣāliḥ b. Kaysān and Yaḥyā b. ʿUrwa narrated to me, from al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām, Maḥmūd b. Labīd, and ʿĀṣim b. ʿUmar b. Qatāda. Each of them relates that when the Prophet ﵌ was taken, the Jews and Christians gloated over the people of Islam. Hypocrisy appeared in Medina among those who had concealed it before, and the people surged and fell into disorder.

Mālik b. al-Tayyihān al-Anṣārī came forward and stood before his people. He said:

“Company of the Anṣār, be silent and listen to my words, and understand what I put to you. Know that the Jews and Christians have gloated over the death of our Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ and the spite of the people of apostasy (ḥasīka) has appeared. The worst of our calamities is that Musaylima the Liar has come out in the land of the Yamāma with thunder and lightning. You know that he claimed prophethood in the lifetime of our Prophet ﵌. Now word has reached me that Ṭulayḥa b. Khuwaylid al-Asadī has also claimed prophethood in the land of Najd. By God, I fear that the tribes of the Arabs will turn away from the religion of Islam. If a man of the Banū Hāshim or a man of Quraysh does not take charge of this matter, then by God it is ruin and destruction.”

Then Abū l-Haytham began to recite:

He said: Then Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq ﵁ turned to the Muslims and said:

“People, whoever worshipped God, God is living and does not die. Whoever worshipped Muḥammad, Muḥammad has died. By God, God said to Muḥammad ﷺ in His words ﵎: You will surely die, just like they will die. (39:30). Then He said: We never decreed immortality for anyone before you; should you die, are they immortal? Every soul will taste death (21:34–35). Then He said: Muḥammad was no more than a messenger like the messengers before him. Should he die or get killed, would you turn back on your heels? (3:144, the verse). Muḥammad ﷺ has gone his way, and this matter must have someone to take charge of it. So deliberate, consider, and bring what you have, God have mercy on you.”

Reports of the Saqīfa of Banū Sāʿida

He said: People called out to him from every side: “We will wake in the morning and consider that, God willing.” The people went their way that day. The next morning a group of the Muhajirun gathered around Abū Bakr ﵁ and a group of the Anṣār gathered around Saʿd b. ʿUbāda al-Khazrajī in the Saqīfa of Banū Sāʿida. ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib, God honor his face, sat in his house, grieving over the Prophet, with a number of Banū Hāshim, among them al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām. Muslims gathered from all quarters of Medina to hear what the Muhajirun and the Anṣār would say.

The first of the Anṣār to speak that day was Khuzayma b. Thābit, Dhū l-Shahādatayn. He said: “Company of the Anṣār, you have put Quraysh ahead of yourselves, and they will go ahead of you until the Day of Resurrection. You are the Anṣār in the Book of God, mighty and glorious, and to you the hijra was made, and among you is the grave of the Prophet ﵌. So unite your affair on a man whom Quraysh fears and the Anṣār trust.”

The Anṣār said: “You have spoken truly, Khuzayma. What you say is right. We are content with our man, Saʿd b. ʿUbāda.” The Muhajirun frowned and looked at one another.

Then Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr al-Anṣārī al-Awsī sprang up. His word was accepted among the Anṣār and he was obeyed among them. He said: “Company of the Anṣār, God’s favor to you is great, since He named you the Anṣār and made the hijra come to you, and in your midst the Messenger Muḥammad ﷺ was taken. So make that for God. This matter lies with Quraysh and not with you. Whom they advance, advance, and whom they put back, put back.” A group of the Anṣār sprang at him, spoke harshly to him, and silenced him, and he fell silent.

Then Bashīr b. Saʿd al-Anṣārī, the one-eyed, who was also among the best of the Anṣār, sprang up and said: “Company of the Anṣār, you are what you are through Quraysh, and Quraysh through you. If what you claim were your right, it would not be put to you as a question. If you say that we gave shelter and help, then what God has given them is better than what He gave you. So do not be like those who responded to God’s blessings by disbelieving, and thus brought disaster upon their own families (14:28).”

Then ʿUwaym b. Sāʿida al-Anṣārī sprang up. He was one of the group about whom God sent down, concerning the mosque of Qubāʾ, In it, there are people who love to be purified. God loves those who purify themselves. (9:108). He said: “Company of the Anṣār, you were the first to fight for the religion, so do not be the first to fight its people over it. The caliphate belongs only to the people of prophecy.

So put it where God, mighty and glorious, put it, for they have the prayer of the prophet Abraham ﵇.”

Then Maʿn b. ʿAdī al-Anṣārī sprang up and said: “Company of the Anṣār, if this matter is yours apart from Quraysh, tell them so, so that they pledge to you over it. If it is theirs and not yours, hand it over to them. By God, the Messenger of God ﵌ did not die before Abū Bakr ﵁ led us in prayer. So we knew that he had approved him for us, because prayer is the pillar of religion.”

While the Anṣār were still in this exchange, Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, and Abū ʿUbayda b. al-Jarrāḥ arrived, with a group of the Muhajirun following them ﵃. Saʿd b. ʿUbāda lay wrapped in garments in the Saqīfa of Banū Sāʿida because of an illness he felt in his body, and some of the Anṣār stood around him, wanting nothing else in his place.

He said: The Muhajirun sat and were silent for a while without saying anything. Then [Thābit b.] Qays b. Shammās al-Anṣārī spoke. He was the orator of the Anṣār and had been so since the time of the Prophet ﵌. He said:

“Company of the Muhajirun, you know and we know that God ﵎ sent His Prophet Muḥammad ﵌ and that at the beginning of his mission he stayed in Mecca under harm and denial, and God, mighty and glorious, commanded him only to restraint and gracious forbearance. Then after that He commanded him to emigrate and prescribed fighting for him and moved him from his home. So we were his helpers, and our land was his place of emigration and rest. Then you came to us, and we shared our property with you and spared you labor, we lodged you in our homes and preferred you in the comforts of life. We are the helpers of God and the battalion of Islam, and we are those about whom God ﵎ sent down: As for those who provided them with a home and a refuge, and were believers before them, they love those who immigrated to them, and find no hesitation in their hearts in helping them. In fact, they readily give them priority over themselves, even when they themselves need what they give away. (59:9). There are other verses in the Book of God, mighty and glorious, that no one can deny us.

Furthermore, you know what the Prophet ﵌ said about us of noble merits. He left the world without appointing a specific man as successor. He did not entrust the people to anyone; he entrusted them only to God, mighty and glorious, through the Book and the comprehensive Sunna. And God ﵎ does not unite this community upon error. So we are the helpers of God, and ours is the leadership (imāma) over the people. Bring what you have, company of the Muhajirun. Peace.”

When Thābit b. Qays had finished speaking, Abū Bakr ﵁ turned to him and said: “Thābit, by my life, you are just as you described your people, and no one can deny them that. We are those about whom God, mighty and glorious, sent down: They were evicted from their homes and deprived of their properties, because they sought God’s grace and pleasure, and because they supported God and His messenger. They are the truthful. (59:8), in the Book of God, mighty and glorious. God has honored you by making you among the truthful, in His words: O you who believe, you shall reverence God, and be among the truthful. (9:119). And furthermore, you know that the Arabs acknowledge this matter only for Quraysh, because they are the middle of the Arabs in homeland and have the prayer of Abraham ﵇. I approve for you one of these two men, ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb and Abū ʿUbayda b. al-Jarrāḥ. Pledge to whichever of them you wish.”

Thābit b. Qays said: “Company of the Muhajirun, are you content with what Abū Bakr says?” They said: “We are content.” He said: “Men, you must not ascribe disobedience of his Messenger to Abū Bakr.” They said: “How is that?” He said: “Because you said that the Messenger of God ﵌ chose him and approved him for you in his lifetime and put him forward to lead the prayer, and he did this only because he had made him successor over you. So Abū Bakr disobeyed the Messenger of God by removing himself from the caliphate. And he said, ‘I approve for you one of these two men, ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb and Abū ʿUbayda b. al-Jarrāḥ.’ How can these two be your model, when the Messenger of God ﵌ chose him and preferred him over them? Perhaps it is you, company of the Muhajirun, who disobeyed God in your testimony about your Prophet, that he made Abū Bakr his successor.”

The Muhajirun said: “You know, company of the Anṣār, that the first to worship God on the face of the earth and to believe in the Messenger of God ﵌ were his friends and his clan, and they are the people most entitled to this matter after him. Only a wrongful aggressor contests it with them. As for you, company of the Anṣār, we do not deny your merit or your precedence in Islam. God named you the helpers of the religion and made the hijra come to you. No one after the first Muhajirun is dearer to us than you. We are the amirs and you are the viziers. You will not be preempted in counsel, and affairs will not be decided without you.”

Then al-Ḥubāb b. al-Mundhir b. al-Jamūḥ al-Anṣārī sprang up and let out a shout among his cousins. Then he said: “Company of the Anṣār, take care that you are not deceived out of your right. By God, God was not worshipped openly except in your lands, and the prayer was not gathered except in your mosques, and the Arabs did not submit to faith except by your swords. So today you have the greatest share in the religion and the merit in Islam, and you are the people most entitled to this matter. If these men refuse what we say, then from us an amir and from you an amir.”

Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr and Bashīr b. Saʿd al-Anṣārī, the two Anṣārīs, sprang up and said: “What a bad thing you said, Ḥubāb. It is not sound counsel that two amirs should rule one land, with one opposing the other.” Ḥubāb said: “By God, Usayd and Bashīr b. Saʿd, I intended only your honor. Since you refuse, I am with you. If anything they dislike should come, we will come to these two as emigrants, and the matter will be yours.”

Then Ḥubāb b. al-Mundhir began to recite (ṭawīl):

  1. Ibn Ḥuḍayr strove in corruption for his own need,and Bashīr was quicker than he in corruption.
  2. The two think we have committed a grave act,while their own concern in what is sought is small.
  3. They became small only through what came from them,and their concern, but for the corruption, would be great.
  4. But whoever does not watch over his people isfew, abased, and, as far as I know, contemptible.
  5. O Ibn Ḥuḍayr and Ibn Saʿd, both of youare well versed in what men mean by it.
  6. Do you not know, God bless your father,that people are only blind or seeing,
  7. that we, and the enemies of the Prophet Muḥammad,are lions whose roar is heard in both thickets?
  8. We helped and sheltered the Prophet, and he hadno helper but us among the people of the two faiths.
  9. We ransomed him with sons, our blood and our wealth,while the polytheists were many.
  10. We were for him, in every matter he wanted,sharp arrows whose harm is forbidden.
  11. Who, then, is more entitled to it than a companywho are thus ... soldier and vizier.
  12. It was a grave thing that I said: from them an amir,and from us, Bashīr, an amir.

When al-Ḥubāb b. al-Mundhir had finished his poem, ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb ﵁ turned to him and said: “Yes, Ḥubāb, you said a grave thing. Two swords do not fit in one sheath. The Arabs will not agree to make you their amirs when their Prophet is from someone other than you. They will make amir those among whom prophecy was. What you said, Ḥubāb, corrupts religion and this world together. God is one, Islam is one, religion is one, and affairs are set right only on one. If two imams rule today, two imams will rule tomorrow, and it is not permissible for Islam to be anything but one. So fear God, and hand this matter to the man on whom the Muhajirun and the Anṣār of Quraysh agree.”

Ḥubāb b. al-Mundhir said: “Company of the Anṣār, do not listen to this man and his companions, or your share in this matter will be lost. If these men refuse you, drive them out of Medina and take charge of these affairs over them, for the Arabs knew your might and your protection in the Jāhiliyya and in Islam. By God, no one will answer me after this without my striking his nose with the sword.”

ʿUmar said: “Then God will kill you, Ḥubāb.” Ḥubāb said: “No, it is you he will kill, ʿUmar.” [ʿUmar said:] “All the Arabs know that you are the helpers of God and of His Messenger Muḥammad ﷺ. You are our brothers in Islam and our partners in religion. By God, we were never in good or in ill without you being with us in it. You are the dearest of people to us and the most honored of creation to us. You are those who ‘prefer others over themselves in want’. By God, you have always preferred your brothers among the Muhajirun with your wealth. It is incumbent on you that the disunity of this community and its breaking apart should not come at your hands. And furthermore, you should not envy your brothers a good that God drove to them.” Then ʿUmar said: “Saʿd is not fit for it.”

Thābit said: “Not so, ʿUmar. Saʿd is fitter for it than anyone else and more entitled to it, because the house is his house and you are guests with him.” Then Ḥassān b. Thābit sprang up and said (basīṭ):

  1. Let Quraysh not deny the merit of our manSaʿd. There is no crookedness in my words today.
  2. Quraysh said, “The authority is ours, not yours.Let no one today covet this matter.”
  3. We said to them, “Prove a right, and we will follow it.We want nothing else, ever.
  4. If you have a covenant with a cause behind itafter the Messenger, then what we said is not false.
  5. And if you have no covenant, then he hasthe veterans of Badr and the people of the glen of Uḥud.”
  6. We are those who struck the people with a broad blowuntil they stood straight and were the “egg of the land”.
  7. Every day we have an affair that we win,for which God gave the Garden of Eternity.
  8. You are not more entitled to it than we, because we havein the middle of Medina the merit of might and numbers.
  9. And on the day we sold our selves to God,no fear remained for property or child.
  10. People are at war with us, and all peopleare like foxes that fear the attack of the lion.

The Muhajirun clamored and the Anṣār clamored, until some of them moved against others. Maʿn b. ʿAdī al-Anṣārī sprang up and quieted the people. Then he said: “Company of the Muhajirun, by God, no one among God’s creation is dearer to us than you. But we fear what will come after this. What is nearer to justice in the community of Muḥammad ﵌ [is what] he used to say: ‘The imams are from Quraysh, and this will be only among them’.”1

Bashīr b. Saʿd al-Anṣārī said: “Yes, by God, we heard that from him ﷺ and I know that his people are the holders of command after him. By God, God will not see me contesting this matter with them. So fear God, company of the Anṣār, and do not oppose them.”

Abū Bakr ﵁ said: “You did well, God have mercy on you and reward you well for Islam. I do not want this matter. Here are ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb and Abū ʿUbayda b. al-Jarrāḥ ﵄. Pledge to whichever of them you wish.”

ʿUmar and Abū ʿUbayda said: “No one but you will take charge of this matter. You are the best of the Muhajirun, the second of the two in the cave, and the deputy of the Messenger of God ﵌ over the prayer. Who would go ahead of you and take charge of this matter over you? Stretch out your hand so that we may pledge to you.”

Bashīr b. Saʿd al-Anṣārī said: “By God, no one pledges to him before me.” He stepped forward and clapped his hands on Abū Bakr’s hands in pledge. Al-Ḥubāb b. al-Mundhir said to him: “Bashīr, what drove you to do what you did? Did you begrudge your cousin Saʿd b. ʿUbāda being amir?” Bashīr said: “No, by God. I disliked contesting a people over a right that God gave to them and not to me.”

Al-Ḥubāb b. al-Mundhir put his hand to his sword and drew it from its sheath and was about to do something. The Anṣār rushed to him, took hold of his hand, and calmed him. He said: “You calm me when you have done what you have done! By God, I can see your sons standing at their doors asking people for water and not being given any.”

Abū Bakr ﵁ said: “And when do you fear that, Ḥubāb?” He said: “I do not fear it from you. I fear those who come after you.” Abū Bakr said: “When that happens and you see what you dislike, then the matter at that time is yours.” Ḥubāb said: “Far from it, Abū Bakr! How could that be, once you and I have gone and a people come after us who inflict evil torment on our sons? God is the one whose help is sought.”

The Anṣār followed one another in pledging to Abū Bakr ﵁ and the Khazraj especially were broken, because of what they had resolved concerning their man Saʿd b. ʿUbāda. Then al-Ḥārith b. Hishām began to recite (kāmil):

  1. Return the sharp blade to its sheath, Nawār!The wrangling has ended and the Anṣār have pledged.
  2. They are a people who helped the Messenger Muḥammad,while all the people disbelieved in him.
  3. They gave their lives and shared their property,so we have homes and estates from them.
  4. They pressed forward with Saʿd for the caliphateafter the hearts inclined and the eyes swerved.
  5. O Saʿd, Saʿd of Banū ʿUbāda, leave it,pardon, and let excess not be your lot.
  6. What your soul promised you in solitudeis a shame on you, and in your wish is ruin.
  7. The caliphate is in Quraysh, not yours.Yours is a standing among us, and the house.
  8. To you came the emigration, and that whichthe Aws and al-Najjār preceded to.
  9. And the Khazrajites, whose spears are the enemy’s poison,and among whom are the best.
  10. They are the protectors when wars blaze,the sufficers, the lords, the free men.

The people crowded to pledge to Abū Bakr until they nearly trampled Saʿd b. ʿUbāda underfoot. A man of the Anṣār said: “People, beware of Saʿd, for he is ill, severely ill.” A man of the Muhajirun then began to recite (kāmil):

  1. Thanks be to him who deserves praise:the wrangling is gone, and the Ṣiddīq has been pledged to.
  2. After a deed slipped for Saʿd,and he hoped a hope with al-ʿAyyūq [the star] beneath it.
  3. The Anṣār surrounded him, his head bound,and al-Ṣiddīq and al-Fārūq forbade them,
  4. and Abū ʿUbayda, and those toward whomthe soul of the one hoping for survival yearns.
  5. They made it good with the right course and pledgedan old man sound in his opinion,
  6. after they had arranged Saʿd’s affair,and no creature took steps like theirs.
  7. The caliphate is in Quraysh. By the Lord of Muḥammad,they have no tafrūq in it.

ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf al-Zuhrī came up and stood over a group of the Anṣār. He said: “Company of the Anṣār, though you have the merit, nobility, and help that you mention, and by God none of it is denied you, there is none among you like Abū Bakr and ʿUmar, or ʿUthmān, or Abū ʿUbayda b. al-Jarrāḥ. It is not incumbent on you to place on a level in merit one who is better than you.”

Zayd b. Arqam al-Anṣārī said to him: “Ibn ʿAwf, we do not deny the merit of those you name. Among us is the master of the Khazraj, Saʿd b. ʿUbāda. Among us is the master of the Aws, Saʿd b. Muʿādh, [the one] at whose death the Throne shook. Among us is Ubayy b. Kaʿb, the best reciter of his time. Among us is the one who will come as imam of the scholars on the Day of Resurrection, Muʿādh b. Jabal. Among us is the best expert in the law of inheritance shares of his age, Zayd b.

Thābit. Among us is the one whom the hornets protected, Khubayb b. ʿAdī. Among us is the one washed by the angels, Ḥanẓala b. Abī ʿĀmir. Among us is the one whose testimony the Messenger of God ﵌ validated as the testimony of two, Khuzayma b. Thābit. And there are others whose affair is not hidden from you, whom it would take long to enumerate, with what they did before the Messenger of God ﵌. Ibn ʿAwf, were it not that ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib ﵁ and others of Banū Hāshim were occupied with burying the Prophet ﵌ and with their grief for him, so that they sat in their houses, none of those who coveted it would have coveted it. So go away, and do not stir up against your companions what you cannot withstand.”

He said: ʿAbd al-Raḥmān went back to Abū Bakr and told him what he had said to the Anṣār and how they had answered him. Abū Bakr said to him: “You could have done without this: going to a people who had pledged and fallen silent, and reminding them of what had passed.”

Then Abū Bakr sent for ʿAlī and summoned him. He came while the people were present, greeted them, and sat. Then he turned to the people and said: “Why did you call me?” ʿUmar said to him: “We called you for the pledge on which the Muslims have agreed.” ʿAlī said: “Men, you took this matter from the Anṣār by the argument against them, and by kinship to Abū Bakr ﵁. You claimed that Muḥammad ﵌ was from you, so they gave you the lead and handed the matter over to you. I argue against you with the same argument you used against the Anṣār. We are nearer to Muḥammad ﵌ living and dead, because we are his household and the nearest of creation to him. If you fear God, be fair to us and acknowledge for us in this matter what the Anṣār acknowledged for you.”

ʿUmar ﵁ said to him: “You, man, will not be left alone until you pledge as others have pledged.” ʿAlī ﵁ said: “Then I will not accept from you, and I will not pledge to a man whom I am more entitled to the pledge than.” Abū ʿUbayda b. al-Jarrāḥ said to him: “By God, Abū l-Ḥasan, you are fit for this matter because of your merit, your precedence, and your kinship. But the people have pledged and have accepted this old man, so accept what the Muslims accepted.” ʿAlī, God honor his face, said to him: “Abū ʿUbayda, you are the trustee of this community, so fear God for your own sake. This day has days after it. It is not right for you to take the authority of Muḥammad ﵌ out of his house and the depths of his home, into your houses and the depths of your homes. In our houses the Qurʾān came down. We are the mine of knowledge, jurisprudence, religion, Sunna, and the inheritance shares, and we know the affairs of creation better than you. So do not follow desire, or your share will be the lowest.”

He said: Bashīr b. Saʿd al-Anṣārī spoke and said: “Abū l-Ḥasan, by God, had the people heard this speech from you before the pledge, no two men would have differed over you, and all the people would have pledged to you. But you sat in your house and did not attend to this matter, so the people thought you had no need of it. Now the pledge has already gone to this old man, and you are at the head of your own affair.”

ʿAlī said to him: “Woe to you, Bashīr. Was I to leave the Messenger of God ﵌ in his house and not bring him to his grave, and go out to contest people for the caliphate?”

Abū Bakr ﵁ turned to him and said: “Abū l-Ḥasan, had I known that you would contest this matter with me, I would not have wanted it or sought it. The people have pledged. If you pledge to me, that is what I expect of you. If you do not pledge at this time and wish to consider your affair, I will not compel you to it. Go in good guidance whenever you wish.”

He said: ʿAlī ﵁ went back to his house and did not pledge until Fāṭima ﵂ died. He then pledged seventy-five nights after her death. Some say it was after six months, and God knows which it was.

“This, God honor you, is what happened at the Saqīfa of Banū Sāʿida, and this is the narration of the scholars. I did not wish to write here any of the additions of the Rāfiḍa, so that this book would not fall into the hands of anyone but you and you would be ascribed to some position. May God protect you. We return to what followed the Saqīfa of the fighting against the people of the apostasy, and God is the granter of success to what is right.”

Chapter 2

Accounts of the Apostasy

“People, I have been given authority over you though I am not the best of you. If I do well, help me. If I do wrong, set me straight. Truthfulness is a trust and lying is betrayal. The weak among you is strong in my eyes until I secure his right for him, and the strong among you is weak in my eyes until I take the right from him. No people abandon jihād in the way of God, mighty and glorious, without God striking them with humiliation, and indecency does not spread among a people without God afflicting them all with calamity. Obey me as long as I obey God, and when I disobey Him, you owe me no obedience.”

He then came down from the minbar, led the people in prayer, and went into his house. Only a few days passed before the Arabs turned back on their heels as unbelievers. Some of them apostatized and claimed prophethood, and some apostatized and withheld the zakāt.

The Banū Asad apostatized and made Ṭulayḥa b. Khuwaylid al-Asadī their chief. He is the one who claimed prophethood in the land of Banū Asad. Fazāra apostatized and made ʿUyayna b. Ḥiṣn al-Fazārī their chief. Banū ʿĀmir and Ghaṭafān apostatized and made Qurra b. Salama al-Qushayrī their chief. Banū Sulaym apostatized and made al-Fujāʾa b. ʿAbd Yālīl al-Sulamī their chief. A part of Banū Tamīm apostatized and made a woman called Sajāḥ their chief.

A part of Kinda apostatized and made al-Ashʿath b. Qays and other kings of Kinda their chiefs. Banū Bakr b. Wāʾil in the land of Bahrain apostatized and made al-Ḥakam b. Zayd of Banū Qays b. Thaʿlaba their chief. Banū Ḥanīfa gathered around Musaylima the Liar in the land of the Yamāma, put their affair in his hands, and he claimed to be their prophet.

The news reached Abū Bakr and he was distressed. He hurried to the mosque, called out among the Arabs, and stood to speak to the people. He praised God and extolled Him, then said: “People, I am only a man among you. I suffice as you suffice and I defend as you defend. You are my partners in this matter, so bring what you have of opinion.”

ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb ﵁ stood and said: “Caliph of the Messenger of God ﵌ the Arabs have turned back on their heels as unbelievers, as you know. You want to send out the army of Usāma b. Zayd, and in Usāma’s army are the multitudes of the Arabs and their heroes. If you kept it with you, you would be strengthened by it against those of these Arabs who have apostatized.”

Abū Bakr ﵁ said: “If I knew that beasts of prey would eat me in this city, I would still send out the army of Usāma b. Zayd, as the Prophet ﵌ said: ‘Proceed, army of Usāma’. Say, “Nothing happens to us, except what God has decreed for us. He is our Lord and Master. In God the believers shall trust.” (9:51). As for those of these Arabs who have apostatized, some of them do not pray, having disbelieved in prayer, and some of them pray but have withheld zakāt. No, by God, Abū Ḥafṣ, I do not distinguish between prayer and zakāt, because the two are paired.”

ʿUmar said to him: “Caliph of the Messenger of God, if you were to close your eyes and overlook the zakāt of these Arabs this year and be gentle with them, I hope they would return from what they are on. I know the Prophet ﵌ used to say: ‘I was commanded to fight people until they say, There is no god but God, and I am the Messenger of God. When they say it, they have protected from me their blood and their property, except by its right, and their reckoning is with God’.”

Abū Bakr ﵁ said: “By God, if they withheld from me even a hobble (ʿiqāl) of what the Prophet ﵌ used to take from them, I would fight them over it forever, as long as I live. We will fight them forever until God fulfills His promise and keeps His covenant with us. He said, and His word is true and is not broken: God promises those among you who believe and lead a righteous life, that He will make them sovereigns on earth, as He did for those before them, and will establish for them the religion He has chosen for them, and will substitute peace and security for them in place of fear. All this because they worship Me alone; they never set up any idols beside Me. (24:55).”

ʿUmar said: “Caliph of the Messenger of God, God has opened your breast to fighting these people. We hear and obey.”

The people followed Abū Bakr’s opinion. Then al-Ḥārith b. Hishām al-Makhzūmī began to recite (kāmil):

  1. ʿUmar held an opinion, and God will carry out His purpose,and al-Ṣiddīq opposed his opinion,
  2. when he said, “Shut your eyes a little in guidance,and be gentle, for you are gentle in affairs,
  3. and pass over their property.” But success refused himanything except the fighting of his enemy.
  4. What our souls were set upon in the misfortunes of warsstill yearns for it.
  5. The caliph’s word: “Fight your enemies.The base thing is the retreat of hindrance.”
  6. By God, if they withheld a single hobble,or a tafrūq slipped from what he holds,
  7. I would strike a people with the tribes and the lances,who withheld the zakāt, and I am maḥūq
  8. in fighting them, in small number or large,as long as the feathered arrow has a notch.
  9. How great is His favor to us, a favorin which, for war on our enemy, there is precedence.
Chapter 3

The Departure of Usāma b. Zayd

He said: Then Abū Bakr ﵁ turned to Usāma b. Zayd, who was encamped outside Medina, and said to him: “Go, God have mercy on you, on your course, which the Prophet ﵌ commanded you. Do not fall short in your affairs. If you see fit to let ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb stay with me, I find company in him and seek help in his opinion.” Usāma said: “I have done so.”

Usāma went with his army to the place the Prophet ﵌ had ordered him to go, and he met no unbeliever there. So he returned to Medina. Abū Bakr ﵁ had meanwhile resolved to fight the people of the apostasy and to go out against them himself. The Muslims tried to dissuade him and said: “Caliph of the Messenger of God, we adjure you by God not to go out against them yourself. You know the condition of the people. If you perish, it is the perishing of the Muslims. Instead write to ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ and stay in Medina yourself, and let him come to you from Oman. Write also to Abān b.

Saʿīd and have him come to you from Bahrain. Gather the armies to you, then join them to a man of the Muhajirun or of the Anṣār whom you know for courage and toughness, and send him against the enemies of God, the apostates. Perhaps God ﵎ will give you victory over them.”

Then Abū Bakr wrote to ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ, who was then in Oman, where the Prophet had appointed him earlier. When Abū Bakr’s letter reached him, he turned to the people of Oman and said: “Men, you know that the Prophet ﵌ sent me to you as governor, amir, and summoner. You accepted the matter and answered the call to Islam, and you were on what God and His Messenger love. But the Messenger of God ﵌ has died, and Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq ﵁ has taken charge of the Muslims’ affairs. Whoever obeyed the Prophet living must obey him dead. This apostasy has occurred, and I know that Abū Bakr will fight them until he returns them to the religion of Islam. Here is his letter, commanding me to come to him. What opinion do you have?”

Abū Ṣufra, whose name was Ẓālim b. Sarrāq, sprang up and said: “ʿAmr, we obey you today with yesterday’s obedience, and we will obey you tomorrow with today’s obedience. We have not disobeyed the one who sent you to us. Peace.” Then ʿAbbād b. al-Julandī sprang up and said: “ʿAmr, the choice is not ours, but the choice belongs to God, mighty and glorious, and to His Messenger Muḥammad ﵌. He chose you and sent you to us. Obedience to him dead is like obedience to him living. We do not dislike your staying with us, but the matter is yours. Peace.”

Then Jaʿfar b. Khaythām sprang up and said: “ʿAmr, the Messenger of God ﵌ sent you to us, you called us, and we answered you. If the Messenger has died, God, mighty and glorious, is living and does not die. If you stay with us, we will obey you, and if you wish to travel, we will give you safe-conduct (khafar). Peace.”

ʿAmr said: “May God reward you well. You have spoken and spoken well. I would like you to give me safe-conduct.” They said: “We will do that.”

ʿAmr made ready and set out, with Abū Ṣufra Ẓālim b. Sarrāq, Jufayr b. Jaʿfar, and ʿUbāda b. al-Julandī [as the text names them], with seventy horsemen from the notables of Oman. ʿUqba b. al-Nuʿmān al-ʿAtakī began to recite about this (ṭawīl):

  1. We kept faith with ʿAmr on the day of ʿAmr, as though he werean outcast driven away by Madhḥij and the Sakāsik.
  2. The messenger of the Messenger of God! How great is his rightover us, and whoever does not know the right perishes.
  3. We brought him back, so that Luʾayy b. Ghālib was not reviledthrough him now, when the roads had closed in on him.
  4. ʿIbād and Jayfar took him in, from our side,and Ẓālim, the mūdī toward whom the vagabonds turn.
  5. So ʿAmr woke in Medina safe,roaring with laughter, with cushions heaped about him.
  6. We are a people among whom the neighbor is safeon a day when the sun is eclipsed and black.
  7. By that the women of goodness charged their people,and ʿImrān, and the guardian of the sacred, Mālik.

He said: ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ also praised them in a poem of his in which he says (ṭawīl):

  1. I say, with the house of Fihr b. Mālik around me:may God reward the Azd for me with the best reward.
  2. I came to Oman while misfortunes were many,and it is not a land of mine, nor a sky.
  3. So welcome to the Azd, lords of bounty,people of sincere gift and of fidelity.
  4. ʿIbād and Jayfar took me in from among them,and Ẓālim, the summoner to every height.
  5. I came to the people of Medina safe,trailing in it my waist-wrapper and my cloak,
  6. at a time when Maʿadd boiled with apostasy,and the riffraff of this tribe, the tribe of Ḍibāʾ,
  7. with nothing between us but swords, and at timesbi-hasm and the ropes of the slaughtered camel, thirsty,
  8. deaths brought near, ours and theirs,in every state, with nothing hidden.
  9. The mill of death turns among us and among them,in a turn of annihilation or a turn of survival.

He said: The men came to Medina and went in to Abū Bakr ﵁ and greeted him. Then they took ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ by the upper arm and said: “Caliph of the Messenger of God, and company of the Muslims, this is our amir ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ whom the Messenger of God ﵌ sent. We are thankful to him. This was a trust that lay on our necks and a deposit with us, and we have discharged it to you. Peace.”

Abū Bakr ﵁ and the Muslims praised them well and rewarded them well. A man of Quraysh then began to recite (khafīf):

  1. O ʿIbād, and son of Sāriq of the good,O Jufayr b. Jifr, best of chiefs,
  2. you did what the Azd were given good news of,and you had been in that with Islam,
  3. and you brought ʿAmr back when the people had turnedfrom religion, the deed of a noble people.
  4. Yemenis, with trust among the Azd,readiness of hospitality, and good speech,
  5. and by good neighborliness people were preferred,by protecting the preserve and the slaying of ḥamām.
  6. You went in fidelity on the best of journeys,with eyes to the outcomes of days,
  7. from Oman to Medina while the peopleroamed in blindness and darkness,
  8. with the messenger of the Prophet, when the matter grew greatand the steady minds went faint.
  9. When he reached Medina, ʿAmr, you said:“We have paid obligation after obligation.”
  10. So peace be upon you as long as the wind blowsand as long as the bereaved doves moan.
  11. We have discharged the right of the journey to him,and we have discharged to him the right of protection.

He said: The Muslims and Abū Bakr ﵁ were glad of ʿAmr’s arrival among them. He said: Abū Bakr ﵁ wrote to Abān b. Saʿīd to summon him from the land of Bahrain, where the Prophet ﵌ had sent him as amir. When the letter reached him he called to the people of Bahrain and gathered them, read them Abū Bakr’s letter, and said: “You know that the people of Oman kept faith with their companion ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ.” Then a man from the chiefs of ʿAbd al-Qays called al-Jārūd b. al-Muʿallā sprang up and said: “Abān, you know that our Islam was voluntary, without fighting, “...so God ﵎ sent down about us: Are they seeking other than God’s religion, when everything in the heavens and the earth has submitted to Him, willingly and unwillingly, and to Him they will be returned? (3:83). You know that we carried the zakāt of our property to the Messenger of God ﵌ before anyone else carried it to him. If you stay with us we will obey you, and if you leave us we will give you safe-conduct.”

Then Harim b. Ḥayyān al-ʿAbdī sprang up. He was at that time the shaykh of ʿAbd al-Qays and the eldest of them. He said: “Abān, God, mighty and glorious, has increased our number through Islam and strengthened our hearts and tongues with it, so we do not fear our enemies among the people. You refuse to stay in our land. If something about us made you uneasy, we will make you safe from it. If you fear you cannot manage our governance, we will help you with our own persons. If you want something better than what you now have, we will spend our wealth for you.”

Then al-Mundhir b. ʿĀʾidh al-ʿAbdī sprang up. He is the one whom the Prophet ﵌ made chief over the delegation of ʿAbd al-Qays when they came to him. He said:

“Abān, your staying with us is better for you and for us. If you wanted to stay for your own sake, we would follow your desire, but we want you for our own sake, and your leaving us is disobedience to your imam and a reproach to us. If you insist on leaving us, you are neither commanded to go nor driven out.”

Abān b. Saʿīd said: “May God reward you well, company of ʿAbd al-Qays. By God, I have seen no trait of goodness that is not found in you. If I stayed among you, I would know that you would protect me with what you protect yourselves and your sons. But here is the letter of Abū Bakr ﵁ which has reached me, and joining him is obligatory. This apostasy has occurred, and I want to be one of the hands of the Muslims against it.”

The people agreed. Abān b. Saʿīd left Bahrain, and with him went Harim b. Ḥayyān, his brother Ṣabāḥ b. Ḥayyān, al-Jārūd b. al-Muʿallā, al-Ashajj b. ʿĀʾidh, ʿAbd Allāh b. Sawwār, and al-Ḥārith b. Murra. These men went out with him, thirty horsemen from the notables of ʿAbd al-Qays. Abān began to recite (majzūʾ al-ramal):

  1. May al-Jārūd be rewarded wellon behalf of Abān b. Saʿīd,
  2. and Ṣabāḥ and his brotherHarim, the best of chiefs,
  3. and the Ashajj of the people, master of nobilityand sound opinion,
  4. and may al-Ḥārith be rewarded afterwardwith a reward and an increase,
  5. and Ibn Sawwār, how excellent a manin the year of hardship.
  6. They submitted willingly and restrainedevery rebellious devil,
  7. and kept the covenant and the protectionand the praiseworthy conduct.
  8. Their wishes will come to themfrom near and from far.
  9. The praise that has worn thin in meis new again.

He said: The men went with Abān until they brought him safely to Medina. A man of ʿAbd al-Qays began to recite about it (ṭawīl):

  1. Abān came to us while troubles were many,as amir, and we said, “Welcome, Abān.”
  2. The messenger of the Messenger of God! How great is his rightover every ʿAdnānī and every Yemeni.
  3. We obeyed and did not disobey Abān by a fingernail-paring,and no hurt from us reached him by tongue.
  4. In every matter he wished we were for himlike two sucklings at the breast of Abān’s mother.
  5. When news of the Prophet Muḥammad’s death came,a doubt born of events overcame him.
  6. We told Abān to stay where he was,with confidence in his affair and clarity.
  7. We said to him: Bahrain is a bright land,with religion and this world in it, at any time.
  8. The neighbor of ʿAbd al-Qays among them is never betrayed,as long as the heights of ʿIdān stand.
  9. When he refused anything but to join his people,we set for him what the people of Oman had set.
  10. Thirty riders took him into his people, while the people were men of lances.

He said: When ʿAbd al-Qays came to Abū Bakr ﵁ with Abān b. Saʿīd, Abū Bakr and the Muslims praised them well. Abān b. Saʿīd said: “By God, Caliph of the Messenger of God, I did not leave the people and go out because of anything I disliked in them. They are on the religion of Islam. They have not changed or altered. They offered me to stay among them, but your letter reached me and I answered you willingly, and I wanted to be with you against the people of the apostasy.”

People kept gathering to Abū Bakr ﵁ from every quarter, seeking nearness to him and to God, exalted, by fighting the people of the apostasy.

The tribes of Ṭayyiʾ were about to apostatize from the religion of Islam. Their chief ʿAdī b. Ḥātim al-Ṭāʾī stood and said: “Company of Ṭayyiʾ, if you hold to the religion of Islam, you will gain this world and the next. If you go back from it, you will lose this world and the next, and God will do without you. You know that God ﵎ has taken your Prophet Muḥammad ﵌ and this is his caliph, who has taken charge of his affair in his community.

So give him your alms in full and do not withhold them, because withholding them destroys wealth and brings the appointed term near. Hurry to fight the apostates of Asad, Ghaṭafān, and Fazāra, for the caliph has resolved to raid them. They are their [the people’s] chiefs (aqyāl) in the Jāhiliyya and their brave men in Islam2, and today you are better than you were yesterday. Peace.”

Then ʿAdī b. Ḥātim began to recite (ṭawīl):

  1. Truly, the people of this religion have come to beon something like a sword’s edge after Muḥammad.
  2. This is neither from abasement nor from fearfor religion and world, but to fulfill a promise.
  3. We were stricken by the Prophet, and our nightis long, like the night of the sore-eyed, bewildered man.
  4. And though all of Fazāra seethe,and Dhubyān, in a surge of foaming sea,
  5. and Ṭulayḥa, refuge of every misguided heretic,has let out for them the trains of his delusion,
  6. we will leave them with the horses until we set them straightwith hard lances and Indian blades,
  7. and until they confess that prophethoodis from God, truly, and the Book is for Aḥmad.
  8. It has pleased me about you, tribes of Ṭayyiʾ:your defense of this religion against every aggressor,
  9. and your selling of your wealth and your persons,hoping for what God will repay tomorrow,
  10. and your giving of what you owed in almswithout a fight by tongue or hand.

When his poem ended, Zayd al-Khayl al-Ṭāʾī sprang up and said: “Company of Ṭayyiʾ, know that you are like the crown (iklīl) of Muḍar, and the people nearest to you are Asad and Ghaṭafān. You were their brave men in the Jāhiliyya, and God has disowned them because they went back from the religion of Islam and withheld the zakāt. This is Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq, the caliph of the Messenger of God ﵌ and he has resolved to send Khālid b. al-Walīd against them with the Muhajirun and the Anṣār. So be his cutting sword, his piercing spear, and his true arrow.”

The tribes of Ṭayyiʾ answered him in all he wished. Zayd al-Khayl then recited (ṭawīl):

  1. God refused what you fear, sister of Banū Naṣr.Abū Bakr has taken charge of the clear matter.
  2. The confidant of the Messenger of God, alone in the cave,and his companion the Ṣiddīq in the weight of the great affair.
  3. Umāma, the people are covered in a trialthat will be upon them like the bellowing of a young camel.
  4. Banū Asad after Dhubyān: Ṭulayḥahas turned them back from error into unbelief.
  5. Tell Banū Badr when you meet them:Since when were you the tails, house of Banū Badr?
  6. If you withhold the due of the zakāt and leave prayer,in these two is the back-breaker.
  7. We are a target for the misfortunes of events,and no living tribe is like us in hardship and ease.
  8. We fight you for God until we set you straightwith hard lances and tawny blades,
  9. and until they say, “It was only ...”

He said: ʿAdī b. Ḥātim and Zayd al-Khayl gathered what they had of sadaqa camels and other animals and came with it to Abū Bakr ﵁. The people of Medina were alarmed at the number of sadaqa camels they saw, and thought it was an army that had come against them. ʿAdī b. Ḥātim then came forward, greeted Abū Bakr ﵁ and said: “Caliph of the Messenger of God ﵌ do you know me?”

Abū Bakr said: “Yes, you are ʿAdī b. Ḥātim, who became Muslim when they disbelieved, came forward when they turned back, and kept faith when they betrayed. I know you and I know your companion Zayd al-Khayl, and if I had not known you both, God would have made you known.”

ʿAdī b. Ḥātim said: “Caliph of the Messenger of God, we obeyed the Messenger of God by obeying God, and we obeyed you by obeying the Messenger of God ﵌. Here are the tribes of Ṭayyiʾ. We have brought them to you, and we are setting out to fight the people of the apostasy if you resolve on it. No one possesses power except God (18:39).”

Abū Bakr prayed good for them and praised them well.

Then a youth of Ṭayyiʾ stepped forward and began to recite (ṭawīl):

  1. We kept faith with a faithfulness no people ever saw the like of,and ʿAdī b. Ḥātim clothed us in glory.
  2. Zayd al-Khayl was in it the son of a free woman,an enemy to whoever was hostile and at peace with the peaceable.
  3. The two brought back to al-Ṣiddīq the cattle of Ṭayyiʾ,both of them discerning of the heights and winners of noble deeds.
  4. We have the word of the two sincere advisers,which the riders tell of to the people of the seasons.
  5. Truly, this religion is obedience to God,so cast to whoever wants you your crimes.
  6. And Mālik: away with the Tamīmī Mālik,and the companion of Qays the oppressor b. ʿĀṣim.
  7. Nor [was it right] what al-Badrī and his people did in it,ʿUyayna. That opinion is the opinion of the fools.
  8. They persisted, and were in many suspicions;when you uncover them you will knock the tooth of a regretful man.
  9. When Khālid came to them with his hoststhey called to one another and bit their thumbs.
  10. In the meeting they all becametales of Ṭasm or like muddled dreams.

He said: Then al-Zibriqān b. Badr al-Tamīmī came to his people of Banū Saʿd and said:

“Company of Banū Zayd Manāt, our Prophet ﷺ has gone his way. This is Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq ﵁ who has taken charge of the matter after him, and he has resolved to send Khālid b. al-Walīd against those who turned away from this religion and withheld the zakāt. You have heard what the Banū Āl Ṭayyiʾ did, how they answered him to the truth and paid the zakāt. So fear God for your own sakes, do not shed your own blood, and do not reject my words, for I am your adviser.”

A man of his people said to him: “Man, by God, we are more entitled to our alms than Abū Bakr. We gathered them for you and handed them over so that you would take them to Muḥammad ﵌. Now he has gone his way, so return our alms.” Al-Zibriqān b. Badr was angry at this. He said: “What a bad thing you thought, Banū Tamīm! I should return these camels? I took them only for God and in the right of God, mighty and glorious, which is the zakāt due from your property. By God, I will never return them to you. I will go to Abū Bakr, so do as you please.”

Then al-Zibriqān began to recite (ṭawīl):

  1. Qays and Khindif know that I kept faithwhen the horseman of war held back.
  2. I did what God knows, when it is mentioned,to be more chaste and more honorable.
  3. I married her off from the house of Ḥarq, and she wokescattering pebbles with her hands, [her wealth] shattered.
  4. Men said, “Leave our alms.”I said, “Yes, that is what breeds blindness.”
  5. Shall I take them for God and then return themto you? You were ignorant in speech, and how bad ...
  6. You thought, Banū Saʿd b. Zayd, ...with the elders in the clan, that no sin would rise against us.
  7. God refused for me that I should be wretched by returning themto you while you are not wretched, and that I be wretched as colocynth.
  8. By God’s praise, I did not turn back from your enemywhen the near kin about me took bodily form.
  9. I am ashamed before Badr and his shaykhthat they should in any state be blamed and cursed.

Then al-Zibriqān b. Badr came to Abū Bakr ﵁ with the zakāt of his people. Whenever one of the chiefs of his people came to Abū Bakr, he took the zakāt from him and attached him to Khālid b. al-Walīd, until Khālid had a large army.

Then Abū Bakr appointed Masʿūd to guard Medina and keep watch over it, and ordered him to let none of the bedouin enter Medina or come near it. Abū Bakr marched out with the Muslims and pitched his camp at a place called al-Jurf. Then Abū Bakr ﵁ called Khālid b. al-Walīd ﵄ and tied a banner for him and joined the army to him. Then he said: “Khālid, march toward Ṭulayḥa b. Khuwaylid al-Asadī and those with him of Banū Asad, Ghaṭafān, and Fazāra. When you reach the people and alight in their lands and hear the call to prayer, do not fight anyone until you have given them your excuse and warned them. Then slip word to their amirs and nobles and give them money according to their rank. When you come upon them, do not camp by them in daytime, or they will see your army and learn who is in it. Camp by them at night, at the time they sleep. Then let your camels graze, shake your weapons, and put as much terror in them as you can. If God gives you victory over Ṭulayḥa b. Khuwaylid and his companions, go on to al-Buṭāḥ in the land of Tamīm, to Mālik b. Nuwayra and his companions. [Perhaps] I will come to you from another direction if we are able, God willing. There is no power except with God, the High, the Mighty.”

Khālid said: “Caliph of the Messenger of God, when I reach the people, to what shall I call them?” He said: “Call them to ten things: the testimony that there is no god but God alone, who has no partner, and that Muḥammad is His servant and messenger; the establishing of prayer; the giving of zakāt; the fast of Ramaḍān; the pilgrimage to the House; commanding the good; and forbidding the evil; obedience; and the community.”

Then Abū Bakr ﵁ wrote to them:

“In the name of God, Most Gracious, Most Merciful. From ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿUthmān, caliph of the Messenger of God ﵌ to everyone to whom this my letter is read, of the elite and the common, whether he stayed in his Islam or went back from it. Peace on whoever follows guidance and returns from error and ruin. I testify that there is no god but God alone, who has no partner, and I testify that Muḥammad is His servant and messenger, whom He sent with the guidance and the religion of truth and will make it dominate all religions, in spite of the idol worshipers (9:33), and to preach to those who are alive, and to expose the disbelievers (36:70). God guides whoever turns to Him and strikes with the truth whoever turns away from Him and goes back.

I counsel you to fear God, and I call you to what your Prophet Muḥammad ﵌ brought. You know that whoever does not believe in God is astray, whoever God does not give security is fearful, whoever God does not protect is lost, whoever does not believe him is a liar, whoever He does not make happy is wretched, whoever He does not provide for is deprived, and whoever He does not help is forsaken. So be guided by the guidance of God your Lord and by what your Prophet ﵌ brought, Whomever God guides is the truly guided one, and whomever He sends astray, you will not find for him a guiding teacher. (18:17).

News has reached me that some of you have gone back from your religion after confessing Islam and acting by its laws, out of delusion about God, mighty and glorious, ignorance of His command, and obedience to Satan. The devil is your enemy, so treat him as an enemy. He only invites his party to be the dwellers of Hell. (35:6). Now, I have sent Khālid b. al-Walīd to you with an army of the Muhajirun and the Anṣār, and I have ordered him not to fight anyone until he calls him to God, mighty and glorious, gives his excuse, and warns. Whoever enters obedience, hurries to the community, returns from disobedience to what he knew of the religion of Islam, then repents to God, exalted, and does good, God accepts that from him and helps him in it.

Whoever refuses to return to Islam after Khālid b. al-Walīd has called him and given his excuse, I have ordered him to fight him with the utmost fighting, in person and with those with him of the helpers of God’s religion and His aides. He is to leave no one he can reach without burning him with fire, to take the children and women captive, and to seize the property. He who has warned has given his excuse. Peace be upon the believing servants of God, and there is no power except with God, the High, the Mighty.”

He said: Then he folded the letter, sealed it, gave it to Khālid, and ordered him to act on what was in it.

  9. and the host of the tyrants Banū Faqʿas,and the host of the insolent Banū Dāhir.

He said: Yazīd b. Ḥudhayfa also wrote to his cousins with these verses (ṭawīl):

Chapter 4

Account of al-Fujāʾa b. ʿAbd Yālīl

He said: Khālid b. al-Walīd set out toward Banū Asad. A man of Banū Sulaym called al-Fujāʾa b. ʿAbd Yālīl came to Abū Bakr ﵁ went in to him, greeted him, and said: “Caliph of the Messenger of God ﵌ I am a Muslim man and I have been on the religion of Islam since I was, and I have neither changed nor altered. I am eager to fight the people of the apostasy, and I would like you to help me with a force of horses and weapons, so that I may distribute them among my people and my cousins of Banū Sulaym, join Khālid b. al-Walīd, and fight Ṭulayḥa b. Khuwaylid and his companions with him.”

Abū Bakr ﵁ gave him ten horses and a great quantity of weapons, swords, spears, bows, and arrows, and sent ten Muslims with him. Al-Fujāʾa left Medina as though making for Khālid b. al-Walīd. Then he left the road to Khālid and turned to the abodes of Banū Sulaym. He sent for some of them and they answered. He set them on the ten men who had gone with him and killed them to the last. Then he distributed those horses and the weapons Abū Bakr had given him among those of the foolish men of his people who followed him, and went on killing people, sparing neither his own people nor others, reciting (wāfir):

  1. Have you not seen how I deceived the people untilI grew strong with the weapons I took?
  2. I said to him, “Abū Bakr, help meagainst those at al-Buzākha and al-Buṭāḥ,
  3. and I said: I will fight whoever disobeys youand help you against the people of sin.”
  4. He strengthened me with every lean, big-bodied mountand white blades like the lightning’s flashes, and spears.
  5. I fell with them on the far ones with slaughter,and on the near ones are the marks of wounds.
  6. I see no sin in killing my own people,nor in killing the distant.
  7. Only that I say, when the cares of the soulcome upon me from every side:
  8. “Death will meet me rising upamong the pillars of men, the men of arms.”
  9. That is my nature: I am passionatelyfond of preferring corruption to righteousness.

He said: Al-Fujāʾa kept doing what he did, and people of depravity and corruption gathered to him. The news reached Abū Bakr ﵁. He turned to those with him of Banū Sulaym and other Qays ʿAylān and told them what al-Fujāʾa had done. Banū Sulaym especially were severely distressed and said: “By God, Caliph of the Messenger of God, we had a presentiment of some of this. The enemy of God has put on us by his deed a shame that will never be washed off us.”

Then al-Ḍaḥḥāk b. Sufyān al-Kilābī sprang up. He was an elder of Banū Kilāb, their horseman, chief, and poet, and had been a Companion of the Messenger of God ﵌. He said: “By God, Caliph of the Messenger of God, the enemy of God was bent on corruption. I never thought he would dare such a thing. I used to warn my people of Banū Dhakwān not to listen to him or follow his opinion, but God ﵎ refused anything but what He wished.”

Then al-Ḍaḥḥāk b. Sufyān began to recite (ṭawīl):

  1. Alas for my people at the events of this age,and a people’s agreement with al-Fujāʾa on unbelief!
  2. A misled man called a people of the misled to a trial,and a man may perish from where he does not know.
  3. I told my people: he will throw youtomorrow, Banū Dhakwān, into the depth of the sea.
  4. You will have from him, so do not rouse him,a grim day, hotter than the coals.
  5. When he called them, he entrusted his secretto them, and made the horses stride in the empty land.
  6. God fight al-Fujāʾa! He has comewith his great treachery, a grave matter.
  7. Al-Ṣiddīq thought well of him, and he betrayed himand trailed the garments of betrayal and deceit.
  8. Deceit afflicts only its own people.So is God’s decree in the firm Books.
  9. I am ashamed before God to be seen,in any circumstance, set against Abū Bakr,

He said: Then Abū Bakr ﵁ wrote to Khālid b. al-Walīd, told him what al-Fujāʾa had done, what weapons he had taken, and how many Muslims he had killed, and ordered him to send a force to look for him and bring him wherever he might be.

The account of Mālik ibn Nuwayra and the Liar Musaylima

He said: When Khālid ibn al-Walīd had finished fighting Banū Asad, Ghaṭafān, and Fazāra, and God had given him power over them, he turned to the Muslims who were with him and said, “You know that the successor of the Messenger of God ﵌ had ordered me to go to al-Biṭāḥ, in the territory of Banū Tamīm, to Mālik ibn Nuwayra and his companions. I am setting out; what is your opinion?” He said: The Anṣār replied, “Abū Sulaymān, we do not suspect you of anything, but Abū Bakr gave us no instruction concerning this. If he ordered you to march to Banū Tamīm, then go, may you be rightly guided; we are not setting out.” Khālid said, “I will not compel you to do anything. I am setting out with the Emigrants who are with me, until I carry out Abū Bakr’s order.”

He said: Khālid then marched with the Emigrants who were with him, intending for the territory of Banū Tamīm, while the Anṣār remained in their positions. When Khālid had marched for that day, it was as though he was distressed that the Anṣār had stayed behind.

He said: The Anṣār also reproached one another. Then some said to others: “By God, if a calamity befalls this army tomorrow, it will be a disgrace to us; people will say that you abandoned the Emigrants and handed them over to their enemy. And if they win a victory, it will be a good thing from which you were deprived. So march and catch up with your brothers.”

He said: The Anṣār marched until they caught up with Khālid ibn al-Walīd, and the people became one body. Khālid entered the middle of the territory of al-Biṭāḥ. At al-Biṭāḥ that day was a man from the notables of Banū Tamīm, called al-Jufūl, because he had driven off the zakāt camels and withheld the alms. He began saying to his people:

“O Banū Tamīm, you know that Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd Allāh had placed me in charge of your alms before his death. Muḥammad has died and gone his way, and this affair must have someone to take charge of it. So do not let anyone covet your property; you have more right to it than anyone else.” He said: Some of his people blamed him for that, while others praised him and affirmed that his judgment was sound.   4. Otherwise, we are no truffle in an open wasteland, nor the fat of sheep or gazelles on a stony plain.
