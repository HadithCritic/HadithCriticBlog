Entry1003/ 14
Muntaqā Ibn al-Jārūd · p. 345
English

Muhammad b. Yahya narrated to us; he said: 'Abd al-Razzaq narrated to us; he said: Ma'mar informed us, from Hammam b. Munabbih; he said: This is what Abu Hurayra, ؓ, narrated to us from Muhammad, the Messenger of God ﷺ, and the Messenger of God ﷺ said: A vow does not bring the son of Adam anything I had not already decreed for him, but the vow meets him with what I had decreed for him; through it I draw out (wealth) from the miser, and he gives it to Me, what he had not given Me before.

Matn

لا يأتي النذر ابن آدم بشيء لم أكن قد قدرته له ، ولكن يلقيه النذر قد قدرته له ، أستخرج به من البخيل ، يؤتيني عليه ما لم يكن أتاني من قبل

Entry1004/ 14
Muntaqā Ibn al-Jārūd · p. 345
English

'Ali b. Khashram narrated to us; he said: Isma'il—meaning Ibn 'Ulayya—narrated to us, from Ayyub, from Abu Qilaba, from Abu l-Muhallab, from 'Imran b. Husayn, ؓ, who said: Thaqif were allies of Banu 'Uqayl. Thaqif captured two men from the Companions of the Messenger of God ﷺ, and the Companions of the Messenger of God ﷺ captured a man of Banu 'Uqayl, and took with him al-'Adba' (the she-camel). The Messenger of God ﷺ passed by him while he was in bonds, and he said: O Muhammad! O Muhammad! He came to him and said: What is the matter with you? He said: Why did you seize me, and why did you seize the swift one of the pilgrims? He said (as an exaggeration of that): I seized you for the crime of your allies, Thaqif. Then he turned away from him, and he called out to him: O Muhammad! O Muhammad! He said: And the Messenger of God was merciful and gentle, so he returned to him and said: What is the matter with you? He said: I am a Muslim. He said: Had you said it while you were in control of your affair, you would have been completely successful. Then he turned away from him, and he called out to him: O Muhammad! O Muhammad! He came to him and said: What is the matter with you? He said: I am hungry, so feed me, and thirsty, so give me drink. He said: This is your need. He said: Then he was ransomed in exchange for the two men. A woman of the Ansar was captured and al-'Adba' was taken. The woman was in bonds, and the people used to graze their livestock in front of their houses. One night she slipped free from the bonds and came to the camels; whenever she approached a camel, it groaned and she left it, until she reached al-'Adba', which did not groan—she was a docile camel. She sat on her hindquarters, then urged her on, and she set off. They noticed and pursued her, but she outran them. He said: She vowed that if God saved her, she would sacrifice her. When she arrived in Medina, the people saw her and said: al-'Adba', the she-camel of the Messenger of God ﷺ! She said: She vowed that if God saved her, she would sacrifice her. They came to the Prophet ﷺ and mentioned that to him, and he said: Glory be to God! What a wretched repayment of her—that God saved her, and she would sacrifice her? There is no fulfillment in disobedience to God, nor in what a servant does not own.
