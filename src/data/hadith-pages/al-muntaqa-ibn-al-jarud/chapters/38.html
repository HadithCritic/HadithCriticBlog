Muntaqā Ibn al-Jārūd · p. 72
English
Isnad

Ibn al-Muqri', Harun b. Ishaq and Yusuf b. Musa narrated to us; they said: Sufyan informed us, from al-Zuhri, from Salim, from his father:

Matn

That he saw the Prophet ﷺ, when he began the prayer, raise his hands until they were level with his shoulders, and [do so] when he was about to bow and after he raised his head from bowing; and he did not raise them between the two prostrations.

Matn

Muntaqā Ibn al-Jārūd · p. 72
English
Isnad

Muhammad b. Yahya narrated to us, saying: Ya‘qub b. Ibrahim b. Sa‘d narrated to us, saying: the son of the brother of Ibn Shihab narrated to me, from his paternal uncle, who said: Salim b. ‘Abdullah informed me that ‘Abdullah b. ‘Umar (ؓ) said:

Matn

When the Messenger of God (ﷺ) stood for prayer, he raised his hands until, when they were level with his shoulders, he said the takbir. Then, when he wanted to bow, he raised them until they were level with his shoulders and said the takbir while they were thus, and bowed. Then, when he wanted to raise his back, he raised them until they were level with his shoulders; then he said: “Sami‘a Allahu li-man hamidah.” Then he would prostrate, and he did not raise his hands in prostration; and he raised them in every rak‘a and every takbir he made before bowing, until his prayer was finished.

Matn

كان رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم إذا قام إلى الصلاة رفع يديه حتى إذا كانتا حذو منكبيه كبر ، ثم إذا أراد أن يركع رفعهما حتى يكونا حذو منكبيه كبر وهما كذلك فركع ، ثم إذا أراد أن يرفع صلبه رفعهما حتى يكونا حذو منكبيه ، ثم قال : سمع الله لمن حمده ، ثم يسجد فلا يرفع يديه في السجود ، ورفعهما في كل ركعة وتكبيرة كبرها قبل الركوع حتى تنقضي صلاته

Muntaqā Ibn al-Jārūd · p. 73
English
Isnad

Muhammad b. Yahya narrated to us; he said: Hajjaj b. Minhal and Abu Salih, the scribe of al-Layth, narrated to us,

Matn

both together, from 'Abd al-'Aziz b. 'Abd Allah b. Abi Salama, from his paternal uncle al-Majishun b. Abi Salama, from al-A'raj, from 'Ubayd God b. Abi Rafi', from 'Ali b. Abi Talib, ؓ, from the Messenger of God ﷺ: That when he began the prayer he would say the takbir, then say: “I have turned my face toward the One who created the heavens and the earth, as a hanif, and I am not of the polytheists. Indeed, my prayer, my sacrifice, my living and my dying are for God, Lord of the worlds; He has no partner; and of this I have been commanded, and I am the first of the Muslims. O God, You are the King; there is no god but You. You are my Lord and I am Your servant; I have wronged myself and acknowledged my sin, so forgive me all my sins; none forgives sins but You. Guide me to the best of character; none guides to the best of it but You; and turn away from me its evil; none turns away its evil but You. At Your service and Your pleasure; all good is in Your hands, and evil is not attributed to You. I am by You and to You. You are blessed and exalted. I seek Your forgiveness and repent to You.” And when he bowed, he would say: “O God, to You I have bowed, in You I have believed, to You I have submitted; my hearing, my sight, my brain, my bones and my sinews are humbled before You.” And when he raised his head, he would say: “God hears whoever praises Him; our Lord, to You is the praise, filling the heavens and filling the earth, and filling whatever You wish thereafter.” And when he prostrated, he would say: “O God, to You I have prostrated, in You I have believed, to You I have submitted; my face has prostrated to the One who created it and shaped it and made its shape excellent, and opened its hearing and its sight; so blessed is God, the best of creators.” And when he had finished his prayer and given the salam, he would say: “O God, forgive me what I have sent ahead and what I have left behind, what I have concealed and what I have declared, what I have been excessive in, and what You know better than I. You are the Advancer and the Delayer; there is no god but You.” Abu Salih said, in both [places]: “there is no god for me but You.”

O God, I seek refuge in You from the accursed Satan: from his puffing (nafkh), his spitting (nafth), and his whispering/prodding (hamz).”

Ishaq b. Mansur narrated to us; he said: 'Abd al-Rahman—meaning Ibn Mahdi—narrated to us, from Za'ida b. Qudama, from 'Asim b. Kulayb, who said: My father informed me that Wa'il b. Hujr informed him; he said:

Matn

I said: I will surely watch the Messenger of God ﷺ to see how he prays. He said: So I watched him. He stood and said the takbir and raised his hands until they were level with his ears. Then he placed his right palm on the back of his left palm, the wrist, and the forearm. Then he bowed and raised his hands likewise. Then he prostrated and placed his palms level with his ears. Then he sat, spreading out his left foot, and placed his left palm on his left thigh and knee, and rested the edge of his right elbow on his right thigh. Then he closed two of his fingers and formed a ring, then raised his finger, and I saw him moving it as he supplicated. Then I came afterward in a time of cold, and I saw the people, wearing heavy garments, moving their hands from beneath the garments.
