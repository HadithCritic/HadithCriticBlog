Matn

Introduction. In the name of God, the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful. Praise belongs to God; we praise Him, seek His help, and ask His forgiveness, and we seek refuge in God from the evils of our souls and from our evil deeds. Whomever God guides, none can lead astray, and whomever He leads astray, none can guide. To proceed: The fascicle of “Sufyan b. 'Uyayna” is among the important hadith heritage of Islam, for several reasons, among them, by way of example and not exhaustive enumeration: 1 - The elevation of the fascicle ('uluww al-juz'), an important feature unique to these great fascicles. 2 - The presence of some important chains of transmission that may render a hadith sound (sahih) or fair (hasan); this too is among the most noble features found in these fascicles. 3 - The presence of some rare benefits that are found only in these fascicles. And there are other important reasons that prompted me to examine a group of hadith fascicles, among them this valuable fascicle. They said of old: “Editing a venerable manuscript is better than composing a feeble book.” So I have preferred the former over the latter, relying upon the help of God Most High and His grant of success; indeed, He is capable of all things.

Matn

Definition of the Juz'. The word juz' in the language means a part of a thing; likewise, that of which a thing and other things are composed; likewise, a share. Its plural is ajza'. See: al-Mu'jam al-Wasit (1/124), Mukhtar al-Sihah (101, 102), and al-Misbah al-Munir (1/137–138, al-Matba'a al-Amiriyya edition). In technical usage: it is a small book comprising one of two things: First: a collection of the hadiths narrated from one of the Companions, or from those after them, such as: (a) “Juz' of What Abu Hanifa Narrated from the Companions” by Abu Ma'shar 'Abd al-Karim b. 'Abd al-Samad al-Tabari, who died in 478 AH. (b) “A Juz' Containing the Hadith of Abu al-'Ushara' al-Darimi” by Abu al-Qasim Tammam b. Muhammad al-Razi, who died in 414 AH. Second: a collection of what some of the leading memorizers among the imams narrated of hadiths relating to a single topic, treated extensively and exhaustively, such as: (a) the juz' on raising the hands in prayer; (b) the juz' on recitation behind the imam. Both are by Imam al-Bukhari. And there are many other fascicles of this kind. See: “Usul al-Takhrij wa-Dirasat al-Asanid” by Dr. Mahmud al-Tahhan (137, Dar al-Kutub al-Salafiyya edition).

Methodology of the Edition. My methodology in the edition was as follows: 1 - I provided a biography of the author of the fascicle, Sufyan b. 'Uyayna ﵀. 2 - I provided a biography of the narrator of this fascicle, Abu Yahya Zakariyya b. Yahya al-Marwazi. 3 - I provided biographies of all the narrators of the chain of transmission. 4 - I traced the hadiths and reports (athar) contained in the fascicle, to the extent of my ability. 5 - I established the text and corrected the body of the book, which is among the difficult tasks. 6 - I explained the rare words (gharib) and the obscure, unintelligible matters in the hadiths. 7 - I commented on some of the hadiths from the juristic perspective. 8 - I mentioned the hadith-related benefits that it — that is, the fascicle — contains. 9 - I prepared the scholarly indexes for it. Finally, I ask God for help and support; indeed, He is capable of that. Written by: Abu 'Abd al-Rahman Mis'ad b. 'Abd al-Hamid b. Muhammad al-Sa'dani al-Sharqawi al-Salafi, may God pardon him and his parents.

Biography of the Author of the Fascicle, Sufyan b. 'Uyayna. He is: the imam, Shaykh of Islam and of the hadith scholars, and one of the luminaries of the community, Abu Muhammad Sufyan b. 'Uyayna b. Maymun b. Abi 'Imran al-Hilali al-Kufi al-Makki. He was born in Kufa in the year 107 AH. Sufyan is counted among the Kufans and among the mawali (clients), his clientship being to Muhammad b. Muzahim, brother of al-Dahhak b. Muzahim. He was blind in one eye, and it is said that Abu 'Uyayna was known by the kunya Abu 'Imran. Sufyan heard from each of the following: 'Amr b. Dinar, Ibrahim b. 'Uqba, Ishaq b. 'Abd Allah b. Abi Talha, Isma'il b. Abi Khalid, Ayyub al-Sijistani, Zakariyya b. Abi Za'ida, Salama b. Dinar, 'Abd Allah b. Tawus, Abu Ishaq al-Sabi'i, al-Zuhri, Zayd b. 'Ilaqa, al-Aswad b. Qays, 'Ubayd Allah b. Abi Yazid, 'Asim b. Abi al-Najud, Zayd b. Aslam, Ibn al-Munkadir, and others. Narrating from him were: al-A'mash, Ibn Jurayj, and Shu'ba — who were among his own shaykhs — as well as Ahmad b. Salih al-Misri, Ishaq b. Rahawayh, 'Ali b. al-Madini, Abu Kurayb Muhammad b. al-'Ala', Zuhayr b. Mu'awiya, Ya'qub b. Ibrahim, Hammad b. Zayd, Ibn al-Mubarak, 'Abd al-Razzaq al-Humaydi, Zakariyya b. Yahya b. Asad al-Marwazi — the narrator of this fascicle —, Bishr b. Mutar, al-Zubayr b. Bakkar, Ahmad b. Shayban, and others.

Matn

The scholars praised him. 'Ali b. al-Madini said, as in Tarikh Baghdad (9/178): “Among the companions of al-Zuhri there was none more precise than he.” Al-'Ijli said: “A Kufan, reliable (thiqa), firmly established in hadith.” Al-Shafi'i said: “Were it not for Malik and Sufyan, the knowledge of the Hijaz would have vanished.” Ibn al-Mubarak was asked about him and said: “He is one of the singular ones.” Al-Shafi'i also said: “I have not seen anyone more restrained from issuing legal opinions (fatwa) than he.” Ibn Wahb said: “I have not seen anyone more knowledgeable of the Book of God than Ibn 'Uyayna.” It was said to 'Ali b. al-Madini: “Is he an imam in hadith?” He ﵀ said: “He has been an imam for forty years.” It was said to Yahya b. Ma'in: “Do you prefer Ibn 'Uyayna regarding 'Amr b. Dinar, or al-Thawri?” He said: “Ibn 'Uyayna knew him better.” It was said to him: “Then do you prefer Ibn 'Uyayna regarding him, or Hammad b. Zayd?” He said: “Ibn 'Uyayna knew him better.” It was said to him: “And Shu'ba?” He said: “And what did Shu'ba narrate from him? He narrated only about a hundred hadiths from him.” Ahmad b. Hanbal said: “When he was asked about the rites of pilgrimage (manasik), he was lenient, and when he was asked about divorce, he was strict.” Sufyan ﵀ has a Qur'anic exegesis (tafsir), which is printed, praise be to God. He ﵀ died on Saturday, the first of the month of Rajab, in the year 198 of the Hijra, in Mecca the Venerated. ﵀ — with a vast mercy.
