English
Isnad

Ahmad b. Khalid transmitted from Abu Ya'qub, from 'Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from al-Zuhri: “

Matn

Civil strife erupted when five men were counted among the most politically resourceful: from Quraysh, Mu'awiya and 'Amr; from the Ansar, Qays b. Sa'd; from the Emigrants, 'Abd Allah b. Budayl b. Warqa' al-Khuza'i; and from Thaqif, al-Mughira b. Shu'ba.”

Jāmiʿ Maʿmar ibn Rāshid · p. 350
English
Isnad

Ma'mar transmitted from Ishaq b. Rashid, from 'Amr b. Wabisa al-Asadi, from his father: “

Matn

I was at home in Kufa when someone at the door said, ‘Peace be upon you. May I come in?’ I invited him, and it was 'Abd Allah b. Mas'ud. I asked why he had come in the heat of midday. He said the day had grown long, so he thought of someone with whom to talk. We exchanged reports from the Messenger of God ﷺ. He said that he heard the Messenger ﷺ say: ‘There will be a time of civil strife when one who is asleep is better than one lying down, one lying down better than one sitting, one sitting better than one standing, one standing better than one walking, one walking better than one riding, and one riding better than one racing ahead. All who are killed in it will be in the Fire.’ I asked when. He said: ‘In the days of harj, when a man cannot feel safe from the person sitting beside him.’ I asked what to do if I lived to see it. He said: ‘Restrain yourself and your hand, and enter your house.’ I asked what if someone entered the house. He said: ‘Go into your room.’ I asked what if he entered my room. He said: ‘Go into your prayer place, do this’—grasping his right wrist with his hand—‘and say, “My Lord is God,” until you die holding to that.’”

العربية
Isnad

أَخْبَرَنَا عَبْدُ الرَّزَّاقِ، قَالَ أَخْبَرَنَا مَعْمَرٌ، عَنْ إِسْحَاقَ بْنِ رَاشِدٍ، عَنْ عَمْرِو بْنِ وَابِصَةَ الْأَسَدِيِّ، عَنْ أَبِيهِ، قَالَ:

Matn

إِنِّي لَبِالْكُوفَةِ فِي دَارِي إِذْ سَمِعْتُ عَلَى بَابِ الدَّارِ: السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ أَأَلِجُ؟ قُلْتُ: وَعَلَيْكَ السَّلَامُ فَلِجْ، فَلَمَّا دَخَلَ إِذَا هُوَ عَبْدُ اللَّهِ بْنُ مَسْعُودٍ، قَالَ: فَقُلْتُ: يَا أَبَا عَبْدِ الرَّحْمَنِ، أَيَّةُ سَاعَةِ زِيَارَةٍ هَذِهِ وَذَلِكَ فِي نَحْرِ الظَّهِيرَةِ، قَالَ: طَالَ عَلَيَّ النَّهَارُ فَتَذَكَّرْتُ مَنْ أَتَحَدَّثُ إِلَيْهِ، قَالَ: فَجَعَلَ يُحَدِّثُ عَنْ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ وَأُحَدِّثُهُ، قَالَ: ثُمَّ أَنْشَأَ يُحَدِّثُنِي فَقَالَ: سَمِعْتُ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ يَقُولُ: «§تَكُونُ فِتْنَةٌ، النَّائِمُ فِيهَا خَيْرٌ مِنَ الْمُضْطَجِعِ، وَالْمُضْطَجِعُ فِيهَا خَيْرٌ مِنَ الْقَاعِدِ، وَالْقَاعِدُ فِيهَا خَيْرٌ مِنِ الْقَائِمِ، وَالْقَائِمُ خَيْرٌ مِنَ الْمَاشِي، وَالْمَاشِي خَيْرٌ مِنَ الرَّاكِبُ، وَالرَّاكِبُ خَيْرٌ مِنَ الْمُجْرِي، قَتْلَاهَا كُلُّهَا فِي النَّارِ» ، قَالَ: قُلْتُ: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، وَمَتَى ذَلِكَ؟ قَالَ: «ذَلِكَ أَيَّامَ الْهَرْجِ» ، قُلْتُ: وَمَتَى أَيَّامُ الْهَرْجِ؟ قَالَ: «حِينَ لَا يَأْمَنُ الرَّجُلُ جَلِيسَهُ» قَالَ: فَبِمَ تَأْمُرُنِي إِنْ أَدْرَكْتُ ذَلِكَ الزَّمَانَ؟ قَالَ: «اكْفُفْ نَفْسَكَ وَيَدَكَ وَادْخُلْ دَارَكَ» قَالَ: قُلْتُ: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، أَرَأَيْتَ إِنْ دَخَلَ رَجُلٌ عَلَيَّ دَارِي؟ قَالَ: «فَادْخُلْ بَيْتَكَ» ، قَالَ: قُلْتُ: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، أَرَأَيْتَ إِنْ دَخَلَ عَلَيَّ بَيْتِي؟ قَالَ: «فَادْخُلْ مَسْجِدَكَ، وَاصْنَعْ هَكَذَا» وَقَبَضَ بِيَمِينِهِ عَلَى الْكُوعِ وَقُلْ: «رَبِّيَ اللَّهُ، حَتَّى تَمُوتَ عَلَى ذَلِكَ»

English
Isnad

Ma'mar transmitted from Qatada, from al-Hasan, from Abu Bakra, who said the Prophet ﷺ said: “

Matn

When two Muslims confront one another with swords and one kills the other, both the killer and the killed are in the Fire.” They asked why the killed man should be. He answered: “He wanted to kill his brother.”

Matn

إِذَا تَوَجَّهُ الْمُسْلِمَانِ بِسَيْفَيْهِمَا فَقَتَلَ أَحَدُهُمَا صَاحِبَهُ فَالْقَاتِلُ وَالْمَقْتُولُ فِي النَّارِ» ، قَالُوا: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، هَذَا الْقَاتِلُ فَمَا بَالُ الْمَقْتُولِ؟ قَالَ: «إِنَّهُ كَانَ يُرِيدُ قَتْلَ أَخِيهِ»

English
Isnad

'Abd al-Razzaq transmitted from Ma'mar, from Abu 'Imran al-Jawni, from 'Abd Allah b. al-Samit, Abu Dharr's nephew, from Abu Dharr: “

Matn

One day I rode behind the Messenger of God ﷺ on a donkey. After we passed beyond Medina's houses, he said: ‘Abu Dharr, what will you do when famine strikes Medina so severely that, having risen from your bed, you cannot reach your mosque without hunger exhausting you?’ I said God and His Messenger knew best. He said: ‘Remain dignified and refrain from begging, Abu Dharr.’” The report continues in unnumbered source paragraphs below.

العربية
Isnad

أَخْبَرَنَا عَبْدُ الرَّزَّاقِ، عَنْ مَعْمَرٍ، عَنْ أَبِي عِمْرَانَ الْجَوْنِيِّ، عَنْ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ الصَّامِتِ، وَهُوَ ابْنُ أَخِي أَبِي ذَرٍّ، عَنْ أَبِي ذَرٍّ، قَالَ:

Matn

كُنْتُ رَدِيفًا خَلْفَ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ يَوْمًا عَلَى حِمَارٍ، فَلَمَّا جَاوَزْنَا بُيُوتَ الْمَدِينَةِ، قَالَ: «§كَيْفَ بِكَ يَا أَبَا ذَرٍّ، إِذَا كَانَ بِالْمَدِينَةِ جُوعٌ، تَقُومُ عَنْ فِرَاشِكَ لَا تَبْلُغُ مَسْجِدَكَ حَتَّى يُجْهدَكَ الْجُوعُ؟» ، قَالَ: قُلْتُ: اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ أَعْلَمُ، قَالَ: «تَعَفَّفْ يَا أَبَا ذَرٍّ»

He continued: “Abu Dharr, what will you do when mortality in Medina is so severe that a burial plot costs a slave?”—meaning a grave would be sold for the price of a slave. Abu Dharr replied that God and His Messenger knew best. The Prophet ﷺ said: “Be patient.”

Unnumbered continuation, printed page 352

He continued: “Abu Dharr, what will you do when killing in Medina is so great that blood floods the stones of al-Zayt?” Abu Dharr replied that God and His Messenger knew best. He said: “Join your own people.” Abu Dharr asked whether he should arm himself. He said: “Then you would be taking part with them.” Abu Dharr asked what to do. The Prophet ﷺ said: “If you fear being dazzled by a sword blade, put the edge of your garment over your face, so that the attacker bears both his own sin and yours.”

قَالَ: «§كَيْفَ بِكَ يَا أَبَا ذَرٍّ إِذَا كَانَ بِالْمَدِينَةِ قَتْلٌ تَغْمُرُ الدِّمَاءُ حِجَارَةَ الزَّيْتِ؟» ، قَالَ: قُلْتُ: اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ أَعْلَمُ، قَالَ: «تَأْتِي مَنْ أَنْتَ مِنْهُ» قَالَ: قُلْتُ: وَأَلْبِسُ السِّلَاحَ؟ قَالَ: «شَارَكْتَ الْقَوْمَ إِذًا» ، قُلْتُ: وَكَيْفَ أَصْنَعُ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ؟ قَالَ: «إِنْ خَشِيتَ أَنْ يُبْهِرَكَ شُعَاعُ السَّيْفِ فَأَلْقِ نَاحِيَةَ ثَوْبِكَ عَلَى وَجْهِكَ لِيَبُوءَ بِإِثْمِكَ وَإِثْمِهِ»

English
Isnad

Ma'mar transmitted from Tariq, from Mundhir al-Thawri, who said: “

Matn

Woe to the Arabs because of evil that has drawn near! The wings—what are the wings? Long-lasting woe lies in the wings. One wind blows in them, another whips up its gusts, and a third follows their blowing. Woe to the Arabs after the year 125, with rampant killing, rapid death, and dreadful famine! Affliction will be poured over them, embittering their hearts, overturning their joys, and tearing down their veils. Through their sins their renegades will emerge; their tent-pegs will be torn out and their ropes cut. Woe to Quraysh because of a heretic among them who brings new disturbances and denies its religion”—or words to that effect—“its prestige will be stripped away, its walls demolished around it, and its troops prevail against it. At that time mourners will arise: one weeping for her religion, another for her worldly life, another for humiliation after dignity, another for her children's hunger, another for her infants killed in their mothers' wombs, another for subjection, another for the violation of her chastity, another for bloodshed, another fearing armies, and another longing for her grave.”

Isnad

English
Isnad

'Abd al-Razzaq transmitted from Ma'mar, from Ziyad b. Jil, from Abu Ka'b al-Harithi, called ‘the Man of the Waterskin’: “

Matn

I went in search of stray camels, carrying milk in a waterskin. Then I reproached myself: how will I perform ablution? I poured out the milk and filled the skin with water, saying it could serve both for purification and drinking. For three days as I searched, whenever I poured from the skin to wash, water came out; whenever I poured to drink, milk came out. Asma' al-Najraniyya asked whether the milk had been curdled or fresh. I said: ‘What an idle question! It satisfied hunger and quenched thirst.’ I told people of my tribe, including their chief 'Ali b. al-Harith, who doubted my account. That night someone appeared to him and rebuked him for calling a man a liar when he described God's blessings. I then went to Medina to ask 'Uthman religious questions. I told him I was a Yemeni from Banu al-Harith and asked that his doorkeeper let me enter. He ordered Waththab to admit me whenever I came. On one visit 'Uthman sat among silent men, and I refrained from questioning him. Messengers arrived reporting that someone refused his summons. 'Uthman ordered them to bring the man forcibly if necessary. They returned with a tall, dark-skinned man, bald at the front save a few hairs, with some hair at the nape. I learned he was 'Ammar b. Yasir. 'Uthman rebuked him for refusing the summons. They spoke about something I did not understand. Later, I followed 'Uthman to the mosque, where 'Ammar sat beside a pillar amid weeping Companions of the Prophet ﷺ. 'Uthman summoned his guards and dispersed the group, then led the prayer. As he began it, a woman spoke from her chamber, calling on the people to hear her and reminding them of the Messenger ﷺ and his mission, saying they had abandoned God's command and opposed His Messenger—or words to that effect. Another woman spoke likewise: they were 'A'isha and Hafsa. After the prayer, 'Uthman called them two women stirring up discord during people's prayers and threatened to answer their abuse in kind if they did not stop. Sa'd b. Abi Waqqas objected: ‘Do you say that to the beloved wives of the Messenger of God ﷺ?’ 'Uthman advanced angrily toward Sa'd, who slipped out of the mosque. At its gate 'Uthman met 'Ali. The two exchanged words, and 'Uthman accused 'Ali of staying behind at Tabuk; 'Ali replied by accusing 'Uthman of fleeing at Uhud. The people separated them. I returned to Kufa and found unrest there too: its people had barred Sa'id ibn al-'As from entering. Seeing this, I returned to my tribe.”

English
Isnad

'Abd al-Razzaq transmitted from Ma'mar, from several persons including al-Hasan, that the Prophet ﷺ said to 'Abd Allah b. 'Amr: “

Matn

What will you do when you are left among the dregs of humanity, whose agreements and trusts are confused and who clash like this?” He intertwined his fingers. 'Abd Allah asked what he should do. He replied: “Hold to what you recognize as right, leave what you reject, concern yourself with those close to you, and beware of their masses.” Al-Hasan added a remark about how difficult it would be to restrain oneself in such a time; the final words are somewhat obscure in the source.

Matn

'Abd al-Razzaq transmitted from Ma'mar, from Aban, from Sulaym b. Qays al-Hanzali: '

Matn

Umar preached: “What I most fear for you after me is that an innocent man will be seized, cut apart as a sacrificial camel is cut, and have his flesh stripped as its flesh is stripped, while people call him a rebel although he is not.” 'Ali, standing below the pulpit, asked—or spoke of—the time when calamity would intensify, partisanship arise, descendants be taken captive, and trials grind people as millstones grind grain and fire consumes wood. 'Abd al-Razzaq transmitted from Ma'mar, from Aban, from al-Hasan, from Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, that the Prophet ﷺ said: “

Matn

He said: “Not killing polytheists, but killing one another.” They asked whether that would happen even though God's Book remained among them and they had their reason. 'Abd al-Razzaq transmitted from Ma'mar, from al-Zuhri, from Abu Idris al-Khawlani: “

Matn
