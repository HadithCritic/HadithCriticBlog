Jāmiʿ Maʿmar ibn Rāshid · p. 389
English
Isnad

'Abd al-Razzaq told us: Ma'mar told us, from al-Zuhri, from Salim, from Ibn 'Umar:

Matn

The Messenger of God ﷺ passed by Ibn Sayyad with a group of his Companions, among them 'Umar b. al-Khattab. He was playing with the boys at the fortress of Banu Maghala, and he was a boy. He did not notice until the Messenger of God ﷺ struck his back with his hand and said: "Do you testify that I am the Messenger of God?" Ibn Sayyad looked at him and said: "I testify that you are the messenger of the unlettered." Ibn Sayyad said to the Prophet ﷺ: "Do you testify that I am the messenger of God?" The Prophet ﷺ said: "I believe in God and His messenger." Then the Prophet ﷺ said to him: "What comes to you?" Ibn Sayyad said: "A truthful one and a liar come to me." The Prophet ﷺ said: "The matter has been confused for you." Then the Messenger of God ﷺ said: "I have concealed something for you," and he concealed for him [the verse] "the Day when the sky brings a clear smoke" [44:10]. Ibn Sayyad said: "It is the dukh." The Prophet ﷺ said: "Begone! You will not exceed your measure." 'Umar said: "Messenger of God, permit me to strike his neck." The Messenger of God ﷺ said: "If he is the one, you will not be given power over him, and if he is not, there is no good for you in killing him."

Isnad

'Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from al-Zuhri, from Sinan b. Abi Sinan, that he heard Husayn b. 'Ali narrate that the Prophet ﷺ concealed [the word] "smoke" for Ibn Sayyad and asked him what he had concealed, and he said: "Dukh." He said: "

Matn

Begone! You will not exceed your measure" (or: your term). When he turned away, the Prophet ﷺ said: "What did he say?" Some said: "Dukh," and others said: "No, he said rih [wind]." The Prophet ﷺ said: "You have differed while I am among you, and after me you will differ even more."

English
Isnad

'Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from al-Zuhri, from Salim, from several people. Ibn 'Umar said:

Matn

The Messenger of God ﷺ and Ubayy b. Ka'b went one day to the palm grove where Ibn Sayyad was. When they entered the grove, the Messenger of God ﷺ began to shield himself behind the trunks of the palms, stealthily approaching Ibn Sayyad to hear something from him before he saw him. Ibn Sayyad was lying on his bed in a velvet cloth in which there was a murmuring. His mother saw the Messenger of God ﷺ shielding himself behind the trunks and said: "Saf" (his name), "this is Muhammad!" He rose up. The Messenger of God ﷺ said: "If she had left him, he would have made things clear."

'Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from al-Zuhri, from Salim, from Ibn 'Umar, who said: The Messenger of God ﷺ stood among the people, praised God as He deserves, then mentioned the Dajjal and said: "I warn you of him. There was no prophet who did not warn his people of him; Noah warned his people. But I will tell you something about him that no prophet told his people: you know that he is one-eyed, and that God is not one-eyed." [This continuation is in the supplied English only; it is not in the Arabic text.] Al-Zuhri said: 'Umar b. Thabit al-Ansari told me that some of the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ told him that the Messenger of God ﷺ said that day to the people, warning them of the trial of the Dajjal: "None of you will see his Lord until he dies. And between his [the Dajjal's] eyes is written 'unbeliever,' which is read by whoever dislikes his deeds."

Jāmiʿ Maʿmar ibn Rāshid · p. 391
English
Isnad

'Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada, from Shahr b. Hawshab, from Asma' bint Yazid al-Ansariyya, who said:

Matn

The Messenger of God ﷺ was in my house and mentioned the Dajjal. He said: "Before him are three years. In the first, the sky withholds a third of its rain and the earth a third of its plants. In the second, the sky withholds two-thirds of its rain and the earth two-thirds of its plants. In the third, the sky withholds all its rain and the earth all its plants, so that no cloven-hoofed or molar-toothed animal remains but perishes. Among the most severe trials is that he will come to a Bedouin and say: 'Do you see, if I revive your camels for you, will you not know that I am your Lord?' He will say: 'Yes.' So Satan will take the shape of his camels, with the finest udders and the largest humps. And he will come to a man whose brother and father have died and say: 'Do you see, if I revive your father and your brother for you, will you not know that I am your Lord?' He will say: 'Yes,' and Satan will take the shape of his father and his brother." She said: Then the Messenger of God ﷺ went out for a need of his and returned, while the people were in anxiety and distress over what he had told them. She said: He took hold of the two doorposts and said: "What is it, Asma'?" I said: "Messenger of God, you have wrenched out our hearts by mentioning the Dajjal." He said: "If he emerges while I am alive, I will be the one to argue against him. Otherwise my Lord is my successor over every believer." Asma' said: I said: "Messenger of God, by God, we knead our dough and do not bake it before we are hungry. How will the believers be that day?" He said: "What suffices the people of heaven will suffice them: glorification and sanctification."

The remainder of the Muslims that day will be taking refuge on the summit of one of the mountains of Syria, and the Dajjal will besiege them, encamped at its base. When the affliction has lasted long upon them, a man among the Muslims will say: 'Muslims, how long will you remain like this, with the enemy of God camped in your land like this? Are you not between one of the two best outcomes: that God make you martyrs or give you victory?' So they will pledge allegiance to die, a pledge that God knows to be sincere from their very selves. Then a darkness will seize them in which a man cannot see his palm. Then the son of Mary will descend and lift the veil from their sight, and among them will be a man in his armor. They will say: 'Who are you, servant of God?' He will say: 'I am the servant of God and His messenger, His spirit and His word, 'Isa b. Maryam. Choose one of three: that God send down upon the Dajjal and his troops a punishment from the sky, or cause the earth to swallow them, or empower your weapons against them and withhold their weapons from you.' They will say: 'This last one, Messenger of God, is more healing for our chests and our selves.' That day you will see the huge, tall Jew, a great eater and drinker, whose hand cannot lift his sword for trembling. They will rise against them and be given power over them. The Dajjal will melt when he sees the son of Mary, as lead melts, until 'Isa reaches him or overtakes him and kills him."
