Hadith343/ 15
Shamāʾil al-Tirmidhī · p. 191
English
Isnad

ʿAbbās b. Muḥammad al-Dūrī narrated to us; he said: ʿAbd Allāh b. Yazīd al-Muqriʾ narrated to us; he said: Layth b. Saʿd narrated to us; he said: Abū ʿUthmān al-Walīd b. Abī al-Walīd narrated to me, from Sulaymān b. Khārija, from Khārija b. Zayd b. Thābit, who said:

Matn

A group of men came in to Zayd b. Thābit and said to him: “Narrate to us reports of the Messenger of God ﷺ!” He said: “What shall I narrate to you? I was his neighbour, and when revelation came down to him he would send for me and I would write it down for him. When we mentioned this world, he mentioned it with us; when we mentioned the Hereafter, he mentioned it with us; and when we mentioned food, he mentioned it with us. All of this I narrate to you from the Messenger of God ﷺ.”

Isnad

Isḥāq b. Mūsā narrated to us; he said: Yūnus b. Bukayr narrated to us, from Muḥammad b. Isḥāq, from Ziyād b. Abī Ziyād, from Muḥammad b. Kaʿb al-Qurazī, from ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ, who said:

Matn

The Messenger of God ﷺ used to turn his face and his conversation toward the worst of the people in order to win them over. So he used to turn his face and his conversation toward me, until I thought I was the best of the people. I said: “Messenger of God, am I better or Abū Bakr?” He said: “Abū Bakr.” I said: “Messenger of God, am I better or ʿUmar?” He said: “ʿUmar.” I said: “Messenger of God, am I better or ʿUthmān?” He said: “ʿUthmān.” When I asked the Messenger of God ﷺ, he told me the truth, and I wished I had not asked him.

English
Isnad

Qutayba b. Saʿīd narrated to us; he said: Jaʿfar b. Sulaymān al-Ḍubaʿī narrated to us, from Thābit, from Anas b. Mālik, who said:

Matn

I served the Messenger of God ﷺ for ten years, and he never said “Uff” to me, and never said about anything I did: “Why did you do it?” nor about anything I left undone: “Why did you not do it?” The Messenger of God ﷺ was among the best of people in character. I never touched silk-and-wool cloth (khazz), nor silk, nor anything softer than the palm of the Messenger of God ﷺ; and I never smelled musk or perfume more fragrant than the sweat of the Prophet ﷺ.

Aḥmad b. ʿAbda al-Ḍabbī narrated to us; he said: Fuḍayl b. ʿIyāḍ narrated to us, from Manṣūr, from al-Zuhrī, from ʿUrwa, from ʿĀʾisha, who said:

Matn

I never saw the Messenger of God ﷺ take revenge for a wrong done to him, so long as none of God's sacred limits was violated; but when any of God's sacred limits was violated, he was the most intense of them in anger over it. And he was never given a choice between two matters but he chose the easier of them, so long as it was not a sin.

Ibn Abī ʿUmar narrated to us; he said: Sufyān narrated to us, from Muḥammad b. al-Munkadir, from ʿUrwa, from ʿĀʾisha, who said:

Matn

English
Isnad

Sufyān b. Wakīʿ narrated to us; he said: Jumayʿ b. ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-ʿIjlī narrated to us; he said: a man of Banū Tamīm, from the descendants of Abū Hāla, the husband of Khadīja, who was known as Abū ʿAbd Allāh, informed us, from Ibn Abī Hāla, from al-Ḥasan b. ʿAlī, who said: al-Ḥusayn said:

Matn

I asked my father about the conduct of the Prophet ﷺ with those who sat with him. He said: “The Messenger of God ﷺ was always cheerful, of easy disposition, gentle in manner; not harsh, not coarse, not clamorous, not given to coarse speech, not a fault-finder, not stingy. He would overlook what he did not desire, yet would not make one who hoped from him despair, nor disappoint him. He had left himself free of three things: disputation, excess, and what did not concern him. And he left people free of three things: he never censured anyone or found fault with him, never sought out anyone's private failings, and never spoke except about what he hoped would earn reward. When he spoke, those sitting with him bowed their heads as though birds were on their heads; and when he fell silent, they spoke. They did not dispute over speech in his presence; whoever spoke before him, they listened to until he finished; their talk in his presence was the talk of the first among them. He laughed at what they laughed at, and marvelled at what they marvelled at. He was patient with the stranger over roughness in his speech and in his questioning, so that his Companions would even bring such people to him. And he would say: ‘When you see someone seeking a need, help him!’ He accepted praise only from one who was fair in it, and he did not cut off anyone's speech until he overstepped, and then he would cut it off by forbidding or by rising.”
