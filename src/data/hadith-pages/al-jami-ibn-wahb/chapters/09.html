Al-Jāmiʿ fī al-Ḥadīth of Ibn Wahb · Vol. 1, p. 387
English
Isnad

Ibn Wahb reported to me: Yahya b. Ayyub reported to me from ‘Ubayd God b. Zahr, from Sulayman, from Mujahid, from Ibn ‘Abbas, who said '

Matn

If two mountains oppressed one another, God would make the oppressor into a flat plain.' Sulayman said: 'A flat plain means leveled ground.'

العربية
Isnad

قَالَ ابْنُ وَهْبٍ: أَخْبَرَنِي يَحْيَى بْنُ أَيُّوبَ، عَنْ عُبَيْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ زَحْرٍ، عَنْ سُلَيْمَانَ، عَنْ مُجَاهِدٍ، عَنِ ابْنِ عَبَّاسٍ أَنَّهُ قَالَ: «

Matn

لَوْ أَنَّ جَبَلَيْنِ بَغَى أَحَدُهُمَا عَلَى الْآخَرِ جَعَلَ اللَّهُ الْبَاغِيَ دَكًّا» قَالَ سُلَيْمَانُ: الدَّكُّ: الْأَرْضُ الْمُسْتَوِيَةُ "

Entry279/ 20
Al-Jāmiʿ fī al-Ḥadīth of Ibn Wahb · Vol. 1, p. 392
English
Isnad

Ibn Lahi‘ah reported to me from Ibn al-Had, who said: I heard Yahya b. Sa‘id say: '

Matn

There was a man in the pre-Islamic era who was an oppressor, and an Arab man used to say: I have never seen an oppressor leave this world without being afflicted with a calamity. The oppressor died without anything befalling him. The man was amazed: The oppressor died without anything befalling him. By God, I do not think there is any recompense after death.'

العربية
Isnad

قَالَ وَأَخْبَرَنِي ابْنُ لَهِيعَةَ، عَنِ ابْنِ الْهَادِ، قَالَ: سَمِعْتُ يَحْيَى بْنَ سَعِيدٍ، يَقُولُ: "

Matn

كَانَ رَجُلٌ فِي الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ بَاغِيًا، وَكَانَ رَجُلٌ مِنَ الْعَرَبِ يَقُولُ: مَا رَأَيْتُ بَاغِيًا قَطُّ خَرَجَ مِنْ هَذِهِ الدُّنْيَا حَتَّى يُصِيبَهُ بِلًى. قَالَ: فَهَلَكَ الرَّجُلُ الْبَاغِي وَلَمْ يُصِبْهُ شَيْءٌ، قَالَ: فَجَعَلَ الرَّجُلُ يَتَعَجَّبُ: هَلَكَ فُلَانٌ الْبَاغِي وَلَمْ يُصِبْهُ شَيْءٌ، فَوَاللَّهِ إِنِّي لَا أَظُنُّ فِيمَا بَعْدَ الْمَوْتِ جَزَاءً "

Entry283/ 20
Al-Jāmiʿ fī al-Ḥadīth of Ibn Wahb · Vol. 1, p. 395
English
Isnad

Harmalah b. ‘Imran al-Tujibi narrated to me from some of their elders that ‘Amr b. al-‘As said '

Matn

Men are of three types: a complete man, half a man, and nothing. The complete man is one whom God has perfected in his religion and intellect. When he intends something, he does not act upon it until he consults people of sound judgment. If they agree with him, he praises God and acts upon his opinion, and he remains rightly guided and successful. The half-man is one whom God has perfected in his religion and intellect, but when he intends something, he does not consult anyone, saying: Which of the people should I obey and abandon my opinion for theirs? He is sometimes right and sometimes wrong. The one who is nothing is one who has neither religion nor intellect and does not seek counsel in matters. He remains in error. ‘Amr b. al-‘As said: When I want to seek counsel, I even consult some of my servants. I do not care if people present their opinions to me and I listen.'

العربية
Isnad

قَالَ وَحَدَّثَنِي حَرْمَلَةُ بْنُ عِمْرَانَ التُّجِيبِيُّ، عَنْ بَعْضِ مَشَايِخِهِمْ، أَنَّ عَمْرَو بْنَ الْعَاصِ، قَالَ: "

Matn
