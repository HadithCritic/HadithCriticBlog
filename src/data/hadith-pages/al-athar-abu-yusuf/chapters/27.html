English
Isnad

Yusuf narrated from his father, from Abu Hanifah, from Alqamah b. Marthad, from Abd al-Rahman b. Sabit,

Matn

who said A judge among the Children of Israel perished, leaving behind a young man from among their scholars. They approached a man and asked him to sit as a judge, but he refused. Then, he was visited in his dream and asked, "What prevents you from sitting as a judge?" He replied, "I fear that I may be unjust." It was said to him, "Shall We place between you and injustice a sign—so-and-so—so that if you stand with him and he is taller than you, then you have been unjust?" He said, "In the morning, he sat as a judge and would send for that man, standing with him several times a day whenever something was unclear or he had doubt about it." One day, he stood with him, and the man was taller than him. So, he stepped down from the judiciary, grieved and distressed. He was then visited in his dream and asked, "You stepped down from the judiciary?" He replied, "I sent for the sign you placed between me and injustice, and when I stood with him, he was taller than me." It was said to him, "That was not from the injustice you feared. Rather, two disputing parties came before you, and you wished the right to be for one of them, so you ruled in his favor." He said, "Do you not see that I am unjust within myself before I even speak? I will never sit as a judge after this, ever."

Isnad

Yusuf reported from his father from Abu Hanifah from Ali b. al-Aqmar

Matn

who said I was sitting with Shurayh when a man came with a dyer and said, "I gave my garment to this man, but his house burned down, as he claims." Shurayh said, "Is that so?" The man replied, "Yes." Shurayh said, "Compensate him for his garment." The man asked, "How can I compensate him for his garment when my house has burned down?" Shurayh said, "Tell me, if his house had burned down, would you have left him any of your wages?"

Isnad

Yusuf reported from his father, from Abu Hanifah, from Alqamah b. Marthad, from Ibn Buraydah, from his father,

Matn

that the Prophet, ﷺ, was approached by Ma'iz b. Malik, ؓ, who said, “This wretched man has committed adultery.” The Prophet turned him away. Ma'iz came again, and the Prophet turned him away; he came a third time, and the Prophet turned him away; then he came a fourth time. The Prophet asked Ma'iz’s people, “Do you notice anything wrong with his reason?” They said, “No.” So he ordered that Ma'iz be stoned. He was taken to a place with few stones. When death was slow to come, he ran toward a place with many stones, and the people followed him until they killed him. When the Prophet was told what had happened, he said, “Why did you not let him go?” Some people of Medina said, “Ma'iz is ruined and has ruined others,” while others said, “We hope his repentance will be accepted.” When this reached the Prophet, he said, “He made such a repentance that, if a multitude of people made it, it would be accepted from them.” His people then sought his body and spoke to the Prophet about it. He said, “Do for him what you do for your dead: shroud him and pray over him.”

English
Isnad

Yusuf reported from his father from Abu Hanifah from Hammad from Ibrahim

Matn

that he said regarding a man when he dies: "Whatever is in the house of men's belongings belongs to the men, and whatever is of women's belongings belongs to the woman. As for what belongs to both men and women, it goes to the one who remains afterward, unless the other produces evidence. If he divorces, it is the same, except that what belongs to both women and men goes to the man because he is the owner of the house, and everything in the house belongs to him except what is of women's belongings. If they dispute and he does not divorce, it is the same."

who said regarding lost property: “Announce it for a year. If its owner comes, [give it to him]; otherwise, give it in charity. If you wish, you may keep it. If its owner later comes, he has the choice: he may hold you liable, or he may choose the reward [of the charity].”

العربية
Isnad

قَالَ: ثنا يُوسُفُ عَنْ أَبِيهِ عَنْ أَبِي حَنِيفَةَ، عَنْ أَبِي إِسْحَاقَ، عَنْ عَاصِمٍ ، عَنْ عَلِيِّ بْنِ أَبِي طَالِبٍ رضي الله عنه

Matn

أَنَّهُ قَالَ فِي اللُّقَطَةِ: " عَرِّفْهَا حَوْلًا، فَإِنْ جَاءَ صَاحِبُهَا وَإِلَّا فَتَصَدَّقْ بِهَا، وَإِنْ شِئْتَ أَمْسَكْتَ، فَإِنْ جَاءَ صَاحِبُهَا فَهُوَ بِالْخِيَارِ: إِنْ شَاءَ ضَمِنَكَ، وَإِنْ شَاءَ اخْتَارَ الْأَجْرَ "

English
Isnad

No isnad: the text is given as a direct statement.

Matn

…That was mentioned, and Ibn Abbas said to him: “Must you pray when the prayer time comes, put on your clothes, and break your oath? Then put on your clothes, pray in the Sacred Mosque, and expiate your oath. Satan only wanted to toy with you.” He said: “I mentioned that to Ibn Umar, ؓ, and Ibn Umar said: ‘Who can match Ibn Abbas’s hidden insights, ؓ?’”

العربية
Isnad

English
Isnad

Yusuf narrated from his father, from Abu Hanifah, from Hammad, from Ibrahim,

Matn

who said: “The expiation for an oath is to feed ten poor people, clothe them (one garment for each), or give each person half a sa' of wheat or flour, or one sa' of dates; or to provide them with lunch and dinner; or to free a slave. Whoever cannot do so must fast three consecutive days. He may choose among these alternatives, for God says: ‘or … or …’”

العربية
Isnad

قَالَ: حَدَّثَنَا يُوسُفُ عَنْ أَبِيهِ عَنْ أَبِي حَنِيفَةَ، عَنْ حَمَّادٍ، عَنْ إِبْرَاهِيمَ،

Matn

أَنَّهُ قَالَ " فِي كَفَّارَةِ الْيَمِينِ إِطْعَامُ عَشْرَةِ مَسَاكِينَ أَوِ الْكِسْوَةُ لِكُلِّ مِسْكِينٍ ثَوْبٌ ثَوْبٌ، أَوِ الطَّعَامُ لِكُلِّ مِسْكِينٍ نِصْفُ صَاعٍ مِنْ بُرٍّ أَوْ دَقِيقٍ، أَوْ صَاعٍ مِنْ تَمْرِ، أَوْ يُغَدِّيهِمْ وَيُعَشِّيهِمْ، أَوْ تَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍ، فَمَنْ لَمْ يَجِدْ فَصِيَامُ ثَلَاثَةِ أَيَّامٍ مُتَتَابِعَاتٍ، وَهُوَ فِيهِ بِالْخِيَارِ لِأَنَّ اللَّهَ يَقُولُ: أَوْ أَوْ "
