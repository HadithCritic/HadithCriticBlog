Kitāb al-Āthār of Abū Yūsuf · Vol. 1, p. 76
English
Isnad

Yusuf narrated from his father, from Abu Hanifah, from Hammad, from Ibrahim, who said regarding washing the deceased:

Matn

He is stripped and placed on a washing platform, and a cloth is placed over his private parts. Then he is given the ablution performed for prayer, beginning with his right side. His mouth is not rinsed, nor is water drawn into his nose. His head and beard are washed with marsh-mallow, and his hair is not combed. Then he is laid on his left side and washed with plain water until clean, ensuring that the water reaches the side facing the platform. Beforehand, you have the water heated with lote-tree leaves; if there are no lote-tree leaves, use soapwort; if neither is available, water alone suffices. Then he is laid on his right side and washed with that water until clean, ensuring that it reaches the side facing the platform. Then he is sat up and supported against someone, and his abdomen is pressed. If anything comes out, it is wiped away. Then he is laid on his left side and washed again with plain water until clean, ensuring that the water reaches the side facing the platform. Then he is dried with a cloth. You have already ordered that his shrouds and bier be fumigated an odd number of times. Then funerary perfume is applied to his beard and head, and camphor is put on the places of prostration, if available. Then he is dressed in a shirt, if one is available; if not, that is no problem. His upper garment is spread out, and his lower wrap is spread over it. The lower wrap is then folded over him from the left, covering his face, head, and entire body. It is folded over him in the same way from the right, and the upper garment is folded over likewise. His shrouds are tied if you fear they may come undone. He is not to be followed by fire, for Ibrahim disliked fire being the deceased’s last worldly provision and something that follows him. It is also mentioned that a woman’s hair is let down and her head-covering is draped over it like a veil. A woman is shrouded in a dress, a lower wrap, an outer sheet, a head-covering, and a cloth strip tied over the shroud. He said: I asked Abu Hanifah about cotton being placed in the mouth, ears, nose, and genital area. He said: “That is good.” Abu Yusuf said: “I see no harm in it.” In Ibrahim’s report: When you reach the grave, it does not matter whether those placed in it are an even or odd number. Untie the knot if you tied one. The person who places him in the grave says: “In the name of God, and according to the religion of the Messenger of God, ﷺ.”
