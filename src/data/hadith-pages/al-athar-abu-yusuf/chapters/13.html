Entry454/ 8
Kitāb al-Āthār of Abū Yūsuf · Vol. 1, p. 92
English
Isnad

Yusuf narrated from his father from Abu Hanifah from Hammad from Ibrahim who said When a man intends

Matn

to enter ihram for Hajj while combining Hajj and Umrah, he may bathe if he wishes or perform ablution; bathing is preferable. He enters ihram after his prayer, or after he has mounted and his camel has risen to its feet. On arriving in Mecca, he circumambulates the House for his Umrah: three circuits at a brisk pace from the Black Stone back to it, then four at an ordinary pace. Each time he passes the Stone, he touches it without harming a Muslim; if he cannot, he faces it and says the takbir. Then he prays two rak‘ahs at the Station of Ibrahim, or wherever convenient, returns to the Stone, and touches it. He then goes to Safa and Marwah. At Safa he stands facing the Kaaba where he can see it, praises God, and supplicates for himself. He descends toward Marwah at an ordinary pace, walks briskly through the bottom of the valley, and after passing it continues at an ordinary pace until he reaches Marwah. He does there as he did at Safa. He makes seven passages between the two, beginning at Safa and ending at Marwah, walking briskly through the valley. Then he circumambulates the House for Hajj and performs sa‘i between Safa and Marwah. He remains in ihram; none of the things forbidden by ihram becomes lawful to him. He circumambulates the House as he wishes and recites the talbiyah. At midday he sets out for Mina, where he prays Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib, Isha, and the Fajr prayer of the Day of Arafah. Then in the morning he proceeds to Arafat and prays Dhuhr and Asr there. If he prays with the Imam, he combines the two; if he prays at his camp, he prays each at its proper time. He does not depart until he has prayed Asr. Then he stands behind the Imam if he can. When the sun sets, he departs.

Entry455/ 8
Kitāb al-Āthār of Abū Yūsuf · Vol. 1, p. 93
English
Isnad

Yusuf narrated from his father, from Abu Hanifah, from Hammad, from Ibrahim, from Al-Aswad and Alqamah, that they set out

Matn

with Umar b. al-Khattab, ؓ, and he said "O people, maintain tranquility, for righteousness is not in making the camels run or spurring the horses." They said: "He did not urge his mount beyond its natural pace, even as it trotted along." Then he returned to Ibrahim’s narration: Then you descend at Al-Muzdalifah and pray Maghrib and Isha there with one call to prayer and one iqamah. When you pray the morning prayer, stand with the Imam. When he departs, you depart until you reach Mina, where you stone Jamrat al-Aqabah. Then you cease the talbiyah at the first pebble you throw. Then you slaughter, shave your head, and visit the House on the same day. You stay in Mina, stoning the jamarat the next day when the sun has passed its zenith, before praying. You begin with the one near the mosque, stoning it with seven pebbles. Then you stand where the people stand. Then you do the same with the middle one. Then you stand where the people stand and stone Jamrat al-Aqabah, without stopping there. You do all this the next day as well. If you leave, there is no harm. If the sun sets on you, stay until the next day, then stone the jamarat as you did the day before. Then depart and perform the farewell tawaf. Hammad said: I mentioned this to Sa‘id ibn Jubayr, and he and Ibrahim did not differ on anything regarding Hajj.

Kitāb al-Āthār of Abū Yūsuf · Vol. 1, p. 94
English
Isnad

Yusuf reported from his father from Abu Hanifah from Hammad from Ibrahim that he said When a man intends

Matn

to enter ihram for Hajj, he performs ablution or bathes; bathing is preferable. He then wears an izar and a rida’, applies whatever oil he wishes, and prays two rak‘ahs. He enters ihram after that prayer, after an obligatory prayer, or once he has mounted and his camel has risen to its feet. His izar and rida’ should be new or washed, provided they are not saturated with safflower, saffron, or wars. The talbiyah is to say: “Here I am, O God, here I am. Here I am; You have no partner; here I am. All praise and blessing are Yours, and sovereignty; You have no partner.” When he says this, he has entered ihram.

Kitāb al-Āthār of Abū Yūsuf · Vol. 1, p. 95
English
Isnad

Yusuf reported from his father, from Abu Hanifah, from Qays b. Muslim, from Tariq b. Shihab, from Ibn Mas'ud, ؓ, that he said "The best of Hajj

Matn

is al-‘ajj and al-thajj. As for al-‘ajj, it is raising the voice with talbiyah, and as for al-thajj, it is the slaughter of camels."

العربية
Isnad

قَالَ: ثنا يُوسُفُ عَنْ أَبِيهِ عَنْ أَبِي حَنِيفَةَ، عَنْ قَيْسِ بْنِ مُسْلِمٍ، عَنْ طَارِقِ بْنِ شِهَابٍ، عَنِ ابْنِ مَسْعُودٍ رضي الله عنه أَنَّهُ قَالَ «أَفْضَلُ الْحَجِّ

Matn

الْعَجُّ وَالثَّجُّ، فَأَمَّا الْعَجُّ فَالْعَجِيجُ بِالتَّلْبِيَةِ، وَأَمَّا الثَّجُّ فَنَحْرُ الْبُدْنِ»

English
Isnad

Yusuf reported from his father, from Abu Hanifah, from Hammad, from Ibrahim, that he said regarding the takbir

Matn

: "People say the takbir in what they should not, and they abandon it in what they should."

العربية
Isnad

قَالَ: حَدَّثَنَا يُوسُفُ عَنْ أَبِيهِ عَنْ أَبِي حَنِيفَةَ، عَنْ حَمَّادٍ، عَنْ إِبْرَاهِيمَ، أَنَّهُ قَالَ فِي التَّكْبِيرِ

Matn

: «كَبَّرَ النَّاسُ فِيمَا لَا يَنْبَغِي لَهُمْ، وَتَرَكُوا ذَلِكَ فِيمَا يَنْبَغِي لَهُمْ»

Entry461/ 8
Kitāb al-Āthār of Abū Yūsuf · Vol. 1, p. 95
English
Isnad

Yusuf narrated from his father, from Abu Hanifah, from Hammad, from Ibrahim, that he said "The one in ihram should say

Matn

the talbiyah after every prayer, whenever he has mounted and his camel has risen to its feet, whenever he ascends a hill, and in the last part of the night.

العربية
Isnad

قَالَ: حَدَّثَنَا يُوسُفُ عَنْ أَبِيهِ عَنْ أَبِي حَنِيفَةَ، عَنْ حَمَّادٍ، عَنْ إِبْرَاهِيمَ، أَنَّهُ قَالَ «يُلَبِّي

Matn

الْمُحْرِمُ فِي دُبُرِ كُلِّ صَلَاةٍ، وَكُلَّمَا اسْتَوَى بِهِ بَعِيرُهُ، وَكُلَّمَا عَلَا شَرَفًا، وَبِالْأَسْحَارِ»
