English
Isnad

Yusuf reported from his father from Abu Hanifah from ‘Ata’ that he used to wear

Matn

a cap while in ihram, and he would say: “I suffer from my head, so I wear it, and I slaughter a sheep.”

العربية
Isnad

قَالَ: حَدَّثَنَا يُوسُفُ عَنْ أَبِيهِ عَنْ أَبِي حَنِيفَةَ، عَنْ عَطَاءٍ، أَنَّهُ كَانَ يَلْبَسُ

Matn

قَلَنْسُوَةً وَهُوَ مُحْرِمٌ، وَكَانَ يَقُولُ: «إِنِّي أَشْتَكِي رَأْسِي فَأَلْبَسُهَا، وَأَذْبَحُ شَاةً»

Entry464/ 16
Kitāb al-Āthār of Abū Yūsuf · Vol. 1, p. 95
English
Isnad

Yusuf reported from his father from Abu Hanifah from Hammad from Ibrahim, who said Women are not required

Matn

to perform ramal around the House, nor to run between al-Safa and al-Marwah in the bottom of the valley. Women may wear whatever they wish when in ihram, except the face-veil, and except what is dyed with wars or saffron, or saturated with safflower.

العربية
Isnad

قَالَ: حَدَّثَنَا يُوسُفُ عَنْ أَبِيهِ عَنْ أَبِي حَنِيفَةَ، عَنْ حَمَّادٍ، عَنْ إِبْرَاهِيمَ، أَنَّهُ قَالَ «لَيْسَ عَلَى النِّسَاءِ

Matn

Isnad

Yusuf narrated from his father from Abu Hanifah from Hammad from Ibrahim, who said There is no harm

Matn

for a pilgrim in ihram to eat yellow khabis (a sweet made with dates and ghee) or yellow khashkanan (a filled pastry). Nor is there harm in wearing a garment dyed red with safflower or yellow with saffron, provided it has been washed and the dye no longer rubs off; likewise, any dyed garment whose dye does not rub off.

English
Isnad

Yusuf narrated from his father, from Abu Hanifah, from Ibrahim b. Muhammad b. al-Muntashir, from his father, who said I asked Ibn Umar

Matn

, ؓ, “Does a man apply perfume before entering ihram?” He said, “No. I would rather wake up with tar on me than wake up with perfume on me while in ihram.” He continued: “Then I went to Aisha, ؓ, and told her what Ibn Umar had said. She replied, ‘I perfumed the Messenger of God, ﷺ; he then went to his wives and afterward entered ihram.’”

العربية
Isnad

قَالَ: حَدَّثَنَا يُوسُفُ عَنْ أَبِيهِ عَنْ أَبِي حَنِيفَةَ، عَنْ إِبْرَاهِيمَ بْنِ مُحَمَّدِ بْنِ الْمُنْتَشِرِ، عَنْ أَبِيهِ، قَالَ سَأَلْتُ ابْنَ عُمَرَ

Matn

رضي الله عنهما: أَيَتَطَيَّبُ الرَّجُلُ قَبْلَ أَنْ يُحْرِمَ؟ فَقَالَ: لَا، لَأَنْ أُصْبِحَ أَنْتَضِحُ قَطِرَانًا أَحَبُّ إِلَيَّ مِنْ أَنْ أُصْبِحَ أَنْتَضِحُ طِيبًا وَأَنَا مُحْرِمٌ. قَالَ: فَأَتَيْتُ عَائِشَةَ رضي الله عنها، فَذَكَرْتُ لَهَا قَوْلَ ابْنِ عُمَرَ، فَقَالَتْ: «أَنَا طَيَّبْتُ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم، فَطَافَ فِي أَزْوَاجِهِ، ثُمَّ أَحْرَمَ»

Entry473/ 16
Kitāb al-Āthār of Abū Yūsuf · Vol. 1, p. 97
English
Isnad

Yusuf narrated from his father, from Abu Hanifah, from Hammad, from Ibrahim, that Umar b. al-Khattab, ؓ, was standing

Matn

at Arafat when he saw a man whose head was dripping with perfume. Umar said to him, “Are you not in ihram, woe to you?” The man replied, “Yes, Commander of the Faithful.” Umar said, “Why do I see your head dripping with perfume when a pilgrim in ihram should be disheveled and dusty?” The man replied, “I entered ihram for Umrah alone and came to Mecca with my family. I completed my Umrah, then on the eve of the Day of Tarwiyah I entered ihram for Hajj.” Umar judged that the man had told the truth: his last contact with women and perfume had been only the day before. Umar then forbade tamattu‘ in Hajj and said, “By God, if I let you practice tamattu‘—completing Umrah and then entering ihram for Hajj—you would soon lie with your wives beneath the arak trees of 'Uranah, then set out as pilgrims.”

العربية
Isnad

حَدَّثَنَا يُوسُفُ عَنْ أَبِيهِ عَنْ أَبِي حَنِيفَةَ، عَنْ حَمَّادٍ، عَنْ إِبْرَاهِيمَ، عَنْ عُمَرَ بْنِ الْخَطَّابِ رضي الله عنه أَنَّهُ بَيْنَا هُوَ وَاقِفٌ

Matn

بِعَرَفَاتٍ، إِذْ أَبْصَرَ رَجُلًا يَقْطُرُ رَأْسُهُ طِيبًا، فَقَالَ لَهُ عُمَرُ: «أَلَسْتَ مُحْرِمًا؟ وَيْحَكَ» فَقَالَ: بَلَى يَا أَمِيرَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ. قَالَ: «مَا لِي أَرَاكَ يَقْطُرُ رَأْسُكَ طِيبًا، وَالْمُحْرِمُ أَشْعَثُ أَغْبَرُ» ؟ قَالَ: أَهْلَلْتُ بِالْعُمْرَةِ مُفْرَدَةً، وَقَدِمْتُ مَكَّةَ وَمَعِي أَهْلِي، فَفَرَغْتُ مِنْ عُمْرَتِي، حَتَّى إِذَا كَانَ عَشِيَّةُ التَّرْوِيَةِ أَهْلَلْتُ بِالْحَجِّ، قَالَ: فَرَأَى عُمَرُ أَنَّ الرَّجُلَ قَدْ صَدَقَهُ، إِنَّمَا عَهْدُهُ بِالنِّسَاءِ وَالطِّيبِ بِالْأَمْسِ، فَنَهَى عُمَرُ عِنْدَ ذَلِكَ عَنِ الْمُتْعَةِ، وَقَالَ: «إِذًا وَاللَّهِ لَأَوْشَكْتُمْ لَوْ خَلَّيْتُ بَيْنَكُمْ وَبَيْنَ الْمُتْعَةِ أَنْ تُضَاجِعُوهُنَّ تَحْتَ أَرَاكِ عُرَنَةَ، ثُمَّ تَرُوحُونَ حُجَّاجًا»

English
Isnad

Yusuf reported from his father, from Abu Hanifah, from Hammad, from Ibrahim, from Ibn Mas‘ud, ؓ, that he came out on the morning of the Day of Sacrifice

Matn

from the Mosque of al-Khayf, pronouncing the talbiyah, intending to stone the Jamrat al-‘Aqabah. The people gathered around him, saying, "A man pronouncing the talbiyah for Hajj on the Day of Sacrifice!" He asked, "What is the matter with the people? Have they forgotten, or are they ignorant, or has it been too long since they knew?" Then he raised his voice, pronouncing the talbiyah, "Labbayk as many times as the number of dust particles, labbayk." When the people realized it was Ibn Mas‘ud, they dispersed from him, knowing that he was more knowledgeable about the matter than they.
