Entry171/ 9
Kitāb al-Āthār of al-Shaybānī · Vol. 1, p. 449
English

Muhammad said Abu Hanifah reported to us from Hammad, from Ibrahim regarding "a man who doubts in the prostration, or the testimony (Tashahhud), or something similar in his prayer—so long as it is not a complete unit (rak‘ah)—that he should complete whichever of those acts he is unsure about, and also prostrate two prostrations of forgetfulness for it, for they rectify, by God’s permission, whatever preceded them of forgetfulness. It was said: They are the ones that humiliate Satan. And he said: To prostrate two prostrations of forgetfulness for that, in what has not become obligatory upon me, is more beloved to me than to leave them." Muhammad said: We adopt this position. If one is frequently afflicted by such doubts, he should proceed according to the stronger of his opinions and prostrate two prostrations of forgetfulness. This is the opinion of Abu Hanifah, ؓ.

Kitāb al-Āthār of al-Shaybānī · Vol. 1, p. 453
English
Isnad

Muhammad said Abu Hanifah reported to us from Hammad, from Ibrahim,

Matn

regarding one who forgets the obligatory prayer and does not know whether he prayed four or three rak'ahs. He said: "If it is the first time he forgets, he should repeat the prayer. If he frequently forgets, he should strive to determine what is correct. If his stronger assumption is that he completed the prayer, he should perform the two prostrations of forgetfulness. If his stronger assumption is that he prayed three, he should add one more and then perform the two prostrations of forgetfulness." Muhammad said: We adopt this, and it is the opinion of Abu Hanifah, ؓ.

Isnad

Matn

English
Isnad

Muhammad said Abu Hanifah reported to us from Hammad, from Ibrahim,

Matn

that Umar b. al-Khattab, ؓ, would strike a man if he saw him performing consecutive prostrations without having made a mistake. Muhammad said: It is not appropriate for a man to prostrate more than twice for one unit of prayer unless he has made a mistake and does not know whether he prostrated once or twice, so he proceeds according to what he deems most likely. All of this is the opinion of Abu Hanifah, ؓ.

Entry174/ 9
Kitāb al-Āthār of al-Shaybānī · Vol. 1, p. 454
English
Isnad

Muhammad said Abu Hanifah reported to us from Hammad, from Shaqiq b. Salamah, from Abdullah b. Mas'ud, ؓ, who said:

Matn

When one of you is in doubt during his prayer and does not know whether he has prayed three or four rak'ahs, let him reflect and consider what his best judgment is. If his best judgment is that it was three, let him stand and add the fourth, then recite the tashahhud, give the salutation, and prostrate the two prostrations of forgetfulness. If his best judgment is that he has prayed four, let him recite the tashahhud, then give the salutation, then prostrate the two prostrations of forgetfulness. Muhammad said: This is what we adopt, except that we prefer for him, if this is the first time it has happened to him, to repeat the prayer.

Isnad

Matn
