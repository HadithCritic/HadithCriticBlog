Entry194/ 6
Muwaṭṭaʾ Ibn Wahb: Sales · p. 41
English
Isnad

Ibn Wahb said: Malik said to me

Matn

regarding a man who is absent and has property present, and his creditors come wanting to sell his property and collect their right, though perhaps he is one who borrows a great deal and one fears that he owes debts to others than those who are present. He said: The living are not like the dead. It is not proper that someone who has come forward claiming a right by right be delayed from his right pending an inquiry into his debts. Rather, [the property] is sold for those present, and he is given his right, unless there is a known debt, in which case [that creditor] shares in it, and the claimant is given his right; for the dead man's account has reached its end, whereas the living man's account remains for his creditors.

English
Isnad

And Malik b. Anas said to me

Matn

regarding a man who goes away on some expedition and then dies, and a man comes mentioning a right against the dead man and wants to take his right, and the heirs say: “We fear he owed debts besides this.” He said: If the dead man was not a man known for debt, this man's right is paid and he is not made to wait; but if he was one counted as a debtor according to the visible knowledge of people, and one fears his debts are many, payment to this one is not hastened until that is investigated.

Muwaṭṭaʾ Ibn Wahb: Sales · p. 41
English
Isnad

And Malik b. Anas said to me

Matn

regarding a man who dies, whether in debt or not, both being well known in his case, and then the heirs sell their properties and divide them, and then a debt comes up against him, and the property is found in the hands of the people who bought. He said: As for the one who is known for debt and whose situation is not unknown, the creditors take what they find in the hands of those who bought, and those who bought pursue the heirs for their money; but as for the one who is not known for debt and is not suspected of it, his creditors pursue only the heirs for the price of what they sold, whether it covered their debts or not.

Entry197/ 6
Muwaṭṭaʾ Ibn Wahb: Sales · p. 41
English
Isnad

And I heard Malik say

Matn

regarding a man who dies owing a debt, and then the heirs sell and divide it, and afterward a claim comes against him, and the property is found in the hands of the people who bought. Malik said: If that sale of the dead man's property was done in a way that was not objectionable and the debt was not known, then only the heirs who received the price are pursued for it, and the goods are not taken from their owners who bought them and assumed liability for them. But if the heirs and others knew of the dead man's debt and hurried ahead of the creditors' arrival and their action regarding the property, and sold and bought, [with] the buyer taking part in that haste, then I hold that the creditors take what they find of the dead man's goods. And if anything of those goods, slaves, or animals that people bought has had growth or increase added to it, and the one who bought those slaves prefers to pay their value as of the day he took possession, that is his right.

Isnad

وَسَمِعْتُ مَالِكًا يَقُولُ

Matn

فِي الرَّجُلِ يَهْلِكُ وَعَلَيْهِ دَيْنٌ، ثُمَّ يَبِيعُ الْوَرَثَةَ فَيَقْتَسِمُونَهُ، ثُمَّ يَأْتِي عَلَيْهِ بَعْدُ فَيُوجَدُ الْمَالُ بِأَيْدِي النَّاسِ الَّذِينَ اشْتَرَوْا. قَالَ مَالِكٌ: إِذَا كَانَ ذَلِكَ الْبَيْعُ الَّذِي بِيعَ مِنْ مَالِ الْمَيِّتِ عَلَى وَجْهٍ غَيْرِ مُسْتَنْكِرٍ وَلَمْ يُعْلَمْ بِالدَّيْنِ، فَإِنَّمَا يَتْبَعُ بِذَلِكَ الْوَرَثَةُ بِالثَّمَنِ الَّذِينَ قَبَضُوا، وَلا تُنْتَزَعُ السِّلَعُ مِنْ أَصْحَابِهَا الَّذِينَ اشْتَرَوْا وَضَمِنُوهَا. وَإِنْ كَانَ الْوَرَثَةُ وَغَيْرُهُمْ قَدْ عَلِمُوا بِدَيْنِ الْمَيِّتِ فَبَادَرُوا قُدُومَ الْغُرَمَاءِ وَقِيَامَهُمْ بِالْمَالِ، فَبَاعُوا وَاشْتَرَوْا. الْمُشْتَرِي عَلَى مِثْلِ ذَلِكَ مِنَ الْبِدَارِ، فَإِنِّي أَرَى أَنْ يَأْخُذَ الْغُرَمَاءُ مَا أَدْرَكُوا مِنْ سِلَعِ الْمَيِّتِ. فَإِنْ كَانَ شَيْءٌ مِمَّا ابْتَاعَ النَّاسُ مِنْ تِلْكَ السِّلَعِ وَالرَّقِيقِ وَالْحَيَوانِ قَدْ دَخَلَهُ نَمَاءٌ أَوْ زِيَادَةٌ، فَأَحَبَّ الَّذِي ابْتَاعَ تِلْكَ الرَّقِيقَ أَنْ يُؤَدِّيَ قِيمَتَهُ يَوْمَ قَبْضِهِ كَانَ لَهُ ذَلِكَ.

Entry198/ 6
Muwaṭṭaʾ Ibn Wahb: Sales · p. 41
English
Isnad

And Malik said

Matn

regarding a man who dies and leaves property of goods and coin, and leaves a debt of which it is not known how much the debt is, nor how much property he left, which has not been tallied or known, and one of his heirs comes forward and says to his creditors: “I will take on all his debt, on condition that you leave me with what he left,” and the money he assumes is in cash, with no term set in it. There is no harm in this, and it has always been among people's practices when it is done as a kindness and in seeking good for the dead man's heirs, and is also a kindness on the creditors' part. Part of the explanation of that is that the arrangement of the one who undertakes it is that if what the dead man left exceeds his debt, the surplus belongs to all the dead man's heirs, so long as there is no surplus for the one who assumed the debt to take; and if the property falls short, it is on the one who assumed it alone, and he assumed it only in cash. When this is the manner of what he did and on this basis he entered this suretyship, there is no harm in it. But if the assumer is to have what exceeds the man's debt from his property, or [pays] at a term he sets for him, deferred, then this is not proper; for when it is on this basis it leaves the realm of kindness and is only a sale among sales, with great uncertainty (gharar): he assumed it on the basis that he is liable for any shortfall, while he gets whatever exceeds the debt of the man's property; or he wants to take the man's estate on the basis that he sets a deferred term for it, taking this little in return for giving more than it at a term, and in that there are gold, silver and unspecified, unknown things from the man's estate. So when this is done in this way and a term enters it, it is only a sale, which is lawful by what makes sales lawful and unlawful by what makes sales unlawful.

Isnad

وَقَالَ مَالِكٌ

Matn

فِي الرَّجُلِ يَهْلِكُ وَيَتْرُكُ مَالا مِنْ عَرَضٍ وَعَيْنٍ، وَيَتْرُكُ عَلَيْهِ دَيْنًا لا يُدْرَى كَمِ الدَّيْنِ، وَلا كَمْ تَرَكَ مِنَ الْمَالِ، فَلَمْ يُحَصَّلْ ذَلِكَ وَلَمْ يُعْلَمْ، فَيَقُومُ بَعْضُ وَرَثَتِهِ فَيَقُولُ لِغُرَمَائِهِ: أَنَا أَتَحَمَّلُ بِجَمِيعِ دَيْنِهِ عَلَى أَنْ تُخَلُّوا بَيْنِي وَبَيْنَ مَا تَرَكَ وَيَكُونُ الْمَالُ الَّذِي تَحَمَّلَ بِهِ نَقْدًا، لا يُضْرَبُ فِي ذَلِكَ أَجَلا؛ لَيْسَ بِهَذَا بَأْسٌ، وَلَمْ يَزَلْ مِنْ أَمْرِ النَّاسِ إِذَا كَانَ ذَلِكَ عَلَى وَجْهِ الْمَعْرُوفِ وَالْتِمَاسِ الْخَيْرِ لِوَرَثَةِ الْمَيِّتِ، وَكَانَ أَيْضًا عَلَى وَجْهِ الْمَعْرُوفِ مِنَ الْغُرَمَاءِ. وَمِنْ تَفْسِيرِ ذَلِكَ أَنْ يَكُونَ أَمْرُ الَّذِي تَحَمَّلَ عَلَى أَنَّهُ إِنْ كَانَ فِيمَا تَرَكَ الْمَيِّتُ فَضْلٌ عَنْ دَيْنِهِ كَانَ لِجَمِيعِ وَرَثَةِ الْمَيِّتِ مَا لَمْ يَكُنْ لِلْمُتَحَمِّلِ بِالدَّيْنِ فَضْلٌ يَأْخُذُهُ؛ وَإِنْ نَقَصَ الْمَالُ كَانَ عَلَى الَّذِي تَحَمَّلَ خَاصَّةً، وَكَانَ إِنَّمَا تَحَمَّلَ عَلَى النَّقْدِ. فَإِذَا كَانَ هَذَا وَجْهَ مَا صَنَعَ وَعَلَى هَذَا دَخَلَ فِي هَذِهِ الْحِمَالَةِ فَلَيْسَ بِهَذَا بَأْسٌ. وَإِنْ كَانَ الْمُحْتَمِلُ يَكُونُ لَهُ مَا فَضَلَ مِنْ مَالِ الرَّجُلِ عَنْ دَيْنِهِ أَوْ إِلَى أَجَلٍ يَضْرِبُهُ لَهُ مُسْتَأْخِرًا، فَإِنَّ هَذَا لا يَصْلُحُ؛ وَذَلِكَ أَنَّهُ إِذَا كَانَ عَلَى هَذَا خَرَجَ مِنْ أَمْرِ الْمَعْرُوفِ، وَكَانَ إِنَّمَا هُوَ بَيْعٌ مِنَ الْبُيُوعِ فِيهِ غَرَرٌ عَظِيمٌ تَحَمَّلَ عَلَى أَنْ يَكُونَ ضِمْنَ مَا نَقَصَ، عَلَى أَنَّ لَهُ مَا زَادَ عَلَى الدَّيْنِ مِنْ مَالِ الرَّجُلِ، أَوْ يَكُونُ يُرِيدُ أَنْ يَأْخُذَ تَرِكَةَ الرَّجُلِ عَلَى أَنْ يَضْرِبَ لَهُ أَجَلا مُسْتَأْخِرًا، فَيَأْخُذُ هَذَا الْقَلِيلَ عَلَى أَنْ يُعْطِيَ أَكْثَرَ مِنْهُ إِلَى أَجَلٍ، وَفِي ذَلِكَ ذَهَبٌ وَوَرِقٌ وَأَشْيَاءُ غَيْرُ مُسَمَّاةٍ وَلا مَعْلُومَةٍ مِنْ تَرِكَةِ الرَّجُلِ. فَإِذَا صَنَعَ هَذَا عَلَى هَذَا الْوَجْهِ وَدَخَلَهُ الأَجَلُ، فَإِنَّمَا هُوَ بَيْعٌ يُحِلُّهُ مَا يُحِلُّ الْبَيْعَ وَيُحَرِّمُهُ مَا يُحَرِّمُ الْبَيْعَ.

regarding a man who dies and is owed a debt, for which he has one witness, and he himself owes debts, and his heirs refuse to swear for his right. I hold that the creditors swear and take their rights; and if a surplus remains, I do not think the heirs may swear, or else their right lapses; for the oaths were offered to them and they refused and abandoned them, unless they say: “We did not know our companion had a surplus.” If it is known that they abandoned the oaths only for that reason, we hold that they swear and take what remains.

Matn

فِي الرَّجُلِ إِذَا مَاتَ وَلَهُ دَيْنٌ وَلَهُ شَاهِدٌ وَاحِدٌ وَعَلَيْهِ دَيْنٌ فَيَأْبَى وَرَثَتُهُ أَنْ يَحْلِفُوا عَلَى حَقِّهِ؛ رَأَيْتُ أَنْ يُحَلَّفَ الْغُرَمَاءُ وَيَأْخُذُوا حُقُوقَهُمْ؛ فَإِنْ فَضَلَ فَضْلٌ لَمْ أَرَ لِلْوَرَثَةِ أَنْ يَحْلِفُوا أَوْ يَبْطُلَ حَقُّهُمْ؛ وَذَلِكَ أَنَّ الأَيْمَانَ عُرِضَتْ عَلَيْهِمْ فَأَبَوْا وَتَرَكُوهَا، إِلا أَنْ يَقُولُوا: لَمْ نَعْلَمْ لِصَاحِبِنَا فَضْلا؛ فَإِنْ عُلِمَ أَنَّهُمْ إِنَّمَا تَرَكُوا الأَيْمَانَ مِنْ أَجْلِ ذَلِكَ، رَأَيْنَا أَنْ يَحْلِفُوا وَيَأْخُذُوا مَا بَقِيَ.
