Entry15/ 49
Muwaṭṭaʾ Ibn Wahb: Sales · p. 4
English
Isnad

And Malik said to me:

Matn

When a slave is sold, his 'uhda is three days. If anything befalls him within it, he is returned to the seller. If he is free of anything for the three days, the buyer has the one-year 'uhda: I hold that if madness, vitiligo (baras), or leprosy (judham) strikes the slave before the year is up, he is returned to the seller, unless someone sold with a disclaimer.

العربية
Isnad

وَقَالَ لِي مَالِكٌ:

Matn

إِذَا بِيعَ الْعَبْدُ فَإِنَّ عُهْدَتَهُ ثَلاثٌ، فَإِنْ عَرَضَ لَهُ فِيهَا شَيْءٌ رُدَّ عَلَى الْبَائِعِ، وَإِنْ سَلِمَ مِنَ الثَّلاثِ فَلِلْمُبْتَاعِ عُهْدَةُ السَّنَةِ، أَرَى الْعَبْدَ إِنْ أَصَابَهُ مَا لَمْ تَنْقَضِ السَّنَةُ، جُنُونٌ أَوْ بَرَصٌ أَوْ جُذَامٌ، فَإِنَّهُ رَدٌّ عَلَى الْبَائِعِ إِلا أَحَدٌ بَاعَ بِالْبَرَاءَةِ.

Entry17/ 49
Muwaṭṭaʾ Ibn Wahb: Sales · p. 4
English
Isnad

Ibn Sam'an informed me; he said: I heard men from among our scholars, among them Yahya b. Sa'id, say:

Matn

The governors in Medina, in former times, never ceased to rule a one-year 'uhda for slaves in respect of madness, leprosy and vitiligo: if any of that appears in the slave before a year has passed, he is the seller's loss. And they ruled a three-night 'uhda for slaves: if anything befalls the person in those three nights, whether death or illness, he is the first owner's loss. The three-day 'uhda arose only from quartan fever (al-rub'), which is not evident except within three nights.

العربية
Isnad

وَأَخْبَرَنِي ابْنُ سَمْعَانَ قَالَ: سَمِعْتُ رِجَالا مِنْ عُلَمَائِنَا مِنْهُمْ يَحْيَى بْنُ سَعِيدٍ يَقُولُونَ:

Matn

لَمْ يَزَلِ الْوُلاةُ بِالْمَدِينَةِ فِي الزَّمَانِ الأَوَّلِ يَقْضُونَ فِي الرَّقِيقِ بِعُهْدَةِ السَّنَةِ مِنَ الْجُنُونِ وَالْجُذَامِ وَالْبَرَصِ إِنْ ظَهَرَ بِالْمَمْلُوكِ شَيْءٌ مِنْ ذَلِكَ قَبْلَ أَنْ يَحُولَ الْحَوْلُ عَلَيْهِ، فَهُوَ مِنَ الْبَائِعِ؛ وَيَقْضُونَ فِي عُهْدَةِ الرَّقِيقِ بِثَلاثِ لَيَالٍ، فَإِنْ حَدَثَ بِالرَّأْسِ شَيْءٌ فِي تِلْكَ الثَّلاثِ لَيَالٍ حَدَثَ مِنْ مَوْتٍ أَوْ سُقْمٍ، فَهُوَ مِنَ الأَوَّلِ، وَإِنَّمَا كَانَتْ عُهْدَةُ الثَّلاثِ مِنَ الرُّبْعِ، لا يَسْتَبِينُ الرُّبْعَ إِلا فِي ثَلاثِ لَيَالٍ.

English
Isnad

Makhrama informed me, from his father, who said:

Matn

It is said: Any man who buys a slave girl who menstruates, and she is placed in the hands of a man until she menstruates, and she dies, she is the loss of her owner [the seller] until she menstruates; and every 'uhda is on that basis. Bukayr said: It is said: And any man who also buys a slave girl and wishes to litigate over her, it is not proper for him to have intercourse with her while he bears in himself a claim against her owner concerning her.

العربية
Isnad

وَأَخْبَرَنِي مَخْرَمَةُ، عَنْ أَبِيهِ قَالَ:

Matn

يُقَالُ: أَيُّمَا رَجُلٍ ابْتَاعَ وَلِيدَةً تَحِيضُ، فَوُضِعَتْ عَلَى يَدَيْ رَجُلٍ حَتَّى تَحِيضَ، فَمَاتَتْ فَهِيَ مِنْ صَاحِبِهَا حَتَّى تَحِيضَ؛ وَكُلٌّ عُهْدَةٌ عَلَى ذَلِكَ. قَالَ بُكَيْرٌ: يُقَالُ: وَأَيُّمَا رَجُلٍ أَيْضًا ابْتَاعَ وَلِيدَةً فَأَرَادَ أَنْ يُخَاصِمَ فِيهَا، لَمْ يَصْلُحْ لَهُ أَنْ يَطَأَهَا، وَفِي نَفْسِهِ خُصُومَةُ صَاحِبِهَا فِيهَا.

Isnad

And I heard Malik say

Matn

regarding a man who buys a slave and then discovers in him a defect for which he may be returned, while another defect has arisen with the buyer. He said: If the damaging defect is amputation, loss of an eye, or the like, then the one who bought the slave has the better of two options. If he prefers a deduction from the slave's price equal to the defect the slave had on the day he bought him, it is deducted from him; and if he prefers to pay the amount of the damage the slave suffered with him and return the slave, he returns him. If the slave dies with the one who bought him, the slave is appraised with the defect he had on the day he was bought, and one considers how much it reduced his price. If the slave's value on the day he bought him without defect was one hundred dinars, and his value on that day with the defect was eighty dinars, the buyer is relieved of the difference between his value sound and his value with that defect. The appraisal is made as of the day he bought him.

Matn

Muwaṭṭaʾ Ibn Wahb: Sales · p. 7
English
Isnad

And Malik said

Matn

regarding a man who buys cloth goods, slaves, or other things in a single transaction, and some of those goods are found to be stolen, or defects for which return is permitted are found in some of them, and the buyer wishes to return all those goods or all the slaves. He said: One looks at the part in which the defect was found, of the cloth or the slaves. If the defect was found in the principal part of the goods and most of them, and it is for its sake that they were bought in the view of people, and it is the part in which the surplus [profit] is hoped for, the whole sale is rescinded. But if the defect is in a minor part of it that is not the principal part of the goods and was not the reason for buying, that part is returned by value. A man may buy a bale of cloth containing high-priced Basran cloth and the like, along with a few small cuts (shaqa'iq) beside the Basran; he buys the whole bale only because of the Basran, and then the Basran is found spoiled, so the whole sale is rescinded. But when some of the varieties that came with it, which were not the reason for buying the goods, have the defect, they are returned by their value, and the whole sale is not rescinded on their account.
