# Computation and evidence boundaries

- **Van Putten 2019**: 23 locations × 14 codices = 322 possible ancient-manuscript cells. His Table 2 reports 144 attested ancient manuscript cells; two bracketed later-hand readings are excluded for 142 unbracketed observations. At 21 loci all observed original-form entries agree; two mixed loci are Q 16:114 (8 t, 1 h) and Q 37:57 (4 t, 2 h). Within-position pairs: 383/399 agree. Cairo is a separate modern comparator and is not included in counts. The full matrix is in `van_putten_23x15_published_matrix.csv`.
- **Independent transcription-audit stage (Phase II)**: reported 401 attestations and 399 distinct feature cells from scholarly diplomatic editions, of which 77 cells concern ni'mah, 142 recorded regional feature candidates, and 180 Ibrāhīm observations. Some codices were only provisionally identified. Most were *not* newly image-deciphered. The number of attestation records is not a count of independent manuscripts.
- **Additional hold-out tests (Phase III)**: among qualifying positions that had comparative overlap in the selected and conservatively filtered dataset, niʿmah positional prediction scored 59/59; Ibrāhīm positional prediction scored 104/111. The source selection, coding, overlapping relatives, ties and missingness prevent converting these percentages into a probability that an archetype existed.
- **Published secondary benchmark (Phase IV)**: Sidky Appendix B (2020), 63 reported features and 30 manuscript identifiers, with 1,024 transcribed data entries including 75 shaded correction indications. A conservatively encoded binary projection contained 462 observations for 32 suitable features. A chosen eight-tip comparison had one minimum-change tree among 10,395 trees. This is a computation on a **published scholarly collation**, not independent inspection of all the original manuscripts.
- **Independently assembled six-tip regional data**: all 105 trees initially tied. A coverage-selected expanded test reduced this to 35 but lost the distinction when the disputed CPP Q42:30 cell was removed; this smaller independent dataset does not establish a resolved regional genealogy.
- **Source conflicts**: CPP Q42:30 (Corpus Coranicum / Sidky / Alrahawan), CPP Q3:184, BL Q7:43 and E20 Q4:66 need palaeographic verification from the actual manuscript image and attention to correction layers. No competing representation is automatically assumed to be original ink.
- **Manuscript pictures**: data graphics are analytical visualizations, not ancient manuscripts. The decorative banner is clearly not an original folio. Tübingen OpenDigi images are remote institutional images with separate rights provenance. No restricted manuscript facsimile has been copied into this bundle.
- **Narrative inference**: The written common ancestor is strongly supported by inherited position-specific orthographic variation. Sidky independently corroborates four ancestral regional textual types. The historical attribution to ʿUthmān combines those manuscripts, early chronology and historical information; it is not established by an original signed commission document.

Main publications: [Van Putten 2019](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0041977X19000338); [Sidky 2020](https://doi.org/10.5913/jiqsa.5.2020.a005); [Alrahawan 2021](https://doi.org/10.31436/alburhn.v5i2.174).
